Bayān al-Tabdīl — the last type of explanation (closing the Book chapter)

Before the Sunna begins, the matn closes the discussion of bayān with bayān al-tabdīl, which is simply naskh (abrogation). Only the Lawgiver may abrogate; a servant cannot revoke his own statement. Three fiqh consequences follow: exception of the whole from the whole is void, retraction of an iqrār/ṭalāq/ʿitāq is invalid, and a qualifier that contradicts the earlier statement (e.g. 'counterfeit' after 'a loan') is tabdīl according to Abū Ḥanīfa, so it fails even if joined. This links the Qurʾānic part (bayān types) to the Sunna part that follows.
What is bayān al-tabdīl and who can perform it?
وَهُوَ النّسخ فَيجوز ذَلِك من صَاحب الشَّرْع وَلَا يجوز ذَلِك من الْعباد
It is naskh; permitted from the Lawgiver, not from servants.tap to reveal
Is exception of the whole from the whole valid?
وعَلى هَذَا بَطل اسْتثِْنَاء الْكل عَن الْكل لِأَنَّهُ نسخ الحكم
Void, because it would cancel the whole ruling, i.e. naskh.tap to reveal
Can one retract an iqrār, ṭalāq or ʿitāq?
وَلَا يجوز الرُّجُوع عَن الْإِقْرَار وَالطَّلَاق وَالْعتاق لِأَنَّهُ نسخ وَلَيْسَ للْعَبد ذَلِك
No; retraction would be naskh and the servant has no such power.tap to reveal

Kinds of khabar: mutawātir, mashhūr, āḥād

The second source is the Sunna of the Messenger ﷺ. A khabar from him is like the Book in obligating knowledge and action; everything said about khāṣṣ, ʿāmm, mushtarak and mujmal applies equally. The only difference is doubt about its authentic transmission (thubūt and ittiṣāl). On that basis the khabar is of three kinds: mutawātir (no doubt), mashhūr (some doubt) and āḥād (possibility and doubt). The matn then defines each and states the status of each: mutawātir gives certain knowledge and rejecting it is kufr; mashhūr gives ʿilm al-ṭumaʾnīna and rejecting it is bidʿa; both must be acted on by agreement, and the dispute is only about āḥād.
How is the Sunna related to the Book in authority, and where does the difference lie?
خبر رَسُول الله ﷺ بِمَنْزِلَة الْكتاب فِي حق لُزُوم الْعلم وَالْعَمَل بِهِ فَإِن من أطاعه فقد أطَاع الله فَمَا مر ذكره من بحث الْخَاص وَالْعَام والمشترك والمجمل فِي الْكتاب فَهُوَ كَذَلِك فِي حق السّنة إِلَّا إِن الشُّبْهَة فِي بَاب الْخَبَر فِي ثُبُوته من رَسُول الله ﷺ واتصاله بِه

Two kinds of narrator: those known for ijtihād and those known only for memory

Narrators are of two kinds. First, those known for knowledge and ijtihād (the four caliphs, Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, ibn ʿAmr, Zayd b. Thābit, Muʿādh and their like): once their report is sound, acting on it comes before qiyās, and Muḥammad left qiyās for four ḥadīths. Second, those known for memory and ʿadāla but not for fatwā (Abū Hurayra, Anas): if their report agrees with qiyās there is no doubt; if it conflicts, qiyās is better. Examples: wuḍūʾ from what fire touched (rejected by Ibn ʿAbbās with qiyās) and the muṣarrāh report, which our companions left by qiyās and by considering narrator differences.

Conditions for acting on khabar al-wāḥid: not against the Book, the mashhūr Sunna, or the apparent

The matn sets two conditions for acting on a solitary report: it must not oppose the Book or the well-known Sunna, and it must not oppose the ẓāhir (what is apparent and widely known). The proof is the ḥadīth 'many ḥadīths will be reported about me... present them to the Book of Allah' and the report from ʿAlī dividing narrators into three: a sincere Companion, a Bedouin who heard part and narrated by meaning wrongly, and a hidden hypocrite who fabricated. Hence the 'presenting' (ʿarḍ) on the Book and the mashhūr Sunna. Examples: touching the private part (against Q 9:108), marriage without a walī (against Q 2:232) and judging by one witness and an oath (against 'the burden of proof is on the claimant'). The second condition is applied to reports in matters of ʿumūm al-balwā that did not become well-known, with fiqh examples on radāʿ, death/ṭalāq news, qibla and blood in water.
What is the condition for acting on a solitary report?
شَرط الْعَمَل بِخَبَر الْوَاحِد قُلْنَا شَرط الْعَمَل بِخَبَر الْوَاحِد أَن لَا يكون مُخَالفا للْكتاب وَالسّنة الْمَشْهُورَة وَأَن لَا يكون مُخَالفا للظَّاهِر

Khabar al-wāḥid as proof in four categories of right

The matn divides rights into four and gives the level of proof required in each. In pure right of Allah that is not a penalty, a solitary report is accepted (the Prophet accepted the Bedouin on the Ramaḍān crescent). In pure servant's right involving total imposition, number and ʿadāla are required (disputes). In pure servant's right without imposition (muʿāmalāt) a single report is accepted whether ʿadl or fāsiq. In pure servant's right with imposition from one aspect (ʿazl, ḥajr) Abū Ḥanīfa requires either number or ʿadāla.

أما ألآول فَيقبل فِيهِ خبر الْوَاحِد فَإِن رَسُول الله ﷺ قبل شَهَادَة الْأَعرَابِي فِي هِلَال رَمَضَان
Yes; the Prophet ﷺ accepted the Bedouin's testimony on the Ramaḍān crescent.tap to reveal

Ijmāʿ: authority, four ranks, and composite ijmāʿ

The third source is ijmāʿ. After the Prophet ﷺ's death, the consensus of this Umma in the furūʿ of the Dīn is a proof that obligates action, as an honour for the Umma. It has four ranks: (1) Companions' explicit agreement, like an āya; (2) one speaks and the rest are silent, like mutawātir; (3) later generations on a matter where no earlier statement exists, like mashhūr; (4) later agreement on one of the earlier opinions, like a sound āḥād. Only mujtahids count, not laity, theologians or muḥaddiths without insight in uṣūl. Ijmāʿ is also muraqqab (composite: same ruling, differing ʿilla) or not. Composite ijmāʿ ceases to be proof once the ground of one side is shown to be wrong.
Is ijmāʿ a proof?
إِجْمَاع هَذِه الْأمة بَعْدَمَا توفّي رَسُول الله ﷺ فِي فروع الدّين حجَّة مُوجبَة للْعَمَل بهَا شرعا كَرَامَة لهَذِهِ الْأمة
Yes; the Umma's agreement in the furūʿ is a proof obligating action, an honour for it.tap to reveal
What are the four kinds of ijmāʿ?

Lifting of composite ijmāʿ when its basis fails, with fiqh examples

The matn finishes composite ijmāʿ: unlike the earlier kinds, this one can be lifted when the fault of its basis appears. Analogies: a judge's ruling is voided when the witnesses are found to be slaves or retract; al-muʾallafa qulūbuhum dropped from the eight categories because their ʿilla ended; the share of dhawī al-qurbā dropped when its ʿilla ended; vinegar-washed najis cloth is judged pure because the ʿilla of najāsa is gone, which also shows the difference between ḥadath and khabath, since vinegar removes impurity but does not give purity to a ḥadath, which only the purifier, water, does.
Can composite ijmāʿ be lifted?

ʿAdam al-qāʾil bi-l-faṣl (no one distinguishes between the two cases)

A type of ijmāʿ-based argument: when scholars agree on no difference between two cases, proving one entails the other. It has two forms. If both disputes arise from one root (one manshaʾ), it is a proof; if the roots differ, it is not. Examples for the first: if prohibition of legal acts is shown to confirm them, then vowing to fast on the Day of Sacrifice and a fāsid sale giving ownership both stand, since none separates them; if taʿlīq is a cause when the condition occurs, then taʿlīq of ṭalāq and ʿitāq on ownership stands; the freeman's wealth does not bar marrying a slave, and so a Kitābī slave woman as well. Example of the second: vomiting nullifies wuḍūʾ so a fāsid sale gives ownership, or intentional killing demands qiṣāṣ. This is no proof because validity of a branch shows the validity of its own root but not of another root.

What is required of the mujtahid; conflict of proofs

The mujtahid must seek the ruling first from the Book, then the Sunna, by explicit text or indication, as practising opinion while a text is possible is not allowed. Fiqh examples: if he is unsure of the qibla and one person tells him, he may not use taḥarrī; if a just person tells him the water is najis he tayammums. Because doubt in the object is stronger than doubt in thought, a father's wealth right in his son's slave girl removes ḥadd even if he says 'I knew she was haram', but a son with his father's slave girl has taḥarrī recognised. If proofs conflict: between two āyas go to the Sunna, between two Sunnas go to Companions and sound qiyās, between two qiyāses use taḥarrī. A traveller with a pure and a najis vessel makes tayammum; with two garments he does taḥarrī, because water has a substitute and a garment does not. Taḥarrī that has been acted on cannot be cancelled by mere new taḥarrī, though the qibla can change.

Definition of sharʿī qiyās; ʿilla known from the Book, Sunna and ijmāʿ

Sharʿī qiyās is attaching a ruling in a non-stated case to a meaning that is the ʿilla of that ruling in the stated case. The meaning is known as ʿilla by the Book, the Sunna, ijmāʿ, or ijtihād and istinbāṭ. From the Book: frequent going around (ṭawwāf) as ʿilla for lifting the hardship of asking permission, so the Prophet ruled the cat's leftover water not najis and our companions extended it to mouse and snake; and 'Allah intends ease', so the traveller who in Ramaḍān intends another obligatory fast has it count for the other (Abū Ḥanīfa). From the Sunna: only the one who sleeps lying down must make wuḍūʾ (loosened joints); extended to leaning on a support, fainting, intoxication; and 'blood from a vein that burst' extended to cupping and bloodletting. From ijmāʿ: minority as ʿilla of the father's wilāya extended to the minor girl; majority with intellect lifts it; blood bursting nullifies ṭahāra for the mustaḥāḍa and extends to others. Two kinds of qiyās: ruling of the same nawʿ or same jins.

ʿIlla known by opinion and ijtihād (munāsaba and ghalabat al-ẓann)

The third source of ʿilla is opinion and ijtihād: when a description suitable (munāsib) for the ruling is found, accompanies the ruling in the place of ijmāʿ, the ruling is ascribed to it by suitability, not by testimony of the Sharīʿa. Like seeing someone give a poor man a dirham, we judge he did it to meet his need and gain reward. Probability (ghalabat al-ẓann) obliges action when nothing higher exists, like the traveller who thinks water is near and so may not tayammum; so also the taḥarrī cases. This qiyās is voided by a suitable farq. Al-ḥukm: sabab and ʿilla, their difference and uses

The ruling is linked to its sabab, established by its ʿilla and found at its shart. A sabab is what is a path to something through an intermediary (wāsiṭa), like a road to the destination through walking, and a rope to water through lowering. The intermediary is the ʿilla. Examples: opening a stable door, the cage, loosening the slave's fetter cause loss through what the animal, the bird or the slave does. When sabab and ʿilla both exist the ruling is ascribed to the ʿilla, unless ascription to the ʿilla is impossible, then to the sabab. Our companions: handing a knife to a boy who kills himself, no ḍamān; if it falls from his hand and wounds him, ḍamān; putting a boy on a mount that he drives and he falls, no ḍamān; guiding another to a man's property that he steals, to a person he kills, to a caravan he robs, no ḍamān on the guide, unlike the muwdaʿ who guides a thief to the deposit or the muḥrim who points a game animal, because the ḍamān is for abandoning protection or committing a prohibition of iḥrām, not for guidance. Sometimes the sabab is in the meaning of ʿilla: if one drives an animal that destroys something, the driver is liable; a witness who destroys property by testimony then retracts is liable. A sabab may stand in for the ʿilla when the ʿilla is hard to ascertain: complete sleep for ḥadath, valid khalwa for intercourse (full mahr, ʿidda), travel for hardship (rukhsa, even for a sultan touring his realm). A non-sabab may be named sabab figuratively: the oath for expiation, taʿlīq of ṭalāq/ʿitāq on a condition.

Sharʿī rulings are tied to their asbāb (asbāb al-wujūb)

Obligation is hidden from us, so a sign is needed by which the servant knows the ruling is due; hence rulings are ascribed to asbāb. The sabab of ṣalāt is the waqt: the address to perform ṣalāt does not come before the time, the address informs the servant of the sabab of obligation, and the obligation holds for the sleeper and the unconscious who are not addressed. Then there are two ways: (1) the sababiyya moves from the first portion of the time to the second if not performed, and so on to the end, and the servant's state and the quality of that portion are considered: boy becoming bāligh, disbeliever becoming Muslim, ḥāʾiḍ becoming pure in that portion: ṣalāt is obligatory; the reverse (ḥayḍ, nifās, madness, ongoing unconsciousness) drops it; a traveller who becomes resident in the end prays four, resident becoming traveller prays two. For quality: Fajr's end is complete (the time becomes defective only by sunrise, after the time), so sunrise during prayer voids the fard; ʿAṣr's end (reddening of the sun) is defective so the obligation is established defective, so it is valid despite defective time (Ḥanafīs). (2) every portion is a sabab without transfer (since transfer cancels a sababiyya established by Sharīʿa), without multiplying the obligation because the later portion establishes the same thing as the earlier (tarādufu al-ʿilal, many witnesses). Other asbāb: Ramaḍān fast: witnessing the month; zakāt: ownership of the growing niṣāb; Ḥajj: the House; ṣadaqat al-fiṭr: a head one supports; ʿushr: land with real growth; kharāj: land fit for cultivation; wuḍūʾ: ṣalāt (some) or ḥadath (others, from Muḥammad); ghusl: ḥayḍ, nifās, janāba.

The four obstacles (mawāniʿ)

Farḍ, wājib, sunna and nafl

Farḍ in language is estimation; the fard of Sharīʿa are its estimates with no room for increase or decrease. In Sharīʿa it is what is established by a decisive proof with no doubt; its ruling is that action and belief in it are obligatory. Wājib in language is falling; in Sharīʿa it is what is established by a proof with a doubt, such as an interpreted āya or a sound āḥād; some say it is from wajba, agitation, as it is between farḍ and nafl. So it is farḍ in action (it may not be left) and nafl in belief (we need not believe it firmly). Sunna is the followed praiseworthy way in religion, from the Messenger ﷺ or the Companions ('hold to my sunna and the sunna of the rightly guided caliphs'); its ruling is that one is asked to revive it and deserves blame for leaving it without excuse. Nafl in language is increase (and booty is called nafl); in Sharīʿa what is in addition to the farāʾiḍ and wājibāt; one is rewarded for doing it and not punished for leaving it. Nafl and taṭawwuʿ are synonyms.

ʿAzīma and rukhṣa

ʿAzīma in language is resolve at its strongest degree; hence resolve to have intercourse is a return (ʿawd) in ẓihār, and one who says 'aʿzimu' is swearing. In Sharīʿa it is what was obligatory on us from the start, called ʿazīma because of the force of its sabab: the Commander is owed obedience as our God and we are slaves. Its kinds are farḍ and wājib. Rukhṣa in language is ease; in Sharīʿa, turning the matter from hardship to ease because of an excuse in the mukallaf. Its types differ with causes and fall in the end into two kinds: (1) rukhṣa of the action while the prohibition remains, like pardon in crime: uttering words of kufr under duress with a calm heart, insulting the Prophet ﷺ, destroying a Muslim's property, killing unjustly; ruling: if he is patient until killed he is rewarded because he refrained from the forbidden, honouring the prohibition. (2) Changing the quality of the act so it becomes permissible ('whoever is compelled by hunger'): duress to eat carrion and drink khamr; ruling: if he refuses until killed he is sinful, like one who kills himself.

Argument without proof: istidlāl bi-ʿadam al-ʿilla, istiṣḥāb al-ḥāl, and the amber closing

The closing discussion is on arguing without proof (al-iḥtijāj bi-lā dalīl), with several types. (1) Arguing from absence of an ʿilla to absence of the ruling: vomit is not a nāqiḍ because it does not leave the two passages; a brother is not freed on his brother because there is no offspring between them. Muḥammad, asked whether qiṣāṣ falls on a partner of a boy, said no, since the pen is lifted from the boy; the questioner replied then it should fall on the partner of the father; the answer: this is as saying 'so-and-so did not die because he did not fall from the roof', unless the ʿilla is restricted to a meaning, then that meaning is necessary for the ruling and its absence proves absence of the ruling: Muḥammad said the offspring of a usurped thing is not guaranteed because it was not usurped; no qiṣāṣ on a witness who retracts in a qiṣāṣ case because he is not a killer, since usurpation is necessary for ḍamān al-ghaṣb and killing for qiṣāṣ. (2) Istiṣḥāb al-ḥāl is clinging to absence of proof, since existence of a thing does not entail its continuation; so it serves to repel not to impose. Examples: a person of unknown lineage claimed as a slave who then injures someone: the arsh of a free man is not due, since that is imposition and cannot be established without proof; excess bleeding beyond ten days for a woman with a known habit is returned to her habit days and the excess is istiḥāḍa; beginning with bulūgh as mustaḥāḍa, her ḥayḍ is ten days; the missing person (mafqūd) does not make others heirs to his estate, and does not inherit from relatives who die during his absence, repelling another's claim without proof and not creating a claim for him. The matn ends by explaining Abū Ḥanīfa's statement that there is no khums in amber: not a clinging to lack of proof; but his ʿudhr for not holding khums in amber; Muḥammad's exchange: 'why no khums in amber?' 'because it is like fish', 'why no khums in fish?' 'because it is like water, and no khums in it'. Wa-llāhu aʿlam bi-l-ṣawāb.

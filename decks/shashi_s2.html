Chapter 10: al-Amr — definition and what it necessitates

The command (amr) opens the next block of the text. The book defines amr linguistically (a speaker saying 'do!' to another) and legally (imposing the doing of an act on another), then rejects two readings of the claim that amr is restricted to the sīgha 'ifʿal': God's speech is eternal while the sīgha is not, and obligation can arise without the sīgha (belief binds one whom the call never reached). The core ruling follows: an absolute amr (free of any qarīna) necessitates wujūb, because disobeying a command is maʿṣiya just as obeying is ṭāʿa, and the binding force of a command is proportionate to the commander's wilāya, which for Allah is total ownership. The section closes by entering the rule that amr does not demand repetition, illustrated with wakāla and ṭalāq cases.
What is amr linguistically and legally?
الْأَمر فِي اللُّغَة قَول الْقَائِل لغيره افْعَل وَفِي الشَّرْع تصرف إِلْزَام الْفِعْل على الْغَيْر
Linguistically: the speaker's saying 'do!' to another. Legally: an act of imposing the doing of an act on another.tap to reveal
Is the reality of amr restricted to the sīgha 'ifʿal'?
واستحال أَن يكون مَعْنَاهُ إِن حَقِيقَة الْأَمر يخْتَص بِهَذِهِ الصِّيغَة فَإِن الله تَعَالَى مُتَكَلم فِي الْأَزَل
No: Allah speaks eternally ('ʿindanā'), His speech is amr, nahy, khabar and istikhbār, and the sīgha cannot exist in eternity.tap to reveal
Can obligation arise without the sīgha?
واستحال أَيْضا ان يكون مَعْنَاهُ أَن المُرَاد بِالْأَمر لِلْأَمْرِ يخْتَص بِهَذِهِ الصِّيغَة فَإِن المُرَاد للشارع بِالْأَمر وجوب الْفِعْل على العَبْد وَهُوَ معني الِابْتِلَاء عندنَا وَقد ثَبت الْوُجُوب بِدُونِ هَذِه الصِّيغَة أَلَيْسَ أَنه وَجب الْإِيمَان على من لم تبلغه الدعْوَة بِدُونِ وُرُود السّمع
Yes: wujūb is established without the sīgha. Example: īmān became obligatory on one the call never reached, without any revealed command arriving.tap to reveal

Amr and repetition; muṭlaq vs muqayyad ma'mūr bihi

Two linked questions. First, does amr demand repetition? The book says no: 'ifʿal' asks for realising the act in the abridged form, so it addresses the jins (genus) whose default is the minimum and which may carry the whole genus by intention. Cases: wakīl's divorce, 'zawwijnī', 'tazawwaj' to a slave, 'lā yashrab al-māʾ', 'ṭallaqī nafsaki'. Recurring worship (prayers, fasts) recurs because its sabab recurs, not because of amr. Second, the ma'mūr bihi is either muṭlaq (free of time) or muqayyad (timed). The muṭlaq is due ʿalā al-tarākhī provided it is not missed in a lifetime (Muḥammad on nadhr of iʿtikāf/ṣawm, zakāt, ṣadaqat al-fiṭr), with al-Karkhī reporting wujūb ʿalā al-fawr. A timed amr is of two kinds: the time as ẓarf (ṣalāt) or miʿyār (ṣawm), with differing rulings on niyya and on adding other obligations. The section ends with a rule that a servant's own naming of a time does not alter the Lawgiver's rulings (nadhr of a day, khulʿ and sukna).
If a man says 'ṭalliq imraʾatī' and the wakīl divorces her, then he remarries her, can the wakīl divorce her again under the first order?
وَلِهَذَا قُلْنَا لَو قَالَ طلق امْرَأَتي فَطلقهَا الْوَكِيل ثمَّ تزَوجهَا الْمُوكل لَيْسَ للْوَكِيل أَن يطلقهَا بِالْأَمر الأول ثَانِيًا
No: the first command does not cover repeated action.tap to reveal
'Zawwijnī imraʾa': does it cover repeated marriage?
وَلَو قَالَ زَوجنِي امْرَأَة لَا يتَنَاوَل هَذَا تزويجا مرّة بعد أُخْرَى
No; it covers one marriage.tap to reveal

Amr and ḥusn of the ma'mūr bihi; adāʾ and qaḍāʾ (kāmil/qāṣir)

The command from a wise commander indicates the goodness (ḥusn) of what is commanded, which is either ḥasan li-nafsihi (īmān, shukr, ṣidq, ʿadl, ṣalāt) or ḥasan li-ghayrihi (saʿy to Jumuʿa, wuḍūʾ; near to it ḥudūd, qiṣāṣ, jihād). The first drops only by performance or by the Lawgiver's removal (junūn, ḥayḍ, nifās at the end of the time); the second drops when its wāsiṭa drops. The wājib by amr then divides into adāʾ (delivering the very thing due) and qaḍāʾ (delivering its mithl). Adāʾ is kāmil or qāṣir. Kāmil discharges the duty (ghāṣib selling the usurped thing back to the owner, feeding or clothing him unknowingly). Qāṣir delivers the thing with a defect in its quality; it is repaired by a mithl if possible, otherwise the defect lapses except for the sin (tark taʿdīl al-arkān, ṭawāf muḥdithan with dam, zuyūf for jiyād). Qaḍāʾ is also kāmil (mithl in form and meaning, e.g. a qafīz of wheat) or qāṣir (mithl in meaning only, e.g. the value of a sheep). Branches: Shāfiʿī's view on the usurped thing that changes, manāfiʿ not being guaranteed by itlāf, and mithl shar'ī (fidya, diya).
What does a command indicate about the thing commanded?
إِذا كَانَ الْآمِر حكيما لَان الْأَمر لبَيَان أَن الْمَأْمُور بِهِ مِمَّا يَنْبَغِي أَن يُوجد فَاقْتضى ذَلِك حسنه
If the commander is wise, the commanded is good, because amr clarifies that it ought to exist.tap to reveal
Into which two kinds does the ma'mūr bihi fall regarding ḥusn?
ثمَّ الْمَأْمُور بِهِ فِي حق الْحسن نَوْعَانِ حسن بِنَفسِهِ وَحسن لغيره
Ḥasan bi-nafsihi and ḥasan li-ghayrihi.tap to reveal
What is the rule of ḥasan li-nafsihi once it is due?
فَحكم هَذَا النَّوْع أَنه إِذا وَجب على العَبْد أَدَاؤُهُ لَا يسْقط إِلَّا بِالْأَدَاءِ وَهَذَا فِيمَا لَا يحْتَمل السُّقُوط
It does not drop except by performance, this being for what cannot be dropped, like īmān; what can be dropped falls by performance or by the commander's removal.tap to reveal

Chapter 11: al-Nahy (prohibition)

Nahy is of two kinds: prohibition of ḥissī (physical) acts such as zinā, drinking khamr, lying and injustice, and prohibition of sharʿī dispositions (taṣarrufāt) such as fasting on the day of naḥr, ṣalāt in makrūh times, and selling a dirham for two. In the first, the prohibited is the very act, which is intrinsically ugly and never lawful. In the second, the prohibited is something other than what the prohibition was attached to: the disposition remains ḥasan li-nafsihi and qabīḥ li-ghayrihi, so the doer commits ḥarām li-ghayrihi. Hence the Ḥanafī principle that nahy on sharʿī dispositions entails their iqrār (they remain valid): otherwise the servant would be incapable, and nahy of the incapable is impossible from the Lawgiver. Branches: bayʿ fāsid gives milk at qabḍ yet must be rescinded; a marriage prohibited by the text (mushrikāt, the father's wife, muʿtadda, maḥārim, no witnesses) is treated as negation because ḥill is the muwjib of nikāḥ; nadhr of fasting the naḥr day is valid; nafl begun in makrūh times is binding; wiṭʾ of a ḥāʾiḍ has effects; unlawful acts that still carry effects; and the fāsiq is capable of testimony.
What are the two kinds of nahy?
وَالنَّهْي نَوْعَانِ نهي عَن الْأَفْعَال الحسية كَالزِّنَا وَشرب الْخمر وَالْكذب وَالظُّلم
Nahy ʿan al-afʿāl al-ḥissiyya (zinā, khamr, kadhib, ẓulm) and nahy ʿan al-taṣarrufāt al-sharʿiyya (fasting on naḥr, ṣalāt in makrūh times, selling a dirham for two).tap to reveal
What is the ruling of nahy on ḥissī acts?
وَحكم النَّوْع الأول أَن يكون المنهى عَنهُ هُوَ عين مَا ورد عَلَيْهِ النَّهْي فَيكون عينه قبيحا فَلَا يكون مَشْرُوعا أصلا
The prohibited is the very act it falls on, so it is ugly in itself and not lawful at all.tap to reveal
What is the ruling of nahy on sharʿī dispositions?
وَحكم النَّوْع الثَّانِي أَن يكون المنهى عَنهُ غير مَا أضيف إِلَيْهِ النَّهْي فَيكون هُوَ حسنا بِنَفسِهِ قبيحا لغيره وَيكون الْمُبَاشر مرتكبا لِلْحَرَامِ لغيره لَا لنَفسِه
The prohibited is other than what it is attached to: the disposition is ḥasan in itself, qabīḥ li-ghayrihi, and the doer commits ḥarām li-ghayrihi, not li-nafsihi.tap to reveal

Chapter 12: ways of knowing the intended meaning of texts; weak arguments

This chapter gives three tarjīḥ guidelines for deciding which meaning of a nuṣūṣ is intended, each illustrated with a Ḥanafī-Shāfiʿī dispute. (1) If a word is ḥaqīqa in one meaning and majāz in another, the ḥaqīqa is preferred: the girl created from zinā-water is a 'bint' in reality, so the verse of taḥrīm covers her. (2) If one of two possible readings forces takhṣīṣ in the text and the other does not, prefer the one not requiring takhṣīṣ: lāmastum al-nisāʾ taken as jimāʿ keeps the text working in all instances. (3) If a text has two qirāʾāt or two riwāyāt, act in a way that honours both: arjulakum (naṣb/khafḍ) and ḥattā yaṭhurna (takhfīf/tashdīd). Then the text lists weak arguments (tamassukāt ḍaʿīfa): citing a text for what is not in dispute, since the dispute is elsewhere. Examples: the Prophet vomited and did not make wuḍūʾ; 'ḥurrimat ʿalaykum al-mayta'; 'ḥutti-hi thumma uqriṣ-hi'; 'fī arbaʿīn shāt shāt'; 'wa-atimmū al-ḥajj wa-l-ʿumra'; 'lā tabīʿū al-dirham bi-l-dirhamayn'; the khuṭba on the days of tashrīq; each text establishes only what is already agreed.
A word is ḥaqīqa in one meaning and majāz in another: which is intended?
ان اللَّفْظ اذا كَانَ حَقِيقَة لِمَعْنى ومجازا لآخر فالحقيقة أولى
The ḥaqīqa is preferred.tap to reveal
What follows from this dispute?
وَيتَفَرَّع مِنْهُ الْأَحْكَام على المذهبين من حل الْوَطْء وَوُجُوب الْمهْر وَلُزُوم النَّفَقَة وجريان التَّوَارُث وَولَايَة الْمَنْع عَن الْخُرُوج والبروز
The rulings follow on both madhhabs: lawfulness of wiṭʾ, wujūb of mahr, lużūm of nafaqa, mutual inheritance, and the right to forbid her from going out and appearing.tap to reveal
Between two readings, one entailing takhṣīṣ, which is preferred?
وَمِنْهَا أَن أحد المحملين إِذا أوجب تَخْصِيصًا فِي النَّص دون الآخر فالحمل على مَا لَا يسْتَلْزم التَّخْصِيص أولى
The reading that does not entail takhṣīṣ is preferred.tap to reveal

Chapter 13: ḥurūf al-maʿānī (1) — wāw, fāʾ, thumma, bal, lākin

The Ḥanafī usūl uses ḥurūf al-maʿānī to settle fiqh cases of ṭalāq, ʿitāq, iqrār and wakāla. Wāw is for absolute jamʿ (muṭlaq al-jamʿ), not tartīb (Shāfiʿī is reported to make it for tartīb, hence ordering in wuḍūʾ); it may be ḥāl, where it carries the meaning of sharṭ, but only as majāz and with an indicating qarīna (e.g. 'ad ilayya alfan wa-anta ḥurr'). Fāʾ is for taʿqīb with waṣl (immediate connection) and may state ʿilla ('ad ilayya alfan fa-anta ḥurr' frees at once). Thumma is for tarākhī: Abū Ḥanīfa has tarākhī in both lafẓ and ḥukm, the two Ṣāḥibayn in ḥukm only, which changes the number of ṭalāq that fall. Bal corrects an error by making the second take the place of the first: in ṭalāq, 'wāḥida lā bal thintayn' leaves only one for a non-consummated wife and three for a consummated one, whereas in iqrār 'ʿalayya alf lā bal alfān' gives only two thousand (Zufar: three, because iqrār is ikhbār where error is possible). Lākin is for istidrāk after negation and confirms what follows it; it needs ittisāq: 'lā wa-lākinnahu ghaṣb' binds the money, while a non-concordant clause is a new statement.

Chapter 13: ḥurūf al-maʿānī (2) — aw, ḥattā, ilā, ʿalā, fī, bāʾ

The second half of the particle chapter. Aw is for one of the mentioned: 'hādhā ḥurr aw hādhā' equals 'aḥaduhumā ḥurr' with the right of bayān left to the speaker; in negation it negates each (lā ukallimu hādhā aw hādhā: breaks on either), in affirmation takhyīr with general permissibility (kaffāra); it may mean ḥattā (lā adkhulu hādhihi al-dār aw adkhulu hādhihi). Ḥattā is for ghāya like ilā when the first can extend and the second can be its end; otherwise it is jazāʾ (like lām kay), otherwise pure ʿaṭf. Ilā is for ending the ghāya; it either extends the ruling (the ghāya is not included) or serves isqāṭ (the ghāya is included): the elbows and ankles are in ghusl, the knee is part of the ʿawra; 'anti ṭāliq ilā shahr' delays the ṭalāq. ʿAlā is for ilzām (a debt in 'li-fulān ʿalayya alf'), may be bāʾ in muʿāwaḍa or sharṭ ('ṭalliqnī thalāthan ʿalā alf'). Fī is for ẓarf of zamān, makān or fiʿl: Abū Ḥanīfa differs from the two on 'anti ṭāliq fī ghad'; with fiʿl it carries sharṭ ('fī dukhūlik al-dār'). Bāʾ is for ilṣāq, which makes it accompany thaman: the item is the aṣl and the price the tabaʿ; Hence 'bi-kurr min al-ḥinṭa' makes the slave the sold item and the kurr the price, while the reverse is salam.

Chapter 14: kinds of bayān (1) — taqrīr, tafsīr, taghyīr

Bayān is of seven kinds: taqrīr, tafsīr, taghyīr, ḍarūra, ḥāl, ʿaṭf and tabdīl. Bayān taqrīr confirms the apparent meaning of a word that could carry another (qafīz al-balad, naqd al-balad, 'ʿindī alf wadīʿa'). Bayān tafsīr clarifies a word whose intent is not clear ('ʿalayya shayʾ' explained as a garment; 'ʿashara darāhim wa-nayyif'; 'darāhim'); both kinds are valid connected or disconnected. Bayān taghyīr changes the meaning of the speech: taʿlīq and istithnāʾ; it is valid only when connected (mawṣūl). The Ḥanafī-Shāfiʿī dispute: for us the muʿallaq is a sabab at the existence of the sharṭ, not before; Shāfiʿī: taʿlīq is a sabab immediately but absence of the sharṭ prevents its effect. Hence 'in tazawwajtuki fa-anti ṭāliq' to a stranger is void for him but valid for us; the condition of validity of taʿlīq in the absence of milk is that it be attributed to milk or its sabab. Shāfiʿī's consequences: ṭawl al-ḥurra blocks nikāḥ of the slave, no nafaqa for the mabtūta unless pregnant, no nikāḥ of a Kitābī slave; Hanafīs: absence of the sharṭ is no preventer. Istithnāʾ: for us it is speaking of what remains after the exception; for Shāfiʿī the opening is a ʿilla and the istithnāʾ prevents it from working (hence the tafḍīl of ḥafna for ḥafnatayn in the hadith on ṭaʿām).

Chapter 14: kinds of bayān (2) — ḍarūra, ḥāl, ʿaṭf

Three more kinds of bayān. Bayān ḍarūra is a clarification that arises from necessity of the speech itself: in 'wa-warithahu abawāhu fa-li-ummihi al-thuluth' the text establishes shared inheritance of the parents and then states the mother's share, which becomes the bayān of the father's. Hence in muḍāraba and muzāraʿa, stating one party's share while remaining silent about the other's is valid; likewise bequest to two with the share of one stated; and ṭalāq of one of two wives followed by wiṭʾ of one is a bayān that the other was divorced (unlike wiṭʾ in an indefinite ʿitq according to Abū Ḥanīfa, since ḥill al-wiṭʾ in slave women has two roads). Bayān ḥāl is the silence in the place of need: the Lawgiver seeing a matter and not forbidding it shows it is permitted; the shafīʿ's silence on learning of a sale is consent; a virgin's silence on learning of her guardian's marriage is consent; the master's silence on seeing his slave trade is idhn; the defendant's nukūl is rida to the claim (iqrār for the two, badhl for Abū Ḥanīfa); and ijmāʿ is formed by the words of some and silence of the rest. Bayān ʿaṭf is when a measured or weighed item is attached to a vague total, showing the whole is from that jins: 'miʾa wa-qafīz ḥinṭa', 'miʾa wa-thalāthat athwāb'; not 'miʾa wa-thawb'; Abū Yūsuf differs.

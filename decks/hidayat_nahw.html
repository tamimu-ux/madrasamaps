Muqaddima 1: Definition, aim and subject of naḥw

The author opens by naming the book Hidāyah (guidance) and dividing it into a muqaddima, three maqālāt (the ism, the fiʿl, the ḥarf) and a khātima. The muqaddima gives the mabādī (prerequisites) of the science. Faṣl 1 defines naḥw as knowledge of principles by which the states of the endings of the three kinds of words are known (iʿrāb and bināʾ) and how they are combined; its aim is protecting the tongue from verbal error in Arabic speech, and its subject matter is the kalima and the kalām.
Define naḥw and give its aim and subject.
النحو علم باصول تعرف بها احوال اواخر الكلم الثلاث من حيث الاعراب و البناء و كيفيه تركيب بعضها مع بعض
Knowledge of principles that reveal the states of word-endings (iʿrāb/bināʾ) and the way words are combined; aim: protect the tongue; subject: kalima and kalām.tap to reveal

Muqaddima 2: The kalima and its three kinds

A kalima is an utterance put for a single meaning. It is limited to three kinds by rational division: either it does not signify a meaning in itself (ḥarf), or it does and the meaning is attached to one of the three times (fiʿl), or it does without time (ism). The author then gives the ḥadd of each, its ʿalāmāt (identifying marks) and why it is so named: ism from sumūw (elevation), fiʿl from the maṣdar called fiʿl, ḥarf from 'edge'. The ism may be spoken about and with; the fiʿl only with; the ḥarf neither.
Why is the kalima limited to exactly three kinds?
لانها اما ان لا تدل على معنى فى نفسها و هو الحرف او تدل على معنى فى نفسها و اقترن معناها باحد الازمنه الثلاثه و هو الفعل او على معنى فى نفسها و لم يقترن معناها باحد الازمنه و هو الاسم
Because of a rational division: no meaning in itself = ḥarf; meaning + time = fiʿl; meaning without time = ism.tap to reveal
What are the marks of the ism?
و علامته ان يصح الاخبار عنه و به كزيد قائم و الاضافه كغلام زيد و دخول لام التعريف كالرجل و الجر و التنوين و التثنيه و الجمع و النعت و التصغير و النداء
Predicability of and by it, iḍāfa, alif-lām, jarr, tanwīn, dual, plural, naʿt, taṣghīr, nidāʾ.tap to reveal
What are the marks of the fiʿl?
و علامته ان يصح الاخبار به لا عنه و دخول قد و سين و سوف و الجزم نحو قد ضرب و سيضرب و سوف يضرب و لم يضرب
It can be predicated with, not of; takes qad, sīn, sawfa, jazm, conjugates into past/present/command-prohibition, takes the visible rafʿ pronouns, tāʾ al-taʾnīth sākina and nūn al-tawkīd.tap to reveal

Muqaddima 3: The kalām

Kalām is an utterance that contains two kalimāt by isnād (predication) such that the listener gets complete benefit and silence is proper. Isnād needs a musnad and musnad ilayh, so kalām is only formed from two nouns (jumla ismiyya) or a verb and a noun (jumla fiʿliyya). The apparent counter-example of nidāʾ (yā Zayd) is answered: the ḥarf of nidāʾ stands in for adʿū.
What is kalām?
الكلام لفظ تضمن الكلمتين بالاسنادو الاسناد نسبه احدى الكلمتين الى الاخرى بحيث تفيد المخاطب فائده تامه يصح السكوت عليها
An utterance with two words in isnād that gives complete benefit.tap to reveal
From which word-classes can kalām arise?
فعلم ان الكلام لا يحصل الا من اسمين نحو زيد قائم و يسمى جمله اسميه او فعل و اسم نحو قام زيد و يسمى جمله فعليه اذ لا يوجد المسند و المسند اليه معا فى غيرهما
Only from two nouns or a verb and a noun, because the musnad and musnad ilayh occur together only in these.tap to reveal

Qism 1, Bāb 1: the muʿrab ism, iʿrāb and ʿāmil

The first qism treats the ism, which is either muʿrab or mabnī. Bāb 1 (the muʿrab) has a muqaddima in three faṣls, three maqāṣid (marfūʿāt, manṣūbāt, majrūrāt) and a khātima (tawābiʿ). Faṣl 1 defines the muʿrab: every noun composed with another that does not resemble a mabnī al-aṣl (ḥarf, past verb, imperative). Its ending changes with the ʿawāmil, verbally or in estimation. Iʿrāb is that by which the ending of the muʿrab differs; the ism has three iʿrābs (rafʿ, naṣb, jarr). The ʿāmil is what produces them; maḥall al-iʿrāb is the last letter.
What is the muʿrab ism?
و هو كل اسم ركب مع غيره و لا يشبه مبنى الاصل‏ اعنى الحرف و الفعل الماضى و امر الحاضر
A noun in composition that bears no resemblance to a mabnī al-aṣl.tap to reveal
What is the ruling (ḥukm) of the muʿrab?
و حكمه ان يختلف آخره باختلاف العوامل اختلافا لفظيا نحو جائنى زيد رايت زيدا مررت بزيد او تقديريا نحو جائنى موسى و رايت موسى و مررت بموسى
Its ending varies with the ʿawāmil, either audibly or by estimation.tap to reveal
How many iʿrābs does the noun have?
و اعراب الاسم ثلاثه انواع رفع و نصب و جر
Three: rafʿ, naṣb, jarr.tap to reveal

The nine classes of iʿrāb of the ism

The ism's iʿrāb falls into nine classes depending on which word-type it is: (1) the sound singular munṣarif and broken plural with ḍamma/fatḥa/kasra; (2) the sound feminine plural with ḍamma and kasra for naṣb and jarr; (3) the ghayr munṣarif with fatḥa in jarr; (4) the five/six asmāʾ with wāw/alif/yāʾ; (5) the dual with alif/yāʾ; (6) the sound masculine plural with wāw/yāʾ; (7) the maqṣūr and the noun annexed to yāʾ al-mutakallim with estimated vowels; (8) the manqūṣ with estimated ḍamma and kasra; (9) the sound masculine plural annexed to yāʾ al-mutakallim.
What is the first (basic) class of iʿrāb?
ان يكون الرفع بالضمه و النصب بالفتحه و الجر بالكسره و يختص بالاسم المفرد المنصرف الصحيح
Ḍamma/fatḥa/kasra for sound singular munṣarif, quasi-sound and broken plural munṣarif.tap to reveal
How is the sound feminine plural declined?
ان يكون الرفع بالضمه و النصب و الجر بالكسره و يختص بالجمع المؤنث السالم كمسلمات
Ḍamma in rafʿ, kasra in naṣb and jarr.tap to reveal
How is the ghayr munṣarif declined?
ان يكون الرفع بالضمه و النصب و الجر بالفتحه و يختص بغير المنصرف كعمر
Ḍamma in rafʿ, fatḥa in naṣb and jarr (no tanwīn).tap to reveal

Muqaddima 3: munṣarif and ghayr munṣarif (the nine causes)

The muʿrab ism is either munṣarif (no two of the nine causes; takes the three vowels with tanwīn) or ghayr munṣarif (two of the nine causes, or one that stands for two; no kasra and no tanwīn, fatḥa in jarr). The nine causes are ʿadl, waṣf, taʾnīth, maʿrifa, ʿajma, jamʿ, tarkīb, the extra alif-nūn and wazn al-fiʿl. The author gives the condition of each cause, notes which cannot combine, and that a non-munṣarif becomes munṣarif when made indefinite. Finally, when annexed or given alif-lām it takes kasra in jarr (bi-aḥmadikum, bi-l-aḥmar).
What is the ruling of munṣarif and ghayr munṣarif?
و حكمه ان تدخله الحركات الثلاث مع التنوين مثل ان تقول جائنى زيد رايت زيدا و مررت بزيد
Munṣarif: three vowels with tanwīn.tap to reveal
How is the ghayr munṣarif declined?
و حكمه ان لا تدخله الكسره و التنوين و يكون فى موضع الجر مفتوحا كما مر
No kasra and no tanwīn; fatḥa in jarr.tap to reveal
What are the nine causes?
و الاسباب التسعه هى العدل و الوصف و التانيث و المعرفه و العجمه و الجمع و التركيب و وزن الفعل و الالف و النون الزائدتان
ʿAdl, waṣf, taʾnīth, maʿrifa, ʿajma, jamʿ, tarkīb, wazn al-fiʿl, extra alif-nūn.tap to reveal

Maqṣad 1: the marfūʿāt — the fāʿil

The marfūʿāt are eight: fāʿil, nāʾib al-fāʿil, mubtadaʾ, khabar, khabar inna, ism kāna, ism mā/lā, khabar lā li-nafy al-jins. The fāʿil is every noun preceded by its verb or its like, to which the verb is attributed as 'performing it', not as 'upon whom it fell'. Every verb needs a marfūʿ fāʿil, overt or hidden; the verb stays singular with an overt agent; agreement with a pronoun agent. A true feminine requires a feminine verb unless separated; non-true and broken plural are optional. The agent precedes the object when both are maqṣūr and confusion is feared; the object may precede when a qarīna exists.
What is the fāʿil and how is it declined?
و هو كل اسم قبله فعله او شبهه اسند اليه على معنى انه قائم به لا واقع عليه نحو قام زيد و زيد ضارب ابوه و ما ضرب زيد عمرا
Any noun preceded by its verb or verb-like, attributed to it as doer; always marfūʿ.tap to reveal
Does every verb need a fāʿil?
و كل فعل لا بد له من فاعل مرفوع مظهرا كان كذهب زيد او مضمرا كزيد ذهب و ان كان متعديا كان له مفعول به ايضا منصوب نحو زيد ضرب عمرا
Yes — a marfūʿ fāʿil, overt or hidden; a transitive verb also has a manṣūb object.tap to reveal

The nāʾib al-fāʿil (mafʿūl mā lam yusamma fāʿiluh)

The mubtadaʾ and the khabar

Mubtadaʾ and khabar are two nouns free of verbal operators; the first is the musnad ilayh, the second the musnad. Their ʿāmil is abstract: ibtidāʾ. The mubtadaʾ is normally definite and the khabar indefinite, but an indefinite mubtadaʾ may be allowed if described or specified. The khabar may be a noun, or a jumla (nominal, verbal, conditional, or ẓarfiyya) with a returning pronoun. It may precede the mubtadaʾ when it is a ẓarf, and the mubtadaʾ may have several khabars. A special mubtadaʾ is the ṣifa after nafy or istifhām with a noun after it.
What is the default definiteness of mubtadaʾ and khabar?
و اصل المبتدا ان يكون معرفه و اصل الخبر ان يكون نكره
Mubtadaʾ is originally definite, khabar indefinite.tap to reveal
When may an indefinite be mubtadaʾ?
و النكره اذا وصفت جاز ان تقع مبتدا نحو قوله تعالىو لعبد مؤمن خير من مشرك و كذا اذا تخصص بوجه آخر نحو ا رجل فى الدار ام امرئه و ما احد خيرا منك و شر اهر ذا ناب و فى الدار رجل و سلام عليك
If described or specified in another way: after istifhām/nafy, ḍarūra, preceding khabar ẓarf, or in prayer-form.tap to reveal
How to decide which is mubtadaʾ when one is definite and one indefinite or both definite?
و ان كان احد الاسمين معرفه و الاخر نكره فاجعل المبتدا معرفه و الخبر نكره البته كما مر و ان كانا معرفتين فاجعل ايهما شئت مبتدا و الاخر خبرا

Khātima: the tawābiʿ and the naʿt

The nouns discussed so far are muʿrab by origin (through ʿawāmil). Some nouns get their iʿrāb by following the preceding noun; these are the tawābiʿ: every second noun muʿrab with the iʿrāb of its predecessor in one respect. There are five: naʿt, ʿaṭf bi-l-ḥurūf, tawkīd, ʿaṭf bayān, badal. The naʿt indicates a meaning in its matbūʿ or in what is connected to it (jāʾanī rajulun ʿālimun abūhu); a real naʿt agrees in four of ten matters (iʿrāb ×3, taʿrīf/tankīr, ifrād/tathniya/jamʿ, gender); the sababī agrees in the first five only. Its benefits: takhṣīṣ (two nakiras), tawḍīḥ (two maʿrifas), praise, blame, emphasis.

ʿAṭf bi-l-ḥurūf (ʿaṭf al-nasaq)

ʿAṭf bi-l-ḥurūf is a tābiʿ to which is attributed what was attributed to its matbūʿ, both being intended in the nisba. It needs a ḥarf ʿaṭf between. A rafʿ muttaṣil pronoun must be reinforced by a separate pronoun before ʿaṭf; a majrūr muttaṣil pronoun needs repetition of the jār. The maʿṭūf is in the ruling of the maʿṭūf ʿalayhi (ṣifa, khabar, ṣila, ḥāl). ʿAṭf on two maʿmūls of two different ʿawāmil is allowed when the maʿṭūf ʿalayhi is majrūr and precedes the marfūʿ — with other views (Farrāʾ allows absolutely, Sībawayh forbids).

The tawkīd

Tawkīd is a tābiʿ that confirms the matbūʿ in what was ascribed to it or shows that the ruling covers all its parts. It is lafẓī (repeating the same word, also for ḥurūf) or maʿnawī (limited to words: nafs, ʿayn, kilā, kiltā, kull, ajmaʿ, aktaʿ, abtaʿ, abṣaʿ). Nafs and ʿayn vary with the pronoun and form; the attached pronoun requires a separate rafʿ pronoun before nafs/ʿayn. Kull and ajmaʿ only for what has parts that can separate. Aktaʿ and the like are followers of ajmaʿ.

The badal and ʿaṭf al-bayān

The badal is a tābiʿ to which is attributed what was attributed to its matbūʿ and it alone is intended by the nisba. There are four: badal kull min kull, badal baʿḍ, badal ishtimāl, badal ghalaṭ. A nakira badal from a maʿrifa must be described. The ʿaṭf bayān is a non-ṣifa tābiʿ that clarifies its matbūʿ, being the better-known of two names of one thing (qāma Abū Ḥafṣ ʿUmar); it may resemble a badal in wording.

Bāb 2: the mabnī ism — introduction and the ḍamāʾir

Bāb 2 treats the mabnī noun: one that occurs uncomposed (like alif, bāʾ, tāʾ; aḥad, ithnān…) or that resembles a mabnī al-aṣl by need of a qarīna (ishāra), being under three letters, or implying a ḥarf-meaning (hādhā; aḥada ʿashar to tisʿata ʿashar). Its ending does not vary and its vowels are called ḍamm, fatḥ, kasr and sukūn (waqf). There are eight kinds: ḍamāʾir, ishāra, mawṣūlāt, asmāʾ al-afʿāl, aṣwāt, murakkabāt, kināyāt and some ẓurūf. The ḍamīr is a noun placed to indicate a speaker, addressee or absent person already mentioned, attached (muttaṣil) or detached (munfaṣil) — 70 forms; the hidden marfūʿ, ḍamīr al-shaʾn/qiṣṣa, and ḍamīr al-faṣl.

Asmāʾ al-ishāra and the mawṣūlāt

Ishāra nouns indicate an indicated object: five words for six meanings; dhā (m.), dhān/dhayn (m. dual), tā/tī/dhī/ta/dha/tahī/dhahī (f.), tān/tayn, ulāʾ (plural), with hāʾ al-tanbīh in front and a ḥarf al-khiṭāb attached (25 forms by multiplying five by five); dhā near, dhāka middle, dhālika far. The mawṣūl is a noun that cannot be a complete part of a sentence except with a ṣila after it, a khabariyya sentence, with an ʿāʾid returning to it. The mawṣūlāt: alladhī, allatī, duals, alladhīna, allātī/allawātī, mā, man, ayy, dhū of Ṭayy, and alif-lām whose ṣila is an ism fāʿil/mafʿūl. The ʿāʾid maḥdhūf if mafʿūl. Ayy is muʿrab unless the beginning of its ṣila is dropped.

Asmāʾ al-afʿāl, aṣwāt, murakkabāt and kināyāt

Further mabnī kinds: asmāʾ al-afʿāl are nouns meaning an imperative or past (ruwayda Zaydan, hayhāta); on fiʿāl with imperative meaning from triliteral is qiyāsī (nazāl, tarāk); aṣwāt are nouns imitating sounds (qāq, nakh, ṭāq, ṭaq); murakkabāt are nouns composed of two words without isnād or iḍāfa: aḥada ʿashar to tisʿata ʿashar built on fatḥa (ithnā ʿashar muʿrab as dual), and baʿlabakk-type with three dialects; kināyāt are nouns for an unspecified number (kam, kadhā) or story (kayta, dhayta); kam is istifhāmiyya (tamyīz manṣūb singular) or khabariyya (majrūr, singular or plural) with many iʿrāb positions.

The built ẓurūf

The eighth kind of mabnī are some ẓurūf. Those cut off from iḍāfa by dropping the muḍāf ilayh (qabl, baʿd, fawq, taḥt: 'li-llāhi al-amru min qablu wa min baʿdu') are called al-ghāyāt; ḥaythu is built by resemblance to them as it is always annexed, to a sentence usually. Khātima on the ism: maʿrifa and nakira

The khātima treats the remaining rulings of the ism besides iʿrāb and bināʾ. The ism is either maʿrifa or nakira. The maʿrifa is a noun placed for a specific thing, in six kinds: pronouns, proper names, mubhamāt (ishāra and mawṣūl), nouns with alif-lām, the muḍāf to one of them by iḍāfa maʿnawiyya, and the munādā made definite by nidāʾ. The amr, the passive (majhūl), and lāzim vs mutaʿaddī

The amr is a verb by which an action is demanded from the addressed agent; formed by dropping the muḍāriʿ letter, adding a hamzat waṣl (ḍamma if the third letter has ḍamma, kasra if it has fatḥa or kasra) when the next letter is sākin; if voweled no hamza needed. It is mabnī on the sign of jazm. The majhūl: a verb whose agent is dropped and the object put in its place; only for transitive; in the past first letter ḍamma and before last kasra; with initial extra tāʾ, the first two are ḍamma; hamzat waṣl follows the ḍamma; in the muḍāriʿ prefix ḍamma and before last fatḥa; the hollow verb takes kasra or ishmām or wāw. The mutaʿaddī requires a connected thing other than the agent; lāzim is the opposite. A transitive verb may take one, two, or three objects; with ʿalima/arā etc. the first object with the other two behaves like aʿṭā; the second and third like ʿalimtu.

Maṣdariyya, tafsīr, taḥḍīḍ and tawaqquʿ

The maṣdar particles are three: mā, an, anna; mā and an for the verbal sentence (ḍāqat ʿalayhim al-arḍu bi-mā raḥubat = bi-raḥbihā), anna for the nominal sentence (ʿalimtu annaka qāʾim = ʿalimtu qiyāmaka). The tafsīr particles: ay (wa-sʾal al-qarya, i.e. ahl al-qarya) and an which explains only a verb with the meaning of qawl (wa-nādaynāhu an yā Ibrāhīm). The taḥḍīḍ particles are four: hallā, alā, lawlā, lawmā; they have ṣadr and urge the act when entering the muḍāriʿ; when entering a past they reproach (hallā ḍarabta Zaydan); a noun after them implies a verb. Lawlā and lawmā have another meaning: the second sentence is blocked by the existence of the first (lawlā ʿAliyyun la-halaka ʿUmaru), needing two sentences, the first always nominal. Qad is the tawaqquʿ particle: with the past it draws it near the present (qad rakiba al-amīr, 'ḥarf taqrīb'), needs the past to be ḥāl; may confirm (qad qāma Zayd as an answer); with the muḍāriʿ it denotes taqlīl (inna al-kadhūb qad yaṣduq) or taḥqīq (qad yaʿlamu Allāhu); may be separated from the verb by an oath; the verb may be dropped after it (wa-kaʾan qad).

The ḥurūf al-istifhām, sharṭ and radʿ

Tāʾ al-taʾnīth, the tanwīn and nūn al-tawkīd

The last three ḥarf kinds. Tāʾ al-taʾnīth al-sākina attaches to the past to indicate the femininity of its agent (ḍarabat Hind); if a sākin meets it, it takes kasra (qad qāmati al-ṣalāh), and the vowel does not restore a deleted letter (ramat, not ramāt); signs of dual/plural added to the verb are weak (qāmā al-zaydān, qāmū al-zaydūn) and are not pronouns but signs. Tanwīn is a sākin nūn following the vowel of the end of the word, not entering a verb; five kinds: tamakkun (zayd), tankīr (ṣah), ʿiwaḍ (yawmaʾidhin), muqābala (muslimāt) — the first four noun-only — and tarannum at the end of verses. The ʿalam with ibn annexed to another ʿalam loses tanwīn (jāʾanī Zayd ibn ʿAmr). Nūn al-tawkīd confirms the amr and muḍāriʿ with ṭalab, in two kinds: khafīfa (sākina) and thaqīla (mushaddada); fatḥa unless an alif precedes (kasra); enters amr, nahy, istifhām, tamannī, ʿarḍ optionally; and the qasam obligatorily.

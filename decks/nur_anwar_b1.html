Preface: the mabādī of uṣūl al-fiqh and the khuṭba of the Manār

Mullā Jīwan opens, before any text of the Manār, by laying down what every student must know about a science: its definition, subject (mawḍūʿ), goal, issues (masāʾil), the sciences it borrows from (istimdād) and its prerequisites (mabādī). He then explains the Manār's khuṭba line by line: hidāya, the straight path as a mean between excess and neglect, the 'great character' of the Prophet, and the meaning of dīn. Shāshī has no such preface, so everything here is new ('full'); it is the frame inside which all the later chapters are read.
How does Mullā Jīwan define fiqh and what does each clause exclude?
وفي عرف المتشرعين الفقه مخصوص بالعلم الحاصل بجملة من الأحكام الشرعية الفروعية بالنظر والاستدلال
Fiqh is knowing a number of sharʿī, furūʿī rulings by reasoning. 'Knowledge' excludes bare ẓann (even if the common usage calls ẓann fiqh); 'a number' excludes knowing one or two rulings; 'sharʿī' excludes rational and sensory facts; 'furūʿī' excludes knowing that proofs are of such-and-such kinds (that is not fiqh in the uṣūlī usage); 'by reasoning and inference' excludes the knowledge of Allāh, Jibrīl and the Prophet by revelation.tap to reveal
What is 'aṣl' and what are the 'uṣūl al-fiqh'?
أصل كل شيء هو ما يستند تحقق ذلك الشيء إليه فأصول الفقه هي أدلة الفقه
Mullā Jīwan treats 'uṣūl al-fiqh' as an iḍāfa: you cannot know the mudāf before the mudāf ilayhi, so first fiqh, then uṣūl. The aṣl of anything is what its existence is traced back to, so the uṣūl of fiqh are its proofs.tap to reveal
What are the two definitions (ḥadd iḍāfī and ḥadd laqabī) of uṣūl al-fiqh?
Iḍāfī: defined by its two parts — 'aṣl' (what something is built on, whether physically or in the intellect as a ruling is built on its proof) and 'fiqh'. Laqabī (as the name of a particular science): knowledge of the rules by which fiqh is reached with verification. Mullā Jīwan says the definition he gave at the start is the laqabī one.tap to reveal

The four uṣūl al-sharʿ and their derivation

The Manār begins: 'Know that the sources of the Sharīʿa are three — the Book, the Sunna and ijmāʿ of the Umma — and the fourth source is qiyās.' Mullā Jīwan explains why the author says 'sharʿ' and not 'fiqh', which part of the Book is meant, whose ijmāʿ, and why qiyās is deliberately stated separately and after the three, with examples of qiyās derived from each of the three. He closes with the logical derivation of the number four and shows where other things (earlier laws, ʿurf, Companion reports, istiḥsān) are attached. Shāshī treats ijmāʿ and qiyās in detail in its last chapters; here only the frame is new.
How does the Manār count the sources and why 'three, then a fourth'?
الكتاب والسنة وإجماع الأمة
'Al-Kitāb wa al-sunna wa ijmāʿ al-umma' is a badal or bayān of 'three'; then 'the fourth source is qiyās'. The three are the Book, the Sunna and ijmāʿ; qiyās is drawn from them and is fourth in rank and in function.tap to reveal
What are the examples of qiyās derived from Book, Sunna and ijmāʿ?
From the Book: the prohibition of sodomy is analogised to the prohibition of intercourse during ḥayḍ by the ʿilla of 'adhā' taken from 'qul huwa adhan, fa-ʿtazilū al-nisāʾ fī al-maḥīḍ' (2:222). From the Sunna: the prohibition of surplus in gypsum (jiṣṣ) and lime (nūra) is analogised to the six commodities by the ʿilla of kayl/wazn and jins, from 'al-ḥinṭa bi-l-ḥinṭa … mithlan bi-mithl yadan bi-yad'. From ijmāʿ: the prohibition of the mother of the zawja is analogised to the mother of the slave-girl he has had intercourse with, by the ʿilla of juzʾiyya/baʿḍiyya (the child is a part of both).tap to reveal
Why does the author say 'the fourth is qiyās' and give an example from ijmāʿ instead of saying 'four: Book, Sunna, ijmāʿ, qiyās'?
والأصل الرابع القياس
The wording is a hint that the first three are qaṭʿī in origin while qiyās is ẓannī; it also keeps qiyās, which the deniers reject, in view, and signals rank (a ruling that already exists in the first three needs no qiyās). Mullā Jīwan qualifies this as 'usually': a specified ʿāmm and khabar al-wāḥid are ẓannī, while qiyās with a stated ʿilla is qaṭʿī.tap to reveal

The definition of the Book (al-Kitāb)

The Manār defines the Book as 'the Qurʾān, sent down on the Messenger, written in the muṣḥafs, transmitted from him mutawātiran without doubt'. Mullā Jīwan takes each phrase and shows what it excludes (non-heavenly books, earlier scriptures, abrogated recitation, āḥād/mashhūr readings like Ibn Masʿūd's), whether the 'without doubt' adds anything (al-Jaṣṣāṣ vs. the majority), and why the basmala is a verse of the Qurʾān yet its denier is not declared a disbeliever. Shāshī gives no definition of the Book, so the whole is 'full'.
How does the Manār define the Book?
أما الكتاب فالقرآن المنزل على الرسول
The Qurʾān, sent down on the Messenger, written in the muṣḥafs, transmitted from him mutawātiran without doubt — and the name 'Qurʾān' is for the wording and the meaning together. If 'Qurʾān' is a proper name the first word is only a verbal definition and the real definition begins at 'al-munazzal'; if it means 'the recited/joined' it is the genus and what follows is the differentia.tap to reveal
What does 'transmitted from him mutawātiran' exclude?
المنقول عنه نقلا متواترا بلا شبهة
What was transmitted by āḥād — e.g. Ubayy's reading in making up Ramaḍān 'fa-ʿiddatun min ayyāmin ukhara mutatābiʿāt' — and what was transmitted by shuhra only, such as Ibn Masʿūd's 'fa-qṭaʿū aymānahumā' in theft and 'fa-ṣiyām thalāthat ayyāmin mutatābiʿāt' in the expiation of an oath.tap to reveal
Does 'without doubt' add anything beyond 'mutawātir'?
For the majority: no, it is emphasis, since whatever is mutawātir is without doubt. For al-Khaṣṣāf it excludes the mashhūr, which he counts as a type of tawātur but with a shubha because its root is from āḥād. If the lām of 'muṣāḥif' is ʿahd, the unreliable readings are excluded by 'in the muṣḥafs' and 'transmitted from him …' just describes the actual case.tap to reveal

'Qurʾān' names wording and meaning together

After the definition, the Manār adds that the Qurʾān is the name of both the naẓm and the maʿnā, as groundwork for the divisions that follow. Mullā Jīwan draws the Ḥanafī consequence in ṣalāt: Abū Ḥanīfa's permission to recite in Persian, his reasons, his retraction in the later fatwā, and how outside ṣalāt he keeps both wording and meaning in view. He explains why the Manār says 'naẓm' instead of 'lafẓ', and where creed enters: the kalām nafsī is eternal but the meaning that translates the naẓm is created. The Shāshī base has nothing on this topic.
Is 'Qurʾān' the name of the wording, the meaning, or both?
وهو اسم للنظم والمعنى جميعا
Both together: the attributes in the definition (sent down, written, transmitted) are by implication true of the meaning too, because the meaning is as if it had been sent down, written and transmitted by means of the wording. This is why the Manār 'defines' the Qurʾān as naẓm and maʿnā and not as naẓm alone.tap to reveal
Is it valid to recite the Qurʾān in Persian in ṣalāt while able in Arabic?
Abū Ḥanīfa's first ruling: it is valid, because the Qurʾān is the name of wording and meaning and the meaning is preserved in translation; the permission is for a ruling-based excuse (ʿudhr ḥukmī): ṣalāt is intimate converse with Allāh, the Arabic naẓm is miraculous and so eloquent that he may not cope with it, or he may be drawn by its eloquence, rhythm and verse-endings and lose presence with Allāh — Abū Ḥanīfa was engrossed in the ocean of tawḥīd. Outside ṣalāt he respects both: the junub and ḥāʾiḍ may not recite Persian translation nor touch a muṣḥaf so written.tap to reveal

The four divisions: introduction and the first (khāṣṣ, ʿāmm, mushtarak, muʾawwal)

The Manār says rulings are known by knowing the aqsām of naẓm and maʿnā. Mullā Jīwan shows that 'aqsām' here means taqsīmāt — four crossing divisions — and gives the logical derivation of the first: by how the wording is set up (wiḍʿ) it is khāṣṣ, ʿāmm, mushtarak or muʾawwal. The four categories themselves are taught in Shāshī; what is new in Nūr al-Anwār is the derivation and the grammar of the divisions. The ḥāshiya stresses that the restriction to four is by induction, not strict logic.
What does 'aqsām' mean in 'tuʿraf aḥkām al-sharʿ bi-maʿrifat aqsāmihimā'?
وإنما تعرف أحكام الشرع بمعرفة أقسامهما
It means taqsīmāt: there are several divisions, each with its own aqsām. Aqsām of one division are mutually exclusive; those of different divisions combine (a word may be khāṣṣ, ḥaqīqa and ṣarīḥ). The dual 'aqsāmihimā' shows the basis is both the naẓm and the meaning.tap to reveal
What is the derivation of the first division (khāṣṣ, ʿāmm, mushtarak, muʾawwal)?
وهي أربعة: الخاص والعام والمشترك والمؤول
A word signifies one meaning or more by wiḍʿ. If one: either it signifies it as isolated from the afrād (khāṣṣ) or with sharing among the afrād (ʿāmm). If more: either one of the meanings is made preponderant by ta'wīl (muʾawwal) or not (mushtarak). 'Wujūh' here means ṭuruq (ways), not adilla; 'ṣīgha wa lugha' as one stands for wiḍʿ; the first division regards the word's origin, not its use.tap to reveal

The second to fifth divisions: clarity, use, ways of grasping the intent, and the 'covering' fifth

Mullā Jīwan gives the logical derivation of each remaining division. Second, by clarity: ẓāhir, naṣṣ, mufassar, muḥkam, with their opposites khafī, mushkil, mujmal, mutashābih. Third, by use: ḥaqīqa, majāz, ṣarīḥ, kināya. Fourth, by how the mujtahid reaches the intent: ʿibārat, ishārat, dalālat, iqtiḍāʾ al-naṣṣ. Fifth, a division that 'covers' all twenty: knowing their mawāḍiʿ, maʿānī, tartīb and aḥkām. The categories are taught in Shāshī; the logic of the divisions and the status of the fifth are what Nūr al-Anwār adds.
What is the derivation of the second division (ẓāhir, naṣṣ, mufassar, muḥkam)?
وهي أربعة أيضا: الظاهر والنص والمفسر والمحكم
If the meaning is clear: either the word admits ta'wīl or not. If it admits ta'wīl: if the clarity is by the bare ṣīgha, it is ẓāhir; if the speech was driven (masūq) for that meaning, naṣṣ. If it does not admit ta'wīl: if it admits naskh, mufassar; if not, muḥkam. They overlap: the lower is found in the higher, and the difference is by consideration (iʿtibār).tap to reveal
What is the derivation of the third division (ḥaqīqa, majāz, ṣarīḥ, kināya)?
وهي أربعة أيضا: الحقيقة والمجاز والصريح والكناية
If the naẓm is used in the meaning it was set for, it is ḥaqīqa; if in another, majāz. Each of them used with its meaning clear is ṣarīḥ; otherwise kināya. So ṣarīḥ and kināya combine with ḥaqīqa and majāz.tap to reveal
What is the derivation of the fourth division (ʿibāra, ishāra, dalāla, iqtiḍāʾ)?
والرابع في معرفة وجوه الوقوف على المراد
The mustadill either uses the naẓm or the meaning. If he uses the naẓm: where the naẓm was driven for that meaning, ʿibārat al-naṣṣ; where not, ishārat al-naṣṣ. If he uses the meaning: when that meaning is understood lexically, dalālat al-naṣṣ; when the validity of the naẓm depends on it (sharʿan or ʿaqlan), iqtiḍāʾ al-naṣṣ; and other attempts are fāsid istidlālāt. Though it looks an attribute of the mujtahid, it returns to the meaning and, via the meaning, to the word.tap to reveal

Khāṣṣ — the definition analysed and its three forms

Shāshī gives khāṣṣ in two lines; Nūr al-Anwār takes the Manār's definition apart clause by clause, showing what each part excludes (muhmal, mushtarak, ʿāmm), why the Manār says 'lafẓ' here and 'naẓm' in the divisions, and why 'kull' is allowed in a definition though logicians avoid it. It then gives the three forms of khuṣūṣ — jins, nawʿ and ʿayn — and notes that the Uṣūlīs use jins and nawʿ in a sense different from the logicians' (by purpose, not essence).
What do the parts of 'kull lafẓ wuḍiʿa li-maʿnā maʿlūm ʿalā al-infirād' exclude?
أما الخاص فكل لفظ وضع لمعنى معلوم على الانفراد
'Kull lafẓ' is the genus (every word) and the rest is the differentia. 'Wuḍiʿa li-maʿnā' excludes the muhmal, whether unused or used. 'Maʿlūm': if it means 'known as intended' it excludes the mushtarak (its intent is not known); if it means 'known as explanation' it does not. 'ʿAlā al-infirād' means the meaning isolated from the afrād and from another meaning, so it excludes the mushtarak and the ʿāmm (rijāl: its afrād are in view); the dual (muthannā) remains khāṣṣ.tap to reveal
What are the three forms of khuṣūṣ with an example of each?
وهو إما أن يكون خصوص الجنس أو خصوص النوع أو خصوص العين
Khuṣūṣ al-jins: the word's single meaning is a genus covering many that differ in aḥkām/purposes — insān. Khuṣūṣ al-nawʿ: many agreeing in purpose — rajul. Khuṣūṣ al-ʿayn: a single named individual — Zayd; it admits sharing only by multiple impositions.tap to reveal

The ruling of khāṣṣ: it conveys its meaning qaṭʿan and admits no bayān

Shāshī states the ruling of khāṣṣ as a rule of conflict with khabar al-wāḥid and qiyās. What does 'qaṭʿan' mean for khāṣṣ, and who disagrees?

Amr: definition and the exclusive tie between the form and wujūb

The Manār moves from khāṣṣ to two of its species used constantly in the Sharīʿa: amr and nahy. Mullā Jīwan analyses the Manār's definition of amr ('the speaker's words to another, by way of istiʿlāʾ: ifʿal'), says 'amr' here means what the word denotes (musammā), not the word 'amr', and shows what each part excludes. Then 'its intent is tied to a necessary ṣīgha, so that an act is not mūjib': the form and wujūb are tied on both sides, so an act of the Prophet is not in itself a wājib. Shāshī gives definition and exclusivity; the sharḥ adds the disputes, the proofs on both sides and the replies.
How does the Manār define amr and in what sense is it khāṣṣ?
ومنه الأمر وهو قول القائل لغيره على سبيل الاستعلاء: افعل
'Qawl' is the genus, so the speaker's utterance; 'to another' bars self-address; 'ʿalā sabīl al-istiʿlāʾ' excludes iltimās (request between equals) and duʿāʾ (supplication); 'ifʿal' covers every form from the muḍāriʿ with intent of ījāb, so third person, first person and passive forms are included. The form 'lā tafʿal' (nahy) falls outside by 'ifʿal'. Amr is khāṣṣ because it is a word set for the single known meaning, the demand for wujūb.tap to reveal
Is istiʿlāʾ or ʿuluw (actually being higher) the condition of amr?
The Ḥanafī school and the jumhūr: istiʿlāʾ — the speaker counts himself high, even if he is not; so a lower person commanding a higher is blamed (sū' al-adab) but it is still an amr. Some Muʿtazila: ʿuluw is required; a view: neither is required. The matter is detailed in the larger works.tap to reveal
What does 'yakhtaṣṣu murāduhu bi-ṣīgha lāzima' establish?
ويختص مراده بصيغة لازمة حتى لا يكون الفعل موجبا

Amr and repetition (takrār)

The Manār: the amr neither requires nor admits repetition (it is for a single act), and the sharḥ shows its reasons: 'ṭalliqī nafsaki' counts one unless three is intended (a 'fard ḥukmī'), because the ṣīgha is an abbreviation of 'ṭalab al-fiʿl bi-l-maṣdar' and the maṣdar is a single thing; prayers and fasts recur because of their causes, not because the amr recurs; the same logic for the ism al-fāʿil, which gives the Ḥanafī position on the third theft (no cutting of the left hand). The sharḥ gives the opposing views and the replies.
Does amr require or allow repetition?
ولا يقتضي التكرار ولا يحتمله
Neither, in the Ḥanafī school: 'ṣallū' means do the prayer once. It does not demand repetition and it does not bear it. A person intending three in 'ṭalliqī nafsaki' is allowed because three is a single total (fard ḥukmī), not because 'ṭalliqī' denotes number.tap to reveal
Who held what on repetition?
Some (al-Isfarāʾīnī of the Shāfiʿīs): amr requires takrār. Al-Shāfiʿī: it admits takrār with a qarīna (the amr is an abbreviation of 'I ask you for a beating', a nakira in the affirmative that is single but could be general). Ḥanafī: neither. The Ḥanafī reply to the first: al-Aqraʿ asked 'is the ḥajj for every year?' because he felt the hardship of repetition (not because he understood repetition from the form).tap to reveal
Does an amr tied to a condition or attribute repeat with it?
Some of the Shāfiʿīs: yes, it repeats with the repetition of the condition or attribute: ghusl repeats with each janāba ('wa-in kuntum junuban fa-ṭṭahharū') and the qaṭʿ with each theft ('wa-l-sāriqu wa-l-sāriqatu fa-qṭaʿū'). Al-Bushrā ḥāshiya: one report among the Ḥanafīs holds takrār at such a time.tap to reveal

Adāʾ and qaḍāʾ: the definitions and the cause of qaḍāʾ

The Manār divides the ḥukm of amr into adāʾ ('handing over the very thing made wājib by the command') and qaḍāʾ ('handing over its like'). Mullā Jīwan explains 'ʿayn al-wājib' versus Fakhr al-Islām's 'nafs', the meaning of 'tasalīm', why nafl is not adāʾ of a wājib, and how the two words can substitute for each other (a nīya for qaḍāʾ while it is adāʾ and vice versa). The main disputed question is whether qaḍāʾ is established by the same cause as adāʾ (the Ḥanafī verifiers) or needs a new text (the Iraqis, most Shāfiʿīs and the Muʿtazila); the iʿtikāf-in-this-Ramaḍān puzzle is the test case. Shāshī gives the definitions; the disputes and proofs are new.
How does the Manār define adāʾ and qaḍāʾ?
حكم الأمر نوعان: أداء وهو تسليم عين الواجب بالأمر
The ḥukm of the amr is two: adāʾ, handing over the ʿayn of the wājib by the amr (i.e. bringing it out of non-existence into existence at its time); and qaḍāʾ, handing over the like of the wājib by it, not the ʿayn nor in the time. Mullā Jīwan: he should have added 'from his own side' so that a prayer of today's ẓuhr is not counted as qaḍāʾ of yesterday's, since it is God's own obligation, not his; the author omitted it for its fame. Qaḍāʾ is thus the setting aside of a nafl that was his right in favour of the qaḍāʾ that was owed.tap to reveal
Is adāʾ restricted to wājib, and what of nafl?
The qaḍāʾ of a nafl is only due once it became wājib by shurūʿ (a nafl that is begun and broken), so it is no longer nafl but wājib; the performing of a nafl is called adāʾ though not wājib, so 'the ʿayn of the wājib' should be read as 'the established'. The ḥāshiya says other readings: that calling the performance of a nafl 'adāʾ' is a loose usage of the fuqahāʾ and the definition is of the real adāʾ; or that the discussion is about what binds by amr.tap to reveal

The ḥusn of the commanded act: li-ʿaynihi and li-ghayrihi

A commanded act must be good (ḥasan), by necessity of the Commander's wisdom; only the Lawgiver's command shows it. The sharḥ states the three meanings of ḥusn, the positions (Muʿtazila: reason; Ashʿarī: sharʿ; Ḥanafī/Māturīdī: real, known through the command) and the Manār's division into ḥasan li-ʿaynihi (not admitting suqūṭ like īmān; admitting it like ṣalāt; the mulḥaq like zakāt, ṣawm, ḥajj) and ḥasan li-ghayrihi (not performed by the act itself like wuḍūʾ; performed by it like jihād, ṣalāt al-janāza). A sixth 'ḥusn in the condition' (qudra) is explained as a qualification of all. Shāshī has the two kinds; the sharḥ adds the theory and the criticisms.
Must the commanded be ḥasan and how is it known?
ولا بد للمأمور به من صفة الحسن ضرورة أن الآمر حكيم
Yes: the Commander is wise and the wise does not command what is vile, so the commanded must be good in Allāh's sight before the command; it is known by the command. Ḥusn and qubḥ have three meanings: what suits the nature (joy vs grief), a quality of perfection or deficiency (knowledge vs ignorance), and what is reached by praise and blame (worship vs disobedience). The first two are rational by agreement; the third is the dispute.tap to reveal
Who decides ḥusn and qubḥ in the sense of deserving reward or punishment?
Muʿtazila: reason, regardless of sharʿ, and their ḥusn requires the ruling; Ashʿarī: only the Lawgiver, and had the command been reversed the ḥusn would be reversed; Ḥanafī/Māturīdī: they are real (in nafs al-amr) before sharʿ, so that an act deserving thawāb is commanded, an act deserving ʿiqāb is forbidden; the Lawgiver discloses it as medicine discloses benefit and harm. Reason may find it (truthfulness is good, lying evil) or not (fasting the last day of Ramaḍān vs the first of Shawwāl) and sharʿ alone discloses. The two kinds of qudra: mumkina and muyassara

The Manār calls the qudra by which a man can perform what he is bound to do the condition of every command, and divides it into the muṭlaq (mumkina, the minimum) and the kāmil (muyassara, which makes performance easy). Mullā Jīwan shows that it is not the real power that accompanies the act but sound limbs and means that precede it; that the mumkina need only be imagined (tawahhum) rather than real, which explains why a boy who becomes bāligh in the last moments owes the prayer, but not so for ḥajj's provision; and that the muyassara must continue for the wājib to continue — so zakāt, ʿushr and kharāj lapse when the property perishes while ḥajj and ṣadaqat al-fiṭr do not. Shāshī has no topic here, so everything is new (full), with some Shāfiʿī disputes.
What is the qudra that makes the taklīf valid?
والقدرة التي يتمكن بها العبد من أداء ما لزمه
Not the real power, which comes with the act (otherwise the disbeliever who dies a disbeliever would not be commanded, since the real power does not exist), but the soundness of means and limbs preceding the act: ability for wuḍūʾ with water, for facing the qibla with no fear and with knowledge (else taḥarrī), for standing in health, for zakāt with nisāb, for fasting with health and residence, for ḥajj with provisions, a mount, sound limbs and safe roads. The validity of the taklīf rests on this 'istiṭāʿa'.tap to reveal

Nahy: definition and the kinds of qubḥ (li-ʿaynihi / li-ghayrihi)

Nahy on sensory acts and on sharʿī acts: li-ʿaynihi versus waṣfan

The rule the Ḥanafī school draws: the nahy on sensory acts (killing, zinā, drinking wine — whose realities stand unchanged by sharʿ) points to qubḥ li-ʿaynihi, unless there is a proof (like the wiṭʾ in ḥayḍ); the nahy on sharʿī acts (ṣawm, ṣalāt, bayʿ, ijāra — whose meanings sharʿ changed) points to qubḥ in a waṣf attached to them, unless a proof makes it li-ʿaynihi (bayʿ al-maḍāmīn wa-l-malāqīḥ, ṣalāt al-muḥdith). The argument is that if the qubḥ were made li-ʿaynihi in a sharʿī act the nahy would become a nafy and the choice would be void. The corollaries: ribā, fāsid sales and the fast of naḥr are valid in origin and invalid in waṣf, so a fāsid sale yields ownership after qabḍ; while the sale of a free man, maḍāmīn and malāqīḥ and marriage with the maḥārim are read as nafy/naskh.
What does nahy on sensory acts and on sharʿī acts indicate?
والنهي عن الأفعال الحسية يقع على القسم الأول
The nahy on sensory acts (the original meanings known before sharʿ and unchanged by it: killing, zinā, drinking wine) points to the first kind, qabīḥ li-ʿaynihi, unless there is proof to the contrary (wiṭʾ in ḥayḍ is li-ghayrihi, the adhā — 2:222). The nahy on the sharʿī (those whose meanings changed after sharʿ: ṣawm, ṣalāt, bayʿ, ijāra) points to the one to which the qubḥ is attached as a waṣf, unless there is proof that it is li-ʿaynihi (like the nahy on bayʿ al-maḍāmīn wa-l-malāqīḥ and ṣalāt al-muḥdith).tap to reveal
What is the argument that the nahy of sharʿī acts must be qubḥ in the waṣf and not li-ʿaynihi?
Qubḥ comes into the nahy by iqtiḍāʾ (necessity of the Forbidder's wisdom), so it cannot be taken in a way that annuls what required it (the nahy). The nahy means 'no act, by the servant's choice'; if the qubḥ were li-ʿaynihi, the sharʿī act would not exist at all (it would be void and impossible shar'an) and the nahy of an impossible is futile: so it would become a nafy. A servant's choice is whatever suits the act: for a sensory act, power in fact; for a sharʿī act, that its choice be from the Lawgiver, who cannot permit and forbid in the same respect — unless the act is lawful in origin and vile in its waṣf; sensory choice does not suffice for sharʿī acts.tap to reveal

The Shāfiʿī position on nahy and its corollaries

The Manār ends the part with al-Shāfiʿī's position: the nahy, in both sensory and sharʿī acts, points to the first kind, qabīḥ li-ʿaynihi, 'by perfect qubḥ'; since nahy is the counterpart of amr (which points to ḥusn li-ʿaynihi), and the forbidden is a disobedience, which cannot be mashrūʿ. Four corollaries follow: ḥurmat al-muṣāhara is not established by zinā; ghaṣb does not give ownership; a journey of disobedience is not a cause of rukhṣa; and the disbeliever does not own a Muslim's property by seizure. The sharḥ answers each: the Ḥanafī school lets zinā establish ḥurma through the child that is a part of both; lets the usurper own after ḍamān; lets the rukhṣa follow the safar itself; and counts the kuffār's seizing as ownership. The text ends here, at the start of the ʿĀmm.

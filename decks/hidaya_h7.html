Kitāb al-Maʾdhūn (the authorised slave)

Qudūrī lists the scope of an authorised slave's acts. Hidāya supplies the theory: idhn is a lifting of the bar, not agency, which explains his own liability, the untimed idhn, the neck-and-earnings debt, and why many acts (hiring himself, muzāraʿa) are allowed. The disputes are with Zufar and al-Shāfiʿī on type, silence and neck liability, and with the Ṣāḥibayn on gross ghabn, post-ḥajr admission, manumission and favoured sale to the master. The chapter ends with the authorised child.
What is idhn legally, and why does that matter?
الإذن: الإعلام لغة، وفي الشرع: فك الحجر وإسقاط الحق عندنا
Idhn is lifting the bar and waiving the master's right, not agency; so the slave then trades by his own capacity, bears the liabilities himself (no recourse on the master), and the permission cannot be time-limited: allowed for a day or month he stays authorised until interdicted.tap to reveal
Is a master's silence while his slave trades an authorisation?
يصير مأذونا عندنا خلافا لزفر والشافعي
Yes for the Ḥanafīs: silence on seeing his slave buy and sell makes him authorised. Zufar and al-Shāfiʿī: no.tap to reveal
May an authorised slave sell at gross ghabn?
وكذا بالفاحش عند أبي حنيفة ﵀ خلافا لهما
AH: yes, like a free man, since it is still trade. The Ṣāḥibayn: no, gross ghabn is like a donation (taken from the sick man's third), so the permission does not cover it. The authorised child follows the same dispute.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Ghaṣb (usurpation)

Qudūrī gives the rulings on usurpation. Hidāya adds the definition and two ideas that organise the chapter: the usurper acquires ownership (by retroactive payment) when his act changes the thing's name or main benefit, and benefits and growth are a trust, not guaranteed. The main disputes are with al-Shāfiʿī (owner keeps altered thing, rent due, fruit guaranteed), with the Ṣāḥibayn on date of value, coins, tanned hide and instruments, and between AH and Muḥammad/AY on real estate.
How is ghaṣb defined?
أخذ مال متقوم محترم بغير إذن المالك على وجه يزيل يده
Taking valued and protected property without the owner's permission so as to remove his hand. Thus using a slave or loading a beast is ghaṣb, sitting on a carpet is not. With knowledge: sin and liability; without: liability only, since it is the owner's right and the mistaken is not sinful.tap to reveal
What is the evidence for the like-for-like duty?
فَمَنِ اعْتَدَى عَلَيْكُمْ فَاعْتَدُوا عَلَيْهِ بِمِثْلِ مَا اعْتَدَى عَلَيْكُمْ
The Qurʾān (2:194) requires a like; reason adds that like is fairest as it keeps both kind and value, hence least harm. A mixed wheat-barley has no like, so value is due.tap to reveal
If the like is unobtainable, which day's value is taken?
وقال أبو يوسف: يوم الغصب. وقال محمد: يوم الانقطاع
AH: the day of the dispute and judgment, because the shift to value happens by the qāḍī's ruling (he could wait until the like reappears). Abū Yūsuf: the day of usurpation (it joins the no-like class, and the cause is then). Muḥammad: the day of disappearance (the like was owed until then).tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Shufʿa (pre-emption)

Qudūrī sets out who may claim and how. Hidāya argues why: it proves the neighbour's shufʿa against al-Shāfiʿī, fixes the order of claimants, and distinguishes the cause (adjoining) from the occasion (sale) and from the moment of ownership (taking or ruling). The three demands (muwāthaba, taqrīr, khuṣūma) and the delay dispute (AH vs Muḥammad) are the core of procedure, followed by price questions, then what lies outside shufʿa (dower, khulʿ, gifts, invalid sale, partition), what voids it, and the devices.
Does the neighbour have shufʿa?
وقال الشافعي لا شفعة بالجوار
Ḥanafī: yes, by 'The neighbour of the house is more entitled to it' and 'The neighbour is more entitled to his neighbouring property (saqab)', and because his ownership is joined permanently to the buyer's, the joining being the cause (to avert harm of neighbourhood). Shāfiʿī: no, from 'Shufʿa is in what is not divided; once boundaries fall and the road is turned there is no shufʿa'; shufʿa departs from analogy and the partition cost falls only on the original sharer.tap to reveal

As an adjoining neighbour you have a claim; you must demand it at once, then witness it. A delay in suing does not void it (AH); Muḥammad gives a month.discuss first — then tap

Kitāb al-Qisma (partition of shared property)

Qudūrī gives the rulings of partition. Hidāya supplies the theory: partition combines separation (ifrāz) and exchange (mubādala), which tells when the judge may compel it and when consent is needed. Disputes: AH vs the Ṣāḥibayn on proof of inheritance, the divider's fee, slaves and jewels, merging houses; Muḥammad vs the Shaykhayn on upper and lower floors; Muḥammad and al-Shāfiʿī on the dividers' testimony. The closing bāb covers claims of error, third-party claims on a share, and tahāyu' (turn-taking), which Qudūrī does not discuss.
Heirs claim real estate in their hands: is proof of death needed?
وقال صاحباه: يقسمها باعترافهم
AH: the judge does not divide until they prove the death and number of heirs (his division is a judgment against the dead whose estate is still his: debts and bequests attach until partition). Ṣāḥibayn: he divides on their admission (hand shows ownership and nobody disputes), recording that it was by their admission. For movables, by all, division goes ahead; and if they claim to have bought the real estate, he divides.tap to reveal

The present heirs need proof of death and the heirs (AH); the qāḍī appoints an agent for the absent brother's share. Ṣāḥibayn accept their admission.discuss first — then tap

Kitāb al-Muzāraʿa (sharecropping)

Qudūrī gives rulings. Hidāya turns to the central dispute: AH calls muzāraʿa invalid (nahy, qafīz al-ṭaḥḥān, unknown wage), the Ṣāḥibayn valid (Khaybar, need). It lists eight conditions and the four lawful forms, then the stipulations that cut the partnership, the fair wage if invalid, compulsion, death and end of term.
Is muzāraʿa valid?
وهي فاسدة عند أبي حنيفة، وقال: هي جائزة
AH: invalid. The Ṣāḥibayn (and al-Shāfiʿī only as incidental to musāqāh): valid. Definition: a contract on cultivation for part of the produce. Fatwā follows the Ṣāḥibayn for need and the people's practice (qiyās yields to ta'āmul, as in istiṣnāʿ).tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Musāqāh (orchard sharecropping)

Musāqāh follows muzāraʿa: AH invalidates it, the Ṣāḥibayn and al-Shāfiʿī allow it. Hidāya adds the reason for allowing an unstated term, fruit already grown, who may carry on after death, trees that cannot be hired, and the invalid planting arrangement.
Is musāqāh valid?
وقالا: جائزة إذا ذكر مدة معلومة وسمى جزءا من الثمر مشاعا
AH: void. The Ṣāḥibayn: valid with a known term and a common share. Shāfiʿī: valid (muʿāmala), muzāraʿa only incidentally (analogy to muḍāraba, a partnership in the increase, not the capital). Ḥanafī reply: the proofs are those of muzāraʿa.tap to reveal
Is a term needed?
وفي الاستحسان: إذا لم يبين المدة يجوز ويقع على أول ثمر يخرج
Qiyās needs a term (a hire in sense); istiḥsān: if not stated, it applies to the first crop (ripening has a known time and differs little), and also the first crop of seed in vegetable roots. A planted but not yet fruiting sapling needs a term; palms or roots 'to look after' without end are void.tap to reveal
Which trees?
وقال الشافعي في الجديد: لا تجوز إلا في الكرم والنخل
Ḥanafī (and Ṣāḥibayn): palms, trees, vines, vegetables and aubergine roots, because it is by need which is general; the Khaybar report does not restrict, as its people also tended other trees. Shāfiʿī (new): only vines and palms, as it is by report.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Dhabāʾiḥ (slaughter)

Qudūrī gives the rules. Hidāya explains them: dhakāt is the condition of lawfulness and purity (ordinary and emergency), who may slaughter, the basmala dispute (deliberate vs forgetful: Ḥanafī, Shāfiʿī, Mālik), the number of vessels (AH, AY, Muḥammad, Shāfiʿī, Mālik), tools, disliked acts, the foetus (AH vs the Ṣāḥibayn), forbidden animals and the sea creatures.
Omitted basmala: deliberate versus forgetful?
وقال الشافعي: أكل في الوجهين. وقال مالك: لا يؤكل في الوجهين
Ḥanafī: deliberate omission, carrion; forgetful, eaten. Shāfiʿī: eaten in both. Mālik: not eaten in either. The ruling is the same for the hawk, dog and arrow. Shāfiʿī's view contradicts consensus on deliberate omission; AY and the masters say no ijtihād there, and a judge's ruling for its sale does not stand.tap to reveal
How many vessels must be cut?
وهو حجة على الشافعي في الاكتفاء بالحلقوم والمريء
Four: ḥulqūm, marīʾ and two wadajs; 'Cut the wadajs with whatever you like'. AH: any three, or most of them. Abū Yūsuf (and Qudūrī attributes it to both Ṣāḥibayn): ḥulqūm, marīʾ and one wadaj; Muḥammad: most of each vessel. Shāfiʿī: ḥulqūm and marīʾ. Mālik: all four. If half is cut: not eaten.tap to reveal
A dead foetus in the slaughtered animal?
وقال أبو يوسف ومحمد رحمهما الله: إذا تم خلقه أكل وهو قول الشافعي
AH (with Zufar, Ḥasan): not eaten whether covered in hair or not. Abū Yūsuf and Muḥammad and Shāfiʿī: eaten if fully formed, by 'The slaughter of the foetus is the slaughter of its mother' and it is a part of the mother. AH: it is a separate life, with its own ghurra, manumission, bequest; blood is not released by wounding the mother.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Uḍḥiya (sacrifice)

Qudūrī sets rules. Hidāya argues that it is wājib (AH, Muḥammad, Zufar) vs sunna (AY, Shāfiʿī), then conditions, shares (seven; Mālik), time (after prayer for towns, devices, the three days vs Shāfiʿī), missed sacrifices, defects and their limits (AH vs AY/Muḥammad), the co-sharer's intention, the meat and the swapped-animals istiḥsān.
Is uḍḥiya obligatory?
وعنه أنها سنة، ذكره في الجوامع وهو قول الشافعي
AH, Muḥammad, Zufar, Ḥasan and one report of AY: wājib. AY (another) and Shāfiʿī: sunna. Ṭaḥāwī: wājib for AH, a confirmed sunna for AY and Muḥammad. Sunna proof: 'Whoever intends to sacrifice must not take from his hair or nails' (intention is against obligation), and it would bind a traveller. Wājib proof: 'Whoever has means and does not sacrifice, let him not approach our prayer-ground', and the time is named after it (Yawm al-Aḍḥā).tap to reveal
Why can a cow serve seven but a sheep one only?
نحرنا مع رسول الله ﷺ البقرة عن سبعة والبدنة عن سبعة
By analogy it would be for one (one shedding of blood is the act), left by Jābir's report: 'We sacrificed a cow for seven and a camel for seven with the Prophet'. Fewer than seven are allowed, not eight, nor a share below a seventh. Mālik: one household even over seven, not two households, from 'On every household each year an uḍḥiya and ʿatīra' (meaning its head). Meat shared by weight.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Karāhiya (3): istibrāʾ, sale, miscellany

The last faṣls of Karāhiya cover istibrāʾ (cause, period, device), kissing and handshake, sales that are disliked (dung, hoarding, price-fixing, arms, Makka land), a single informant's report, the Muṣḥaf, dhimmīs in the mosque, castration, supplications, a slave merchant's gift, a foundling's finder and the judge's stipend.
Why and when is istibrāʾ due?
ألا لا توطأ الحبالى حتى يضعن حملهن، ولا الحيالى حتى يستبرأ
From the Prophet on the Awṭās captives: 'Pregnant women are not to be had until they give birth, nor the others until one period'. It is due on the buyer, not the seller, because he intends intercourse; the cause is new ownership with possession (so also by gift, inheritance, khulʿ, kitāba), even for a virgin or when bought from a child. The period must come after possession.tap to reveal
How long for one who does not menstruate?
وعن زفر سنتان وهو رواية عن أبي حنيفة
A month for the young or old (Ḥanafī); Muḥammad: four months and ten days, or two months and five days (like a slave woman's widowhood); Zufar: two years (a report from AH). Not by the period before possession, nor by one that arrives before receipt.tap to reveal
Is a device to avoid istibrāʾ disliked?
ولا بأس بالاحتيال لإسقاط الاستبراء عند أبي يوسف خلافا لمحمد

Kitāb al-Ashriba (drinks)

Qudūrī lists four forbidden drinks. Hidāya explains: khamr is raw grape juice (AH adds foam), its ten rulings, the lesser rank of the other three forbidden drinks, the lawful drinks (AH vs Muḥammad), juice reduced to a third (AH/AY vs Muḥammad/Mālik/Shāfiʿī), vinegar-making (against Shāfiʿī), dregs, and the arithmetic of the third.
What is khamr?

Kitāb al-Ṣayd (hunting)

Qudūrī sets the rules; Hidāya adds the evidence (verses and ʿAdī's ḥadīth), the proof of training (three times vs judgement), the logic of basmala at sending, what spoils a catch (eating, partners, vanishing, falls), blunt weapons, cut limbs (against Shāfiʿī) and two shooters.
How is training proven?
وهذا عندهما وهو رواية عن أبي حنيفة
Dog: leaves eating three times; hawk: returns when called (a dog can be beaten to leave, a hawk cannot; a dog is tame and learns to stop its habit, a hawk is wild). Ṣāḥibayn (and one report of AH): three times (fewer may be satiety; three is the period of trial like a khiyār term). AH (Aṣl): no fixed number; the hunter's sound judgment, since fixed amounts are by text. On AH's first report the third catch is lawful; for the Ṣāḥibayn not (it is a untrained dog's game).tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Rahn (1): formation, liability, costs

Qudūrī gives the rules of pledge. Hidāya explains them by the nature of the pledgee's hand (istīfāʾ) which makes the pledge guaranteed, against al-Shāfiʿī (trust, lā yughlaq) and Zufar (full value). It adds the offer as pillar and possession as binding (against Mālik), procedure on demand, neutral keeper, misuse and costs.
What is rahn and what proves it?
جعل الشيء محبوسا بحق يمكن استيفاؤه من الرهن كالديون
Legally: making a thing held for a right that can be taken from it, such as debts. Proof: 'a pledge in hand' (2:283) and the Prophet buying food from a Jew and pledging his armour; consensus; and it is a security for payment like surety is for the duty.tap to reveal
Is the pledge guaranteed by the pledgee?
وقال الشافعي ﵀: هو أمانة في يده، ولا يسقط شيء من الدين بهلاكه
Ḥanafī: yes, in the pledgee's liability (hand of istīfāʾ). Shāfiʿī: a trust; nothing of the debt falls on loss ('The pledge is not closed; its gain is its owner's, its loss his', and the pledge is only a security). Ḥanafī: consensus of Companions and followers that it is guaranteed; 'lā yughlaq' means total confiscation (Karkhī); 'pledge' implies perpetual holding; hand of istīfāʾ with loss completes payment; the thing itself is a trust (so upkeep is the pledger's).tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Rahn (3): the neutral keeper, disposals, offences, growth

The remaining bābs: the neutral keeper (against Mālik), agency to sell, a third party's claim, the pledger's sale (suspended), manumission by the pledger, destruction by pledger, stranger or pledgee, the loaned pledge, the pledged slave's offence (AH vs Ṣāḥibayn), growth, increase of pledge or debt, and waiver of the debt.
Is a pledge with a neutral keeper valid?
وقال مالك: لا يجوز
Ḥanafī: yes; the keeper's hand is the pledger's as to keeping (trust) and the pledgee's as to value (guaranteed), like two persons. Mālik: no (his hand is the owner's; he claims from him if evicted). Neither party may take it back; if it perishes, in the pledgee's liability; if the keeper hands it to either, he is liable. If he pays the value, it cannot be pledged in his hand (he would be both payer and receiver) but they agree to a third place.tap to reveal
Agent to sell the pledge at maturity?
وإن شرطت في عقد الرهن فليس للراهن أن يعزل الوكيل
Valid. If stipulated in the contract it becomes an attribute of the pledge and the pledger cannot dismiss, nor forbid a credit sale; the pledgee cannot dismiss either; the pledger's death does not end it and the agent sells without the heirs. The agent's death ends it, his heirs do not succeed (agency is not inherited). If stipulated after, either compulsion to sell is disputed (sounder: compelled).tap to reveal
If the keeper sells the pledge?
فقد خرج من الرهن، والثمن قائم مقامه فكان رهنا
The price stands for it and is a pledge, even if not yet received; if lost it is the pledgee's loss; the same if the slave is killed and the killer pays value, or a slave is given in compensation (it replaces life and flesh).tap to reveal

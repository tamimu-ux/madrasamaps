Al-Kalam wa ma yata'allafu minhu

The opening chapter fixes the vocabulary of the whole science. Kalam is a useful, complete utterance; kalim is three or more words; kalima is one word; qawl covers all. Every word is an ism, fi'l or harf, each recognised by its own signs, and anything which carries a verb's meaning but lacks a verb's signs (saha, hayhala) is an ism fi'l. Everything later in the book (i'rab, mubtada) presupposes these definitions.
What is kalam according to the grammarians, and what does each word in the definition exclude?
كلامنا لفظ مفيد كاستقم ... واسم وفعل ثم حرف الكلم
Kalam = an utterance (genus; includes meaningful and meaningless) + useful (excludes meaningless like 'diz') + giving a complete benefit on which silence is fitting (excludes a single word and the kalim). The example istaqim: an imperative verb with a hidden subject 'anta'.tap to reveal
From what can a kalam be composed?
ولا يتركب الكلام إلا من اسمين نحو زيد قائم أو من فعل واسم كـ" قام زيد" وكقول المصنف "استقم" فإنه كلام مركب من فعل أمر وفاعل مستتر والتقدير استقم أنت
Only from two nouns or a verb and a noun (at least a musnad and musnad ilayhi).tap to reveal
Why 'kalamuna'?
وإنما قال المصنف كلامنا ليعلم أن التعريف إنما هو للكلام في اصطلاح النحويين لا في اصطلاح اللغويين وهو في اللغة اسم لكل ما يتكلم به مفيدا كان أو غير مفيد. _________ = حذفت من خير وشر لكثرة استعمالهما وأصلهما أخير وأشر،
To show the definition is the grammarians' technical usage, not the linguists', for whom kalam is anything spoken.tap to reveal

Al-Mu'rab wa'l-mabni

A word is mu'rab when its ending changes with the agent, and mabni when it keeps one fixed form. For Ibn Malik the single cause of bina' in nouns is likeness to the harf, which shows in four ways (form, meaning, function, constant need). Among verbs the past and the imperative are built, the mudari' is declined except when a nun of emphasis or the feminine nun is attached; every harf is built. This chapter prepares the next, which gives the signs of i'rab.
What are the two divisions of the noun and what is the cause of bina' for nouns?
والاسم منه معرب ومبني ... لشبه من الحروف مدني
Mu'rab and mabni. For Ibn Malik the only cause of bina' is nearness to the harf; the noun is built because it resembles it.tap to reveal
What are the four kinds of resemblance to the harf?
كالشبه الوضعي في اسمي جئتنا ... والمعنوي في متى وفي هنا وكنيابة عن الفعل بلا ... تأثر وكافتقار أصلا
Wad'i (ta and na in ji'tana), ma'nawi (mata, hunna), isti'mali/niyaba (nazal, without being affected), iftiqari (the relative nouns). The mabni nouns fall in six chapters: pronouns, conditional and interrogative nouns, demonstratives, relatives, verb-nouns, and some others.tap to reveal
What is the mu'rab among nouns?
ومعرب الأسماء ما قد سلما ... من شبه الحرف كأرض وسما
The noun free from harf-resemblance, e.g. ard and sama. It is sound (not ending in a weak letter) or weak, and fully declinable or diptote.tap to reveal

Signs of i'rab

The four classes of i'rab (raf', nasb, jarr, jazm) take damma, fatha, kasra and sukun by default. Six conditions of exceptions follow: six nouns, dual and sound masculine plural take letters; sound feminine plural and diptote take harakat in substitution; the verbs with dual alif, plural waw or ya of the addressed take the nun; and the weak-ended noun or verb have their signs estimated (or shown where light). This is the core reference for any later i'rab exercise.
What are the original signs of i'rab and which class has which?
فارفع بضم وانصبن فتحا وجر ... كسرا كذكر الله عبده يسر
Raf' by damma, nasb by fatha, jarr by kasra, jazm by sukun; whatever else is used is substitute. Raf' and nasb are for nouns and verbs, jarr for nouns only, jazm for verbs only.tap to reveal
How are the six nouns declined?
وارفع بواو وانصبن بالألف ... واجرر بياء ما من الأسما أصف
Waw for raf', alif for nasb, ya for jarr: ja'a abuhu, ra'aytu abahu, marartu bi-abihi.tap to reveal
What conditions govern dhu and fam in this declension?
من ذاك ذو إن صحبة أبانا ... والفم حيث الميم منه بانا
Dhu must mean 'possessor' (not the Ta'i relative 'dhu') and fam must lose its mim.tap to reveal

Al-Nakira wa'l-ma'rifa

The chapter splits nouns into indefinite and definite. Nakira is tested by al (with a defining effect) or by standing in the place of such a word; everything else is ma'rifa, which comes in five or six kinds listed in one verse, each taken up in turn in the following chapters: pronoun, demonstrative, proper name, al-defined, relative.
What is nakira?
نكرة قابل أل مؤثرا ... أو واقع موقع ما قد ذكرا
What accepts al with the effect of defining (rajul -> al-rajul), or what takes the place of such a word (dhu, ma meaning 'possessor' or 'something').tap to reveal
What are the kinds of ma'rifa?
وغيره معرفة كهم وذي ... وهند وابني والغلام والذي
Pronoun (hum), demonstrative (dhi), proper name (Hind), al-defined (al-ghulam), relative (alladhi), and what is annexed to any of them (ibni). The text then treats each in turn.tap to reveal
4 · Why?

Why is nakira defined by acceptance of al and not by meaning?

Because acceptance of al is an observable test usable on any word; the definition by meaning (not specific) would be vague. Words in nakira positions (dhu, ma) are brought in with the clause "or stands in its place".explain it in your own words — then tap

The pronoun (within al-Nakira wa'l-ma'rifa)

The pronoun is the first definite noun treated. It is built throughout. It is attached (ka, ya, ta, na) or detached (ana, iyyaka) or hidden in four obligatory places. The principle: where the attached can be used, the detached is not allowed; the exceptions are restriction, fronting and a few others. When two accusative pronouns meet, the more specific goes first. The chapter closes with the nun of protection added before the speaker's ya.
What is the pronoun?
فما لذي غيبة أو حضور ... كأنت وهو سم بالضمير
What denotes the absent or the present (speaker or addressee): huwa, anta, ana.tap to reveal
What is the attached pronoun?
وذو اتصال منه ما لا يبتدا ... ولا يلي إلا اختيارا أبدا
What cannot be put first in a sentence and cannot follow illa except in poetry by necessity (ma akramtu ilaka is wrong).tap to reveal
What is the state of all pronouns and what are the forms common to cases?
وكل مضمر له البنا يجب ... ولفظ ما جر كلفظ ما نصب
All are built, by resemblance to the harf in rigidity (they cannot be diminutive, dual or plural). Na serves raf', nasb and jarr; every attached nasb pronoun is also used for jarr (ka, ha, ya); alif, waw and nun are for the absent and others (qama, i'lama).tap to reveal

Al-'Alam (the proper name)

The 'alam is a name that specifies its referent without recourse to speaker, addressee or absent. It is divided into ism, kunya and laqab (laqab put last), into murtajal and manqul, and into single words and composites (idafi, mazji, isnadi). Names of genera (Usama) share the lexical rules of a personal name but are generic in meaning. The Basri/Kufi disagreement arises over a single-word ism followed by a single-word laqab.
What is the 'alam?
اسم يعين المسمى مطلقا ... علمه كجعفر وخرنقا
A noun specifying its referent absolutely; 'noun' is a genus, 'specifies its referent' excludes the nakira, and 'absolutely' excludes the other definites like the pronoun (which depends on speaker/addressee/absent). Examples: Ja'far, Khirniq (a woman), Qaran, 'Adan, Lahiq (horse), Shadqam (camel), Hayla (sheep), Washiq (dog).tap to reveal
Into what is the 'alam divided as regards ism, kunya and laqab? How are they ordered?
واسما أتى وكنية ولقبا ... وأخرن ذا إن سواه صحبا
Ism, kunya, laqab. When the laqab accompanies another it comes last: Sa'id Kurz, not Kurz Sa'id. With the kunya, either order is open (Abu 'Abd Allah Zayn al-'Abidin or Zayn al-'Abidin Abu 'Abd Allah).tap to reveal
If the ism and laqab are both single words, what is the construction?
وإن يكونا مفردين فأضف ... حتما وإلا أتبع الذي ردف
Annex the first to the second (Sa'id Kurz); if not both single, make the second an apposition.tap to reveal

The demonstrative noun

Dha points to a masculine singular, dhi/ti/ta to a feminine singular, dhan/tan and dhayn/tayn to duals and ula to plurals. Distance is marked by adding the kaf of address (far) with or without lam; Ibn Malik recognises two ranks while the majority hold three. For place the forms are huna, hunaka/hunalika and thamma. The Basrans hold the alif of dha to be original; the Kufans hold it to be added.
What are the demonstratives for the singular?
بذا لمفرد مذكر أشر ... بذي وذه تي تا على الأنثى اقتصر
Dha for the masculine singular; dhi, dhih, ti, ta (also tih, dhat) for the feminine.tap to reveal
What are the demonstratives for the dual?
وذان تان للمثنى المرتفع ... وفى سواه ذين تين اذكر تطع
Dhan (m.) and tan (f.) in raf'; dhayn and tayn in nasb and jarr.tap to reveal
What is the demonstrative for the plural?
وبأولى أشر لجمع مطلقا ... والمد أولى ولدى البعد انطقا
Ula for any plural, masculine or feminine; usually for rational ones but also for irrational (as in the verse dhammal-manazila ba'da manzilat al-liwa wal-'aysha ba'da ulaika al-ayyam). Two dialects: mad (Hijaz, in the Quran) and qasr (Tamim).tap to reveal

The relative (mawsul)

The relative is a noun that needs a silah and an 'a'id. The specific relatives are alladhi and allati with their duals and plurals; man, ma, al, dhu of Tayy and dha after ma/man serve all genders and numbers. Harfi relatives (an, anna, ma, kay, law) give a masdar. The silah is a declarative sentence or semi-sentence; al takes only an explicit adjective. The 'a'id may be dropped under conditions, depending on its case. Ayy is declined except when annexed with the head of the silah dropped, when it is built on damma.
What are the specific relative nouns?
موصول الأسماء الذي الأنثى التي ... واليا إذا ما ثنيا لا تثبت بل ما تليه أوله العلامة ... والنون إن تشدد فلا ملامة
Alladhi (m.), allati (f.). The dual drops the ya and adds alif (allad(h)ani, allatani; ayn/ayni in nasb and jarr); the nun may be doubled (verse on tashdid) as compensation.tap to reveal
What are the relative nouns for the plural?
جمع الذي الألى الذين مطلقا ... وبعضهم بالواو رفعا نطقا باللات واللاء التي قد جمعا ... واللاء كالذين نزرا وقعا
Al-ula and al-ladhina for the plural masculine (ladhina in every case; Hudhayl say aladhun in raf'). Allati, allat, allai and ula'i (al-la') for the feminine plural; al-la' sometimes used for the masculine.tap to reveal

Al-Ibtida' (the mubtada)

The mubtada is the noun free of verbal agents at the opening of a sentence. It has either a khabar (Zayd 'adhir) or a marfu' that replaces the khabar (a-sar dhani), the latter being a wasf after negation or istifham (Basrans) or without it (Kufans, Akhfash). The khabar and its rulings

The khabar is the part completing the benefit. It is single (rigid with no pronoun, derived carrying one), a sentence (with a linking rabit unless it is the mubtada in meaning), or a zarf/jar wa majrur (tied to an elided word). A nakira is allowed as mubtada only when it gives a benefit. The default order is mubtada then khabar; the khabar must follow in five cases and must come first in four; either may be dropped when known, and the khabar obligatorily after lawla, an oath, wa of accompaniment, and before a hal. A mubtada may have several khabars. The Basri/Kufi differences recur (fronting, pronoun in rigid khabar, ibraz).
What is the khabar?
والخبر: الجزء المتم الفائدة ... كالله بر والأيادي شاهده
The part that completes the benefit (Allah barr, the benefits are witness). It admits the fa'il as an objection; the answer: the khabar is the part that forms with the mubtada a sentence, the fa'il forms it with the verb.tap to reveal
When the khabar is a sentence, what must link it to the mubtada?
ومفردا يأتي ويأتي جمله ... حاوية معنى الذي سيقت له وإن تكن إياه معنى اكتفى ... بها: كنطقي الله حسبي وكفى
If the sentence is the mubtada in meaning (nutqi Allahu hasbi) no link is needed; otherwise a rabit: a pronoun (Zayd qama abuhu; sometimes dropped: al-saman manwan), an ishara (libas al-taqwa dhalika khayr), repeating the mubtada (al-haqqa ma al-haqqa) or a general term including it.tap to reveal
Does a khabar carry a pronoun?
والمفرد الجامد فارغ وإن ... يشتق فهو ذو ضمير مستكن وأبرزنه مطلقا حيث تلا ... ما ليس معناه له محصلا
A rigid khabar has none (Zayd akhuka); a derived one has a pronoun (Zayd qa'im). The pronoun is shown if the khabar is not in fact the predicate of its mubtada's meaning (Zayd Hind dariburha huwa).tap to reveal

Kana and its sisters

Kana and its sisters enter the nominal sentence, raising the mubtada as ism and making the khabar their khabar in nasb. Eight act unconditionally, four need a negation and dama needs 'ma' of duration. Their forms mostly conjugate; laysa and dama are rigid. The khabar may stand in the middle, but not before ma al-nafiya, and Ibn Malik also forbids it before laysa. Most can be tamm. The ma'mul of the khabar cannot be next to kana except a zarf or jar. Kana may be added, dropped after in/law, or replaced by ma after 'an'.
What is the action of kana and its sisters, and which are they?
ترفع كان المبتدا اسما والخبر ... تنصبه ككان سيدا عمر ككان ظل بات أضحى أصبحا ... أمسى وصار ليس زال برحا فتىء وانفك وهذي الأربعة ... لشبه نفي أو لنفي متبعه ومثل كان دام مسبوقا بما ... كأعط ما دمت مصيبا درهما
They raise the mubtada as their ism and put the khabar in nasb as their khabar: kana, zalla, bata, adha, asbaha, amsa, sara, laysa; then zala, bariha, fati'a, infakka (need nafy or shibh nafy); and dama (needs ma of duration).tap to reveal

Fasl: ma, la, lat and in resembling laysa

Four negative particles can act like laysa, raising a noun and putting the khabar in nasb. Ma acts in the Hijazi dialect (not Tamimi) provided no 'in' is added, the nafy is not broken by illa and the order is kept; its khabar often takes the added ba. La acts on nakira only; lat acts on hin and loses one of its two terms; the negative in is a matter of disagreement between Basrans, Kufans and later grammarians.

Af'al al-muqaraba (verbs of proximity)

These eleven verbs enter the nominal sentence like kana but demand a mudari' khabar. They fall in three groups: proximity (kada, karaba, awshaka), hope (asa, harra, ikhlawlaqa) and inception (ansha'a, tafiqa, ja'ala, akhadha, 'aliqa). Asa usually has 'an' in its khabar and kada usually not; harra and ikhlawlaqa require it; the inception verbs forbid it. Only kada and awshaka conjugate. 'Asa, ikhlawlaqa and awshaka can be tamma with an + verb as fa'il, and 'asa alone may or may not carry a pronoun after a preceding noun (Tamim v. Hijaz).
What is the action of kada and its sisters?
ككان كاد وعسى لكن ندر ... غير مضارع لهذين خبر

Inna and its sisters

Inna and five sisters enter the nominal sentence acting the reverse of kana: nasb the ism and raf' the khabar (the Kufans leave the khabar raf' as it was). The ism comes before the khabar, except that a zarf/jar khabar may come first. The hamza of inna is opened when a masdar can replace anna and its terms, obligatorily kasra at the start of sentences, silah, oath, quotation and similar, and optional in a few places. The lam of ibtida' is delayed to the khabar. A connected ma stops them (except layta); and the lightened forms (inn, an, ka'an) mostly stop acting, with specific rules for each.
What is the action of inna and its sisters, and what do they mean?
لإن أن ليت لكن لعل كأن ... عكس مالكان من عمل كإن زيدا عالم بأني ... كفء ولكن ابنه ذو ضغن
They act the reverse of kana: nasb the ism and raf' the khabar (inna Zaydan 'alimun bi-anni kuf'un). Meaning: inna, anna: emphasis; ka'anna: simile; lakinna: istidrak; layta: wish; la'alla: hope and fear.tap to reveal
May the khabar come before the ism?
وراع ذا الترتيب إلا في الذي ... كليت فيها أوهنا غير البذي
No, the order is: particle, ism, khabar; except when the khabar is a zarf or jar wa majrur: layta fiha ghayra al-badhi' ('Alas, in it is a non-vulgar one) and layta huna... ; it is obligatory if the ism has a pronoun returning to the khabar (layta fi al-dar sahibuha). The ma'mul of khabar cannot come before ism unless it is a zarf/jar.tap to reveal
When is the hamza of inna opened (anna)?
وهمز إن افتح لسد مصدر ... مسدها وفي سوى ذاك اكسر
When a masdar can take the place of anna and its two terms: as subject (yu'jibuni annaka qa'im), as object ('arastu annaka qa'im) or after a jar (ajibtu min annaka qa'im). Otherwise it is kasra. When a single word fills the place (zanantu Zaydan innahu qa'im) it is kasra since it is in the place of the second object and cannot be reduced to a masdar.tap to reveal

La allati li-nafy al-jins

La for negating the genus is the third of the abrogating harfs. It acts like inna on a nakira. An annexed or annexed-like ism is put in nasb; a single ism is built on what it would have in nasb (fatha, ya or kasra). The khabar is raf' - by la (Ibn Malik), or as the khabar of a mubtada (Sibawayh for the single ism) - and often dropped. A conjunct with repeated la admits five forms; a single adjective directly after the ism three. A preceding hamza of istifham leaves it unchanged, though for wish the Basrans differ (Mazini vs Sibawayh).
What is the action of 'la' for negating the genus and where?
عمل إن اجعل للا في نكره ... مفردة جاءتك أو مكرره
Like inna: it nasbs the ism and rafs the khabar, on a nakira whether single or repeated (la ghulama rajulin qa'im; la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah). It must be explicit (nass) in negating the whole genus; la rajulun qa'iman (raf') could be said to negate one.tap to reveal
Must ism and khabar be nakira, and can anything separate la from its ism?
فانصب بها مضافا أو مضارعه ... وبعد ذاك الخبر اذكر رافعه
Yes, both are nakira; a definite found there is interpreted as a nakira (qadiyya wa la aba Hasanin laha: la musamma bi-hadha al-ism). If the ism is separated from la, la is neglected (la fiha ghawl).tap to reveal

الفوز الكبير في أصول التفسير

al-Fawz al-Kabir fi Usul al-Tafsir (The Great Triumph on the Principles of Tafsir)

The whole book: Muqaddima; Bab 1 (the five sciences of the Quran, with jadal against mushrikun, yahud, nasara, munafiqun, and tadhkir bi-ala' / ayyam / mawt); Bab 2 (points of difficulty: gharib, naskh, asbab al-nuzul, hadhf, ibdal, taqdim/ta'khir, ziyada, muhkam/mutashabih, kinaya, ta'rid, majaz aqli); Bab 3 (the Quran's style: arrangement, fawasil, takrar, ijaz); Bab 4 (types of mufassirun, athar, tawjih, istinbat, gharaib, zahr/batn, other gifted sciences). Bab 5 (gharib and asbab collection) is announced by the author but is not part of this Arabic edition.

Preface and plan of the five abwab

Shah Wali Allah says he recorded these benefits for his brothers who ponder the Book of Allah, hoping that merely grasping these principles opens a wide road to the meanings of the Quran, more than a lifetime spent reading tafsir books. He names the work al-Fawz al-Kabir fi Usul al-Tafsir and divides it into five abwab. The Arabic edition contains the first four; the fifth (gharib and asbab al-nuzul collection) is announced but not included.
What are the five abwab of al-Fawz al-Kabir?
الباب الأول: في العلوم الخمسة التي يدل عليها القرآن الكريم نصًا
Bab 1: the five sciences the Quran indicates explicitly; Bab 2: why the nazm is obscure for people of this age and how to remove it; Bab 3: the subtleties of Quranic style; Bab 4: methods of tafsir and reasons for differences among Sahaba and Tabiʿun; Bab 5: gharib and asbab al-nuzul that a mufassir must memorise.tap to reveal

Bab 1: the five sciences of the Quran (overview)

All the Quranic meanings fall into five sciences: ahkam (rulings), jadal (disputation with four false groups), tadhkir bi-ala' Allah, tadhkir bi-ayyam Allah, and tadhkir bi-al-mawt wa-ma baʿdahu. Each has its natural specialist (faqih, mutakallim, wa'iz). The Quran presents them in the manner of the early Arabs, not in the manner of later scholars: no matn-style brevity, no usuli precision of hudud and qayud, no mantiqi division of proofs, no later adab-style transitions. The author also reinterprets asbab al-nuzul: the real cause of revelation is the moral and doctrinal sickness of the addressees, not the particular anecdotes.
Into what five sciences do all the meanings of the Quran fall?
ليعلم أن المعاني التي يشتمل عليها القرآن لا تخرج عن خمسة علوم:
Ahkam, jadal, tadhkir bi-ala' Allah, tadhkir bi-ayyam Allah, tadhkir bi-al-mawt wa-ma baʿdahu. The last three are referred to the wa'iz/mudhakkir for detail.tap to reveal
In what manner does the Quran present the five sciences?
وقد جاءت هذه العلوم في القرآن الكريم على طريقة العرب الأولين، لا على منهج العلماء المتأخرين
In the manner of the early Arabs: no matn-style concision or usuli qualification in ahkam verses; commonly accepted (mashhur, musallam) and rhetorical (khitabi) proofs rather than mantiqi-refined burhan; no later adab-style thematic transitions.tap to reveal
What does the author say is the real sabab al-nuzul of the Quran?
فالسبب الحقيقي - إذن - في نزول آيات المخاصمة هو وجود العقائد الباطلة في نفوس المخاطبين.
Jadal verses were revealed because of false beliefs in the addressees; ahkam verses because of widespread injustice and corrupt deeds; tadhkir verses because of their heedlessness toward Allah's favours, His days and death. Particular anecdotes matter only in some verses containing taʿrid of an event.tap to reveal

Jadal (1): the two methods; the mushrikun's religion and deviations

The Quran argues with four false groups by two methods: (1) merely stating the false belief and declaring its ugliness and falsity; (2) identifying their doubts and then answering them with demonstrative (burhani) or rhetorical (khitabi) proofs. The mushrikun called themselves hunafa' on the millah of Ibrahim; they retained its shaʿa'ir and many shara'iʿ and believed in Allah as Creator, but deviated into shirk, tashbih, tahrif, denial of the Hereafter, doubting the Messenger, evil deeds and bidʿa. The author defines shirk as ascribing Allah's exclusive attributes to others, and explains it by the analogy of a king delegating to governors.
By what two methods does the Quran argue?
١ - الأول أن تذكر العقيدة الباطلة وينص على شناعتها وفسادها واستنكارها، فحسب.
(1) Mention the false creed and declare its ugliness, falsity and repugnance only; (2) identify the doubts these groups fell into, then present answers with demonstrative or rhetorical proofs.tap to reveal
How does the author explain the mushrikun's shirk by analogy?
إنما إشراكهم في أمور خاصة ببعض العباد، إذ أنهم يظنون أن سلطانًا عظيمًا من السلاطين العظام
They did not associate partners in creation of jawahir or governing great matters, but thought that Allah, like a great sultan, delegates particular affairs to favoured servants whose pleasure and anger then have effect, and whose intercession is accepted. So they thought it necessary to curry favour with these servants as a means of acceptance with the true King.tap to reveal
What objections did mushrikun raise against risala?
كيف يكون النبي محتاجًا إلى الطعام والشراب؟
They assumed the messenger must resemble the sender: so 'why not an angel as messenger?' and 'why is revelation not sent to each person individually?'. Human attributes veiled the prophets' true beauty from them.tap to reveal

Jadal (2): Quranic replies to the mushrikun (shirk, tashbih, tahrif, qiyama, risala)

The Quran answers each deviation with its own method. Against shirk: demand dalil, break reliance on ancestral taqlid, assert that Lord and servants are not equal, quote the consensus of prophets (21:25) and show that stones fall below human perfection. Against tashbih: dalil, the necessity of homogeneity between father and child, and the ugliness of ascribing to Allah what they dislike for themselves (52:39). Against tahrif: no such thing came from imams of the hanif millah; it is the invention of the non-ma'sum. Against istibʿad of resurrection: qiyas on reviving dead earth, tanqih al-manat (power and ease of repetition), and agreement of the people of scriptures. Against deniers of risala: precedent, the nature of wahy, and wise reasons beyond their grasp. The repeated style is explained by their being mostly mushrikun.
How does the Quran refute shirk (four ways)?
٣ - ثالثًاَ: بيان إجماع الأنبياء والمرسلين على هذه الحقيقة الكبرى:
1) Demand their dalil and break taqlid of forefathers; 2) show that no equality exists between Lord and servants, the Lord alone deserves the utmost veneration; 3) show consensus of prophets; 4) show the ugliness of idol worship, stones fall below human perfection so cannot be gods.tap to reveal
How does the Quran refute tashbih?
٣ - ثالثًا: بيان شناعة نسبة ما هو مكروه ومذموم لديهم - كما تدل على ذلك مواقفهم - إلى الله - تعالى -
1) Demand dalil and refute taqlid; 2) necessity of jins-correspondence between father and child (against the claim of sonship); 3) show the ugliness of ascribing to Allah what they themselves dislike, e.g. daughters. It addressed people used to mashhurat and poetic imagination.tap to reveal
How does the Quran refute their tahrif?
١ - أنه لم يؤثر عن أئمة الملة الحنيفية (إبراهيم وإسماعيل وغيرهما من الأنبياء الماضين) ما يقولون من تحريفات.
By proving two things: nothing of their distortions is reported from the imams of the hanif millah, and these are inventions of the non-ma'sum.tap to reveal

Jadal (3): the Jews: tahrif, kitman, ilhaq, denial of Muhammad's risala

The Jews believed in the Torah but strayed through: tahrif (lafzi and maʿnawi), kitman of Torah verses, ilhaq of what is not from it, negligence in its rulings, extreme group partisanship, denying the Prophet's risala and bad manners to him, and avarice. The author, following Ibn ʿAbbas, holds tahrif lafzi occurred only in translation, not in the original; tahrif maʿnawi is corrupt interpretation, of which he gives three examples (the fasiq/kafir distinction attached to a name; permanence of rulings meaning impossibility of naskh; honorific titles like 'sons' read as ethnic privilege). Examples of kitman: rajm and the glad tidings of Hajar and Ismaʿil. Ilhaq arises from istihsan and taqlid. Doubts about Muhammad arose from the differences between the prophets' ways. The purpose of prophethood is islah, not creating the norms of birr/ithm, and diversity of shara'iʿ resembles the physician's different prescriptions.
Was the original Torah verbally distorted?
وقد تحقق لدى الفقير أن تحريفهم اللفظي إنما كان في ترجمة التوراة وما يجري مجراها، لا في أصل التوراة وهذا هو قول ابن عباس ﵄.
The author's tahqiq: lafzi tahrif was in the translation of the Torah and similar, not in its original; he attributes this to Ibn ʿAbbas. Maʿnawi tahrif is the real distortion.tap to reveal
What is the first example of maʿnawi tahrif?
والحقيقة أن مناط الحكم هو الإيمان بالله - تعالى - وباليوم الآخر
Every millah distinguishes the religious fasiq from the kafir-jahid: the kafir is threatened with eternal Fire, the fasiq may leave it by intercession of prophets. Each scripture phrases this by its own name (Torah for Jews, Injil for Christians, Quran for Muslims). But the real manat is iman in Allah and the Last Day, belief in the prophet sent to them, obedience and avoidance of prohibitions, not membership of a named community; the Jews supposed every Jew would be saved and stay in the Fire only a few days.tap to reveal
What is the second example: the claim that naskh is impossible?
ولكن اليهود حملوا ذلك على استحالة النسخ.
Rulings in each earlier millah were given according to the interests of its age and with regard to sound customs, and adherence was commanded; but the adherence was relative (idafiyya), valid until a new prophet brings a new message. The Jews understood it as the impossibility of naskh. Likewise, they supposed that Yaʿqub's wasiyya meant 'Judaism' specifically.tap to reveal

Jadal (4): the Christians and the hypocrites

The Christians believed in ʿIsa but erred by dividing the Lord into three aqanim (Father = origin of the world, Son = the first emanation, Ruh al-Qudus = abstract intellects) and holding the Son incarnated in the Messiah. The author answers the two textual proofs: 'son' in the Old Testament idiom means beloved/chosen, and ascriptions of Allah's acts to ʿIsa are hikaya, like a king's envoy saying 'we conquered the fort'. He also explains the Christian error over the crucifixion (shubbiha lahum) and over the Fāraqlīṭ (the Paraclete), which the Quran shows to be Muhammad. The hypocrites are two kinds: nifaq i'tiqadi (inner disbelief, unknowable after the Prophet) and nifaq 'amali (weak faith, worldly motives, tribal partisanship), the latter very common. He closes with the principle that Quranic disputation is not about vanished people: every fitna of the Prophetic era has a modern counterpart, so the exegete should extract the universal meanings, not the particulars.
How does the author answer the Christian proof from the word 'son' in the Injil?
في العهد القديم كان يستعمل بمعنى المقرب والمحبوب والمختار
Granting the verses are ʿIsa's own and not additions, 'Ibn' in the Old Testament meant 'close, beloved, chosen', supported by many indications in the Injil itself.tap to reveal
How does he answer the claim that ʿIsa ascribed divine acts to himself?
أنها على طريق الحكاية، مثل أن يحكي رسول الملك عنه فيقول: فتحنا البلد الفلاني
If such sayings are authentically his, they are hikaya: the messenger is a translator for the sender and the real act is the sender's. He also allows a special kind of wahy by direct impression on the heart, which may sound like ascribing acts to himself.tap to reveal
What was the Christian error about the crucifixion and how does the Quran correct it?
﴿وَمَا قَتَلُوهُ وَمَا صَلَبُوهُ وَلَكِنْ شُبِّهَ لَهُمْ﴾
The matter was made ambiguous; they took ʿIsa's raf' to heaven for killing and crucifixion and inherited the belief. Sayings in the Injil hinting at killing are only his report of the Jews' audacity, and the disciples' words arise from confusion.tap to reveal

Bab 1, Fasl 2: the remaining sciences (ahkam, tadhkir bi-ala' Allah, bi-ayyam Allah, bi-al-mawt)

The Quran addresses people only with what their knowledge can contain. Allah's existence is affirmed in brief since it is innate; the attributes are expressed through noble human attributes qualified by 'Nothing is like Him', and treating the attributes is tawqifi. Ala' Allah are limited to blessings every Arab and non-Arab grasps (heavens, rain, springs, crafts). Ayyam Allah are the events of reward and punishment, chosen from stories already familiar to the Arabs (Nuh, ʿAd, Thamud, Ibrahim, Bani Isra'il), told only as far as the lesson needs. The oft-repeated stories (Adam and Iblis, the prophets with their peoples, Musa and Firʿawn, Dawud/Sulayman, Ayyub, Yunus, Zakariyya, ʿIsa) are distinguished from those told once or twice (Idris, Yusuf, Khidr, Talut, Dhu al-Qarnayn, Ashab al-Kahf, Ashab al-Fil...). Tadhkir bi-al-mawt covers death, barzakh, the Last Hour, hashr, mizan, sirat, Jannah and Nar. The ahkam are built on the Ibrahimic millat with Arab custom as raw material; the Quran gives the outline and the Sunna the detail, and occasion-bound verses (Badr, Uhud, Khandaq, Hudaybiya, Ifk...) are a kind of tadhkir bi-ayyam Allah that needs the story to be understood.
How does the Quran speak of the divine attributes?
ثم يجعل قوله الفصل: ﴿لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ﴾
It selects noble human attributes that people praise and use them for subtle meanings beyond intellect, with 'Nothing is like Him' as the antidote, and forbids attributes that invite false beliefs such as a child, weeping, anxiety.tap to reveal
How does the Quran prove the Creator?
فأثبت القرآن - مثلًا - وجود الخالق إجمالًا، إذ أن علمه مركوز في فطر بني آدم
Briefly, because knowledge of the Creator is rooted in human fitra, equal among all, and no people in moderate climes denies it.tap to reveal
Which of Allah's blessings does the Quran mention?
لم يتعرض القرآن العظيم من آلاء الله - تعالى - وآيات قدرته العجيبة إلا لما يستوى في إدراكه الحضري والبدوي والعربي والعجمي
Only what is equally graspable: creation of heavens and earth, sending rain, springs, inspiring crafts, plus people's changed behaviour in ease and hardship. It omits the special spiritual favours to prophets and saints and the pleasures of kings.tap to reveal

Bab 2, Fasl 3: asbab al-nuzul and tawjih

The difficulty here is again the difference between early and later usage. When the Salaf say 'nazalat fi kadha' they often mean only that the verse applies to a case (during or after the Prophet's time), not that it was the occasion; it suffices that the root ruling fits, not every qayd. Many hadiths mixed into this chapter are not asbab at all (citations, recitations, fada'il, naming the unnamed, etc.). The mufassir needs only two things: the stories the verses allude to (taʿrid), and the story that specifies a general or turns speech from its apparent sense. Long stories of earlier prophets are from Ahl al-Kitab ('do not believe them nor deny them'). Verses may depict a type (the happy and wretched persons) without a particular individual; some are presented as question and answer without anyone having asked; 'taqdim/ta'khir' of the Companions means rank, not time (Ibn ʿUmar on kanz). Tawjih is solving apparent doubt, contradiction, difficulty or the point of a qayd, with examples: sister of Harun, walking on faces, la yatasa'alun vs yatasa'alun, Safa and Marwa, qasr 'in khiftum'. Ibn Ishaq/al-Kalbi's excess is rejected; knowing every sabab is not a condition.

Ibdal is a many-branched device: one thing is said in place of another for purposes the book does not exhaust. Bab 2, Fasl 5: muhkam, mutashabih, kinaya, ta'rid, majaz ʿaqli

The muhkam is what language experts grasp only one meaning from, judged by the perception of the first Arabs and not by modern hair-splitters, whose over-subtlety turns the muhkam into mutashabih and the known into the unknown. The mutashabih bears two meanings, whether from pronoun reference (two antecedents), ishtirak (lamastum), ʿatf to near or far (wa arjulikum, kasr reading), or ʿatf versus isti'naf (wa-l-rasikhun fi al-ʿilm). Kinaya is affirming something without intending it, but to move the mind to its lazim, customary or rational (ʿazim al-ramad = generous). Depiction of the meaning by a sensory image is akin to it (the Devil with his cavalry as a highwayman's leader; sadd in front and behind; aghlal; adhmum ilayka janahak), and the same is done in gesture (the man who mimes a sword). Ta'rid mentions a general ruling or censure while pointing to a particular person with some of his particulars; the Prophet's way was 'ma bal aqwam yafʿaluna kadha'; Quranic examples: wa ma kana li-mu'min (Zaynab and her brother) and wa la ya'tali ulu al-fadl (Abu Bakr). Majaz ʿaqli is ascribing an act to a non-agent or making a non-object an object by some relation (the emir built the palace; the spring caused the herbs to grow).

Bab 3, Fasl 2: division into ayat and the unique style of the fawasil

The Sunna of Allah divides suras into ayat as poems into bayts; both are 'nashid' that delights speaker and hearer, but bayts are bound by the ʿarud and qawafi codified by al-Khalil, while ayat rest on a natural, general wazn and qafiya. The author explains the shared aesthetic root: when the parts of speech correspond, the soul feels pleasure and expects a like; when the second bayt fulfils it the pleasure doubles and a shared qafiya triples it. Peoples differ in how they achieve correspondence (Arab zihafat, ʿarud, qawafi; Persian dislike of zihaf; Indian letter-count meters; villagers' melodies with radif; Greek maqamat; six Indian ragas), and the shared kernel is only approximate correspondence (tawafuq taqribi). The Quran follows this common aesthetic rather than conventional codes, since adherence to conventions shows inability, while the common beauty that accompanies speech in every state is the ijaz itself. Hence the wazn of the Quran is the extent of the breath (long, medium, short: al-Nisa'; al-Aʿraf/al-Anʿam; al-Shuʿara'/al-Dukhan), the qafiya is the madda ending (alif, waw, ya' over any letter: yaʿlamun/mu'minin/mustaqim; kharuj/marij/tahid/nar/fawaq/ʿujab), the alif-ending wide qafiya (karima, hadithan, basira), iltizam ma la yalzam (Maryam, Furqan), and shared letters (mim in al-Qital, nun in al-Rahman). Sura endings sometimes differ from their openings (idda/hadda in Maryam; salaman/kiraman in al-Furqan; tin/sajidin/tanzurun in Sad). Other devices: ending with a phrase of divine attributes (wa huwa al-hakim al-khabir), itnab (fas'al bihi khabiran), taqdim/ta'khir and qalb/ziyada (Il Yasin, Tur Sinin), short ayat balanced with long (khudhuhu fa-ghulluhu...), three-legged ayat (yawma tabyaddu wujuh), two fasilas in one ayah, and some suras in the style of orators' speeches or letters without any such wazn (stopping where breath ends).

Bab 3, Fasl 3-5: repetition, order of topics, wujuh al-ijaz

Fasl 3: why are the five sciences repeated? Informing a hearer has two modes: teaching the unknown, and bringing a known meaning to presence so that it fills and dyes all faculties (as we repeat a known verse for fresh pleasure). The Quran serves both: teaching the ignorant, and by repetition dyeing the soul of the learned; most ahkam are not repeated since the second aim is not sought there. Hence we are told to repeat recitation. The repeated matters come each time in fresh wording and style. Fasl 4 (ordering of topics): why not arrange (ala', then ayyam, then jadal)? Because wisdom is conforming to the addressees' tongue and style (law la fussilat ayatuhu a'jami wa ʿarabi); the Arabs had no book, divine or human, and knew nothing of the later authors' arrangement, as the mukhadram poets' poems and the letters of the Prophet and ʿUmar show; and unordered speech serves repetition and presence better. A new wazn and qafiya, on the tongue of an ummi Prophet, is itself a sign, and had it come in known poetic meters the kuffar would have called it ordinary poetry. Fasl 5: wujuh al-ijaz are many: the wondrous style (the Arabs knew only qasida, khutba, risala, muhawara); informing of earlier scriptures without learning from them; informing of future events; the summit of balagha, with fresh garb for similar ideas in each sura (compare the prophets' stories in al-Aʿraf, Hud, al-Shuʿara', then al-Saffat, al-Dhariyat); the use of maʿani and bayan fitting unlettered addressees (joining two opposites); and the ijaz of tashriʿ, the five sciences themselves, which a master of shara'iʿ would recognise as unsurpassable, as a physician reading Ibn Sina's al-Qanun knows its author is a master physician; 'the shining sun is proof of itself'.

Bab 4, Fasl 2: reports in the tafsirs of the muhaddithun

The reports in the muhaddithun's tafsirs include asbab al-nuzul of two kinds: (1) an event that tests the faith of believers and hypocrisy of hypocrites (Uhud, al-Ahzab), where the verses allude to many particulars and the story must be summarised; (2) a verse whose meaning is complete by its general wording, 'al-ʿibra bi-ʿumum al-lafz la bi-khusus al-sabab', where the early mufassirun cite incidents only to gather reports or to show what the general wording covers, saying 'nazalat fi kadha' in a sense of application, whether earlier or later, Israelite, Jahili or Islamic. The second kind is thus a matter of ijtihad, which explains the differences. Reports that detail the outline of a story are drawn from Isra'iliyyat or sira; the mufassir must explain what a verse openly alludes to, but not minutiae such as the colour of the Companions of the Cave's dog or the cow's sex. Two principles: transmit the heard stories as they came without rational interference, and do not treat ancient mufassirun's suggestions of possible ta'rid as certain claims (Ibn ʿAbbas on mash al-arjul is not a madhhab but a raised problem). Israelite reports are 'neither believe nor deny', so when the Sunna explains an allusion do not look to Ahl al-Kitab (Sulayman's fitna: he omitted 'insha' Allah', not the legend of Sakhr), and 'necessity is measured by its extent'. Tafsir of the Quran by the Quran (the two statements of 'inni aʿlamu ma la taʿlamun'; ʿIsa in Maryam and Al ʿImran, 'wa rasulan ila Bani Isra'il mukhbiran bi-anni qad ji'tukum'). Gharib: ijtihad enters; the mufassir must weigh a word twice (Arab usage; context). The author's own new insights: 'kutiba ʿalaykum al-qisas fi al-qatla' as equivalence among the slain (so 'al-untha bi-l-untha' needs no naskh), 'yas'alunaka ʿan al-ahilla' as the months (of Hajj), 'li-awwal al-hashr' as the first mustering of troops. Naskh: for the Salaf it is lexical removal (500 verses); for the usulis it is known by the date of revelation, though they may take ijmaʿ of the Salaf or the jumhur as a sign of naskh; the naskh reports are very obscure. Other contents: debates and citations of Companions, the Prophet's recitation as proof, matching hadiths and qira'a.

Istinbat of rulings from the verses is a vast area where the mind finds ishara and iqtida'; the author restricts its types to ten, an even scale to weigh derived rulings (the list itself is in his other works; this text names tawjih among the ten). Tawjih is solving a difficulty in understanding, differing for beginners and finishers; the Companions did it much. The Quran addresses the common reader: in jadal its tawjih is to clarify the sects' views and the force of the ilzam; in ahkam to explain the masala, its forms and examples and the benefit of qualifiers (ihtiraz etc.); in ala' to depict the blessings; in ayyam to show causal order and the ta'rid; in death and afterlife to depict what befalls. Other types of tawjih: bringing near what is strange; removing clash between two proofs, two ta'rids, or between ʿaqli and naqli; separating the confusable; reconciling the different; stating the truth of the promise; stating how the Prophet performed what he was commanded. The right tawjih states the difficulty and its cause, resolves it in detail, and weighs it. The author rejects the kalam excess in ta'wil of mutashabihat: his madhhab is that of Malik, al-Thawri, Ibn al-Mubarak and the early scholars, imrar al-mutashabihat ʿala zawahiriha, and he dislikes polemic over derived rulings and 'tadaru' bi-l-Quran'. The student must hold to what the verse indicates whether it agrees with his madhhab or not, understanding Quranic language from early Arabs' usage and relying fully on the Companions' and Tabiʿun's reports. In Quranic nahw, he rejects the school-bound course of following Sibawayh by far-fetched ta'wil; one must take what best fits context, whether Sibawayh's or al-Farra''s; and on al-muqimin al-salat (ʿUthman: 'the Arabs will fix it with their tongues') any structure attested by authoritative Arabs is sound. Maʿani and bayan are a later science: what the early Arabs generally understood is welcome, the deep technicalities are not required for understanding the Quran. The Sufis' isharat are not tafsir but feelings that pass through the listener's heart (like a lover hearing the story of Layla and Majnun), not explanation. Iʿtibar and istishhad: the Prophet used verses as analogies (fa-amma man aʿta wa-ttaqa, for qadar; wa nafs wa ma sawwaha fa-alhamaha fujuraha wa taqwaha), opening a door for the divinely-given sciences.

Bab 4, Fasl 4-5 and the closing: gharaib al-Quran, zahr and batn, wahbi sciences

Fasl 4: gharaib al-Quran are the suras and verses with special merit emphasised in hadith, classed by science: of tadhkir bi-ala' Allah (Ayat al-Kursi, al-Ikhlas, the end of al-Hashr, the start of al-Mu'min); of ayyam (a rare story or fully detailed one, rich in lessons, as the Prophet said of Musa and al-Khidr 'we wished Musa had been patient so that Allah would tell us more'); of tadhkir bi-al-mawt (verses gathering Qiyama states: 'whoever wishes to see the Day of Resurrection as if with the eye, let him read idha al-shams kuwwirat, idha al-sama' infatarat, idha al-sama' inshaqqat'); of ahkam (hudud and specified measures: hundred lashes for zina, three menses/purities for ʿidda, inheritance shares); of jadal (a rare eloquent reply, or a sensory similitude, as 'mathaluhum ka-mathal alladhi istawqada nara', condemnation of idol worship, the hubut of deeds of the showy); and others for balagha (Surat al-Rahman, called ʿarus al-Quran) or for depicting the wretched and happy. Fasl 5: hadith 'anzala al-Quran ʿala sabʿat ahruf, li-kull aya minha zahr wa batn, wa li-kull hadd muttaliʿ': zahr of the five sciences is the meaning and mantuq; the batn of ala' is reflection and murqaba; of ayyam is knowing the basis of praise and blame, reward and punishment in the stories and drawing lessons; of Jannah and Nar is fear and hope pictured as if seen; of ahkam is deriving subtle rulings by iqtida' and ima'; of jadal is learning the reality of the vices described and attaching their likes. The muttaliʿ of the zahr is knowing Arabic and the reports of tafsir; that of the batn is sharpness and subtlety of mind, straight understanding with light of the inner and calm of heart. Closing (ʿulum wahbiyya): ta'wil of the prophets' stories (in his treatise Ta'wil al-Ahadith: each story had a principle in the prophet's and his people's aptitude per Allah's design, perhaps 'wa yuʿallimuka min ta'wil al-ahadith'); the verification of the five sciences; his Persian translation 'Fath al-Rahman fi tarjamat al-Quran' (close to the Arabic in words and generality, though not always); and khawass al-Quran: earlier scholars spoke of two kinds, du'a-like and sihr-like (he seeks refuge in Allah from it); the author received a door beyond, the Asma' al-Husna, great verses and blessed supplications 'as Our gift to dispose', each conditioned by conditions no rule governs, awaiting the unseen world as in istikhara.

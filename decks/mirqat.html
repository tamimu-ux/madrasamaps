Khuṭba and muqaddima: meanings of ʿilm

The book opens with a short khuṭba (ḥamd, ṣalāt) and announces that it is a set of faṣls on ʿilm al-mīzān that must be memorised and mastered. The muqaddima then defines what ʿilm means, because the aim of manṭiq (protection from error in thinking) can only be understood after knowing what knowledge is and how it is divided. The author lists five senses of ʿilm drawn from different schools (the first four are the philosophers', the fifth is attributed to some mutakallimūn). The ḥāshiya adds that the Māturīdīs call ʿilm a simple attribute with idāfa (ḥāla inkishāfiyya).
What are the meanings of ʿilm listed in the Mirqāt's muqaddima?

Five: (1) arrival of the thing's form in the intellect; (2) the form obtained from the thing; (3) what is present with the perceiver; (4) the soul's acceptance of that form; (5) the relation between knower and known.tap to reveal

Taṣawwur and taṣdīq

Acquired (ḥuṣūlī, ḥādith) knowledge divides into taṣawwur (perception free of ḥukm) and taṣdīq (perception with ḥukm, i.e. assigning a nisba as positive or negative). The ḥukm here is the idhʿān that the nisba is or is not real; it can also be called iqāʿ/intizāʿ. Whether taṣdīq is the simple ḥukm accompanying the three taṣawwurāt (the philosophers) or the sum of ḥukm plus the taṣawwurāt (Imām al-Rāzī) is the key dispute, and the book prefers the simple-ḥukm view as the verified one (the ḥāshiya gives three differences between the two). Example: 'Zayd is standing' involves ʿilm of Zayd, of 'standing', and of the linking relation.

Badīhī, naẓarī and naẓar

Both taṣawwur and taṣdīq each divide into badīhī (ḍarūrī: acquired without thought, like heat and cold, or 'the whole is greater than the part') and naẓarī (kasbī: needs thought, like the jinn and angels, or 'the world is originated'). Not all can be badīhī (then no science would need thought) and not all naẓarī (that leads to dawr or tasalsul). Naẓar is defined as arranging known matters so that the arrangement leads to the unknown. The ḥāshiya explains the two mental movements (from the matlūb to the mabādī, then back arranged) and the debate on whether fikr is both movements, the second one, or the arrangement. The mithāl 'the world is changing, every changing thing is originated, so the world is originated' shows an actual naẓar.

Yes: some taṣawwurāt are badīhī (heat, cold) and some naẓarī (jinn, angels).tap to reveal

The need for manṭiq, its names and its compilation

Since thinking can err (people differ and contradict each other: one reasons that the world is originated, another that it is eternal, and both cannot be right), human fiṭra alone cannot separate sound from unsound thinking. A qānūn that protects from error in fikr and shows how the unknown is acquired from the known is needed, and that is manṭiq (al-mīzān). It is named manṭiq for its effect on outward speech and inward cognition, and mīzān as a scale (qisṭās) of the mind; it is an ʿilm ālī (instrumental science). The ḥāshiya gives its four causes and the history: Aristotle (al-muʿallim al-awwal) compiled it at Alexander's command, al-Fārābī (al-muʿallim al-thānī) refined it, Ibn Sīnā elaborated it.
Why do we need manṭiq?
فجاءت الحاجة في ذلك إلى قانون عاصم عن الخطأ في الفكر يبين فيه طرق اكتساب المجهولات عن المعلومات، وهذا القانون هو المنطق والميزان.
Because human fiṭra can err in thinking (two thinkers contradict each other on the same issue), so a qānūn is needed that shows how unknowns are acquired from knowns and protects from error. That qānūn is manṭiq/mīzān.tap to reveal
Why is it called manṭiq and why mīzān?
أما تسميته بالمنطق فلتأثيره في النطق الظاهري، أعني التكلم؛ إذ العارف به يقوى على التكلم ما لا يقوى عليه الجاهل، وكذا في النطق الباطني أعني الإدراك
It is called manṭiq for its effect on outward speech (the knower speaks better) and on inward nuṭq, i.e. cognition (he knows the genera, differentiae, species, lawāzim and khawāṣṣ of things). It is called mīzān because it is a qisṭās by which sound thoughts are weighed.tap to reveal
Why is manṭiq called al-ʿilm al-ālī?
ومن ثم يقال له: العلم الآلي؛ لكونها آلة لجميع العلوم لا سيما للعلوم الحكمية.
Because it is an instrument for all sciences, especially the philosophical ones, and learning it is not itself the goal.tap to reveal

Definition, subject-matter and aim of manṭiq

Manṭiq is a science of qawānīn whose observance protects the mind from error in fikr. Its mawḍūʿ (subject) is the taṣawwurī and taṣdīqī known things, but not absolutely, only insofar as they lead to an unknown taṣawwurī or taṣdīqī. The mawḍūʿ of any science is that whose ʿawāriḍ dhātiyya are investigated, like the human body for medicine and kalima/kalām for naḥw. Its ghāya (aim) is accuracy in thought and keeping opinion safe from error. The logician does not investigate words as such; he does so only in so far as they are signifiers (hence the next topic, dalāla).
What is manṭiq?

The thing whose essential accidents are investigated: the human body for medicine; kalima and kalām for naḥw.tap to reveal
What is the mawḍūʿ of manṭiq?
فموضوع المنطق: المعلومات التصورية والتصديقية، لكن لا مطلقا، بل من حيث إنها موصلة إلى المجهول التصوري والتصديقي.

Dalāla and its six kinds

Dalāla (signification) is a thing being such that knowing it necessitates knowing something else. It first divides into lafẓiyya (the signifier is a word) and ghayr lafẓiyya, and each of these has three kinds according to what links signifier to signified: waḍʿ (convention), ṭabʿ (nature) or ʿaql (rational causal link). That gives six: lafẓiyya waḍʿiyya, ṭabʿiyya, ʿaqliyya, and the three non-verbal ones (the four dawāl, the neighing of a horse, smoke indicating fire). The logician studies only the lafẓiyya waḍʿiyya, because giving and receiving benefit among people is easy through it, whereas the other kinds are laborious or insufficient. The ḥāshiya notes the six-fold count is by induction (istiqrāʾī) and that al-Sayyid al-Jurjānī counted only five.
Define dalāla and its two main kinds.
فصل في الدلالة: الدلالة لغة هو الإرشاد، وفي الاصطلاح: كون الشيء بحيث يلزم من العلم به العلم بشيء آخر. والدلالة قسمان: لفظية وغير لفظية، واللفظية: ما يكون الدال فيه اللفظ، وغير اللفظية: ما لا يكون الدال فيه اللفظ
Dalāla: a thing such that knowing it necessitates knowing something else. Lafẓiyya if the dāl is a word; ghayr lafẓiyya if not.tap to reveal
List the six kinds of dalāla with an example of each.

(1) lafẓiyya waḍʿiyya: 'Zayd' on its bearer; (2) lafẓiyya ṭabʿiyya: 'uḥ uḥ' on chest pain; (3) lafẓiyya ʿaqliyya: the sound 'dīz' heard from behind a wall on the existence of the speaker; (4) ghayr lafẓiyya waḍʿiyya: the four dawāl; (5) ghayr lafẓiyya ṭabʿiyya: neighing of a horse on a wish for water and fodder; (6) ghayr lafẓiyya ʿaqliyya: smoke on fire.tap to reveal

Muṭābaqa, taḍammun and iltizām

The waḍʿī verbal dalāla has exactly three kinds according to whether the meaning signified is the same as the placed meaning, a part of it, or external to it. The classification is rational (ʿaqlī): the meaning is either ʿayn, dākhil or khārij. Muṭābaqa: a word signifies the whole meaning it was set for (insān → ḥayawān nāṭiq). Taḍammun: it signifies a part (insān → ḥayawān only). Iltizām: it signifies an external, ḥayawān-like lāzim of the placed meaning (insān → qābil al-ʿilm, ṣināʿat al-kitāba; ʿumā → baṣar). Taḍammun and iltizām never exist without muṭābaqa, but muṭābaqa can exist without them (a simple meaning with no part and no lāzim). The ḥāshiya debates what kind of lāzim is meant and Imām al-Rāzī's objection about 'laysa ghayrahu'.
Define the three waḍʿī verbal dalālāt with examples.
أحدها: المطابقية، وهي أن يدل اللفظ على تمام ما وضع ذلك اللفظ له كدلالة الإنسان على مجموع الحيوان والناطق، وثانيها: التضمنية، وهي أن يدل اللفظ على جزء المعنى الموضوع له كدلالته على الحيوان فقط
Muṭābaqa: signifying the whole placed meaning (insān → ḥayawān nāṭiq). Taḍammun: signifying a part (insān → ḥayawān only).tap to reveal
Define iltizām.
وثالثها: الدلالة الالتزامية، وهي أن لا يدل اللفظ على الموضوع له ولا على جزئه، بل على معنى خارج لازم للموضوع له.
Iltizām: the word signifies an external lāzim of the placed meaning, neither the whole nor a part.tap to reveal
Is the division into three rational?
ثلاثة أنحاء: هذا الحصر عقلي ليس فيها احتمال سوى الثلاثة، وذلك لأن اللفظ إذا كان دالا بحسب الوضع على معنى، وذلك المعنى الذي هو مدلول اللفظ إما أن يكون عين المعنى الموضوع له أو داخلا فيه أو خارجا عنه.
Yes: the signified meaning is either the placed meaning itself, inside it, or outside it, so only three possibilities exist.tap to reveal

Mufrad and murakkab

Having treated dalāla, the author divides the signifying word into mufrad and murakkab. A word is mufrad if no part of it is intended to signify a part of its meaning (hamzat al-istifhām, 'Zayd', 'ʿAbd Allāh' as a name) and murakkab if a part is intended to signify a part of its meaning ('Zayd qāʾim', 'rāmī al-sahm'). Mufrad is then divided into ism, kalima and adāt by whether its meaning is independent and linked to one of the three times. The logicians' kalima is not the grammarians' fiʿl: a verb like 'aḍribu' or 'taḍribu' is a verb for the grammarians but murakkab for the logicians, since the hamza signifies the speaker and the root signifies the action; yaḍribu, by contrast, is a kalima for both.

Division of the mufrad: one meaning (juzʾī, mutawāṭiʾ, mushakkik)

After dividing the mufrad by independence of meaning, the book divides it by oneness or plurality of meaning. A mufrad whose meaning is one is either ʿalam/juzʾī ḥaqīqī (the meaning is individuated, like Zayd, hādhā, huwa) or has many instances. If its instances share the meaning equally with no priority, precedence, intensity or excess, the word is mutawāṭiʾ (insān for Zayd, ʿAmr, Bakr). If the meaning applies with awwaliyya, awlawiyya, ashaddiyya or azyadiyya to some instances over others, the word is mushakkik (it confuses the observer as to whether it is mutawāṭiʾ or mushtarak). Wujūd and bayāḍ are the standard examples, and the ḥāshiya gives a long digest of the Mashshāʾī/Ishrāqī dispute on whether tashkīk can occur in quiddities.

Words with several meanings: mushtarak, manqūl, ḥaqīqa, majāz and mutarādif

A word with many meanings was either set separately for each meaning (mushtarak, like ʿayn for gold, eye, knee) or set first for one meaning and later used for another because of a link. If the second use became famous and the first was abandoned it is manqūl, divided by the naqil into ʿurfī (e.g. dābba for the horse), sharʿī (ṣalāt, from supplication to specific acts) and iṣṭilāḥī (ism in naḥw). If the second use did not become famous and the first remains in use, the word is ḥaqīqa for the first meaning and majāz for the second (asad for the predator and for the brave man). Majāz needs a ʿalāqa (25 are counted, 9 per Ṣāḥib al-Tawḍīḥ, 5 per Ibn al-Ḥājib). The ḥāshiya also defines murtajal (set for a second meaning with no link, like Jaʿfar for a person) and adds mutarādif, several words for one meaning (qaʿūd and julūs).

When the word was set from the start for each of several meanings by separate acts of waḍʿ (e.g. ʿayn).tap to reveal

وإن لم يوضع لكل ابتداء، بل وضع أولا لمعنى ثم استعمل في معنى ثان؛ لأجل مناسبة بينهما، إن اشتهر في الثاني وترك موضوعه الأول، يسمى منقولا، والمنقول بالنظر إلى الناقل ينقسم إلى ثلاثة أقسام
Manqūl: placed first for one meaning, then used for a second because of a link, with the second becoming famous and the first abandoned. By the transferrer: ʿurfī, sharʿī, iṣṭilāḥī.tap to reveal

Murakkab tāmm and nāqiṣ: khabar and inshāʾ

A murakkab is tāmm if silence on it is sound (Zayd qāʾim) and nāqiṣ otherwise. The tāmm divides into khabar/qaḍiyya (what is intended as a report and can be true or false: 'the sky is above us', 'the world is originated') and inshāʾ (no reported content: amr, nahy, tamannī, tarajjī, istifhām, nidāʾ). The ḥāshiya defends 'lā ilāha illā Allāh' as khabar: the bare wording admits falsehood in itself even though by the specifics of speaker and sentence it cannot be false. The nāqiṣ is divided into taqyīdī (iḍāfī: ghulām Zayd; tawṣīfī: al-rajul al-ʿālim) and ghayr taqyīdī (fī al-dār). With this the discussion of words ends and the discussion of meanings begins.

Tāmm (silence is sound: Zayd qāʾim) and nāqiṣ (silence is not sound).tap to reveal
What are the two kinds of murakkab tāmm?
المركب التام ضربان، يقال لأحدهما: الخبر والقضية، وهو ما قصد به الحكاية ويحتمل الصدق والكذب. ويقال لقائله: إنه صادق أو كاذب، نحو: السماء فوقنا، والعالم حادث.
Khabar/qaḍiyya (a report that can be true or false: 'the sky is above us', 'the world is originated') and inshāʾ (no hikāya).tap to reveal
Is 'lā ilāha illā Allāh' a khabar even though it cannot be false?
فإن قيل: قولنا: لا إله إلا الله قضية وخبر مع أنه لا يحتمل الكذب؟ قلت: مجرد اللفظ يحتمله وإن كان بالنظر إلى خصوصية الحاشيتين غير محتمل للكذب.
Yes. The test is whether the bare meaning, apart from the specifics of the two sides (the thing's occurrence in itself and the speaker), can be true or false; the actual truth value does not remove its khabar status.tap to reveal

The mafhūm: kullī, juzʾī and the kinds of kullī

With words finished, the book turns to meanings. A mafhūm (what forms in the mind) is either juzʾī, whose very conception prevents its being true of many (Zayd, ʿAmr, this horse, this wall), or kullī, whose very conception does not prevent sharing (insān, faras). The qualifier 'its conception itself' matters: wājib al-wujūd is kullī although outside proof shows it has no partner. The book lists the kinds of kullī according to whether its instances exist; the matn gives four kinds while the ḥāshiya (from Ṭūsī) gives six: impossible instances (lā shayʾ), possible but not existing (ʿanqāʾ), possible with only one existing and no other possible (sun, Allah), only one existing and others possible, many finite (seven planets), many infinite (humans, horses). An objection (the image of a particular egg, a far-off shape, a newborn's sensation) is answered by 'being true together (maʿan)' versus 'in turn (badalan)'.
Define juzʾī and kullī.
فصل: المفهوم أي ما حصل في الذهن قسمان: أحدهما جزئي، والثاني كلي. أما الجزئي: فهو ما يمنع نفس تصوره عن صدقه على كثيرين، كزيد، وعمرو، وهذا الفرس، وهذا الجدار. وأما الكلي: فهو ما لا يمنع نفس تصوره عن وقوع الشركة فيه، وعن صدقه على كثيرين، كالإنسان والفرس.
Juzʾī: its very conception prevents truth of many (Zayd, ʿAmr, this horse, this wall). Kullī: its conception does not prevent sharing (insān, faras).tap to reveal
What kinds of kullī exist by the existence of their instances?
فصل: الكلي أقسام: أحدها: ما يمتنع وجود أفراده في الخارج، كاللاشيء واللاممكن واللاموجود. وثانيها: ما يمكن أفراده ولم توجد، كالعنقاء وجبل من الياقوت.

The four relations between two kullīs and the relative juzʾī

Between any two kullīs there are exactly four possible relations: tasāwī (equality), tabāyun (disjunction), ʿumūm wa khuṣūṣ muṭlaq, and ʿumūm wa khuṣūṣ min wajh. They are set between kullīs only because a juzʾī is not a kasb/muktasab and between two juzʾīs there can only be tabāyun, and between a juzʾī and a kullī either akhaṣṣ or tabāyun. The ḥāshiya notes how each relation reduces to propositions (two universal affirmatives, one universal and one particular negative, two universal negatives, one particular affirmative with two particular negatives) and how their negations relate. The section ends with the second meaning of juzʾī: a juzʾī iḍāfī (any specific falling under a more general).

The five universals: jins and nawʿ and their ordering

The logician's concern is restricted to the five universals: jins, nawʿ, faṣl, khāṣṣa and ʿaraḍ ʿāmm. The ḥāshiya explains the number: a kullī is either the very quiddity of its particulars (nawʿ), inside it (jins if it is the whole common part between that quiddity and another species, otherwise faṣl) or outside it (khāṣṣa if it is limited to one quiddity, ʿaraḍ ʿāmm if not). The jins is a kullī said of many differing in reality in answer to 'mā huwa?' (ḥayawān for insān, faras, ghanam). The nawʿ is a kullī said of many agreeing in reality in answer to 'mā huwa?' It also has a second meaning (nawʿ iḍāfī). Jins is arranged by levels: sāfil (nothing under it but species, ḥayawān), mutawassiṭ (jism nāmī) and ʿālī (jawhar), and a jins mufrad is distinct from all.
What are the five universals and why five?
فصل: الكليات خمس: الأول: الجنس وهو كلي مقول على كثيرين مختلفين بالحقائق في جواب ما هو؟ كالحيوان؛ فإنه مقول على الإنسان والفرس والغنم إذا سئل عنها بما هي؟
Jins, nawʿ, faṣl, khāṣṣa, ʿaraḍ ʿāmm. A kullī is either the quiddity itself (nawʿ), inside the quiddity (jins or faṣl), or outside it (khāṣṣa or ʿaraḍ ʿāmm).tap to reveal

'Kullī' is the jins covering all five; 'differing in realities' excludes nawʿ; 'in answer to mā huwa' excludes faṣl, ʿaraḍ ʿāmm and khāṣṣa.tap to reveal

فصل: الثاني: النوع وهو كلي مقول على كثيرين متفقين بالحقائق في جواب ما هو؟

Faṣl: qarīb and baʿīd, muqawwim and muqassim

The faṣl is a kullī said of a thing in answer to 'ayyu shayʾin huwa fī dhātihi?' (which thing is it in its essence?). Asked this of insān, the answer is nāṭiq. Because the parts of a quiddity are only jins and faṣl, the faṣl is what distinguishes the quiddity from what shares its jins. It is either qarīb (distinguishes from all that shares the nearest jins, nāṭiq for insān) or baʿīd (distinguishes from what shares the remote jins, ḥassās for insān). It has two relations: to the species (it is muqawwim, entering the substance of the species) and to the genus (it is muqassim, dividing it into two). Two rules are given: every muqawwim of the higher is muqawwim of the lower, but not conversely; every muqassim of the lower is muqassim of the higher, but not conversely.

Khāṣṣa, ʿaraḍ ʿāmm, dhātī/ʿaraḍī and lāzim/mufāriq

The muʿarrif: ḥadd, rasm and verbal definition

After the five universals, the book discusses the muʿarrif (qawl shāriḥ), what is predicated of a thing to inform its conception. It is classed by what it uses. Bāb 2: qaḍāyā — ḥamliyya and sharṭiyya and their parts

The second bāb of the Mirqāt deals with the ḥujja and what is linked to it. Because ḥujja is built from propositions, the book first treats the qaḍāyā. A qaḍiyya is a statement that can be true or false (or: a statement of whose speaker one may say ṣādiq or kādhib). Division by the subject: shakhṣiyya, ṭabʿiyya, maḥṣūra, muhmala and the sūr

A qaḍiyya may be classified by its subject. If the subject is a specific individual it is shakhṣiyya/makhṣūṣa (Zayd qāʾim). If the subject is a kullī and the ḥukm is on the reality itself it is ṭabʿiyya (al-insān nawʿ). If the ḥukm is on the instances then either the quantity is stated (maḥṣūra: kull insān ḥayawān) or not (muhmala: al-insān la-fī khusr). The maḥṣūrāt are four: universal affirmative (mūjiba kulliyya), particular affirmative, universal negative, particular negative. The word that states the quantity is the sūr (from sūr al-balad). The logicians symbolise subject and predicate by jīm and bāʾ (kull j b). The book also defines ḥaml as the union of two things different in concept by existence, and divides it into ḥaml bi-l-ishtiqāq (via fī, dhū, lām) and ḥaml bi-l-muwāṭaʾa (ʿAmr ṭabīb).

Khārijiyya, dhihniyya, ḥaqīqiyya; maʿdūla and muḥaṣṣala; and the idea of jiha

A ḥamliyya can be classified by the realm it speaks of. If the subject exists outside and the ḥukm concerns it as outside, it is khārijiyya (al-insān kātib). If the subject exists in the mind and the ḥukm concerns it as in the mind, it is dhihniyya (al-insān kullī). If the ḥukm concerns it as established in the nafs al-amr regardless of outside or mind, it is ḥaqīqiyya (al-arbaʿa zawj). Separately, both mūjiba and sāliba divide into maʿdūla (the particle of negation is part of the subject, the predicate or both: al-lā-ḥayy jamād, Zayd lā ʿālim) and ghayr maʿdūla (muḥaṣṣala if affirmative, basīṭa if negative). To separate a sāliba basīṭa from a mūjiba maʿdūla al-maḥmūl: if the rābiṭa comes after the negation particle the proposition is affirmative (maʿdūla); if the negation particle comes before the rābiṭa it denies it. Finally the qaḍiyya may mention its jiha (the mode of the nisba), when it is muwajjaha (also rubāʿiyya).

Muwajjahāt: the seven compound (murakkabāt)

A murakkaba is a qaḍiyya whose reality is built from an affirmation and a negation. It is called mūjiba or sāliba after its first part. Sharṭiyyāt: muttaṣila and munfaṣila, their types and sūrs

The sharṭiyya divides into muttaṣila and munfaṣila. The muttaṣila judges a nisba on the supposition of another (in the affirmative) or denies a nisba on denial of another (negative). It is luzūmiyya if the ḥukm rests on a ʿalāqa between muqaddam and tālī (cause/effect, both effects of a third, or taḍāyuf), or ittifāqiyya if not. The munfaṣila judges tanāfī between two things; it is ḥaqīqiyya (tanāfī in truth and falsehood), māniʿat al-jamʿ (in truth only) or māniʿat al-khulū (in falsehood only), and each can be ʿinādiyya (by the nature of the two parts) or ittifāqiyya (by mere coincidence). The book carries over shakhṣiyya/maḥṣūra/muhmala to sharṭiyya (not ṭabʿiyya) via taqādīr, gives their sūrs (matā, mahmā, kullamā; dāʾiman; laysa albatta; qad yakūn; qad lā yakūn) and the nine kinds of muttaṣila and six of munfaṣila by the type of their two parts.

Tanāquḍ and its conditions

After the qaḍāyā and their classes the book takes up their ahkām: tanāquḍ and the ʿukūs. Tanāquḍ is the difference of two qaḍiyyas in affirmation and negation such that, by its own nature, the truth of one demands the falsity of the other and vice versa (Zayd qāʾim / Zayd laysa bi-qāʾim). For two shakhṣiyya propositions it requires eight unities: subject, predicate, place, time, potential/actual, condition, part/whole and relation; some reduce them to two (subject and predicate) and others, such as al-Fārābī per the ḥāshiya, to one unity of the ḥukmī nisba. For maḥṣūrāt it also requires difference in quantity (kamm): two universals may both be false and two particulars both true, so the contradictory of a universal is a particular. For muwajjahāt it requires difference in jiha, and the book lists the contradictories of the basāʾiṭ (the compound's contradictory is a disjunction between the contradictories of its two parts). For sharṭiyyāt, the contradictory shares jins and nawʿ and differs in kayf.

ʿAks mustawī (simple conversion)

ʿAks mustawī (also ʿaks mustaqīm) is making the first part of a qaḍiyya second and the second first, while keeping truth (ṣidq) and kayf. The universal negative converts to itself (lā shayʾ min al-insān ḥajar → lā shayʾ min al-ḥajar insān) by proof of khalf. The particular negative does not convert necessarily (some animals are not human; but not: some humans are not animals). The universal affirmative converts to a particular affirmative (kull insān ḥayawān → baʿḍ al-ḥayawān insān), not to a universal. The particular affirmative converts to itself. The ḥāshiya answers two objections (kull shaykh kāna shābban and baʿḍ al-watad fī al-ḥāʾiṭ), gives the three proofs (khalf, iftirāḍ, ʿaks) and the conversion of the muwajjahāt.

ʿAks al-naqīḍ (contraposition)

ʿAks al-naqīḍ, on the ancients' style, is making the contradictory of the first part second and the contradictory of the second part first while keeping truth and kayf. Thus the universal affirmative converts to itself (kull insān ḥayawān → kull lā-ḥayawān lā-insān); the particular affirmative does not convert (some animal is non-human is true, but some human is non-animal is false); the universal negative converts to a particular negative (lā shayʾ min al-insān bi-faras → baʿḍ al-lā-faras laysa bi-lā-insān) and not to a universal; and the particular negative converts to itself (baʿḍ al-ḥayawān laysa bi-insān → baʿḍ al-lā-insān laysa bi-lā-ḥayawān). The later scholars changed the method: they make the contradictory of the second part first and the first part itself second, with opposite kayf. The Mirqāt gives only the ancients' version; the ḥāshiya lists the muwajjahāt's contrapositions.

The four figures: conditions and moods

The first figure is the noblest; its productivity is self-evident. Its conditions are two: the ṣughrā must be affirmative and the kubrā universal (and for the Mutaʾakhkhirūn the ṣughrā must be actual, not possible). Of the sixteen combinations (4×4), twelve fall, leaving four productive moods: (1) universal affirmative + universal affirmative → universal affirmative; (2) universal affirmative + universal negative → universal negative; (3) particular affirmative + universal affirmative → particular affirmative; (4) particular affirmative + universal negative → particular negative. The second figure needs opposite kayf in the premises and a universal kubrā; its conclusion is always negative; four moods; proven by converting the kubrā (or by khalf). The third figure needs an affirmative ṣughrā and at least one universal premise; six moods. The fourth figure's conditions and moods are only mentioned and left to the longer books. The figures are examined by 'kull j b / kull b d → kull j d' letter notation.

Istiqrāʾ, tamthīl and qiyās al-khalf

The book ends the qiyās with its appendages. Istiqrāʾ is judging a kullī by examining most of its particulars (every animal moves its lower jaw when chewing; but the crocodile moves the upper); it gives probable (ẓann ghālib) not certain knowledge, unless taking all particulars (tāmm, the muqassim qiyās). Tamthīl is establishing a ḥukm in one particular because of its presence in another through a common meaning (the world is composed, so it is originated, like a house); jurists call it qiyās (aṣl, farʿ, ʿilla), theologians 'inferring the hidden from the visible'. The main ways of showing the common meaning to be the cause are dawarān (ṭard and ʿaks) and sabr wa taqsīm. The ḥāshiya shows both are weak. Qiyās al-khalf is among the compound qiyāses; it reduces to two qiyāses (an iqtirānī sharṭī of two muttaṣilas and an istithnāʾī) and proves the claim by refuting its contradictory.

Every qiyās has ṣūra (form: the arrangement of premises, already studied in the four figures) and mādda (matter: the premises themselves). By matter, a qiyās is of five kinds, called the five ṣināʿāt: burhānī, jadalī, khiṭābī, shiʿrī and sūfisṭāʾī (mughālaṭa). The ḥāshiya shows the ḥaṣr: premises either give taṣdīq or another effect (takhyīl: poetry); taṣdīq is either ẓann (khiṭāba) or certainty (burhān), and what is not certain is either accepted by the public or conceded by the opponent (jadal) or else mughālaṭa. Burhān is a qiyās composed of yaqīniyyāt, either badīhī or naẓarī ending in the badīhī. The badīhiyyāt are six: awwaliyyāt, fiṭriyyāt (qaḍāyā qiyāsātuhā maʿahā), ḥadsiyyāt, mushāhadāt (ḥissiyyāt by five outer senses and wijdāniyyāt by five inner), tajribiyyāt and mutawātirāt. Burhān is lummī (the awsaṭ is the cause of the akbar's belonging to the aṣghar in reality too) or innī (only in the mind). The ḥāshiya adds the difference between ḥads and fikr, and the sense in which badāha varies by persons and times.

Jadal, khiṭāba and shiʿr

The jadalī qiyās is composed of mashhūrāt or premises conceded by the opponent, true or false. Mashhūrāt are propositions on which a group agrees for the public good ('justice is good, oppression is bad, killing a thief is obligatory'), for soft-heartedness (Indians: slaughtering animals is blameworthy) or by temperament; every people and craft has its own (naḥwīs: the fāʿil is marfūʿ). They resemble awwaliyyāt, and stripping the mind and careful thought separates them. The aim of jadal is to defeat the opponent or defend one's view. The khiṭābī qiyās yields ẓann; its premises are maqbūlāt taken from those one thinks well of (awliyāʾ, sages) and maẓnūnāt; the prophets' reports are not khiṭāba but burhānī, because a truthful reporter's truth is shown by the muʿjiza. Its aim is persuasion (iqnāʿ). The shiʿrī qiyās consists of mukhayyalāt, and its aim is to move the soul by tarhīb or targhīb; meter is not required by the logicians, but adds beauty.

Jadalī qiyās: from mashhūrāt or conceded premises, true or false. Mashhūrāt are agreed by a people for public interest (justice is good, oppression bad, killing a thief is obligatory), soft-heartedness or temperament.tap to reveal

Sophistry (mughālaṭa) and the causes of error

The sūfisṭāʾī qiyās is composed of false wahmiyyāt invented by the wahm, like judging the unsensed by the sensed ('everything existent is pointable'). The wahm has sultan over the faculties; its judgments on sensibles are true, but on intelligibles false. Wahmiyyāt strongly resemble awwaliyyāt, and only the rejection of intellect and sharīʿa would keep them confused. The art is deceptive, useless in itself; useful incidentally: its master is not mistaken, does not deceive, and can deceive another or test him. Called sūfisṭāʾī if he faces the sage (the 'sophisticated wisdom'), mushāghib (quarrelsome) otherwise. The causes of error reduce to two: bad understanding, and confusing falsehoods with truths, or (another view) to one: not distinguishing a thing from its likeness. The latter has a verbal division (mushtarak, majāz; ṣarf; iʿjām and iʿrāb) and a compositional division (the referent of a pronoun, uniting a composite, combining separate terms) and a conceptual division.

A qiyās composed of false wahmiyyāt invented by the wahm, like judging the unsensed by the sensed ('everything existent is pointable').tap to reveal

Fallacies of meaning and form, and famous mughālaṭāt

Fallacies caused by meaning arise from matter (the meanings arranged so that the qiyās is true but not a qiyās, or a qiyās but not true: 'the human is rational insofar as he is rational; nothing rational insofar as rational is an animal') or from form (a figure that does not produce: 'time encompasses events; the sphere encompasses them, so time is the sphere', second figure with two affirmatives). The book then lists formal mughālaṭāt: begging the question (muṣādara ʿalā al-maṭlūb), taking the accidental for the essential, the awsaṭ not repeated in full ('the human has hair; every hair grows'), the awsaṭ not the same in both premises through potential/actual, a faulty ḥaml ('the human alone laughs'), the kubrā not universal (a ṭabʿī kubrā), the order of rābiṭa and negation, taking mental notions for external ones ('the impossible exists'), taking an image for the thing, taking part of a cause for the cause, the argument from priority, ignoring ḥaythiyyāt, 'the equal of the equal is equal', taking the contrary for the opposite, and well-known sophisms (the impossibility of acquiring the unknown; 'if a proposition were not true…'; the all-purpose proof).
How do fallacies of meaning divide?
فصل في الأغاليط التي تقع بسبب المعنى: وهذا أيضا أقسام؛ لأنها إما من جهة المادة أو من جهة الصورة. أما من جهة المادة كما يكون بحيث إذا رتب المعاني فيه على وجه يكون صادقا لم يكن قياسا. وإذا رتب على وجه يكون قياسا لم يكن صادقا
By matter (arranged to be true it is not a qiyās; arranged to be a qiyās it is not true) or by form (the shape is non-productive). Examples: 'human is rational insofar as rational; no rational insofar as rational is an animal'; 'time encompasses events; the sphere encompasses them; so time is the sphere' (second figure, two affirmatives).tap to reveal

Muṣādara: using the conclusion itself in the qiyās (making the claim a part or the whole of the proof). Scholars disagree whether the defect is in matter (Shaykh al-Maqtūl, Rāzī) or form (Ṭūsī); al-Shīrāzī: neither, but that the 'other statement' implied is not other than the premises.tap to reveal

Khātima: the three matters of a science and the eight ruʾūs

The khātima first notes that a qiyās with any non-burhānī premise is not burhānī (the compound follows the weaker), which keeps the five-fold ḥaṣr valid, and that the five ṣināʿāt complete the aims of manṭiq in both its parts (what leads to taṣawwur and what leads to taṣdīq). Then it says every science has three matters: mawḍūʿ (what is investigated for its essential accidents), mabādī (taṣawwurī: ḥudūd; taṣdīqī: muqaddamāt, called ʿulūm mutaʿāraf or if they are not badīhī then uṣūl mawḍūʿa when accepted with good opinion or muṣādarāt when accepted with reluctance) and masāʾil (what the science contains and seeks to prove). Then the eight ruʾūs: gharaḍ, manfaʿa, tasmiya, muʾallif, rutba, from which science it is, qisma and anḥāʾ al-taʿlīm. The author ends with a request for a prayer of good ending, and the ḥāshiya's author closes with his colophon.
What if a premise of a qiyās is not burhānī?
فصل: ولا بد أن يعلم أنه إذا كان إحدى مقدمتي القياس غير برهانية، بل كانت جدلية، أو خطابية، أو شعرية، أو غيرها كان القياس أيضا غير برهاني، وكذا الكلام في القياس الجدلي ونظائره، وبالجملة المؤلف من الراجح والمرجوح مرجوح.
The qiyās is also not burhānī; likewise for jadalī and similar; in short, what is composed of the stronger and the weaker is weaker.tap to reveal
What three matters does every science have?
خاتمة: لكل علم ثلاثة أمور: أحدها: الموضوع، وهو ما يبحث في العلم عن عوارضه ولواحقه الذاتية
Mawḍūʿ (what the science investigates for its essential accidents), mabādī (what the problems rest on), masāʾil (the problems proved in it).tap to reveal

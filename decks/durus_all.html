Faṣāḥa (eloquence) — of word, speech, speaker

The book opens by defining faṣāḥa, which is the precondition of balāgha. Faṣāḥa is a quality that applies to three things: the single word (kalima), the composed speech (kalām) and the speaker (mutakallim). A word is faṣīḥ when it is free of three flaws (tanāfur al-ḥurūf, mukhālafat al-qiyās, gharāba). Speech is faṣīḥ when it is free of tanāfur al-kalimāt, ḍaʿf al-taʾlīf and taʿqīd, while its words are themselves faṣīḥ. The speaker's faṣāḥa is a faculty (malaka) of expressing any intent in faṣīḥ speech.

فصاحة الكلمة: سلامتها من تنافر الحروف

When is a composed speech faṣīḥ?
وفصاحة الكلام: سلامته من تنافر الكلمات مجتمعة، ومن ضعف التأليف، ومن التعقيد، مع فصاحة كلماته
Speech is faṣīḥ when free of tanāfur al-kalimāt, ḍaʿf al-taʾlīf and taʿqīd, and each of its words is faṣīḥ.tap to reveal

Balāgha — conformity to the situation (muqtaḍā al-ḥāl)

Balāgha linguistically means arriving and reaching. Technically it describes speech and the speaker only (not the single word). Balāghat al-kalām = conformity to the muqtaḍā al-ḥāl along with faṣāḥa. The ḥāl (also called maqām) is the matter that induces the speaker to present his wording in a particular form; that form is the muqtaḍā. Praise is a ḥāl demanding iṭnāb; the listener's intelligence is a ḥāl demanding ījāz. Balāghat al-mutakallim is the faculty to express any intent in balīgh speech.
Define balāghat al-kalām.
فبلاغة الكلام: مطابقته لمقتضى الحال مع فصاحته
Balāghat al-kalām is that the speech conforms to the requirement of the situation, while being faṣīḥ.tap to reveal
Distinguish ḥāl, muqtaḍā and muṭābaqa with the book's example.
مثلاً المدح حال يدعو لإيراد العبارة على صورة الإطناب، وذكاء المخاطب حال يدعو لإيرادها على صورة الإيجاز
Praise and the listener's intelligence are each a ḥāl; iṭnāb and ījāz are each a muqtaḍā; presenting the speech in iṭnāb (for praise) or ījāz (for the intelligent) is muṭābaqa.tap to reveal

ʿIlm al-Maʿānī — definition and outline

ʿIlm al-maʿānī is the first of the three balāgha sciences. It studies the states (aḥwāl) of Arabic wording by which it conforms to the requirement of the situation, so that the form of speech varies as the situation varies. The authors illustrate with Qur'an 72:10 where the verb of intending is passive for evil and active (with Rabb as agent) for good, because of the ḥāl: not ascribing evil to Allah. The book divides this science into eight bābs and a khātima. Note the editor's remark: the authors arrange chapters by the iqtiḍāʾāt (requirements) themselves, not by the parts of the sentence as in al-Khaṭīb al-Qazwīnī's Talkhīṣ.
Define ʿilm al-maʿānī.
علم المعاني: هو علم يعرف به أحوال اللفظ العربي التي بها يطابق مقتضى الحال
It is the science of the states of Arabic wording by which it fits the requirement of the situation; thus speech takes different shapes as the situations differ.tap to reveal

Bāb 1 — al-Khabar (declarative speech)

Every utterance is khabar or inshāʾ. Khabar is what can be called true or false; its truth is correspondence to reality. Every sentence has a musnad ilayhi and a musnad. A verbal sentence conveys ḥudūth (occurrence in a particular time) concisely, and with a present verb plus qarāʾin it can convey renewed continuity (istimrār tajaddudī); a nominal sentence conveys thubūt and, by qarāʾin, continuity. The default purpose of khabar is fāʾidat al-khabar (informing the judgment) or lāzim al-fāʾida (informing that the speaker knows it); it may carry other aims such as supplication, showing weakness, grief, joy, reproach. By the addressee's state, khabar has three ḍarbs: ibtidāʾī, ṭalabī, inkārī, with tawkīd graded to the doubt.
What is khabar and what is 'ṣidq' in it?
والخبر: ما يصح أن يقال لقائله: إنه صادق فيه أو كاذب، كسافر محمد، وعلي مقيم. والمراد بصدق الخبر: مطابقته للواقع، وبكذبه عدم مطابقته له
Khabar is speech that is truth-apt; its truth is that it matches reality, its falsity that it does not.tap to reveal
What are the two pillars of every sentence?

The muḥkūm ʿalayhi (musnad ilayhi) and the muḥkūm bihi (musnad).tap to reveal
What does a verbal sentence convey vs a nominal sentence?
فالأولى موضوعة لإفادة الحدوث في زمن مخصوص، مع الاختصار، وقد تفيد الاستمرار التجددي بالقرائن إذا كان الفعل مضارعاً
Verbal: ḥudūth in a specified time, briefly; with a present verb and qarāʾin it may give renewed continuity. Nominal: thubūt, and by qarāʾin continuity even without a verb (e.g. الشمس مضيئة، العلم نافع).tap to reveal

Inshāʾ — ṭalabī vs ghayr ṭalabī

Inshāʾ is speech that is not truth-apt. The book splits it into ṭalabī, which requests something not yet existing at the time of the request, and ghayr ṭalabī, which does not. Ṭalabī inshāʾ is expressed by five things: amr, nahy, istifhām, tamannī and nidāʾ. Each can leave its original meaning for a secondary meaning understood from context and qarāʾin. Ghayr ṭalabī (wonder, oath, contract formulas like biʿtu/ishtaraytu, etc.) is deliberately excluded from ʿilm al-maʿānī.
How does the book divide inshāʾ?
الإنشاء إما طلبي أو غير طلبي. فالطلبي ما يستدعي مطلوباً غير حاصل وقت الطلب. وغير الطلبي ما ليس كذلك. والأول يكون بخمسة أشياء: الأمر والنهي، والاستفهام، والتمني، والنداء
Ṭalabī (five kinds) and ghayr ṭalabī.tap to reveal
Does ʿilm al-maʿānī discuss ghayr ṭalabī inshāʾ?
وغير الطلبي يكون بالتعجب، والقسم، وصيغ العقود كبعت واشتريت، ويكون بغير ذلك. وأنواع الإنشاء غير الطلبي ليست من مباحث علم المعاني، فلذا ضربنا صفحاً عنها
No; the authors leave it out.tap to reveal

Al-Amr — command

Al-amr is the request for an action with istiʿlāʾ (the requester sees himself above the addressee). It has four forms: fiʿl al-amr, the maṣdar substituting for it, ism fiʿl al-amr, and the present verb joined to lām al-amr. The forms often leave the original meaning for secondary meanings, understood from the context: duʿāʾ, iltimās, tamannī, irshād, tahdīd, taʿjīz, ihāna, ibāḥa, imtinān, takhyīr, taswiya, ikrām.

Al-Nahy — prohibition

Al-nahy is the demand to refrain from an act, with istiʿlāʾ. Its only form is the present verb with lā al-nāhiya. Like amr it can depart from its basic meaning to duʿāʾ, iltimās, tamannī, irshād, tahdīd, tayʾīs and bayān al-ʿāqiba.

Al-Istifhām — interrogation

Istifhām is the demand for knowledge of something. Its eleven particles: hamza, hal, mā, man, matā, ayyāna, kayfa, ayna, annā, kam, ayy. The hamza asks about taṣawwur (grasping a single item) or taṣdīq (grasping the nisba). In taṣawwur, the asked-about item follows the hamza and has a muʿādil after 'am' (muttaṣila); in taṣdīq there is no muʿādil, and the answer is 'naʿam' or 'lā'. Hal is for taṣdīq only (basīṭa: existence of a thing itself; murakkaba: existence of a thing for a thing). The other particles ask for specific things. Many istifhām forms carry secondary meanings: taswiya, nafy, inkār, taqrīr, nahy, tashwīq, taʿẓīm, taḥqīr, tahakkum, taʿajjub, tanbīh ʿalā ḍalāl, waʿīd.
Define istifhām and list its particles.
وأما الاستفهام فهو طلب العلم بشيء. وأدواته: الهمزة، وهل، وما، ومن، ومتى، وأيان، وكيف، وأين، وأنى، وكم، وأي.

Al-Nidāʾ — calling

Nidāʾ is the demand for the addressee's turning toward the caller with a particle standing in for 'adʿū' (I call). It has eight particles: yā, hamza, ay, ā, āy, ayā, hayā, wā. Bāb 2 — Dhikr and Ḥadhf (mention and omission)

Two defaults govern this chapter: any word that indicates part of the meaning the hearer must be given is, by default, mentioned (dhikr); any word that can be known from the rest of the speech is, by default, omitted (ḥadhf). One departs from either default only for a reason (dāʿin). The book lists reasons for dhikr (iḥḍār of clarity, weak indication, mocking the hearer's dullness, tasjīl, wonder, exalting or humiliating) and for ḥadhf (hiding from others, ease of denial, testing the hearer's alertness, narrowness of the situation, magnifying or belittling, meter/rhyme, generalising briefly, courtesy, treating a transitive verb as intransitive). Dropping the agent by passivisation is counted as ḥadhf too.

Bāb 3 — Taqdīm and Taʾkhīr (advancing and delaying)

Bāb 5 — Iṭlāq and Taqyīd (unrestricted and restricted predication)

If a sentence is limited to the musnad and musnad ilayhi, the judgment is muṭlaq; if something connected to them is added, it is muqayyad. Iṭlāq is used when there is no aim in restricting the judgment, so that the listener may take it every possible way; taqyīd when the aim demands a specific restriction, without which the intended benefit would be lost. Restrictions come by mafāʿīl and the like (to specify kind, object, place, cause, accompaniment, state), nawāsikh, shart, nafy and tawābiʿ. The book compares inn / idhā / law in conditionals and the six negators, and the roles of each tābiʿ.

Waṣl is joining one sentence to another (here specifically with wāw); faṣl is leaving the join. The book gives two places where waṣl is obligatory: (1) when the sentences agree in khabar/inshāʾ and have a close link (jiha jāmiʿa), and no obstacle to joining; (2) when omitting the wāw would produce an unintended meaning (īhām). Faṣl is required in five places: kamāl al-ittiṣāl (three forms: badal, bayān, tawkīd), kamāl al-inqiṭāʿ (differ in khabar/inshāʾ; or no relation in meaning), shibh kamāl al-ittiṣāl (the second answers a question that arises), shibh kamāl al-inqiṭāʿ (joining would give a false impression), and tawassuṭ bayn al-kamālayn (no intention to share the ruling, with an obstacle).

Bāb 8 — Ījāz, Iṭnāb and Musāwāt

Whatever is in the mind can be expressed in three ways: musāwāt (wording equal to the meaning, as ordinary people speak), ījāz (shorter wording that still suffices) and iṭnāb (extra wording for a benefit). If the short wording does not suffice it is ikhlāl; extra wording without benefit is taṭwīl (if the surplus is non-specific) or ḥashw (if specific). Ījāz is either by condensing (qiṣar) or by omission (ḥadhf of a word, a sentence or more). Iṭnāb is done by many means: khāṣṣ after ʿāmm or the reverse, īḍāḥ after ibhām, tawshīʿ, takrīr, iʿtirāḍ, īghāl, tadhyīl, iḥtirās, takmīl.
What are the three ways to express a meaning?
كل ما يجول في الصدر من المعاني يمكن أن يعبر عنه بثلاث طرق: المساواة، والإيجاز، والإطناب
Musāwāt (equal), ījāz (shorter and sufficient), iṭnāb (longer for a benefit).tap to reveal
Distinguish taṭwīl and ḥashw.

Surplus with no benefit: taṭwīl if non-specific; ḥashw if specific.tap to reveal

ومن دواعي الإيجاز: تسهيل الحفظ، وتقريب الفهم، وضيق المقام، والإخفاء، وسآمة المحادثة. ومن دواعي الإطناب: تثبيت المعنى، وتوضيح المراد، والتوكيد، ودفع الإيهام

ʿIlm al-Bayān and the pillars of tashbīh

ʿIlm al-bayān deals with tashbīh, majāz (including istiʿāra) and kināya. The book begins with tashbīh: 'ilḥāq amr bi-amr fī waṣf bi-adāt li-gharaḍ'. The two sides may be ḥissī (sensory), ʿaqlī, or one of each; the book notes that a khayālī tashbīh has a non-existent mushabbah bihi whose components are sensible, and a wahmī one has a thing the imagination invented. Divisions of tashbīh

By the two sides, tashbīh is: mufrad bi-mufrad; murakkab bi-murakkab (e.g. Bashshār: dust above our heads and our swords like a night whose stars tumble); mufrad bi-murakkab (poppy as banners); murakkab bi-mufrad (a sunlit day mixed with flowers as a moonlit night). Also malfūf (all mushabbahs then all mushabbah bihis) and mafrūq (each mushabbah followed by its mushabbah bihi), taswiya (several mushabbahs for one mushabbah bihi) and jamʿ (one mushabbah, several mushabbah bihis). By wajh: tamthīl (wajh drawn from several things) vs ghayr tamthīl; mufaṣṣal (wajh stated) vs mujmal. By the adāt: muʾakkad (adāt dropped) vs mursal.

Majāz (lughawī) and Istiʿāra

Majāz is a word used for other than what it was set for, because of a ʿalāqa, with a qarīna preventing the original meaning. If the ʿalāqa is resemblance it is istiʿāra; otherwise majāz mursal. Majāz mursal, majāz murakkab, majāz ʿaqlī

Majāz mursal is a majāz whose ʿalāqa is not resemblance. The book lists eight: sababiyya, musabbabiyya, juzʾiyya, kulliyya, iʿtibār mā kāna, iʿtibār mā yakūn, maḥalliyya and ḥāliyya. Majāz murakkab (compound) is a composite used for other than what it was set for; if the ʿalāqa is not resemblance it is majāz murakkab mursal — e.g. a khabar used for inshāʾ; if it is resemblance it is istiʿāra tamthīliyya (proverbs). Majāz ʿaqlī is ascribing the verb (or its like) to other than what it belongs to by the speaker's apparent belief, because of a relation. The book says: linguistic majāz is in the word; ʿaqlī in the isnād.

Ḥusn al-ibtidāʾ, ḥusn al-takhalluṣ, barāʿat al-ṭalab, ḥusn al-intihāʾ

These are the last devices: a pleasing opening, a graceful move to the point, a subtle way of asking, and a pleasing ending. If the opening has a delicate allusion to the aim it is barāʿat al-istihlāl; if the ending has a sign of conclusion it is barāʿat al-maqṭaʿ. Barāʿat al-ṭalab: the asker hints at what is in his mind without saying it outright.

Tanbīh — teacher's exercises

At the end the book gives a 'tanbīh' to the teacher, listing questions to ask after each bāb: after faṣāḥa/balāgha, why given sentences are not faṣīḥ/balīgh; after khabar/inshāʾ, which type each is and its meaning from qarāʾin; after dhikr/ḥadhf, the reason for each mention or omission; after taqdīm/taʾkhīr; after taʿrīf/tankīr; after tashbīh, the purpose; and the muḥassināt of given examples. The exercises are also an index of the shawāhid the authors consider typical.

Decide khabar or inshāʾ; ask why a khabar is given as a sentence (e.g. 'al-ḥaqq ẓahara'); what the hearer gains from 'anā muʿtarif bi-faḍlika'; which ḍarb a quoted verse is.tap to reveal

1What is shibh kamāl al-ittiṣāl?
The second sentence answers a question that the first suggests, so faṣl.
2Name reasons for ḥadhf with the book's examples.
Hiding; ease of denying; testing alertness; narrow situation (e.g. a hunter says 'ghazāl!' for fear of losing the chance); exalting/belittling; meter or saj'; general scope in brie
3What is the 'qalb' (palindrome) embellishment?
A wording that reads the same in both directions.
4What are the default purposes of khabar?
Fāʾidat al-khabar (inform the judgment) or lāzim al-fāʾida (inform that the speaker knows it).
5What is ḥusn al-ibtidāʾ and barāʿat al-istihlāl?
A pleasing, well-made opening; with a delicate hint of the aim = barāʿat al-istihlāl.
6When is a nakira used?
When no way to define the subject is known; or for taksīr/taqlīl; ʿumūm after negation; or an unspecified individual or kind.
7When is a composite majāz and when istiʿāra tamthīliyya?
If the ʿalāqa is other than resemblance: majāz murakkab (e.g. khabar used as inshāʾ); if resemblance: istiʿāra tamthīliyya.
8What do the tashbīh exercises ask?
The type and purpose of each tashbīh in the verses given.
9What are the forms of taqsīm?
(1) Exhaust the parts of a thing; (2) mention several things and assign each its own explicitly; (3) mention the states of a thing and attach to each what suits it.

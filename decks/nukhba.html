Division of the khabar by its routes (mutawātir, mashhūr, ʿazīz, gharīb)

Ibn Ḥajar opens by dividing the khabar according to how many routes (ṭuruq) it has. Four cases are possible: routes without any fixed count, routes limited to more than two, routes limited to two, or one route. These yield the mutawātir, mashhūr, ʿazīz and gharīb. All but the first are āḥād. Mutawātir is accepted outright because it yields certain knowledge; āḥād must be investigated through their narrators and so split into maqbūl and mardūd. This single taqsīm is the gateway to the whole book: everything after it is about judging āḥād reports.
How is the khabar divided according to its routes?
الخَبَرُ: إِمَّا أَنْ يَكُونَ لَهُ طُرُقٌ بِلَا عَدَدٍ مُعَيَّنٍ، أَوْ مَعَ حَصْرٍ بِمَا فَوْقَ الِاثْنَيْنِ، أَوْ بِهِمَا، أَوْ بِوَاحِدٍ
Four possibilities: (1) many routes with no fixed number, the mutawātir; (2) a limited number above two, the mashhūr; (3) exactly two, the ʿazīz; (4) one, the gharīb.tap to reveal
Which of the four are āḥād and what is the difference from the mutawātir regarding acceptance?
وَكُلُّهَا - سِوَى الأَوَّلِ - آحَادٌ. وَفِيهَا المَقْبُولُ وَالمَرْدُودُ ؛ لِتَوَقُّفِ الِاسْتِدْلَالِ بِهَا عَلَى البَحْثِ عَنْ أَحْوَالِ رُوَاتِهَا - دُونَ الأَوَّلِ
All except the mutawātir are āḥād. Among them are the accepted and the rejected, because relying on them depends on investigating their narrators; the mutawātir needs no such investigation.tap to reveal

Gharāba: al-fard al-muṭlaq and al-fard al-nisbī

Singularity (gharāba) is classified by where it occurs in the chain. If the narrator who stands alone is at the root of the sanad, the point where the Companion is, the report is al-fard al-muṭlaq. If the singularity is somewhere in the middle, relative to a particular narrator, it is al-fard al-nisbī. The terms fard and gharīb are synonyms in language and usage, yet scholars tend to say fard for the muṭlaq and gharīb for the nisbī; when they use the verb (tafarrada bihi / aghraba bihi) they do not distinguish.
How is gharāba divided?
ثُمَّ الغَرَابَةُ: إِمَّا أَنْ تَكُونَ فِي أَصْلِ السَّنَدِ، أَوْ لَا. فَالأَوَّلُ: الفَرْدُ المُطْلَقُ. وَالثَّانِي: الفَرْدُ النِّسْبِيُّ
If the singularity is at the root of the sanad it is al-fard al-muṭlaq; otherwise it is al-fard al-nisbī.tap to reveal

Al-ṣaḥīḥ li-dhātihi: its five conditions and degrees

The first of the four kinds of maqbūl. A khabar āḥād is ṣaḥīḥ li-dhātihi when five conditions are met: a ʿadl narrator, complete ḍabṭ, an unbroken chain (ittiṣāl), no ʿilla, and no shudhūdh. Its ranks differ by how strongly these attributes are present, which is why al-Bukhārī is preferred, then Muslim, then what meets both men's conditions. Nuzha adds the famous ranking of the chains called aṣaḥḥ al-asānīd and the seven marātib by source.
What is the ṣaḥīḥ li-dhātihi?
وَخَبَرُ الآحَادِ بِنَقْلِ عَدْلٍ تَامِّ الضَّبْطِ، مُتَّصِلَ السَّنَدِ، غَيْرَ مُعَلَّلٍ وَلَا شَاذٍّ: هُوَ الصَّحِيحُ لِذَاتِهِ
A khabar āḥād narrated by an upright narrator of complete ḍabṭ, with a connected chain, free of ʿilla and shudhūdh.tap to reveal
Do ṣaḥīḥ reports have equal strength?
وَتَتَفَاوَتُ رُتَبُهُ بِتَفَاوُتِ هَذِهِ الأَوْصَافِ، وَمِنْ ثَمَّ قُدِّمَ صَحِيحُ البُخَارِيِّ، ثُمَّ مُسْلِمٍ ، ثُمَّ شَرْطُهُمَا
No. The ranks differ by the strength of these attributes. Hence al-Bukhārī's Ṣaḥīḥ is placed first, then Muslim's, then what meets their condition.tap to reveal
What are the ranks of the ṣaḥīḥ by source?
وَمِنْ ثَمَّ قُدِّمَ صَحِيحُ البُخَارِيِّ، ثُمَّ مُسْلِمٍ ، ثُمَّ شَرْطُهُمَا
Seven ranks: (1) what both al-Bukhārī and Muslim narrated; (2) al-Bukhārī alone; (3) Muslim alone; (4) on the condition of both though not narrated by them; (5) on al-Bukhārī's condition alone; (6) on Muslim's condition alone; (7) ṣaḥīḥ according to other imams (ibn Khuzayma, Ibn Ḥibbān etc.) not on their conditions.tap to reveal

Al-ḥasan li-dhātihi, al-ṣaḥīḥ li-ghayrihi, and 'ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ'

When ḍabṭ is lighter (khaffa al-ḍabṭ) while the other conditions stand, the report is ḥasan li-dhātihi. Through multiple routes it is raised to ṣaḥīḥ, which is ṣaḥīḥ li-ghayrihi. Ibn Ḥajar thus gets four kinds of maqbūl: ṣaḥīḥ li-dhātihi, ṣaḥīḥ li-ghayrihi, ḥasan li-dhātihi and ḥasan li-ghayrihi. He also explains al-Tirmidhī's combined verdict 'ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ' as hesitation about a lone narrator, or, when the report has two chains, an aggregation of verdicts.
What is the ḥasan li-dhātihi?
فَإِنْ خَفَّ الضَّبْطُ : فَالحَسَنُ لِذَاتِهِ
If ḍabṭ is lighter while the rest of the ṣaḥīḥ conditions hold, it is ḥasan li-dhātihi.tap to reveal
How does a ḥasan report become ṣaḥīḥ?
وَبِكَثْرَةِ طُرُقِهِ يُصَحَّحُ
By the multiplicity of its routes, it is graded ṣaḥīḥ, that is ṣaḥīḥ li-ghayrihi.tap to reveal
What does al-Tirmidhī mean by 'ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ'?
فَإِنْ جُمِعَا فَلِلتَّرَدُّدِ فِي النَّاقِلِ حَيْثُ التَّفَرُّدُ ، وَإِلَّا فَبِاعْتِبَارِ إِسْنَادَيْنِ
If the report is singular in route, it expresses the mujtahid's hesitation about the narrator (ḥasan according to some, ṣaḥīḥ to others); if it has more than one chain it is ṣaḥīḥ by one and ḥasan by another.tap to reveal

Ziyādat al-thiqa; al-maḥfūẓ and al-shādhdh; al-maʿrūf and al-munkar

The addition of a ṣaḥīḥ or ḥasan narrator is accepted unless it contradicts one more reliable than himself. When a reliable narrator is contradicted by someone stronger, the stronger version is maḥfūẓ and the weaker is shādhdh. If the one contradicted is weak, the stronger is maʿrūf and the weak one munkar. Ibn Ḥajar insists that shādhdh requires mukhālafa; it is not mere tafarrud, and he criticises the sweeping rule 'additions of the thiqa are always accepted', showing the early imams weighed (tarjīḥ) case by case.
When is the addition of a reliable narrator accepted?
وَزِيَادَةُ رَاوِيهِمَا مَقْبُولَةٌ مَا لَمْ تَقَعْ مُنَافِيَةً لِمَنْ هُوَ أَوْثَقُ
Accepted as long as it does not contradict a more reliable narrator. If it does, tarjīḥ is applied: the stronger is accepted and the weaker is rejected.tap to reveal
What are maḥfūẓ and shādhdh?
فَإِنْ خُولِفَ بِأَرْجَحَ: فَالرَّاجِحُ المَحْفُوظُ؛ وَمُقَابِلُهُ: الشَّاذُّ
If a narrator is contradicted by someone preponderant, the preponderant is maḥfūẓ and its opposite is shādhdh.tap to reveal
What is the adopted definition of shādhdh?
وعُرِفَ من ه
Shādhdh is what an acceptable (maqbūl) narrator reports in opposition to one more preferable than he is.tap to reveal

Al-mutābiʿ, al-shāhid and al-iʿtibār

A fard nisbī, a report thought to be a lone narration, may turn out to have support. If another narrator agrees with the narrator, that is a mutābiʿ. If a text resembling it comes from a different Companion, that is a shāhid. The search through collections to find such support is iʿtibār, which is a method, not a third kind opposite to them. These matter for strengthening and for judging whether a report is truly gharīb.
What are mutābiʿ and shāhid?
الفَرْدُ النِّسْبِيُّ: إِنْ وَافَقَهُ غَيْرُهُ: فَهُوَ المُتَابِعُ . وَإِنْ وُجِدَ مَتْنٌ يُشْبِهُهُ : فَهُوَ الشَّاهِدُ
If another agrees with the fard nisbī narrator, it is a mutābiʿ; if a text resembling it is found, it is a shāhid.tap to reveal
What is iʿtibār?
وَتَتَبُّعُ الطُّرُقِ لِذَلِكَ: هُوَ الِاعْتِبَارُ
Following the routes to find a mutābiʿ or shāhid for the report.tap to reveal

Resolving apparent conflict: muḥkam, mukhtalif al-ḥadīth, nāsikh/mansūkh, tarjīḥ, tawaqquf

The accepted report is also divided by whether it is acted upon. If it faces no equal opposition it is muḥkam. If it is opposed by an equally accepted report, the sequence is fixed: first jamʿ (reconciliation) which yields mukhtalif al-ḥadīth; if not possible, and the later one is known, naskh (nāsikh and mansūkh); if no history, tarjīḥ; if no preponderance, tawaqquf. A rejected report cannot oppose an accepted one, so it is ignored.
What is the order for resolving apparent conflict?
ثُمَّ المَقْبُولُ: إِنْ سَلِمَ مِنَ المُعَارَضَةِ: فَهُوَ المُحْكَمُ. وَإِنْ عُورِضَ بِمِثْلِهِ: فَإِنْ أَمْكَنَ الجَمْعُ: فَهُوَ مُخْتَلِفُ الحَدِيثِ. أَوْ ثَبَتَ المُتَأَخِّرُ: فَهُوَ النَّاسِخُ، وَالآخَرُ المَنْسُوخُ، وَإِلَّا فَالتَّرْجِيحُ ، ثُمَّ التَّوَقُّفُ
First reconcile (jamʿ) → then naskh if the later is known → then tarjīḥ → then tawaqquf.tap to reveal
What is muḥkam?
إِنْ سَلِمَ مِنَ المُعَارَضَةِ: فَهُوَ المُحْكَمُ
The accepted report that no equal opposing report contradicts.tap to reveal
How are 'lā ʿadwā' and 'flee from the leper' reconciled?
فإنْ أَمْكَنَ الجَمْعُ فهو النوعُ المُسَمَّى مُختَلِفَ الحديث
Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ (following others): diseases do not infect by their own nature, but Allāh made mixing with the sick a cause of transmission, which can fail like other causes. Ibn Ḥajar prefers: the denial of ʿadwā stands in general ('nothing infects another'; 'who infected the first?'), and the command to flee the leper is sadd al-dharāʾiʿ, so that someone who contracts it by Allāh's decree does not conclude infection is real.tap to reveal

Al-mardūd: saqṭ at the beginning (muʿallaq) and at the end (mursal)

The mardūd is rejected either because of saqṭ (a dropped narrator) or ṭaʿn (a criticism of a narrator). Saqṭ is classified by where the gap falls: at the start of the chain by a compiler's doing (muʿallaq); at the end, after the Successor (mursal); or elsewhere (muʿḍal, munqaṭiʿ, mudallas). Both are rejected because the dropped narrator is unknown; Nuzha gives the full khilāf over the mursal.
How is the mardūd divided?
ثُمَّ المَرْدُودُ: إِمَّا أَنْ يَكُونَ لِسَقْطٍ أَوْ طَعْنٍ. فَالسَّقْطُ : إِمَّا أَنْ يَكُونَ مِنْ مَبَادِئِ السَّنَدِ مِنْ مُصَنِّفٍ ، أَوْ مِنْ آخِرِهِ بَعْدَ التَّابِعِيِّ، أَوْ غَيْرِ ذَلِكَ
Into two: rejection for saqṭ (a drop) in the chain, or for ṭaʿn (a flaw) in a narrator. Saqṭ is at the beginning of the sanad by a compiler, or at the end after the Successor, or elsewhere.tap to reveal

فَالأَوَّلُ: المُعَلَّقُ

Muʿḍal, munqaṭiʿ; the apparent and hidden saqṭ: mudallas and mursal khafī

Third category of saqṭ: a drop elsewhere than the start or the end. If two or more consecutive narrators are dropped, it is muʿḍal; otherwise munqaṭiʿ. The drop is either apparent (wāḍiḥ), known by non-meeting (hence the need for history), or hidden (khafī), which is the mudallas and the mursal khafī. Ibn Ḥajar's key refinement: tadlīs requires that the narrator actually met the one he reports from, whereas mursal khafī is from a contemporary who did not meet him.
What are muʿḍal and munqaṭiʿ?
إِنْ كَانَ بِاثْنَيْنِ فَصَاعِدًا مَعَ التَّوَالِي: فَهُوَ المُعْضَلُ، وَإِلَّا فَالمُنْقَطِعُ
If two or more consecutive narrators are dropped, it is muʿḍal; otherwise it is munqaṭiʿ.tap to reveal
How is an apparent gap known and why is history needed?
فَالأَوَّلُ: يُدْرَكُ بِعَدَمِ التَّلَاقِي، وَمِنْ ثَمَّ احْتِيجَ إِلَى التَّارِيخِ
It is known by the non-meeting of the narrator and his shaykh: he did not live in his time, or lived but they did not meet and he has neither ijāza nor wijāda from him. For that reason knowledge of history is needed.tap to reveal
What is tadlīs and what is its ruling?
وَالثَّانِي: المُدَلَّسُ، وَيَرِدُ بِصِيغَةٍ تَحْتَمِلُ اللُّقِيَّ : كَـ «عَنْ»، وَ«قَالَ

Ṭaʿn: the ten causes; mawḍūʿ, matrūk, munkar, muʿallal

Criticism (ṭaʿn) of a narrator comes under ten heads, five concerning ʿadāla (lying, suspicion of lying, fisq, jahāla, bidʿa) and five concerning ḍabṭ (gross error, heedlessness, wahm, mukhālafa, poor memory). Ibn Ḥajar deliberately interleaves them in order of severity. This section covers the first six: lying gives the mawḍūʿ; suspicion of lying gives the matrūk; gross error, heedlessness and fisq give munkar on one opinion; and detected wahm gives the muʿallal.
What are the ten causes of ṭaʿn?
ثُمَّ الطَّعْنُ: إِمَّا أَنْ يَكُونَ لِكَذِبِ الرَّاوِي، أَوْ تُهَمَتِهِ بِذَلِكَ، أَوْ فُحْشِ غَلَطِهِ، أَوْ غَفْلَتِهِ، أَوْ فِسْقِهِ، أَوْ وَهْمِهِ ، أَوْ مُخَالَفَتِهِ، أَوْ جَهَالَتِهِ ، أَوْ بِدْعَتِهِ، أَوْ سُوءِ حِفْظِهِ
Lying; suspicion of lying; gross error; heedlessness; fisq; wahm; contradiction (mukhālafa); jahāla; bidʿa; poor memory.tap to reveal
What is the mawḍūʿ, how is it known and what is its ruling?
فَالأَوَّلُ: المَوْضُوعُ
The fabricated report; the lying narrator's fabrication. Known by: the fabricator's admission (though not certain, per Ibn Daqīq al-ʿĪd, it still counts by ghalabat al-ẓann, as one is executed or stoned on confession); a sign from the narrator's state (e.g. al-Maʾmūn b. Aḥmad's 'al-Ḥasan heard from Abū Hurayra'; Ghiyāth b. What is the matrūk?
وَالثَّانِي: المَتْرُوكُ
The report arising from a narrator suspected of lying: the report is known only through him and contradicts established rules, or the narrator is known to lie in his ordinary speech.tap to reveal

Mukhālafa: mudraj, maqlūb, mazīd fī muttaṣil al-asānīd, muḍṭarib, muṣaḥḥaf/muḥarraf

The seventh cause is mukhālafa, which yields distinct types according to the form of contradiction: changing the wording of the chain (mudraj al-isnād), merging a mawqūf into a marfūʿ (mudraj al-matn), reversing order (maqlūb), adding a narrator (mazīd fī muttaṣil al-asānīd), replacing a narrator with no preponderance (muḍṭarib; also deliberately as a test), or changing letters while the writing outline stays (muṣaḥḥaf, muḥarraf). If the contradiction is by changing the sequence of the chain, it is mudraj al-isnād; if by merging a mawqūf with a marfūʿ, it is mudraj al-matn.tap to reveal

Jahāla: many epithets, few narrations, the mubham, majhūl al-ʿayn and al-ḥāl (mastūr)

The eighth cause is jahāla, ignorance of the narrator. It has causes: he is called by many descriptions so is mentioned by other than his famous name (muwaḍḍiḥ works); he narrates little so few narrate from him (wuḥdān works); or he is not named for brevity (mubhamāt). A mubham is not accepted even when described with taʿdīl wording, on the sounder view. If named but only one narrates from him he is majhūl al-ʿayn; if two or more narrate and he is not declared reliable, majhūl al-ḥāl, the mastūr.
Is a mubham's report accepted, even if described as reliable?
وَلَا يُقْبَلُ المُبْهَمُ وَلَوْ أُبْهِمَ بِلَفْظِ التَّعْدِيلِ - عَلَى الأَصَحِّ

Bidʿa: mukaffira and mufassiqa, and the narrator who is a dāʿiya

The ninth cause is bidʿa, defined in Nuzha as believing what was introduced against what is known from the Prophet, not out of obstinacy but through some doubt. It is either mukaffira (leading to kufr) or mufassiqa (leading to fisq). Ibn Ḥajar gives the majority view on the first, the sounder view on the second: the non-dāʿiya is accepted unless he narrates something that supports his innovation, and then he is rejected on the chosen view.
Is the narrator of a kufr-causing bidʿa accepted?
فَالأَوَّلُ: لَا يَقْبَلُ صَاحِبَهَا الجُمْهُورُ
The majority do not accept him. Ibn Ḥajar's verified position: not every one declared kāfir for a bidʿa is rejected, as every sect accuses its opponents; the one whose narration is rejected is who denies a mutawātir matter of the sharīʿa known from the dīn by necessity, or holds its opposite.tap to reveal
Is the narrator of a fisq-causing bidʿa accepted?
وَالثَّانِي: يُقْبَلُ مَنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ دَاعِيَةً - فِي الأَصَحِّ -، إِلَّا إِنْ رَوَى مَا يُقَوِّي بِدْعَتَهُ فَيُرَدُّ - عَلَى المُخْتَارِ

Sūʾ al-ḥifẓ, al-mukhtaliṭ, and how corroboration yields ḥasan li-ghayrihi

The tenth cause is poor memory: one whose correctness does not outweigh his error. If constant in the narrator it is shādhdh on one view; if it arises (age, blindness, loss of books) he is mukhtaliṭ. Then Ibn Ḥajar answers the practical question of what corroboration does: if a poor-memory narrator is supported by someone reliable (his equal or higher), and likewise the mastūr, the mursal and the mudallas whose dropped narrator is unknown, their ḥadīth becomes ḥasan, not li-dhātihi, but by the aggregate.
How is poor memory divided?
ثُمَّ سُوءُ الحِفْظِ: إِنْ كَانَ لَازِمًا فَالشَّاذُّ - عَلَى رَأْيٍ -، أَوْ طَارِئًا فَالمُخْتَلِطُ
Constant in the narrator: shādhdh on one view; occurring later: mukhtaliṭ.tap to reveal

The endpoint of the isnād: marfūʿ, mawqūf, maqṭūʿ; ṣaḥābī, tābiʿī, mukhaḍram

With the textual criticism finished, Ibn Ḥajar turns to the isnād and its endpoint. A chain ends at the Prophet (marfūʿ), at a Companion (mawqūf) or at a Successor (maqṭūʿ), each in explicit form (taṣrīḥan) or by ruling (ḥukman), whether in speech, action or tacit approval. Digressing, he defines ṣaḥābī and tābiʿī and mentions the mukhaḍramūn. Mawqūf and maqṭūʿ are both called athar.

Al-musnad, al-ʿulūw and al-nuzūl

The musnad is a marfūʿ of a Companion with a chain that appears connected. Ibn Ḥajar then treats the shortness of the chain: ʿulūw muṭlaq if it reaches the Prophet with few narrators, and ʿulūw nisbī if it reaches an imam of exalted quality such as Shuʿba. Four sub-forms of relative height are muwāfaqa, badal, musāwāt and muṣāfaḥa. The opposite of each is nuzūl. High chains are desired because every narrator can err, so fewer links mean fewer opportunities.

Types of relationship between narrators: aqrān, mudabbaj, akābir ʿan aṣāghir, sābiq-lāḥiq, muhmal, juḥūd, musalsal

A group of types defined by how narrator and informant relate. Equals in age and meeting are aqrān; if each narrates from the other, mudabbaj. A narrator from one below him is a senior from a junior (including fathers from sons, and a narrator from his father from his grandfather). Two narrating from one shaykh with one dying earlier give sābiq and lāḥiq. Two same-named shaykhs not distinguished make the muhmal, resolved by specialization. The shaykh's denial of a report is addressed, and chains uniform in wording or state are musalsal.
What are aqrān and mudabbaj?
فَإِنْ تَشَارَكَ الرَّاوِي وَمَنْ رَوَى عَنْهُ فِي السِّنِّ وَاللُّقِيِّ فَهُوَ: الأَقْرَانُ . وَإِنْ رَوَى كُلٌّ مِنْهُمَا عَنِ الآخَرِ: فَالمُدَبَّجُ
If narrator and informant share age and meeting, they are aqrān; if each narrates from the other, mudabbaj.tap to reveal

Ṣiyagh al-adāʾ and the ʿanʿana of the contemporary

Eight levels of delivery wordings, from the most explicit to the most ambiguous: samiʿtu / ḥaddathanī; akhbaranī / qaraʾtu ʿalayhi; qurʾa ʿalayhi wa-anā asmaʿ; anbaʾanī; nāwalanī; shāfahanī; kataba ilayya; ʿan and the like. The first two are for one who heard alone from the shaykh's utterance; the plural form shows others heard with him. Ibn Ḥajar also treats the ʿanʿana of a contemporary: carried to hearing, except from a mudallis; he prefers the stricter view requiring proven meeting at least once.

Ṭuruq al-taḥammul: ijāza, munāwala, mukātaba, wijāda, waṣiyya, iʿlām

The matn treats the names and conditions of the modes of receiving ḥadīth other than direct hearing. Mushāfaha and mukātaba are used loosely for spoken ijāza and written ijāza. Munāwala is valid only when joined with permission to narrate, and is the highest kind of ijāza. The same condition applies to wijāda, bequeathal of a book (waṣiyya) and iʿlām. Resemblance of narrators' names: muttafiq/muftariq, muʾtalif/mukhtalif, mutashābih

When names coincide or resemble, confusion arises between narrators. If names and fathers' names (and further) agree while the persons differ: muttafiq wa-muftariq. If names agree in writing and differ in pronunciation: muʾtalif wa-mukhtalif. If names agree and fathers differ, or the reverse: mutashābih; plus compounds such as agreement but for a letter or two or in reversed order (the reversed mutashābih, maqlūb). Each guards against mistaking one person for two or two for one.
What is the muttafiq wa-muftariq?

Narrators whose own names and their fathers' names (or further) agree while their persons differ.tap to reveal

Khātima: ṭabaqāt, vital dates and places, and the ranks of jarḥ and taʿdīl

The closing chapter lists what a muḥaddith must know about narrators: their ṭabaqāt, births, deaths, lands, and conditions (taʿdīl, tajrīḥ, jahāla). Ibn Ḥajar gives the ranks of jarḥ (worst: wording on the pattern 'af'al' like 'akdhab al-nās', then dajjāl, waḍḍāʿ, kadhdhāb; mildest: layyin, sayyiʾ al-ḥifẓ, fīhi adnā maqāl) and of taʿdīl (highest: awthaq al-nās; then thiqa thiqa, thiqa ḥāfiẓ; lowest: shaykh).

Kitāb al-Buyūʿ — opening section

How a sale comes into being: offer and acceptance in the past tense, the session (majlis), bindingness and the options that survive it, what must be known about the counter-values and the price, what is included in a sale, and who delivers first. Underlying principle: ignorance that leads to dispute invalidates a sale.
How is a sale (bay') concluded, and in what verbal form?
البيع: ينعقد بالإيجاب والقبول إذا كانا بلفظ الماضي
By ijab (offer) and qabul (acceptance), both in the past tense (e.g. 'bi'tu' - I sold / 'ishtaraytu' - I bought).tap to reveal
After one party makes an offer of sale, how long does the other party have to accept?
وإذا أوجب أحد المتعاقدين البيع فالآخر بالخيار: إن شاء قبل في المجلس وإن شاء رده
Until the end of the session (majlis): he may accept or reject within the majlis (khiyar al-qabul). Either party (seller or buyer) may be the one who offers.tap to reveal
What happens to an offer if either party gets up and leaves the session before acceptance?
وأيهما قام من المجلس قبل القبول بطل الإيجاب
The offer (ijab) becomes void; a later 'I accept' does not conclude the sale.tap to reveal
1 · Case clinic · which bāb, rule, terms?

Khalid buys a plot advertised as 500 sq m for a lump-sum price. A survey shows 480 sq m. (Variant: the price was quoted per sq m.)

1) Opening section, qadr vs wasf. 2) Lump sum: area of land is a description; he takes it for the full price or rescinds - no proportional reduction (quduri_r_thawb_naqis). 3) Per-sq-m price: each unit is treated as principal - take at the proportionate price or leave; if larger, take all at the rate or rescind (quduri_r_dhira_mufrad).discuss first — then tap

Bāb Khiyār al-Sharṭ

The stipulated option: up to three days per Abū Ḥanīfa, its effect on ownership and loss, ratifying and rescinding within the period, that it lapses on death, and the option when a stipulated description is missing.
Who may stipulate an option (khiyar al-shart), and for how long?
خيار الشرط: جائز في البيع للبائع والمشتري ولهما الخيار ثلاثة أيام فما دونها ولا يجوز أكثر من ذلك عند أبي حنيفة
It is valid for the seller, the buyer, or both (and per al-Lubab for a third party), for three days or less. More than three days is not permitted according to Abu Hanifa.tap to reveal
Is khiyar al-shart inherited if the option-holder dies?
وإذا مات من له الخيار بطل خياره ولم ينتقل إلى ورثته
No. It lapses, the sale becomes complete from his side, and it does not pass to his heirs.tap to reveal
Principle: does a description (wasf) have a share of the price?
لأن الأوصاف لا يقابلها شيء من الثمن لكونها تابعة في العقد
No - descriptions are subordinate and receive no portion of the price; missing them gives an option (take all or leave), not a discount. [al-Lubab 2:15]tap to reveal
2 · Case clinic · which bāb, rule, terms?

Bilal buys a sealed phone; on opening it at home the screen has a hairline crack he could not have seen at purchase. The shop says 'it still works, we'll give 5% off'.

1) Bab: Khiyar al-'Ayb. 2) Is it a defect? Anything reducing price in merchants' custom (quduri_r_ayb_tarif) - a cracked screen clearly does. 3) Unqualified sale implies soundness (quduri_r_q_salama). 4) His options: return it or keep at full price (quduri_r_ayb_asl). 5) He cannot force a discount, nor can the shop force one on him; a discount by mutual agreement would be a separate settlement (sulh). Descriptions have no share of price (quduri_r_q_awsaf).discuss first — then tap

Bāb Khiyār al-Ruʾya

Buying what one has not seen: the sale is valid and the buyer has an option on seeing; what counts as seeing; the blind buyer; Qudūrī's placement of the unauthorised (fuḍūlī) sale here.
Is it valid to buy something one has not seen? What right does the buyer have?
ومن اشترى شيئا لم يره فالبيع جائز وله الخيار إذا رآه: إن شاء أخذه وإن شاء رده
Valid; the buyer has the option of viewing (khiyar al-ru'ya) when he sees it: he may take it or return it.tap to reveal
3 · Case clinic · which bāb, rule, terms?

Aisha orders a handbag online after seeing photos. When it arrives, the leather and colour are not what she expected, though it matches the listing description. She hasn't used it. Can she send it back?

1) Bab: Khiyar al-Ru'ya - she bought an item she had not seen. 2) Rule quduri_r_ruya_asl: sale valid; she has the option on seeing: keep or return, without needing a defect. 3) Her prior 'acceptance' at checkout does not drop it (quduri_r_q_rida). 4) Key question for her teacher: do photos count as 'seeing'? Al-Jawhara says seeing through glass or a mirror is not seeing (quduri_r_ruya_qadr) - contemporary scholars differ on photos/video. 5) If instead there were a real defect, the separate basis is khiyar al-'ayb (quduri_r_ayb_asl). Study aid, not a fatwa; platform return policies are a separate contractual layer.discuss first — then tap

Bāb Khiyār al-ʿAyb

Defects: whatever lowers the price in merchants' custom; the buyer returns or keeps at full price; a new defect bars return and gives a claim for the shortfall; alterations like sewing and dyeing; release from all defects.
A buyer finds a defect in the item. What are his options? May he keep it and claim a discount?
إذا اطلع المشتري على عيب في المبيع فهو بالخيار: إن شاء أخذه بجميع الثمن وإن شاء رده وليس له أن يمسكه ويأخذ النقصان
He may keep it at the full price or return it. He may not keep it and claim the diminution (nuqsan) - unless return has become impossible (see the following rules), or the seller agrees.tap to reveal
What counts as a defect ('ayb) legally?
وكل ما أوجب نقصان الثمن في عادة التجار فهو عيب
Anything that reduces the price according to the custom of merchants (people of the trade).tap to reveal
A new defect arises with the buyer, and then he discovers an old defect from the seller's time. May he return it?
وإذا حدث عند المشتري عيب ثم اطلع على عيب كان عند البائع فله أن يرجع بنقصان العيب ولا يرد المبيع إلا أن يرضى البائع أن يأخذه بعيبه
He cannot return it; he claims the diminution (nuqsan al-'ayb, i.e. arsh) for the old defect - unless the seller agrees to take it back with the new defect.tap to reveal
4 · Case clinic · which bāb, rule, terms?

Zainab buys a used car; the contract states 'sold as is, seller not responsible for any defects'. A week later the gearbox fails due to an old fault.

1) Bab: Khiyar al-'Ayb, final masala. 2) The general release from every defect is valid even if defects are not named (quduri_r_baraa); she cannot return. 3) Khilaf on defects arising between contract and delivery (Abu Yusuf: covered; Muhammad: not). 4) Note: deliberate deception (tadlis/ghish) is a separate sin issue beyond this masala - ask a teacher.discuss first — then tap

Quick quiz

Answer aloud, then tap the row

1What counts as a defect ('ayb) legally?
Anything that reduces the price according to the custom of merchants (people of the trade).
2Once offer and acceptance are complete, can either party cancel unilaterally before leaving the session?
No. The sale is binding (lazim) immediately, even before taking possession; there is no option of the session (khiyar al-majlis). The only options remaining are for a defect ('ayb)
3May a sale be for a deferred price, and on what condition?
Yes: the price may be immediate (hall - the default) or deferred (mu'ajjal), provided the term (ajal) is known.
4Principle: what kind of ignorance (jahala) invalidates a sale?
Ignorance that leads to dispute (mufdiya ila al-munaza'a), thereby obstructing delivery and receipt, prevents validity. [al-Lubab 2:6]
5Principle: what role does custom ('urf / 'ada) play in sale?
Custom determines unstated contractual content (cf. maxims 'al-'ada muhakkama' and 'al-ma'ruf 'urfan ka-l-mashrut shartan' - standard maxims, not from this text).
6What is required when the price is not present but stated in general terms (athman mutlaqa)?
Its quantity (qadr, e.g. 10) and description (sifa, e.g. type/quality of coin) must both be known; otherwise the sale is not valid.
7A new defect arises with the buyer, and then he discovers an old defect from the seller's time. May he return it?
He cannot return it; he claims the diminution (nuqsan al-'ayb, i.e. arsh) for the old defect - unless the seller agrees to take it back with the new defect.
8If the seller stipulated freedom from liability for every defect ('sold as is'), may the buyer return for a defect?
No - even if the defects were neither named nor listed.
9Principle: what does an unqualified sale contract implicitly require regarding quality?
An unqualified contract requires the item to be free of defects; soundness is the default in created things. [al-Lubab 2:19]
10How is a sale (bay') concluded, and in what verbal form?
By ijab (offer) and qabul (acceptance), both in the past tense (e.g. 'bi'tu' - I sold / 'ishtaraytu' - I bought).

The Adjective (Naat)

Al-tawabi (followers) are five: naat, tawkid, atf bayan, atf nasaq, badal; each follows its matbu in irab. The naat completes its matbu by stating one of its own qualities or a quality of something connected to it (sababi). It must agree with the matbu in irab and definiteness/indefiniteness; in number and gender it behaves like a verb (if it raises a hidden pronoun it agrees fully; if it raises a visible noun it stays singular and follows that noun in gender). It must be derived (mushtaqq) or interpretable as derived; sentences may qualify only indefinites and must not be imperative/interrogative; several naats may be followed or cut (qat) for praise, blame or pity.
What is the naat and what is it for?
فالنعت تابع متم ما سبق ... بوسمه أو وسم ما به أعتلق
The naat is the tabi that completes its matbu by stating a quality of it or of what is attached to it. Its purposes: takhsis (specifying), madh, dhamm, tarahhum, tawkid.tap to reveal
Does the naat agree with the matbu in definiteness?
وليعط في التعريف والتنكير ما ... لما تلا ك امرر بقوم كرما
Yes. It takes the matbu's irab and its definiteness or indefiniteness: a definite is described by a definite, an indefinite by an indefinite.tap to reveal
How does the naat agree in number and gender?
وهو لدى التوحيد والتذكيرأو ... سواهما كالفعل فأقف ما قفوا
Like the verb: if the naat raises a hidden pronoun it agrees with the matbu in gender and number; if it raises a visible noun it follows that noun in gender and is always singular.tap to reveal

Emphasis (Tawkid)

Tawkid is the tabi that removes doubt about the matbu or its comprehensiveness. Ma'nawi tawkid uses nafs and ayn (with a pronoun agreeing with the muakkad), kilā/kiltā, kull, jamiʿ, aamma and ajmaʿ with its sisters (ajmaʿ, jamʿāʾ, ajmaʿūn, jumaʿ), and the afʿal/faʿlāʾ forms. Lafzi tawkid repeats the word (or its equivalent). Basrans forbid tawkid of an indefinite; Kufans allow it if it is defined in time. A visible pronoun may be emphasised by nafs/ayn only after a detached (munfasil) pronoun is placed.
How are nafs and ayn used for tawkid?
بالنفس أو بالعين الاسم أكدا ... مع ضمير طابق المؤكدا
Each is attached to a pronoun agreeing with the muakkad, and the pair is put in plural afʿul forms when the muakkad is plural or dual.tap to reveal
Which words stress a dual muakkad and the plural?
وكلا اذكر في الشمول وكلا ... كلتا جميعا بالضمير موصلا
Kila (m.) and kilta (f.) for the dual with a pronoun; kull, jamiʿ, aamma for generality, attached to a pronoun.tap to reveal
Which words follow kull in tawkid?
وبعد كل أكدوا بأجمعا ... جمعاء أجمعين ثم جمعا
After kull come ajmaʿ, jamʿāʾ, ajmaʿūn, jumaʿ; they may also appear without kull.tap to reveal

Explicative Apposition (Atf al-Bayan)

Atf is of two kinds, bayan and nasaq. Atf bayan is a non-derived tabi resembling the naat in clarifying its matbu (it makes the meaning clear), and it agrees with the matbu in irab, definiteness, gender and number. It usually follows the more definite name with a more famous one. The disputed issue is whether it may be a badal: whatever can be atf bayan can usually be badal, except in cases like yā ghulām yaʿmuru and the case of anā ibn al-tārik al-bakri bishr (Basrans make bishr a badal, Farāʾ and Fārisī atf bayan).
What is atf bayan?
فذو البيان تابع شبه الصفه ... حقيقة القصد به منكشفه
A tabi, close to the sifa, whose intended meaning is manifest; it clarifies the matbu as a naat does, but is a jamid (non-derived) noun.tap to reveal
How does atf bayan agree with its matbu?
فأولينه من وفاق الأول ... ما من وفاق الأول النعت ولي
Like the naat: it agrees in irab, definiteness or indefiniteness, gender and number. It can be both nakira or both marifa.tap to reveal

Coordination by Conjunction (Atf al-Nasaq)

Atf nasaq is a tabi connected to its matbu by one of nine particles: wāw, fāʾ, thumma, ḥattā, am, aw, bal, lā, lākin. Wāw is for absolute joining (no order); fāʾ is for order with immediacy; thumma for order with delay; ḥattā for gradation with a ghāya; am is for the question after the hamza of taswiya or hamzat al-taʿyīn; aw for choice, permission, division, doubt and iḍrāb; lākin, bal, lā for negation/affirmation shifts. Specific conditions: after an attached nominative pronoun a separator is needed; a genitive pronoun needs repetition of the khafiḍ according to the majority (Ibn Malik allows without it).
What is atf nasaq?
تال بحرف متبع عطف النسق ... كاخصص بود وثناء من صدق
A tabi that follows its matbu by means of a conjunction that is among the nine particles.tap to reveal
What does the wāw do?
فاعطف بواو لاحقا أو سابقا ... في الحكم أو مصاحبا موافقا
It joins absolutely (mutlaq al-jamʿ) without indicating order: the second may come before, after or with the first. It alone joins what the matbu alone does not give meaning to.tap to reveal
What do fāʾ and thumma do?
والفاء للترتيب باتصال ... وثم للترتيب بانفصال
Both give order; fāʾ with immediacy (no gap), thumma with delay (gap). Fāʾ also joins what is a consequence of the first, and the joined clause not a silah.tap to reveal

The Substitute (Badal)

The badal is the tabi that is the intended subject of the ruling without an intermediary. It has four forms: mutabiq (kull min kull), baʿḍ min kull, ishtimal, and iḍrāb (with kaʾannahu ma'tūf bi-bal), which splits into badal al-iḍrāb (intentional) and badal al-ghalaṭ (slip) and badal al-nisyān. A badal may not be of a first or second person visible pronoun unless it is of iḥāṭa (inclusion), baʿḍ or ishtimāl. A badal can follow a hamza question's member and a verb may be a badal of a verb.
What is the badal?
التابع المقصود بالحكم بلا ... واسطة هو المسمى بدلا
The tabi that is meant to be the subject of the ruling without an intermediary: mutabiq, baʿd, ishtimal or like a matuf with bal.tap to reveal
How does badal al-iḍrāb differ from ghalaṭ?
وذا للإضراب أعز إن قصدا صحب ... ودون قصد غلط به سلب
Idrab: the first was intended then abandoned deliberately; ghalaṭ: the first was a slip of the tongue; nisyan is forgetfulness. The badal of ghalaṭ and nisyan are rarer.tap to reveal
May a badal be taken from a first/second-person pronoun?
ومن ضمير الحاضر الظاهر لا ... تبدله إلا ما إحاطة جلا
Not unless the badal gives iḥāṭa (inclusion) or is baʿd or ishtimal of it.tap to reveal

The Vocative (Nida)

Nida is calling someone with a particle that stands in for adʿū. Yā, ayyā, hayā, āy, ay and ā are for distant (or heedless) addressees; the hamza is for the near; wā is for the mandūb (with yā when no confusion). The munādā is a direct object in meaning and is mansūb in position. A mufrad marifa or a specified nakira is built on what it would take in raf (damma, alif, wāw); a mudāf, a shibh mudāf and an unspecified nakira are mansūb. Followers (tawābiʿ) of a damma-built munādā have fine rules: mudāf without al must be nasb, others may be raf or nasb; a nasaq or badal has the rule of an independent munādā; and ayyuhā/ayyuhādhā need a raf sifa.
Which particles call whom?
وللمنادى الناء أو كالناء يا ... وأي وآ كذا آيا ثم هيا
Yā, ayā, hayā, ay and ā for the distant (or what is treated as distant: sleeper, inattentive); the hamza for the near; wā for the mandūb, and yā when no confusion arises.tap to reveal
When may the vocative particle be omitted?
وغير مندوب ومضمر وما ... جا مستغاثا قد يعرى فاعلما
It may be dropped (mostly with ism jins and ishara it is rare), but not with the mandūb, the pronoun or the mustaghāth.tap to reveal
How is a mufrad marifa munada given irab?
وابن المعرف المنادى المفردا ... على الذي في رفعه قد عهدا
It is built on what it would take in raf: damma, alif (dual) or wāw (plural); and it is in the position of nasb as a maful bihi.tap to reveal

Munada Annexed to Ya al-Mutakallim and Nida-Bound Nouns

When a sound munada is annexed to the speaker's yā there are five forms: delete the yā keeping kasra (yā ʿabdi, most frequent), keep the yā sākina, turn the yā to alif then delete it keeping fatha, keep alif (yā ʿabdā), keep the yā with fatha. Yā ibn ʿamm and yā ibn umm lose the yā (with kasra or fatha on the mim). In 'abat' and 'ummat' the tāʾ is a compensation for the yā and can take kasra or fatha. Some nouns are used only in nida: fulu, luʾmān, nawmān, and patterns of faʿāli (yā khabāthi) and faʿal (yā fasaqi) in cursing.
What are the five forms of a munada mudaf to the yā of the speaker?
واجعل منادى صح إن يصف ليا ... كعبد عبدي عبد عبدا عبديا
1. delete yā and keep kasra (most), 2. keep yā sākina, 3. turn to alif and delete (fatha remains), 4. keep the alif (yā ʿabdā), 5. yā with fatha. Mu'tall (defective) endings follow the rules of the non-munada mudaf.tap to reveal
What of yā ibn umm and yā ibn amm?
وفتح أو كسر وحذف اليا استمر ... في يا ابن أم يا ابن عم لا مفر
The yā is deleted (because of frequent use) and the mim takes kasra or fatha.tap to reveal
Which nouns are bound to nida?
وفل بعض ما يخص بالندا ... لؤمان نومان كذا واطردا
Fulu, luʾmān, nawmān (samāʿī), and qiyās: faʿāli in cursing a woman (yā khabāthi) and the command of a thulāthī verb (yā fasaqi); faʿal in cursing men is frequent but not qiyās.tap to reveal

Calling for Help (Istighatha) and Lament (Nudba)

In istighāṭha the called person is a munādā governed by lām maftūḥa: yā li-Zaydin li-ʿAmrin. The lām of the mustaghāth is fatha (as with a pronoun), the lām of the mustaghāth lahu is kasra; with a repeated yā the lām of the second stays fatha; without repetition kasra. The lām may be replaced by an alif (yā Zaydā li-ʿAmrin) and the same for the object of wonder. Nudba is calling a person for whom one grieves with wā (or yā); only a marifa, not an ishara, is mandūb; a silah of known fame (wā man ḥafara biʾra Zamzamāh) is allowed. An alif is added to the end of the mandūb; the alif and a tanwin are dropped; vowels harmonise; hāʾ al-sakt may be added in waqf.
How is the mustaghath formed?
إذا استغيث اسم منادى خفضا ... باللام مفتوحا كيا للمرتضى
The munada is made majrūr by a lām maftūha, in the position of nasb: yā li-Zaydin li-ʿAmrin; the lām of the mustaghāth lahu is kasra.tap to reveal
What of the second mustaghath joined by wāw?
وافتح مع المعطوف إن كررت يا ... وفي سوى ذلك بالكسر ائتيا
If yā is repeated its lām stays fatha; if not repeated, the lām takes kasra; so does every other lām (the mustaghāth lahu).tap to reveal
May the lām be dropped?
ولام ما استغيث عاقبت ألف ... ومثله اسم ذو تعجب ألف
Yes, an alif is added instead of the lām (yā Zaydā li-ʿAmrin); also the object of wonder (yā ʿajabā li-Zaydin).tap to reveal

The Diptote (Ma La Yansarif)

Ṣarf is the tanwīn that shows the noun is amkan (fully declinable). A noun is denied ṣarf if it has two of nine causes, or one cause that takes the place of two: ʿadl, waṣf, taʾnīth, maʿrifa, ʿujma, jamʿ, tarkīb, alif and nūn zāʾidatān, and wazn al-fiʿl. The Jussive Operators (Awamil al-Jazm)

The jazm particles are of two kinds: those that jazm one verb (lām of command or supplication, lā of prohibition or supplication, lam, lammā) and those that jazm two verbs (in, man, mā, mahmā, ayy, matā, ayyāna, ayna, idhmā, ḥaythumā, annā, and the others). The first clause is the shart and the second the jawāb/jazāʾ. Both can be past, both present, past/present, or present/past (rare). If the shart is past the jazāʾ may be raf or jazm. The jawab must have fāʾ when it can not be a shart (nominal sentence, command, negated by mā or lan, qad, tanfīs); idhā al-fujāʾa may replace the fāʾ. After the jazāʾ, a verb with fāʾ/wāw may be jazm, raf or nasb (tathlith). The shart can replace the jawab and vice versa when known. When shart and oath meet, the jawab of the second is dropped.

The Maqṣūr and the Mamdūd

Maqṣūr is a noun ending in a fixed alif; mamdūd is a noun ending in a hamza after an extra alif. Each is qiyāsī or samāʿī. A noun with a regular equivalent with an alif before the last letter is qiyāsī mamdūd (masdar of verbs starting with a hamzat waṣl: irʿiwāʾ). Words without an equivalent are samāʿī (al-fatā, al-ḥijā; al-fatāʾ, al-ḥidhāʾ). Shortening a mamdūd is accepted by all in poetry; lengthening a maqṣūr is disputed.

The Broken Plural (Jamʿ al-Taksīr)

Jamʿ al-taksīr signifies more than two with a visible or implied change of the singular's form. The Diminutive (al-Tasghīr)

A declinable noun is diminished by putting damma on the first letter, fatha on the second, then a sākin yāʾ, with the rest following. There are three patterns: fuʿayl (triliteral: fulays), fuʿayʿil (quadriliteral: durayhim) and fuʿayʿīl (quinquiliteral/with long vowel before the last: ʿuṣayfīr). Longer words lose letters like in the plural (sufayrij). Compensation by yāʾ is allowed. The letter after the yāʾ is fatha if followed by tāʾ, alif of taʾnīth, afʿāl, faʿlān (sakrān); else kasra. A weak second letter is restored to its origin (qayma → quwayma). A short noun is completed (dam → dumayy). A triliteral feminine takes tāʾ unless confusing. Tasghir tarkhīm exists. Alladhī and dhā are shādhdh.

The Relative Adjective (al-Nasab)

Nisba adds a shaddad yāʾ with kasra before it to a noun to link it to a person, tribe or place (Dimashqī, Tamīmī). What the yāʾ precedes is dealt with as follows: a final yāʾ shadda after three letters is dropped (Shāfiʿī); tāʾ of taʾnīth is dropped (Makkī); alif maqṣūra fifth is dropped, fourth moving second letter dropped (jamazī), fourth with sākin second: drop (preferred) or wāw (ḥublawī); ilḥāq alif fourth: drop or wāw (wāw preferred); an original alif third becomes wāw (ʿaṣawī), fourth wāw (malhawī) or dropped, fifth dropped (muṣṭafawī); defective yāʾ third becomes wāw (shajawī), fifth dropped; fourth: drop preferred to wāw (qāḍī → qāḍī or qāḍawī). Fatha replaces kasra on a fiʿil/fuʿil/fiʿil noun (namirī, duʾalī, ibilī). Dual and sound plural endings are dropped. Fa'īla and fuʿayla take fatḥa of ʿayn and drop yāʾ (ḥanafī, juhanī). Compounds are treated individually. Taking back a deleted lām is required if it returns in the dual/plural (abawī, akhawī), allowed otherwise (yadī, yadawī). A plural is returned to the singular (farḍī) except if used as a name (anṣārī). Faʿʿāl, fāʿil, faʿil may replace the nisba (bazzār, tāmir, ṭaʿim).

Pausal Forms (al-Waqf)

In waqf an indefinite tanwīn after fatha becomes alif (ra'aytu Zaydā), after damma or kasra it is dropped. The attached pronoun hāʾ's ṣila is dropped in waqf (except with fatha: alif is kept). Idhan's nūn becomes alif. A defective noun with tanwin takes either deletion (better) or retention of its yāʾ (Ibn Kathīr: hādī) when not nasb; without tanwin the reverse; a noun with deleted ʿayn/fāʾ keeps yāʾ (murī, yafī). A moving last letter (not the hāʾ of taʾnīth) has five ways: sukūn, rawm, ishmām, taḍʿīf, naql. Naql (moving the vowel to the sākin before) needs a sākin before it and no pattern missing from Arabic; Kufans allow it with any vowel; Basrans not with fatha unless hamza. Tāʾ of taʾnīth in waqf: tāʾ for verbs, plural/sound plurals, and after a sākin healthy letter (bint); hāʾ otherwise. Hāʾ al-sakt follows verbs that lost their last letter; with mā al-istifhāmiyya after a preposition; with built vowel lazim (kayfa → kayfah), not with iʿrāb or temporary vowels.

Inclination (al-Imāla)

Imāla is bringing the fatha towards the kasra and the alif towards the yāʾ. The alif is inclined when final and from a yāʾ or going to a yāʾ (ramā, malhā) without extra or shudhūdh, even if followed by the tāʾ of taʾnīth (fatāt). The alif that replaces a verb's ʿayn is inclined if the verb becomes fiʿlu with kasra (khāfa, dāna) but not fuʿlu (qāla). The alif after a yāʾ (bayān), separated by a letter (yasār) or by two with hāʾ (jaybahā) is inclined; one after kasra (ʿālim) or after a letter after kasra (kitāb), shimlāl etc. Seven isti'lā letters (khāʾ, ṣād, ḍād, ṭāʾ, ẓāʾ, ghayn, qāf) block imāla when after the alif; and non-kasra rāʾ also; but a kasra rāʾ overpowers. A separated cause does not act (Qāsim); a blocker can act when separate. Imāla may be for harmony (ʿimādā, talā). Only declinable nouns take imāla except hā and nā. The fatha before a kasra rāʾ at the end may be inclined (bi-shararin).
When is the final alif inclined?
الألف المبدل من يا في طرف ... أمل كذا الواقع منه اليا خلف
When it is from a yāʾ (ramā, marmā) or goes to a yāʾ in some form (malhā → malhayān), not because of an extra or shudhūdh; also if followed by the tāʾ of taʾnīth (fatāt).tap to reveal
What of the alif that replaces the ʿayn of a verb?
وهكذا بدل عين الفعل إن ... يؤل إلى فلت كماضي خف ودن
It is inclined if the verb becomes fiʿltu with kasra (khāfa, dāna) and not if it becomes fuʿltu (qāla, jāla).tap to reveal
What about the alif after a yāʾ?
كذاك تالي الياء والفصل اغتفر ... بحرف أو مع ها ك جيبها أدر
It is inclined after a yāʾ (bayān), or separated by one letter (yasār) or two if one is hāʾ (adir jaybahā).tap to reveal

Morphology (al-Taṣrīf): Patterns, Roots and Augments

Taṣrīf is the science of the structure of the Arabic word and of its letters' being original or augmented, sound or weak. It applies only to declinable nouns and verbs, not particles. The minimum is three letters (unless a letter has been dropped: yad, qil). A bare noun is triliteral, quadriliteral or at most quintiliteral; with augments up to seven letters (ishahbāb). Triliteral nouns have twelve patterns (fiʿul is unused, fuʿil rare); verbs: faʿala, faʿila, faʿula, and fuʿila for the passive. Quadriliteral nouns have six, quintiliteral four. A letter that always stays is original; one that drops in some forms is augmented. In weighing, the originals match fāʾ, ʿayn, lām (repeating lām for more); augments are named by their letters; an augment that is a doubling of an original takes the original's letter (ifʿawʿala for aghdawdana). Signs of augment: alif with more than two roots, yāʾ and wāw with three roots, hamza and mīm before three roots, hamza and nūn at the end after an alif and 3 roots, nūn in ghaḍanfar; tāʾ in feminine, present, istifʿāl, muṭāwaʿa; hāʾ in waqf; lām in demonstratives. The ten augment letters (saʾaltumūnīhā) are original unless proved.
What is taṣrīf and what does it cover?

Hamzat al-Wasl

One cannot start with a sākin, so a moving hamza is added; this is hamzat al-waṣl which is pronounced when beginning and dropped in the middle of speech. It occurs in the past, the imperative and the masdar of a verb exceeding four letters (inṭalaqa, istakhraja), and in the imperative of the triliteral (ikhsha, imḍi, infudh). In nouns it is heard only in ten: ism, ist, ibn, ibnum, ithnān, imruʾ, imraʾa, ibna, ithnatān, aymun in the oath. In particles only in al; its hamza (open) is replaced by a lengthening alif or eased after the interrogative hamza (āl-amīru, a-l-ḥaqqu).

Substitution (al-Ibdāl) I: Hamza, Yāʾ, Wāw and Alif

Ibdāl is replacing one letter by another. The common substitution letters are nine, collected in 'hadaʾat mūṭiyan': hāʾ, dāl, hamza, tāʾ, mīm, wāw, ṭāʾ, yāʾ, alif. Hamza replaces wāw or yāʾ that are final after an extra alif (duʿāʾ, binā), and the ʿayn of an active participle whose verb is weak (qāʾil, bāʾiʿ); also the extra long vowel after the alif of mafāʿil (ṣaḥāʾif) and the second of two weak letters around it (nayāʾif, awāʾil). Where lām is weak the hamza turns to yāʾ via fatha (khaṭāyā, zawāyā). Two hamzas in a word: if the second is sākin it becomes a long vowel of the first's vowel (āthara, ūthira, īthār); if moving: fatha after fatha/damma becomes wāw (awādim, uwaydim); kasra becomes yāʾ (ayyin, iyyim); damma after kasra... Alif turns to yāʾ after kasra or diminutive yāʾ (maṣābīḥ, ghuzayyil), and wāw turns to yāʾ at the end after kasra, before tāʾ, or before faʿlān's endings, or in the masdar of a weak-ʿayn verb (ṣiyām, qiyām).
Which letters are substituted regularly?
أحرف الابدال هدأت موطيا ... فأبدل الهمزة من واو ويا
The nine of 'hadaʾat mūṭiyan'; others are rare or irregular (ṭajaʿa for iḍṭajaʿa).tap to reveal
When does the hamza replace wāw/yāʾ?
آخرا أثر ألف زيد وفي ... فاعل ما أعل عينا ذا اقتفى
When they are final after an extra alif (duʿāʾ from duʿāw, binā from bināy), and as the ʿayn of an active participle whose verb is weak (qāʾil, bāʾiʿ); not if the alif is not extra (āya, rāya) or the letter is not final (tabāyun).tap to reveal
What of the extra long vowel in the singular and mafāʿil?
والمد زيد ثالثا في الواحد ... همزا يرى في مثل كالقلائد
It becomes a hamza after the alif of the plural: qalāʾid, ṣaḥāʾif, ʿajāʾiz; not if it was not a long vowel (qasāwir) or an original (mafāwiz); maṣāʾib is heard.tap to reveal

Substitution (al-Ibdāl) II: I'lāl, Taṣḥīḥ, Naql, Ḥadhf

When wāw and yāʾ meet with the first sākin (originally) the wāw becomes yāʾ and they are merged (sayyid, mayyit). A moving wāw or yāʾ after fatha becomes alif (qāla, bāʿa) if the following letter is moving, and if sākin there is no i'lal except in the lām (kecuali with alif or shadda yāʾ: ramaya, ʿulwī). The verb of afʿal participle stays sound (ʿawira, a'war); iftaʿala meaning tafāʿala stays sound with wāw (ishtawarū). When both ʿayn and lām are weak, the last is changed (al-ḥayā). The ʿayn before a noun-only augment stays sound (jawalān). The nūn becomes mīm before bāʾ (mim bat anbudhā). The vowel of a weak ʿayn is moved to a healthy sākin before it (yabīnu, yaqūmu) unless the verb is wonder, doubled, or weak-lām; nouns like the verb (tabīʿ, maqām). Mifʿal and mifʿāl stay sound; ifʿāl and istifʿāl drop the alif and take tāʾ (iqāma, istiqāma). Mafʿūl from a hollow verb drops a wāw (mabīʿ, maṣūn); from a weak-lām verb in wāw the sound form is better (maʿdūw). In iftiʿāl the weak fāʾ becomes tāʾ (ittiṣāl); after the ṭabaq letters it is ṭāʾ (iṣṭabara), after dāl, dhāl, zāy it is dāl (iddāna, izdād, iddakara). Fāʾ of the imperative/present of wa'ada drops, as does the second hamza of afʿala (yukrimu); ẓaltu → ẓiltu/ẓaltu; qirna for iqrarna.

إن يسكن السابق من واو ويا ... واتصلا ومن عروض عريا
The wāw becomes yāʾ and the two are merged: sayyid, mayyit; not if in two words (yaʿṭī wāqid) or the sukūn is incidental (ruʾya → ruwya, quwiya).tap to reveal
When does a moving wāw/yāʾ become alif?
من ياء أو واو بتحريك أصل ... ألفا أبدل بعد فتح متصل
When it is original in its vowel and after a fatha in the same word: qāla, bāʿa from qawala, bayaʿa; an incidental vowel does not count (jayal, tawam).tap to reveal
What if the following letter is sākin?
إن حرك التالي وإن سكن كف ... إعلال غير اللام وهي لا يكف
I'lal of other than the lām is prevented (bayān, ṭawīl); the lām is changed unless the sākin is an alif or shadda yāʾ (ramayā, ʿulwī).tap to reveal

Assimilation (al-Idghām)

Two like letters, both moving in one word, are assimilated (the first into the second) unless they begin the word (dadan), the word is an noun on fiʿal, fuʿul, fuʿal or fiʿil (ṣifaf, dhulal, kilal, lubab), the first follows an assimilated letter (jussas), the second's vowel is incidental (ikhṣuṣ abī), the word is mulḥaq (hayyalal, qardad), and shudhūdh as in alil. Doubled yāʾ in ḥayiya allows both. A verb beginning with two tāʾs (tatajallā) may be kept or assimilated with hamzat wasl (attajallā) or one tāʾ dropped (tanazzalu). When the second letter's lām is made sākin by a rafʿ pronoun it is unassimilated (ḥalaltu); in jazm both are allowed (lam yaḥlil/lam yaḥill; Ḥijāz open, Tamīm assimilate). The afʿil of wonder and hallumma: wonder not assimilated, hallumma assimilated.
When are two like moving letters in one word merged?
أول مثلين محركين في ... كلمة أدغم لا كمثل صفف
When both move (the first assimilated into the second), unless a blocker applies: at the start of a word, in a noun on fiʿal, fuʿal, fuʿul or fiʿil, when the first follows an assimilated letter, when the vowel of the second is incidental, or in a word that is mulḥaq.tap to reveal
What are the blockers' examples?

Dadan (initial), ṣifaf/dhulal/kilal/lubab (nouns of the four patterns), jussas, ikhṣuṣ abī, hayyalal/qardad (mulḥaq).tap to reveal
What of ḥayiya and ʿayiya?
وحبى أفكك وادغم دون حذر ... كذاك نحو تتجلى واستتر
Both unmerging and merging allowed if the two yāʾs are of fixed vowels (ḥayya/ḥayiya); not if incidental (lan yuḥyiya).tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Wakāla (Agency)

Hidāya explains Qudūrī's agency chapter by showing its proofs (the Prophet's appointments, need) and, mostly, the disputes. The big themes: who must consent to a litigation agent (AH vs Ṣāḥibayn), when the agent is the legal party of the contract rights (Ḥanafī) versus a pure envoy, the agent's limits in buying and selling (price, relatives, barter), and when the mandate ends. Fatwā-level notes (Zufar on litigation agents) show how custom modifies the earlier school position.
What is the textual proof that agency (wakāla) is lawful?
وكل بالشراء حكيم بن حزام
Hidāya grounds the Qudūrī rule in the Prophet appointing Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām to buy and ʿUmar b. Umm Salama to contract a marriage; and ʿAlī appointing ʿAqīl (later ʿAbd Allāh b. Jaʿfar) to litigate.tap to reveal
May an agent prove or collect a ḥadd/qiṣāṣ claim in the principal's absence?
وقال أبو يوسف ﵀: لا تجوز الوكالة بإثبات الحدود والقصاص بإقامة الشهود أيضا
Abū Ḥanīfa: agency is valid to litigate/prove ḥudūd and qiṣāṣ but not to execute them while the principal is absent (doubt of pardon). Abū Yūsuf: not valid even for proving by witnesses. Muḥammad is reported with either.tap to reveal
Can a litigation agent be appointed against the opponent's wish? Why does Abū Ḥanīfa differ?
وقالا: يجوز التوكيل بغير رضا الخصم
Abū Ḥanīfa: binding only with the opponent's consent unless the principal is ill or a three-day traveller. Ṣāḥibayn (and al-Shāfiʿī): valid without his consent. The author notes there is no disagreement on permissibility, only on bindingness (luzūm).tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Daʿwā (Claims and Evidence)

Hidāya takes Qudūrī's procedure of claims and supplies its logic: why nukūl is a proof, why the oath is not returned, and the long taxonomy of taḥālūf and conflicting proofs. It leans on three pillars: the two ḥadīths (proof on the claimant, oath on the denier; taḥālūf), the qiyās rationale and the Imams' disputes (AH vs Ṣāḥibayn, Shāfiʿī). Only what exceeds Qudūrī is marked as new; the rest are inherited nodes.
Is the Qudūrī definition the only one?
المدعي من لا يستحق إلا بحجة كالخارج، والمدعى عليه من يكون مستحقا بقوله من غير حجة كذي اليد
Hidāya lists other definitions (claimant is whoever relies on the non-apparent, defendant on the apparent) and says Muḥammad: defendant = the denier. Recognition is by fiqh (meaning), not by form.tap to reveal
How is an absent object described in a claim?
لأن العين لا تعرف بالوصف، والقيمة تعرف به
If the object cannot be brought, its value is stated; Abū al-Layth adds stating male/female for a slave.tap to reveal
Land claim: how many boundaries are needed?
فإن ذكر ثلاثة من الحدود يكتفى بها عندنا خلافا لزفر
Stating three boundaries suffices (majority exists); Zufar requires all four. Error in the fourth would change the object and spoils it; omission does not. Hand of the defendant on land must be proved by bayyina or judge's knowledge, not by mere admission, to avoid collusion.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Iqrār (Admission)

Qudūrī gives the rulings of admission; Hidāya adds the reasoning: iqrār is a limited proof; the key analytical tool of connected vs separated clarification (bayān mughayyir) decides most disputes between AH and the Ṣāḥibayn; and illness admissions are ranked below debts of health. Hidāya also adds lineage conditions and the difference between 'I took' and 'you gave' for deposit.
What is iqrār's evidentiary basis and limit?
وهو حجة قاصرة لقصور ولاية المقر عن غيره فيقتصر عليه
It is a report of an existing right and binds by its own indication: the Prophet imposed stoning on Māʿiz and on the woman by their confession. It binds only the speaker. A discerning mahjūr slave's iqrār is not valid for money (it affects his master's property) but is valid for ḥudūd and qiṣāṣ.tap to reveal
Does Abū Ḥanīfa have another figure for 'great wealth'?
وعن أبي حنيفة أنه لا يصدق في أقل من عشرة دراهم وهي نصاب السرقة
A narration from AH: ten dirhams, the theft nisab, since the hand is cut for it; another matches Qudūrī's 200. For dinars twenty; camels 25 (the lowest zakāt nisab of their kind).tap to reveal
Why 10 (AH) vs 200 (Ṣāḥibayn) for 'many dirhams'?
وله أن العشرة أقصى ما ينتهي إليه اسم الجمع
AH: ten is the farthest limit of the plain plural (ten dirhams, then eleven dirhams), so it is the maximum by wording. Ṣāḥibayn: whoever owns a nisab is rich and bound to help others, hence 'many'.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Ṣulḥ (Settlement)

Hidāya supplies Qudūrī's ṣulḥ with the textual proofs (Qurʾān, ḥadīth ‘kullu ṣulḥin jāʾiz’), the answer to Shāfiʿī on settlement under denial, and the master-rule that ṣulḥ is read as the nearest contract (sale, lease, release). Then follow the concrete cases (istiḥqāq, homicide, agents, debts, shared debts, takhārūj).
Proof that settlement on denial is lawful.
كل صلح جائز فيما بين المسلمين إلا صلحا أحل حراما أو حرم حلالا
Qurʾān (‘wa-l-ṣulḥu khayr’) and the ḥadīth. The exception means forbidding what is lawful in itself (as swearing never to touch the other wife) or permitting a haram (wine), not money paid to end a claim.tap to reveal
Is settlement valid with denial or silence?
وقال الشافعي: لا يجوز مع إنكار أو سكوت لما روينا
Shāfiʿī: invalid; the payment was lawful for the payer and haram for the taker, and it is a bribe. Ḥanafī: valid.tap to reveal
Why does ṣulḥ take the rules of sale or lease?
والأصل فيه أن الصلح يجب حمله على أقرب العقود إليه وأشبهها به
Principle: ṣulḥ is carried to the nearest contract to validate the act. So money for money = sale (shufʿa, option, defect); money for usufruct = ijāra (needs a term; void on death).tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Muḍāraba (Profit-sharing Partnership)

Hidāya grounds Qudūrī's muḍāraba in need and in practice, then its distinctive framework: the worker is at once trustee, agent, partner on profit, lessee when invalid and usurper if he breaches. The disputes (sub-muḍāraba, dismissal, tasks) all derive from this framework.
Why is muḍāraba lawful although it resembles an uncertain wage?
وبعث النبي ﷺ والناس يباشرونه فقررهم عليه وتعاملت به الصحابة
Need: people are either rich but ineffective, or skilled but empty-handed. The Prophet was sent and found people practicing it and confirmed them; the Companions dealt in it.tap to reveal
What is the legal character of the muḍārib?
وهو وكيل فيه لأنه يتصرف فيه بأمر مالكه، وإذا ربح فهو شريك فيه
Amāna (taken by owner's command, not as pledge), wakāla, partnership upon profit, ijāra when invalid (ujrat al-mithl), ghaṣb on breach.tap to reveal
What if all profit is stipulated for one party?
ألا ترى أن الربح لو شرط كله لرب المال كان بضاعة، ولو شرط جميعه للمضارب كان قرضا
Without sharing in the profit there is no muḍāraba: all to owner = bidāʿa; all to worker = qarḍ.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Wadīʿa (Deposit)

Hidāya adds the ḥadīth of the non-ghāll, the rationale behind the hands of dependants, and the disputes about mixing (AH vs Ṣāḥibayn), return after breach (vs Shāfiʿī), travel, co-depositors and the second depositee. The principle: a condition is valid only if it is useful.
What is the basis of no liability?
ليس على المستعير غير المغل ضمان ولا على المستودع غير المغل ضمان
Ḥadīth, and need: if liability were imposed people would refuse deposits.tap to reveal
Why his dependants but not others?
لأن المالك رضي بيده لا بيد غيره، والأيدي تختلف في الأمانة
Owner consented to his hand not another's; hands differ in trust; a thing does not contain its like (as an agent cannot appoint another). He cannot always stay home, so dependants are tolerated. Placing it in another's place is a deposit unless he rents the place. Fire/drowning: proof needed from him.tap to reveal
Does withholding after demand matter?
لأنه لما طالبه لم يكن راضيا بإمساكه بعده فيضمنه بحبسه عنه
He is liable if he can return it and refuses: his possession after demand is transgression.tap to reveal

He is liable (AH: liability only); Ṣāḥibayn give Idris the choice of partnership in the mix or compensation.discuss first — then tap

Kitāb al-ʿĀriya (Loan for Use)

Hidāya's contribution is the nature of ʿāriya (tamlīk against Karkhī's ibāḥa), which explains why it is recallable, why the borrower is a trustee (against Shāfiʿī), why he may not rent it out and why returning to the owner's stable is accepted by custom.
Is ʿāriya ownership of use or mere permission?
والمنافع قابلة للملك كالأعيان
Karkhī: ibāḥa (made by the word ‘permission’, needs no term, can be forbidden, no sub-let). Hidāya: tamlīk (ʿāriya is from ʿariyya, gift; formed by ownership words); gratuitous transfer is as valid for use as for things. The word ibāḥa is borrowed for tamlīk as in ijāra. Vagueness is harmless as it is non-binding. No re-letting, to avoid more harm.tap to reveal
Recall at will.
المنحة مردودة والعارية مؤداة
Ḥadīth; also usufruct is owned piece by piece, so what has not come into existence has not been delivered and can be recalled.tap to reveal
Is the borrower liable if it perishes without fault?
ولنا أن اللفظ لا ينبئ عن التزام الضمان
Ḥanafī: no (words of gratuitous use do not imply liability; possession was permitted, not transgression; return cost is like upkeep). Shāfiʿī: liable, as he took another's property for himself without entitlement, like goods taken on approval (sawm); Hidāya: sawm is liable by contract.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Hiba (Gift)

Hidāya supplies the evidence (ḥadīth on qabḍ and retraction) and the disputes: Mālik on ownership without qabḍ, Shāfiʿī on undivided shares and retraction, Zufar and Shāfiʿī on conditional gifts. Principle: gifts survive void conditions but sales do not.
Textual proof of hiba and the need for qabḍ.
لا تجوز الهبة إلا مقبوضة
‘Exchange gifts and you will love one another’ and consensus. Qabḍ is needed for ownership (the ḥadīth negates ownership, as validity without is fixed): Mālik gives ownership before qabḍ (like sale). Ḥanafī: a donor cannot be forced into delivery he did not undertake; wasiyya differs since ownership comes after death.tap to reveal
Taking possession without the donor's leave.
والقياس أن لا يجوز في الوجهين وهو قول الشافعي
In the session: valid by istiḥsān (the offer is a licence like acceptance). After parting: needs the donor's leave. Qiyās/Shāfiʿī: not valid either way, as qabḍ is a disposal of the donor's property. If the donor forbids taking in the session, the express forbidding overrides.tap to reveal
Further wordings.
فيحمل المحتمل على المحكم
‘I clothed you with this’ is a gift; ‘I gave you this woman for use’ is ʿāriya. ‘My house is for you as a gift of living’ (sukna) is ʿāriya (clear for use over the doubtful). But ‘a gift to live in’ (tuskinuhā) is a gift, as it is advice not definition.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Ijārāt I — Formation, wage and invalid contracts

Hidāya first justifies ijāra against qiyās by need and ḥadīth, then defines when the wage is due (against Shāfiʿī), sets out the craftsman's rules (baker, brickmaker, retention), the effect of deviating from stipulation, and the long list of invalid forms (ujrat al-mithl, qafīz al-ṭaḥḥān, wage for worship).
Why is ijāra allowed if the subject-matter is non-existent?
أعطوا الأجير أجره قبل أن يجف عرقه
Qiyās refuses it (usufruct is non-existent), but need and ḥadīths (‘pay the hired man before his sweat dries’; ‘whoever hires a worker, let him know his wage’) allow it. It is concluded moment by moment as usufruct arises; the house stands in for the benefit to connect offer and acceptance.tap to reveal
When is the wage owned?
ولنا أن العقد ينعقد شيئا فشيئا على حسب حدوث المنافع
Ḥanafī: not by the contract: due by stipulation of advance, advance payment or use. Shāfiʿī: by the contract itself (non-existent usufruct is deemed existing for the contract's validity). Handing over a house makes rent due even if not lived in; if usurped, rent lapses (proportionally if partial).tap to reveal
Baker's burnt bread.
وهذا عند أبي حنيفة؛ لأنه أمانة في يده

Kitāb al-Ijārāt II — Worker's liability, options, rescission

Second part of Hidāya's ijāra: the liability of the shared worker (AH vs Ṣāḥibayn), damage by his act (against Zufar and Shāfiʿī), alternative wages, rescission by defect, death and excuse, and disputes.
Is the shared worker liable for goods that perish?
ولأبي حنيفة ﵀ أن العين أمانة في يده؛ لأن القبض حصل بإذنه

Ṣāḥibayn: liable except for overwhelming events (great fire, open robbery), based on ʿUmar and ʿAlī who held him liable; safekeeping is owed to him since he cannot work without it. Damage by his work (tearing cloth, porter slipping).

Liable. Zufar and Shāfiʿī: not (the command was general and includes both safe and damaging work). Ḥanafī: he is liable for what is of his doing (rope snapping from carelessness). Kitāb al-Mukātab (Contract of Manumission)

Hidāya grounds kitāba in Qurʾān 24:33 (read as recommendation), the ḥadīths on payment of the whole, then the disputes on invalid terms, kin and umm walad, shared slaves, inability (AY's two instalments) and death.
Is the Qurʾānic order to contract ‘if you know good in them’ obligatory?
وهذا ليس أمر إيجاب بإجماع بين الفقهاء، وإنما هو أمر ندب
Command of recommendation (not mere permission, as that would nullify the condition ‘if you know good’). ‘Good’ means he will not harm Muslims after freedom; if he will, better not to contract though valid. The slave's acceptance is required (he takes on a debt). He is free only on paying the whole: ‘a mukātab is a slave while a dirham remains’ (Zayd b. Thābit's view among differing Companions). No remission of any part is required.tap to reveal
Immediate payment as kitāba price.
لأنه عاجز عن التسليم في زمان قليل لعدم الأهلية قبله للرق
Ḥanafī: immediate or deferred or instalments are allowed. Shāfiʿī: not immediate; needs two instalments, as a slave has no capacity to own before. Hidāya's reply: the contract itself indicates the capability (unlike his analogy with salam).tap to reveal
Out of his master's hand but not his ownership.
لأنه عقد معاوضة ومبناه على المساواة، وينعدم ذلك بتنجز العتق
Out of hand to fulfil the purpose (he may buy, sell, travel even if forbidden) but ownership remains, since the contract is an exchange and equality would be lost if freedom took effect at once. If the master frees him, he is freed and the price lapses. If the master has intercourse with his mukātaba, ʿuqr is due since she is now exclusive to her body.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Walāʾ (Patronage)

Hidāya sets walāʾ on the idea of mutual support (tanāṣur) in two forms (manumission and contract), grounds it in ḥadīth and Qurʾān 4:33 (against Shāfiʿī on muwālāt), and explains why walāʾ follows the father's side and passes to the nearest ʿaṣaba.
Why walāʾ goes to the one who frees.
الولاء لمن أعتق
Support, he bears the blood-money and brought him to life in meaning by removing slavery, so he inherits and walāʾ is like birth; also ‘gain goes with liability’. Proof: ‘al-walāʾ liman aʿtaq’ and the Prophet divided the freedman of Ḥamza's daughter between her and her daughter. Equal for manumission for money or without. A condition that he be sāʾiba is void.tap to reveal
Child born to freed woman and slave husband.
الولاء لحمة كلحمة النسب لا يباع ولا يوهب ولا يورث
If born within six months of her freedom: patron of the mother (child was freed with her, aim). Later: freed with her by following; if the father is later freed he draws walāʾ to his patrons (walāʾ goes with the father as lineage; mother's side was by necessity), like a child of liʿān. Not so when the freed woman is in ʿidda after death/divorce, as conception predates.tap to reveal
Non-Arab (Nabaṭī) husband with a freed woman: whose patron?
ولهما أن ولاء الموالاة أضعف حتى يقبل الفسخ
Children's patrons are the mother's patrons (AH & Muḥammad): muwālāt walāʾ is weaker (can be rescinded) while ʿatāqa walāʾ cannot be, and the weak yields to the strong. AY: the father's patrons (the father's side is the stronger side). If both are freed: father's side.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Ikrāh (Coercion)

Hidāya defines coercion (action that removes consent), notes that Abū Ḥanīfa's ‘sultan only’ is a difference of age, applies Q 4:29 to contracts, distinguishes permitted acts (carrion, property) from those never permitted (killing), uses the ʿAmmār and Khubayb accounts for kufr, and gives the disputes on qiṣāṣ.
Who may coerce?
فقد قالوا هذا اختلاف عصر وزمان لا اختلاف حجة وبرهان
Anyone able to carry out the threat, sultan or robber. Abū Ḥanīfa said only the sultan, since power needs strength and only the sultan had it then; later scholars: a difference of age, not of proof. The victim must also strongly expect it will be done.tap to reveal
Contracts under coercion.
إِلاَّ أَنْ تَكُونَ تِجَارَةً عَنْ تَرَاضٍ مِنْكُمْ
He may confirm or rescind (the contract lacks consent, Q 4:29). A light threat (one lash, a day's detention) is not ikrāh unless the man is of rank. Voluntary receipt of the price or voluntary handing over ratifies; receipt under coercion does not. For a gift, delivery is part of the coercion. Property lost with the buyer: buyer pays value; the coerced may sue the coercer or the buyer.tap to reveal
Forced to eat carrion or drink wine.
لأن تناول هذه المحرمات إنما يباح عند الضرورة كما في المخمصة
Only a threat to life or limb allows it (beating or detention does not); then it is an obligation to accept and he is sinful if he refuses and is killed (helping against himself). Abū Yūsuf: not sinful, taking the ʿazīma (a licence). Hidāya: the state of need is excepted by the text itself so it is permission, not concession; he sins only if he knows the permission.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Ḥajr (Interdiction)

Hidāya reasons through interdiction: why acts of minors and slaves are suspended, why ḥajr touches words not deeds, and the great disputes: AH against interdicting the spendthrift and the bankrupt (human dignity, imprisonment instead) versus the Ṣāḥibayn and Shāfiʿī; puberty ages; shadowing the debtor; unpaid seller (against Shāfiʿī).
Why is a discerning minor's or slave's sale suspended?
فإن قيل: التوقف عندكم في البيع. أما الشراء فالأصل فيه النفاذ على المباشر
Offer made by a discerning person is valid and awaits the guardian's or master's approval (maṣlaḥa). Slavery guards the master's right; minority is lack of reason (the guardian's leave is the sign of capacity); madness leaves no capacity. Purchase also waits as it does not take effect on him when capacity is lacking.tap to reveal
Why ḥajr affects words not acts.
لأنه لا مرد لها لوجودها حسا ومشاهدة
Acts exist physically and cannot be undone; words depend on intent as the law requires. Exception: acts whose rule is dropped by doubt (ḥudūd, qiṣāṣ), where no intent is a doubt.tap to reveal
Slave's admissions and divorce.
لا يملك العبد والمكاتب شيئا إلا الطلاق
Binding on him not on his master: money after freedom; ḥadd or qiṣāṣ at once (he stays free in blood). Divorce takes effect (ḥadīth; he knows his interest; no loss to the master).tap to reveal

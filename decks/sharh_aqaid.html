Muqaddima: the science of kalām

Taftāzānī opens by defending kalām (ʿilm uṣūl al-dīn) as the noblest of the religious sciences: its subject is the Islamic creed, its goal is success in this world and the next, and its proofs are certain demonstrations backed mostly by revealed texts. The Ṭaḥāwiyya simply states the creed on the authority of the Imams; the Sharḥ adds the discipline's own self-definition, its justification, and its answer to the early critics of kalām, then moves straight to epistemology. Read this as the 'why we argue' preface that explains every later layer (dalīl ʿaqlī, khilāf, jawāb).
On what grounds is kalām the noblest of the religious sciences?
ورئيس العلوم الدينية وكون معلوماته العقائد الإسلامية وغايته الفوز بالسعادات الدينية والدنيوية وبراهينه الحجج القطعية المؤيد أكثرها بالأدلة السمعية
On three grounds: its subject (the Islamic creed), its aim (felicity in this world and the next) and its proofs (certain demonstrations, mostly supported by revelation).tap to reveal
What of the reports that some Salaf censured and forbade kalām?
The censure targets the partisan who uses it to promote his own view, the sceptic who wavers in the religion, and meddling in obscure questions the creed does not need; it cannot mean forbidding the very foundation of the obligations.tap to reveal

Establishing realities and refuting the Sophists

The Sharḥ begins Nasafī's text with 'Ahl al-ḥaqq say: the realities of things are established and knowledge of them is real'. This is a prolegomenon: before proving God one must show that the world and knowledge exist. Taftāzānī distinguishes three Sophist groups (denying realities, making them follow belief, and the doubters), gives a direct proof from certainty of sense and necessary knowledge and an ilzām (reductio), answers the objection that necessities are mistaken, and ends with the notorious advice to 'torture' the obstinate until they admit pain. The Ṭaḥāwiyya has nothing on this; it is a wholly new, philosophical layer.
What does Nasafī assert at the start of the creed?
حقائق الأشياء ثابتة
That the realities of things are established (not illusions) and that knowledge of them is attainable, in opposition to the Sophists.tap to reveal
Is knowledge of these realities real too?
والعلم بها متحقق
Yes: 'and knowledge of them is real' (wa-l-ʿilmu bihā mutaḥaqqiq), at least in some matters, whether in concepts or judgements.tap to reveal
Who are the three kinds of Sophist?
(1) ʿInādiyya deny the realities and call them illusion; (2) ʿIndiyya say realities follow our belief (if we believe a thing a substance, it is one); (3) Lā-adriyya doubt everything, even their doubt.tap to reveal

Causes of knowledge: senses, true report and reason

Nasafī enumerates three causes of knowledge for creatures: sound senses, true report (khabar ṣādiq) and reason; Taftāzānī fills each out. The five outer senses give knowledge by God's creating it at their use; true report is either mutawātir (necessary knowledge) or the report of a Messenger supported by a miracle (inferential knowledge); reason is the faculty that gives both necessary and inferential knowledge. Inspiration (ilhām) is not a cause of knowledge for others. The Ṭaḥāwiyya (accepting the revealed text) has no theory of knowledge, so everything here is new; the section also records the objections (more causes, dawr, Sumaniyya and Brahmins) and the replies.
How many causes of knowledge does Nasafī give?
الحواس السليمة والخبر الصادق والعقل
Three: the sound senses, true report and reason.tap to reveal
What are the five senses and how do they give knowledge?
Hearing (sounds), sight (lights, colours, shapes, movement, beauty), smell, taste, touch (heat, cold, moisture, dryness); each yields certain knowledge of its own objects, God creating the perception when the faculty is used.tap to reveal
Can one sense perceive another's object?
Each sense has its own object; sweetness is tasted, heat touched; it is rationally possible for another sense to perceive it, but habit shows it does not.tap to reveal

Origination of the world: substances, accidents and the indivisible atom

Nasafī states that the world, with all its parts, is originated. Taftāzānī gives the standard kalām proof: the world is substances and accidents, accidents are originated by observation or by the argument from the incompatibility of eternity with change, and substances are never free of originated accidents (motion and rest), hence they too are originated. Along the way he defines jawhar, jism, and the contentious jawhar fard (the indivisible part), reports the proofs of the mutakallimūn and the philosophers' hylomorphism, and works through six objections. The Ṭaḥāwiyya never argues for creation from nothing; this is the biggest 'new' block in the Sharḥ and the foundation of the later proof of the Creator.
What does Nasafī claim about the world?
والعالم بجميع أجزائه محدث
That the world in all its parts, the heavens and what is in them, the earth and what is on it, is originated: brought from non-existence to existence.tap to reveal
Why is the world divided into substances and accidents?
إذ هو أعيان وأعراض وكل منهما حادث
Because whatever subsists by itself is a substance and whatever does not is an accident.tap to reveal
What does 'subsisting by itself' mean?
فالأعيان ما له قيام بذاته
For the mutakallimūn: occupying a space independently, not as a follower of another's occupancy; for the philosophers: independence from any substrate.tap to reveal

The Necessary Maker and the refutation of infinite regress

From the originated world the Sharḥ moves to its originator: the world's origination needs a muḥdith, since preferring one side of the possible without a preferrer is impossible; and that muḥdith must be wājib al-wujūd, not itself contingent. Two routes (dalīl al-ḥudūth and dalīl al-imkān) converge. Taftāzānī then refutes infinite regress (tasalsul) by the argument from the external cause and the famous taṭbīq argument, and defends it against the 'infinite numbers/God's knowables' counter-example. The Ṭaḥāwiyya simply affirms that Allah is One (s01) without arguing; this is a new kalām block.
Why must the originated world have a Maker?
والمحدث للعالم هو الله تعالى
Because the world's existence is one of two equal possibilities; for one to come into being without an external determinant is impossible, so it must have a muḥdith.tap to reveal
Why must the Maker be necessary-by-itself, not contingent?
If contingent, He would be one of the contingents that need a maker and so could not be the maker of all contingents; hence He is wājib al-wujūd.tap to reveal
Is the proof from imkān really different from the proof from ḥudūth?
In result they are one; the Sharḥ remarks that it is a move from one proof to the other and notes that some ḥāshiya authors attribute the second to the author of al-Mawāqif.tap to reveal

Oneness: the proof of mutual hindrance (tamānuʿ)

The Sharḥ argues that the Maker is One. It reads Q 21:22 ('had there been in them gods other than Allah they would have been ruined') as pointing to the tamānuʿ argument: if two gods were possible, they could will contrary things; either both come to be (contradictories joined) or one fails (and is thus contingent). Taftāzānī, however, regards the verse as an iqnāʿī (persuasive) not demonstrative proof, because its logical premise is not strictly necessary; the Māturīdī Abū al-Muʿīn al-Nasafī (cited in the ḥawāshī) held it to be demonstrative. The section also explains the meaning of 'law' in the verse and its time reference.
What is the argument of tamānuʿ?
لو كان فيهما آلهة إلا الله لفسدتا
If two gods were possible, they could differ: one wants motion, the other rest of the same body; each of the wills is possible and so is each object; both being done joins opposites, and one failing shows helplessness, a sign of origination.tap to reveal
Is Q 21:22 a demonstrative proof (burhān) of tawḥīd?
Taftāzānī: it is a persuasive proof (ḥujja iqnāʿiyya) because the entailment is by custom and 'fasād' is not necessarily entailed by mere plurality; others, including Abū al-Muʿīn al-Nasafī, take it as demonstrative since it is addressed to unbelievers by rational argument.tap to reveal
Objection: the entailment is certain, since 'fasād' means non-existence of the world; if neither is the maker, nothing is made.
Taftāzānī: not necessary: the absence of plurality does not entail the absence of the thing made; either one could make it without clash; and the claim of impossibility of difference lacks proof.tap to reveal

Eternity, tanzīh and what is impossible for God

The Sharḥ turns from proving the Maker to describing Him. The Maker is eternal (qadīm, with no beginning); the dispute is whether the attributes are 'wājib li-dhātihā' or only possible-by-themselves-yet-eternal (Taftāzānī's route). Then the nafy of anything that suggests createdness: not a substance, accident, body, form, limit, number, divisible, or composite; not in place or direction; no time passes on Him. Anthropomorphic texts are either left in tafwīḍ (the Salaf) or interpreted (the Khalaf). The Ṭaḥāwiyya affirms the same tanzīh on the Salaf's authority; the Sharḥ now supplies the proofs and the names of opponents (Mujassima, Karrāmiyya).
Why must the Maker be eternal?
والمحدث للعالم هو الله تعالى الواحد القديم الحي القادر العليم السميع البصير الشائي المريد
Because if He had a beginning He would be originated and need a maker, so His existence would be from another, contrary to necessity-by-Himself.tap to reveal
Are wājib and qadīm synonyms, and are the attributes necessary-by-themselves?
Taftāzānī: no, the two are different notions; the attributes are eternal (no beginning in time) but possible by their own nature and dependent on the Essence; some later scholars (e.g. Ḥamīd al-Dīn al-Ḍarīr and followers) say every eternal is necessary-by-itself and that the attributes are necessary-by-themselves.tap to reveal
What does Nasafī deny of God, and why?
ولا مصور ولا محدود ولا معدود ولا متبعض ولا متجزئ ولا متركب
He is not a body, substance or accident, nor formed, limited, numbered, divisible or composite; each would imply composition and so contingency and need for a composer.tap to reveal

The positive attributes: neither identical with the Essence nor other

The Sharḥ's main work on the attributes: God has attributes subsisting in His Essence and eternal; denying them (Muʿtazila: He knows without knowledge; falāsifa: attributes are the Essence regarded with relations) is refuted by the principle that a derived name proves the source of derivation, and by the proof from the perfect order of His acts. The famous formula 'neither He nor other than He' is defended against the charge of joining contradictories and of multiplying eternals by the criterion that plurality arises only from separability (infikāk), contrasted with the Christians' three aqānīm. The Ṭaḥāwiyya affirms the attributes as eternal (s04 azaliyyat al-ṣifāt); the Sharḥ adds the arguments and the opponents.
How is the existence of the divine attributes proved?
وله صفات
(1) A derived noun is true of something only if the source of derivation is present in it; 'knower', 'powerful', 'living' therefore entail knowledge, power, life. (2) The texts affirm His knowledge and power. (3) The perfection of His acts shows existing knowledge and power, not merely a name.tap to reveal
What is the Muʿtazilī reason for denying eternal attributes, and the falāsifa's version?
Muʿtazila: if God had eternal attributes such as knowledge, power and life they would be accidents (they cannot subsist by themselves), and an accident cannot subsist in God nor exist in eternity, so He has none. Falāsifa: His attributes are His Essence regarded with relation to objects of knowledge, power etc.; thus no multiplicity.tap to reveal
Why do the attributes have to be eternal and subsist in His Essence?
قائمة بذاته
Eternal: originated things cannot subsist in Him, as the Karrāmiyya wrongly claim. Subsisting in Him: an attribute of a thing is nothing but what subsists in it; hence the Muʿtazilī claim that God speaks by a kalām existing in another is only a denial that kalām is His attribute.tap to reveal

Kalām and the uncreated Qurʾān

This is the section where the Sharḥ's treatment of the Ṭaḥāwiyya's 'the Qurʾān is the speech of God, not created' (s08) becomes technical. Taftāzānī argues for an eternal kalām nafsī (a meaning subsisting in the Essence), expressed by the created naẓm; the dispute with the Muʿtazila (kalām = created letters and sounds) is thereby reduced to affirmation vs denial of kalām nafsī, since Ahl al-sunna do not claim the letters are eternal. The four existences (ʿaynī, dhihnī, lafẓī, kitābī) explain how the Qurʾān can be 'written, memorised, recited and heard' yet not created. Includes the question whether Mūsā heard the eternal speech itself.
What is God's kalām and how is it proved?
صفة أزلية عبر عنها بالنظم المسمى بالقرآن المتركب من الحروف
It is an eternal attribute, a meaning subsisting in His Essence, expressed by the naẓm called the Qurʾān; proved by the consensus and tawātur that He is speaking, which means He is described by speech, and an originated utterance cannot subsist in Him.tap to reveal
Why are letters and sounds not eternal?
They are accidents, and some are conditional on the passing of others (one cannot say the second letter without the first having passed), so they are originated; this refutes the Ḥanābila and the Karrāmiyya.tap to reveal
Is silence an objection, since God must be free of silence and dumbness?
He is described by a speech attribute; the 'silence' and 'dumbness' that oppose speech are the inner ones (not willing to speak or being unable); as speech is lafẓī and nafsī, so is its opposite.tap to reveal

Takwīn and irāda: the Māturīdī/Ashʿarī dispute

Takwīn (the act of bringing into being) is the most distinctive Māturīdī attribute: eternal, other than the object created (mukawwan), and the root of takhlīq, tarzīq, taṣwīr, iḥyāʾ and imāta. The Ashʿarīs say takwīn is the object itself (a relation of qudra to the object), so the names Khāliq etc. are eternal only in the sense of the capacity. The Sharḥ gives the Māturīdī proofs, remarks that they function as 'reminders' (tanbīhāt) rather than true proofs, reads the Ashʿarī statement charitably as saying takwīn is an iʿtibārī relation rather than a thing, and in the end (per the editor, from his other works) prefers the Ashʿarī view. This section, plus irāda, is where the Ṭaḥāwiyya's 'He was Creator before creating' (s04) gets its theology.
Is takwīn a real eternal attribute?
والتكوين صفة لله تعالى
The Māturīdīs: yes, an eternal attribute, other than power and will; creating, shaping, providing, giving life and death all refer to it. The Ashʿarīs: no, it is the relation of power to the object (an idāfī, iʿtibārī matter).tap to reveal
What are the Māturīdī arguments for the otherness of takwīn from the mukawwan?
التكوين عين المكون
The Māturīdīs argue from evident absurdities: if takwīn were identical with the mukawwan one could say 'the creator of this stone's blackness is black' and 'this stone is the creator of blackness', since 'creator' and 'black' would mean only the one in whom that quality subsists; the Sharḥ treats these as pointers to what is self-evident rather than as proofs.tap to reveal
How does Taftāzānī read the Ashʿarī 'takwīn = mukawwan'?
He asks for a charitable reading: they mean that when an agent acts there is only agent and act; what is called takwīn or ījād is a mental (iʿtibārī) relation between the agent and the object, not a reality distinct from the object; they do not claim the concepts are identical.tap to reveal

The vision of God

The Sharḥ's treatment of ruʾya is among the longest in the book. Taftāzānī proves that the vision of God is rationally possible (jāʾiza bi-l-ʿaql) and, because of reports, necessary (wājiba bi-l-naql) for the believers in the Hereafter, without place, direction or contact with rays. The rational proof turns on a shared cause of visibility (existence), which he refines when the premise that the ʿilla is existence is challenged; the textual proof turns on Mūsā's request and the suspension on the mountain's staying put. Then he answers the Muʿtazilī qiyās al-ghāʾib ʿalā al-shāhid and their scriptural arguments, and allows the vision in dreams. The Ṭaḥāwiyya (s09) affirms the vision briefly with 'without comprehension or how'.
Is the vision of God rationally possible?
جائزة في العقل
Yes: reason by itself affirms possibility unless a proof of impossibility is brought; the default is possibility and the burden is on the denier.tap to reveal
What is the main rational proof?
We see substances and accidents and distinguish them by sight; a shared judgement needs a shared cause; this can only be existence (not origination or contingency, which are shared by what is not seen, and the Maker); thus whatever exists can be seen, including God.tap to reveal
Objection: soundness of vision is only possibility, which is nothing; the shared cause is not necessarily existence; and a single effect may have different causes.
Taftāzānī offers: a species-level effect can have varied causes (like heat from sun and fire) so a shared cause is not demanded; he also admits the objection's force: the cause may be corporeality and its accidents. The 'knowledge of reality' analogue is therefore not a decisive proof, and the textual proof carries the weight.tap to reveal

The creation of the servants' acts

The Ṭaḥāwiyya affirms that all acts are by God's decree and that the servants have acts of choice (s05, s11, s18). The Sharḥ gives the kalām structure: God alone is Creator of the servants' acts, whether unbelief or obedience, against the Muʿtazila who made the servant the creator of his acts (the early ones avoided the word 'khāliq', later Abū ʿAbd Allāh al-Baṣrī, nicknamed Juʿal, took it as literal). The Muʿtazilī arguments (walker versus tremor; takleef and reward would be void) are answered by affirming the servant's kasb. Kasb, goodness and evil, and approval

Kasb is the heart of Sunni mediation between Jabr and Muʿtazila. The Sharḥ defines it, differentiates it from khalq, shows it is not the Muʿtazilī 'sharika', and says why acquiring evil is evil though creating it is not: God creates only with wise ends, while the servant who acquires evil despite the prohibition is blameworthy. The matn then states 'the good acts are by God's approval and the evil are not'. Taftāzānī gives his own definition of kasb from Tahdhīb: an iḍāfī matter that is obliged from the servant and does not bring about the object. The Ṭaḥāwiyya has 'takleef and capacity' but no analysis of kasb.
What is kasb and how does it differ from khalq?
أمر إضافي يجب من العبد ولا يوجب وجود المقدور بل اتصاف الفاعل به وذلك كتعيين أحد الطرفين وترجيحه وصرف القدرة
Kasb is the servant's turning of his power and will to the act; khalq is God's bringing the act into existence after that. The difference (per Abū al-Muʿīn's Tamhīd): kasb occurs with the power as instrument whereas creating needs none; the act of kasb falls within the servant's locus of power, creating does not; and the agent cannot be alone in kasb.tap to reveal
If God creates and the servant acquires, have you not conceded the partnership you charged the Muʿtazila with?
No: partnership is that two share a thing while each alone has his own part, like partners in a village or if the servant were creator and God creator of other things; but attributing one thing to two in two different respects is not partnership, like land that is property of one and possession of another.tap to reveal
Why is acquiring evil evil (and punishable) but creating it is not?
Because it is established that the Creator is wise and creates nothing without a praiseworthy outcome even if we do not know it, as He creates harmful, polluting bodies with benefits; the servant, however, may do good or evil, so making his acquisition of evil despite the prohibition evil and foolish deserves blame and punishment.tap to reveal

Capacity, obligation and generation (tawallud)

Having fixed kasb, the Sharḥ turns to istiṭāʿa (qudra), which the Ḥanafī-Māturīdī tradition states is 'with the act', against the Muʿtazila (before it). It distinguishes two senses: the true power by which the act occurs (an accident God creates with the act) and the soundness of the means, instruments and limbs that precedes it and grounds takleef. The Sharḥ then asserts that God does not charge what is beyond capacity (the dispute being only about permissibility), and refutes tawallud (the Muʿtazilī idea that an act can generate another act in a different locus). The Ṭaḥāwiyya (s18) has 'no one is charged beyond capacity' and 'capacity is two kinds'; this section gives the arguments.
When is the power by which an act is done?
والاستطاعة مع الفعل
With the act, not before it; if before, the act would occur without power since accidents do not persist two instants.tap to reveal
What is the true capacity?
وهي حقيقة القدرة التي يكون بها الفعل
A created attribute that God creates when the servant intends to acquire the act after the means are sound; if he intends good God creates the power of good and if he intends evil the power of evil; he is blamed because he did not direct it to the good.tap to reveal
What is the second meaning of istiṭāʿa, and which verse uses it?
سلامة الأسباب والآلات والجوارح
Soundness of means, instruments and limbs; as in 'pilgrimage to the House for whoever is able to find a way' (3:97) and in the proofs for takleef.tap to reveal

The aṣlaḥ: is God obliged to do what is best for His servant?

The matn: 'what is best for the servant is not obligatory upon God'. The Muʿtazila (Baṣrīs only in religion, Baghdādīs also in worldly matters) held that God must do what is aṣlaḥ, since otherwise He would be miserly and foolish. Taftāzānī replies: no one can make anything obligatory upon God; if it were, He would not have created the poor, tormented unbeliever, no gratitude would be due for guidance, and He would owe nothing special to the Prophet over Abū Jahl; and the Muʿtazilī principle leads to the philosophers' view that God acts by necessity. A ḥāshiya observes that the Māturīdīs affirm that riʿāyat al-aṣlaḥ by wisdom is 'wājib min Allāh' (as flowing from Him) but not 'wājib ʿalayhi'. Is doing what is best for the servant obligatory upon God?
وما هو الأصلح للعبد فليس ذلك بواجب على الله تعالى
No: 'what is best for the servant is not obligatory upon God'.tap to reveal
The Muʿtazila: if He did not do the best it would be miserly and foolish.
Miserliness is withholding what is the right of the one asking; the right of the one asking is not established, and His wisdom and knowledge of consequences are established by decisive proofs; so to omit is neither miserly nor foolish.tap to reveal
What can 'obligatory upon God' mean?
Neither that its omitter deserves blame or punishment (God is above that), nor that it must issue from Him without the ability to omit (that entails the absurd and makes Him a necessitating cause like the philosophers); so the meaning is empty.tap to reveal

The definition of īmān: taṣdīq and iqrār

The Ṭaḥāwiyya says īmān is 'iqrār with the tongue and taṣdīq with the heart' (s13). Taftāzānī defines it with Nasafī as 'taṣdīq of what the Prophet brought from God', explains taṣdīq linguistically and logically (idhʿān and qubūl, an act in the mind and not just ma'rifa), shows the logical and linguistic notions coincide (Avicenna's division of knowledge into taṣawwur and taṣdīq), and distinguishes the sharʿī taṣdīq as narrower in its object (what is known necessarily to be from God, in general). Increase and decrease, īmān and islām, and istithnāʾ

Nasafī: actions increase in themselves, but īmān neither increases nor decreases. The Sharḥ argues (1) deeds are not in īmān's essence (ʿaṭf in the Qurʾān; īmān is a condition for deeds' validity), (2) the reality of īmān is taṣdīq that has reached firm certainty, which has no degrees; texts of increase refer to what is believed, to details, to constancy or to the radiance of īmān. It records Shāfiʿī's view (deeds are part of the perfect īmān) and the verifiers who allow degrees in taṣdīq. It then refutes the Qadariyya's claim that īmān is maʿrifa; identifies īmān and islām (with replies to Q 49:14 and the ḥadīth of Jibrīl); permits 'I am truly a believer'; discusses 'I am a believer if God wills'; and the shaqī/saʿīd issue. What does Nasafī say about increase of deeds and īmān?

Because it is the firm acceptance (jazm and idhʿān) that has reached its limit; what has reached the limit has no more or less.tap to reveal

Prophethood: sending of messengers, the muʿjiza, and the proof of Muḥammad's prophethood

The imāma: its obligation and conditions

The Ṭaḥāwiyya says we do not rebel against our rulers and pray for their well-being (s16). The Sharḥ adds a fuller theory: the imāma is obligatory by consensus; the dispute is on whom and by what proof (God by reason: the Shīʿa; creation by reason: Muʿtazila; creation by revelation: Ahl al-sunna). Taftāzānī gives the proofs (the ḥadīth of dying without knowing the imam of one's time; the Companions placed his appointment before burying the Prophet), answers 'why not a powerful ruler in each area' and the Turkish rulers' rule, and sets the conditions: mukallaf, free, male, just, brave, competent, a Qurayshī not limited to Hāshim or ʿAlī's descendants; not infallible; not hidden or awaited; not deposed by sin or oppression. The Shīʿa (twelve imams, the hidden imam) are refuted at length.
Is appointing an imām obligatory, and on whom?
Obligatory by consensus; the dispute is whether on God, or on creation, by a textual or a rational proof; the Sharḥ's position: on the Community, by revelation, from the ḥadīth 'whoever dies without knowing the imām of his time dies a Jāhilī death' and from the Companions' practice in making the appointment a priority before the Prophet's burial, and after every imām.tap to reveal
Why not make do with a powerful man in each region?
Because it leads to disputes and quarrels that destabilise religion and worldly matters, as we see in our time; and a general ruler with power but no imāma (as the Turkish rulers) gives some order in worldly affairs but spoils the religion, which is the aim.tap to reveal

He is in charge ('sāʾis') able to carry out rulings, guard the borders and give justice to the wronged; he must be mukallaf, free, male, just, brave and competent, Qurayshī.tap to reveal

A cluster of practical markers of Ahl al-sunna. Prayer is valid behind every righteous or sinful person (with dislike for the innovator unless his innovation reaches kufr), and the prayer over the dead is for every believer, even a sinner or innovator. The Companions: refrain from reviling them; the jumhūr hold a reviler is disciplined (taʿzīr) not killed; the Sharḥ gives the khilāf on cursing Muʿāwiya and Yazīd (Taftāzānī's own strong view on Yazīd is quoted), confirms the ten promised Paradise, the people of Badr and Ḥudaybiyya, and Fāṭima, al-Ḥasan and al-Ḥusayn. Literal texts, ilḥād, istiḥlāl, despair and security, and the non-existent

Supplication, signs of the Hour, ijtihād and the angels versus mankind

The last masāʾil. Supplication and charity benefit the dead (against the Muʿtazila's restriction), and God answers prayers and fulfils needs; the Sharḥ notes the dispute whether an unbeliever's prayer is answered. The signs of the Hour (Dajjāl, the Beast, Yājūj and Mājūj, ʿĪsā's descent, the sun rising from the west) are true because they are possible and reported by the Truthful. The mujtahid may err or be right, with the Sharḥ's choice that in the furūʿ there is a determinate ruling with a probable proof and four arguments, against the Muṣawwiba (Ashʿarīs and Muʿtazila who say each mujtahid is right in furūʿ); in ʿaqāʾid and uṣūl only one is right. The matn ends with the ranking: messengers of mankind above messengers of angels, messengers of angels above ordinary men, and ordinary men above ordinary angels, with proofs and the opponents' arguments. The Ṭaḥāwiyya (s19, s21) mentions duʿāʾ for the dead and the signs of the Hour; the Sharḥ gives the proofs and ends with the ranking not found there.
Do supplication and charity benefit the dead?
Yes: the matn says the dead benefit from the prayers and charity of the living, by the prayers for the dead reported in the sound ḥadīths (especially in the funeral prayer), the ḥadīth of Saʿd b. ʿUbāda's charity for his mother, 'charity quenches God's wrath' and the ḥadīth about a village cemetery; the verse 'man has only what he strove for' (53:39) is read to mean his own due, not excluding extra.tap to reveal
Does God answer prayers and fulfil needs?
وأنه تعالى يجيب الدعوات ويقضي الحاجات
Yes: 'He answers prayers and fulfils needs' by verses ('call upon Me, I will answer you', 40:60) and ḥadīths; the condition is sincerity and presence of heart; a stipulation: 'call upon God while sure of the answer'.tap to reveal
Are the signs of the Hour true?
طلوع الشمس من مغربها فهو حق
Yes, whatever the Prophet ﷺ reported of the signs: the Dajjāl, the Beast of the earth, Yājūj and Mājūj, ʿĪsā's descent and the sun rising from the west: because they are possible matters reported by the Truthful; the sound ḥadīths on them are very many.tap to reveal

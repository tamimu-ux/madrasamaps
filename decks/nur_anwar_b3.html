Ḥurūf al-maʿānī: introduction and wāw for muṭlaq al-jamʿ

Nūr al-Anwār ties ḥurūf al-maʿānī to ḥaqīqa and majāz: each particle has a literal and figurative sense, and the list mixes true ḥurūf with sharṭ and ẓarf words by taghlīb. Wāw is for mere joining, in the predicated subject (single words) or just thubūt (sentences), not for order or togetherness. Some Shāfiʿīs argue order from 'nabdaʾu bimā badaʾa Allāh' and 'irkaʿū wa-sjudū'; the Ḥanafīs reply the Prophet may have known it from non-recited revelation, and that 'wa-sjudī wa-rkaʿī' shows the verse cannot prove order. Shāshī gave the wāw rule; this adds the grammar, the opponents and replies.
What are ḥurūf al-maʿānī and why do they follow the majāz?
ويتصل بما ذكرنا حروف المعاني
The ḥurūf al-maʿānī are the grammatical particles, ʿāmila or not, as opposed to the ḥurūf al-mabānī (the letters of words). They are linked with ḥaqīqa and majāz because each particle has a ḥaqīqa and majāz (fī as ẓarfiyya is ḥaqīqa, as ʿalā majāz).tap to reveal
What does wāw mean?
Wāw is for mere joining, with no hint of simultaneity or order. In a ʿaṭf of single words the sharing is in the muḥkūm ʿalayhi (what is said about); in ʿaṭf of sentences, it is only in the thubūt and wuqūʿ. So 'jāʾanī Zayd wa-ʿAmr' may mean together, or one before the other.tap to reveal
Does wāw give order, as some Shāfiʿīs say?
وحجة الشافعي: قوله عليه الصلاة والسلام: «نحن نبدأ بما بدأ الله»
Some Shāfiʿīs say wāw is for tartīb, relying on 'We begin with what Allāh began with' (the Prophet on al-Ṣafā and al-Marwa, Baqara 158: Muslim, Abū Dāwūd, Nasāʾī) and 'irkaʿū wa-sjudū' (Ḥajj 77) from which the ruku' first is derived. and that al-Zarkashī and Abū Manṣūr report that al-Shāfiʿī himself did not say it: wāw is mere jamʿ for him, and tartīb in wuḍūʾ is from other proof.tap to reveal

Furūʿ of wāw: the order comes from the kalām, not the particle

Four furūʿ test the claim that wāw only joins. 'In dakhalti al-dār fa-anti ṭāliq wa-ṭāliq wa-ṭāliq': Abū Ḥanīfa gives one (the sentence entails separation), the two give three (togetherness); with the sharṭ last all three. 'Anti ṭāliq wa-ṭāliq wa-ṭāliq' gives one for all three imams because the first falls before the second is spoken. The fuḍūlī marrying two slave-girls and two sisters show the same: the second marriage or both are lost because of the circumstances or the closing words, not because wāw orders them.
'In dakhalti al-dār fa-anti ṭāliq wa-ṭāliq wa-ṭāliq' to a woman not yet consummated: how many ṭalāqs?
إنما تطلق واحدة عند أبي حنيفة؛ لأن موجب هذا الكلام الافتراق، فلا يتغير بالواو
Abū Ḥanīfa: one. The mūjib of this statement is separation (had he meant all together he would have said 'anti ṭāliq thalāthan'); wāw does not alter it, so each falls in turn, the first falls and nothing remains for the second and third. The ṣāḥibayn: three, since its mūjib is togetherness: all three are attached to one shart in one breath, so all fall at once. Fakhr al-Islām and the author of al-Taqwīm incline to the two's view.tap to reveal
What if the sharṭ is mentioned after: 'anti ṭāliq wa-ṭāliq wa-ṭāliq in dakhalti al-dār'?
لأنه وجد في آخر الكلام ما يغير أوله وهو الشرط
Three fall by agreement, since the end has something that alters the beginning (the sharṭ), so the first part waits for the last and all three are attached together.tap to reveal
'Anti ṭāliq wa-ṭāliq wa-ṭāliq' to a woman not consummated: how many?
لأن الأول وقع قبل التكلم بالثاني والثالث فسقطت ولايته؛ لفوات محل التصرف
One (she is separated by the first), by agreement of the three Ḥanafī imams, since the first took effect before the second and third were spoken and there was no waiting period left to bear more. The order here is from the impossibility of uttering three words at once, not from the wāw: with no wāw at all ('ṭāliq ṭāliq ṭāliq') it is also one by agreement.tap to reveal

Wāw for ḥāl and for ʿaṭf of sentences

Shāshī gave the 'addi ilayya alfan wa-anta ḥurr' rule. Nūr al-Anwār explains why it is wāw al-ḥāl rather than ʿaṭf (a khabar on an inshāʾ; the clause is an assumed state; it stands for the jawāb al-amr; and a state does not precede what it describes), then gives wāw joining sentences (no sharing in the predicate), and the Ḥanafī khilāf on 'ṭalliqnī wa-laka alf': Abū Ḥanīfa nothing owed; the two the thousand is due as in khulʿ.
What does the wāw in 'addi ilayya alfan wa-anta ḥurr' do?
كقوله لعبده: أدّ إلي ألفاً وأنت حر، حتى لا يعتق إلا بالأداء؛ لأنه جعل الحرية حال الأداء، ولا تسبق الحرية الأداء
It is wāw al-ḥāl, a majāz of the wāw: the slave is free only upon paying, because freedom was made the state of the paying, and a state cannot come before what it describes.tap to reveal
Why is it not ʿaṭf?
A khabar ('anta ḥurr') attached to an inshāʾ ('addi') is not allowed as ʿaṭf by the bayān scholars, Ibn Mālik (in Sharḥ al-Tasḥīl) and Ibn ʿUṣfūr, whom Ibn Hishām cites; al-Ṣaffār and others allow it. Moreover if it were ʿaṭf, the freedom would have to be at once and the payment would be only a duty.tap to reveal
Which arguments show that the clause is a ḥāl that makes freedom depend on payment?
(a) qalb: the order is reversed so as if 'addi' were the ḥāl of 'anta ḥurr'; ta'līq of the payment cannot be since tanjīz is not in the speaker's power. (b) ḥāl muqaddara like 'khālidīn fīhā' (Qur'an, forty places): 'pay a thousand while destined to be free during the payment', so freedom depends on the payment. (c) The ḥāl-clause stands in for the answer to the command: 'pay a thousand and you will be free'. (d) The ḥāl is a description in meaning, and a description does not precede its subject; freedom cannot come before payment.tap to reveal

The fāʾ: ḥaqīqa, ʿilal and majāz

Shāshī gave fāʾ for taʿqīb and ʿilla. Nūr al-Anwār separates the lughawī tarākhī (a tiny time) from that of thumma, adds 'biʿtu … fa-huwa ḥurr' as acceptance (but 'wa-huwa ḥurr' as rejection), explains that fāʾ enters an ʿilla only if it lasts (al-Bazdawī) or is ghāʾiyya (al-Tawḍīḥ), and answers the 'conditional' objection. The majāz: 'lahu ʿalayya dirham fa-dirham' means two dirhams for the Ḥanafīs, since taʿqīb cannot attach to a substance; al-Shāfiʿī: one unless he intended ʿaṭf.
What is the ḥaqīqa of fāʾ?
والفاء للوصل والتعقيب
Fāʾ joins and makes the second follow the first. The second is separated from the first by a time, even if tiny; otherwise it would be the same moment and one would use 'maʿa'. The tarākhī here is the lughawī sense (not the uṣūlī sense of thumma). So 'in dakhalti hādhihi al-dār fa-hādhihi al-dār fa-anti ṭāliq' requires entering the second after the first without delay.tap to reveal
'Biʿtu minka hādhā al-ʿabd bi-kadhā' and the other says 'fa-huwa ḥurr': is that acceptance?
Yes: 'fa-huwa ḥurr' is an acceptance of the sale (he said qabiltu and freed): the fāʾ makes the freeing follow the offer, and a freeing following an offer is possible only after acceptance, so acceptance is implied.tap to reveal
When does fāʾ enter an ʿilla instead of its effect?
وقد تدخل على العلل إذا كانت مما تدوم
When the ʿilla is lasting: then it exists after the ḥukm as before it, and the taʿqīb of the fāʾ is realised. 'Addi ilayya alfan fa-anta ḥurr' means 'pay me a thousand, because you are free': the freedom is there from the start and remains, so he is freed at once and the thousand is a debt. Where the ʿilla is not lasting it does not suit fāʾ (as 'abshir fa-qad atāka al-ghawth': the coming of help is done and not repeated). Al-Bazdawī requires dawām; al-Tawḍīḥ says fāʾ enters only on an ʿilla ghāʾiyya.tap to reveal

Thumma: tarākhī and its furūʿ

Shāshī gives thumma for tarākhī. Nūr al-Anwār gives the reasoning: Abū Ḥanīfa puts the gap in the utterance and ruling (so he treats it as a pause), the two put it only in the ruling. The furūʿ with a non-consummated or consummated wife and the position of the sharṭ follow. The majāz is the hadith 'fa-l-yukaffir ʿan yamīnihi thumma l-yaʾti…': for the Ḥanafīs thumma there is borrowed for wāw, so it does not prove al-Shāfiʿī's permission to expiate before breaking the oath.
What does thumma mean and where is its tarākhī?
وثم: للتراخي بمنزلة ما لو سكت ثم استأنف
Thumma is for a gap: 'anti ṭāliq thumma ṭāliq' is as if he said 'anti ṭāliq', kept silent, then began again. That is the perfect form of tarākhī. For Abū Ḥanīfa the tarākhī is in the utterance and the ruling: tarākhī in the ruling with waṣl in the utterance cannot be in inshāʾāt, so the gap is stated in the speech as an assumed pause (taqdīr). The two: the tarākhī is in the ruling only, with waṣl in the utterance, following the wording.tap to reveal
'Anti ṭāliq thumma ṭāliq thumma ṭāliq in dakhalti al-dār' to a woman not consummated: what happens?
حتى إذا قال لغير المدخول بها: أنت طالق ثم طالق ثم طالق إن دخلت الدار، فعنده: يقع الأول ويلغو ما بعده
Abū Ḥanīfa: the first falls at once and the rest are void (it is as if he stopped after the first), since she is not consummated and she has no waiting period. If the shart comes first ('in dakhalti al-dār fa-anti ṭāliq thumma ṭāliq thumma ṭāliq') the first hangs on the sharṭ, the second falls now (a fresh sentence), the third is void; the first matters because if he later marries her and the condition occurs, the first falls then. The two: all three hang and fall in order when the shart occurs, so the non-consummated gets only the first.tap to reveal
What if the woman is consummated?
Abū Ḥanīfa: if the sharṭ is last, the first two fall now and the third hangs on the sharṭ; if the sharṭ is first, the first hangs and the second and third fall now. The two: all hang and fall in order when the sharṭ occurs, giving three for a consummated woman.tap to reveal

Bal and lākin

Shāshī gives bal (iḍrāb, but ṭalāq cannot be corrected) and lākin (istidrāk, ittisāq). Nūr al-Anwār defines tadāruk as correcting a slip of speech, notes Zufar's analogy of iqrār to ṭalāq and the khabar/inshāʾ answer, and clarifies lākin: mukhaffafa is a ʿāṭifa, mushaddada only shares the istidrāk, and it joins only when the clause is connected with the negation not touching the same act.
What does 'bal' do?
وبل: لإثبات ما بعده، والإعراض عما قبله على سبيل التدارك
Bal affirms what follows and turns away from what came before as a correction: the speaker says he slipped (ghalaṭ) in what comes before 'bal', not that it was false in reality. 'Jāʾanī Zayd bal ʿAmr' means the coming is for ʿAmr not Zayd (Zayd's coming left open); with 'lā bal' the denial of Zayd's coming is explicit. In a negated sentence ('mā jāʾanī Zayd bal ʿAmr') some say the negation is referred to ʿAmr and the affirmation to the first.tap to reveal
'Anti ṭāliq wāḥida bal ithnatayn' to a consummated wife: how many?
فتطلق ثلاثاً إذا قال لامرأته الموطوءة: أنت طالق واحدة بل ثنتين؛ لأنه لم يملك إبطال الأول
Three, because he cannot cancel the first: the iʿrāḍ that bal gives is valid only where what came before can be corrected, and ṭalāq is an inshāʾ, which takes effect when spoken; it does not allow withdrawal and the first stays, so one and two are counted.tap to reveal
'Lahu ʿalayya alf bal alfān': why is it two thousand and not three, as in ṭalāq?
بخلاف قوله: له علي ألف بل ألفان
Zufar analogises to ṭalāq (three thousand). The reply: this is an iqrār, a report, and a report can bear iḍrāb and the correction of a slip, so the first is replaced and he owes two. A retraction of the first admission is invalid, but the second admission replacing it is valid, so only two are due. Ṭalāq is an inshāʾ that cannot be corrected.tap to reveal

Aw: its ḥaqīqa (one of two named)

Shāshī gave aw for aḥad al-madhkūrayn and its furūʿ. Nūr al-Anwār adds the competing view (aw for shakk) and its rebuttal, shows why 'hādhā ḥurr aw hādhā' is an inshāʾ that may be a khabar, and that the bayān is both (so the dead slave cannot be claimed). It adds the jahāla rules: wakāla yes, sale/ijāra no unless it is khiyār al-taʿyīn between two or three; mahr (the two vs Abū Ḥanīfa); and the kaffāra khilāf (aḥad al-ashyāʾ vs all in turn).
What is the ḥaqīqa of 'aw'?
أو: لأحد المذكورين
Aw is for one of the two things named: 'hādhā ḥurr aw hādhā' is like 'aḥadukumā ḥurr'. This is the choice of Shams al-Aʾimma and Fakhr al-Islām. A group of uṣūlīs and the grammarians hold aw is set for doubt (shakk).tap to reveal
Is aw set for shakk?
Not so: shakk is not an intended meaning of the speaker; it arises only from the subject matter (ignorance of which one came) in reports, and in inshāʾ the result is takhyīr; and if shakk were meant it would have a word of its own. Taftāzānī: there is no real dispute; they only mean the doubt that first comes to mind. grammarians mostly restrict the shakk sense to reports (Ibn Hishām, Hamʿ).tap to reveal
Is 'hādhā ḥurr aw hādhā' an inshāʾ or a khabar?
وهذا الكلام إنشاء يحتمل الخبر، فأوجب التخيير على احتمال أنه بيان
It is inshāʾ in Sharʿ (the Lawgiver put it to create freedom) and may be a khabar in language (about a prior freedom); with the two aspects it gives takhyīr: the speaker may then fix freedom in either, on the chance the clarification is a bayān.tap to reveal

Aw: its majāz (bal, wāw, ḥattā/illā an)

Four borrowed uses of 'aw'. As 'bal' (āyat al-muḥāraba): Mālik leaves the Imām a choice; the Ḥanafīs match each punishment to its crime. As the way a ḥaqīqa becomes majāz where it is impossible (hādhā ḥurr aw hādhā for slave and animal): Abū Ḥanīfa acts on the possible meaning, the two void it. As wāw for ʿumūm in negation and permission (not the very wāw, since the wāw is for togetherness and aw for each alone). And as ḥattā/illā an when ʿaṭf fails (Āl ʿImrān 128), with al-Zamakhsharī's alternative reading.
Is 'aw' in āyat al-muḥāraba a choice for the Imām?
وفي قوله تعالى: أن يقتلوا أو يصلبوا للتخيير عند مالك، وعندنا: بمعنى بل
Mālik: aw keeps its meaning and the Imām chooses among killing, crucifying, cutting hand and foot from opposite sides, or banishing (Māʾida 33). The Ḥanafīs: aw is borrowed to mean 'bal', turning from one case to another, because the highway robbers' crimes are four and the four punishments match them: the penalty matches the crime in severity and the Wise would not give the harshest crime the lightest penalty.tap to reveal
What penalty matches each crime according to the Ḥanafī reading?
(1) Taking property only: cutting hand and foot from opposite sides. (2) Killing only: qatl. (3) Killing and taking property: ṣalb (crucifixion). (4) Terrifying only: nafy from the land, which is not exile but being kept from appearing on the earth by imprisonment until repentance. The commentary adds a report: Jibrīl brought this ruling when the Prophet had a treaty with Abū Burda al-Aslamī, whose men robbed people travelling to Islam: whoever killed and took property is crucified, whoever killed only is killed, whoever took property only has hand and foot cut, and whoever only terrified is banished.tap to reveal
Does Abū Ḥanīfa tie ṣalb to that case only?
He reads 'man qatala wa-akhadha al-māl ṣulib' as the assignment of crucifixion to that case, not as the assignment of that case to crucifixion: in that case the Imām has the choice: cut, then kill or crucify; or kill or crucify without cutting, since the crime may be taken as one or two, and both sides are considered.tap to reveal

Ḥurūf al-maʿānī: ḥattā

Shāshī gave ḥattā as ghāya and as jazāʾ/ʿaṭf in oaths. Nūr al-Anwār lays out the full ladder: ḥattā is originally for ghāya (the thing after it is normally included in the ruling of what precedes); with nouns it acts as ʿaṭf with ghāya; with verbs it is ghāya if the preceding part can extend and the following can end it; otherwise it is jazāʾ (like lām kay: the second is the result of the first); and if that too fails, the fuqahāʾ read it as a borrowed pure ʿaṭf. The three Ziyādāt oaths test each rung. The section opens by finishing the 'aw bi-maʿnā ilā' discussion (uṣūlīs vs Zamakhsharī on 'aw yatūba ʿalayhim').
What is ḥattā originally for, and is its ghāya included in what precedes?
وحتى للغاية
Ḥattā is originally for ghāya. It is listed among the ʿaṭf particles, but its primary sense is ghāya: what follows is either part of what precedes (akaltu al-samaka ḥattā raʾsahā) or not part of it but adjoining it (ḥattā maṭlaʿ al-fajr, in the verse 'salāmun hiya ḥattā maṭlaʿi al-fajr'). When there is no qarīna, most grammarians hold the ghāya IS included in the ruling of what precedes.tap to reveal
How does ḥattā work with nouns?
With nouns it joins like ʿaṭf yet keeps the sense of ghāya: the thing after it is the highest or lowest member of the group, as in 'istannati al-fiṣālu ḥattā al-qarʿā' (even the scabby young camels frolicked). So it is a ḥaqīqa qāṣira: not a pure mixing of ḥaqīqa and majāz, but a ḥaqīqa that has both aspects.tap to reveal
Where is ḥattā used with verbs?
ومواضعها في الأفعال
With verbs it takes two positions: (1) ghāya together with ilā, so the clause is part of the first sentence (sirtu ḥattā adkhulahā: the entering ends the walking); (2) ghāya of a new sentence (jumla mubtadaʾa), unconnected to what precedes and with no iʿrāb position (kharajati al-nisāʾu ḥattā kharajat Hind).tap to reveal

Ḥurūf al-jarr: bāʾ (ilṣāq, thaman, khabar, illā bi-idhn, mashīʾa, mash al-raʾs)

Nūr al-Anwār turns from the ʿaṭf particles to ḥurūf al-jarr. Bāʾ is originally for ilṣāq (attaching); all other senses are majāz. This governs sales (the bāʾ goes on the thaman), oaths about reports (in akhbartanī bi-qudūm Fulān), the exception 'illā bi-idhnī' (idhn needed at each exit) versus 'illā an ādhana laka' (once is enough), 'bi-mashīʾat Allāh' in ṭalāq, and the famous khilāf over wa-msaḥū bi-ruʾūsikum: Shāfiʿī reads bāʾ as tabʿīḍ, Mālik as zāʾida, the Ḥanafīs as ilṣāq with the amount learned from the Sunna (a quarter, the nāṣiya).
What objection is raised to reading 'illā an ādhana laka' as ghāya, and how is it answered?
Objection: reading 'illā' as 'ilā' is strained; better to assume a bāʾ ('illā khurūjan bi-an ādhana laka'), which would make it identical to 'illā bi-idhnī' (repeat each time); or take the muḍāʿ as a maṣdar used as time ('ātīka khufūq al-najm'), so permission is needed for each exit. Reply: the assumed phrase 'illā khurūjan bi-an ādhana' is faulty speech; and on the second view the oath would break if she leaves after one permission without a new one, while on the first it does not, and an oath is not broken on doubt.tap to reveal
How is repeated permission still required in 'lā tadkhulū buyūt al-nabī illā an yuʾdhana lakum'?
That repetition comes from other indicators, not from 'illā an': the reason (entering another's house without permission is blameworthy for every entry) and the wording itself ('inna dhālikum kāna yuʾdhī al-nabī').tap to reveal
In 'wa-msaḥū bi-ruʾūsikum', what do the three schools take bāʾ to be?
Shāfiʿī: bāʾ is for tabʿīḍ, 'wipe part of your heads', where 'part' is unspecific (hair or what is above it down to near the whole): whatever part is wiped he is performing the command. Mālik: bāʾ is ṣila (zāʾida) so the sense is 'wipe your heads', whose ẓāhir is the whole head, so wiping all is farḍ. Ḥanafīs: bāʾ is for ilṣāq in its true sense; the 'part' comes from another route.tap to reveal

Ḥurūf al-jarr: min and ilā (tabʿīḍ; intihāʾ al-ghāya)

Min is originally for tabʿīḍ: 'man shiʾta min ʿabīdī ʿitqahu fa-aʿtiqhu' lets the agent free all but one for Abū Ḥanīfa, while the two read min as bayān. Ilā is for intihāʾ al-ghāya, and Nūr al-Anwār gives a precise rule on whether the end-point is included: not if it stands by itself (wall to wall; deadlines); yes if the opening of the sentence includes it and ilā only drops what is beyond (the elbows); not if the sentence does not include it (the night in fasting); and in doubt (an oath to the month of Rajab) not by the ẓāhir al-riwāya.
What is the original meaning of min, and what other views exist?
ومن للتبعيض
For most jurists, tabʿīḍ is its aṣl wazʿ and other senses are majāz. Most language scholars say its aṣl is ibtidāʾ al-ghāya (spatial or temporal); some say tabyīn; the author of al-Musallam prefers ishtirāk between these senses, because of how readily each comes to mind.tap to reveal
What is ilā for, and what are the four views on inclusion of the end-point?
وإلى لانتهاء الغاية
Ilā is for intihāʾ al-ghāya (end of the distance). Grammarians have four views: included always; excluded always; included if of the same genus; no indication either way. The Manār gives a division by cases instead.tap to reveal

Ḥurūf al-jarr: fī (ẓarfiyya, ḥadhf wa-ithbāt, makān, fī al-fiʿl)

Fī is for ẓarfiyya, agreed by the Ḥanafīs. They differ on dropping and keeping it with time-words: ghadan vs fī ghad, 'li-aṣūmanna al-dahr' vs 'fī al-dahr'. For place, 'anti ṭāliq fī Makka' is general (the place cannot restrict a divorce). And with an act (fī dukhūlik al-dār) it is read as sharṭ with the act (maʿa al-dukhūl).
'Anti ṭāliq ghadan' versus 'anti ṭāliq fī ghad': what is the ruling?
The two (Abū Yūsuf and Muḥammad): dropping fī or keeping it are the same (it covers all tomorrow, so ṭalāq falls at the start of the day). Abū Ḥanīfa: ghadan is mustaghriq (it covers the whole day); fī ghad is only that it happens in a part of tomorrow, because 'fī' does not require its object to be filled. So without a niyya ṭalāq falls at the start of tomorrow. If he intends the end of the day, his intention is accepted: as printed, in this edition, diyānatan not qaḍāʾan in both wordings (see reviewer note).tap to reveal
How does the same difference appear in vows ('la-aṣūmanna al-dahr' versus 'fī al-dahr')?
Without fī, the vow requires fasting all of life (the time covers everything); with fī, it is only an act of fasting in some part of time. The same difference as 'ghadan' versus 'fī ghad'.tap to reveal
'Anti ṭāliq fī Makka': does it fall only in Makka?
No. Place cannot restrict ṭalāq, because ṭalāq, once it occurs, occurs in all places; so the mention of the place is ignored and ṭalāq falls at once everywhere. The sentence uses 'fī' for ẓarfiyya, which requires a bearer limited by place; ṭalāq has no such limit.tap to reveal

Ḥurūf al-sharṭ: in, idhā, idhā mā, law

The sharḥ turns to ḥurūf al-sharṭ, called 'ḥurūf' by taghlīb though some are ẓurūf. In is the aṣl of sharṭ: it enters a thing that does not yet exist and is on the edge of existing (khaṭar al-wujūd). So 'in lam uṭalliqki fa-anti ṭāliq' falls only near death. Idhā is where Ḥanafī usage forks: Kūfans say it is for time and sharṭ equally; Baṣrans say it is for time and only majāzan for sharṭ. Abū Ḥanīfa: idhā is mushtarak, and when used for sharṭ it loses the time sense (so it behaves as in); the two: it keeps the time and the ṭalāq falls at once. Idhā mā is like idhā but always has jazāʾ. Law is read as sharṭ by report from the two.
Why is 'in' the aṣl of sharṭ?
Because it is used only for sharṭ (the others carry other senses too), so the whole group is named 'ḥurūf al-sharṭ' by taghlīb. (The ḥāshiya objects that 'in' also negates; the answer is that 'in' of sharṭ and 'in' of nafy are two distinct particles, and another account: 'in' is pure sharṭ with no ẓarfiyya, unlike idhā and matā.)tap to reveal
What kind of matter does 'in' enter?

Al-ṣarīḥ: taʿrīf and ḥukm

Al-ṣarīḥ is a word whose meaning has become evident through use, whether the use is literal or figurative. It is thus not another category beside ḥaqīqa and majāz, but cuts across them; it is separated only because some rulings attach to it. Its ḥukm: the ruling hangs on the word itself, which stands in for its meaning, so no niyya is needed (anta ḥurr, anti ṭāliq).
What is ṣarīḥ, and how is it different from ẓāhir, naṣṣ and mufassar?
وأما الصريح فما ظهر به المراد ظهورا بينا حقيقة كان أو مجازا
Ṣarīḥ is what makes the intended meaning evident in a clear way, literal or figurative. The clarity is that of use (kathrat al-istiʿmāl), so ẓāhir (clear only by wazʿ, with room for doubt) is excluded; and naṣṣ, mufassar and muḥkam are clear by the speaker's purpose or by qarāʾin, not by use, so they are outside the definition. So ṣarīḥ and kināya are two divisions of ḥaqīqa and majāz that are given their own names because some rulings are attached to them.tap to reveal
What is the ruling of ṣarīḥ?
وحكمه تعلق الحكم بعين الكلام
The ruling is tied to the very utterance, and the utterance takes the place of its meaning, so no niyya is required. 'Anta ḥurr' and 'anti ṭāliq' are ṣarīḥ: ʿitq and ṭalāq take place by the wording, as in 'biʿtu and ishtaraytu', even if he intended only to say 'subḥān Allāh' and these words slipped on his tongue. In court the judge goes by the zāhir. A joker's ṭalāq also takes place because his wish does not change the lawgiver's ruling (the ḥadīth that jidd and hazl are equal in ṭalāq).tap to reveal
Are 'anta ḥurr' and 'anti ṭāliq' ḥaqīqa or majāz?
Apparently both are examples of ṣarīḥ by ḥaqīqa: they are literal sharʿī terms (ḥaqīqa sharʿiyya) for removing slavery and marriage. They may also be seen as both ḥaqīqa and majāz from two sides: majāz in language (an informing, not a creation, of the removal), ḥaqīqa in sharīʿa.tap to reveal

Al-kināya: taʿrīf, ḥukm, alfāẓ al-ṭalāq

Al-kināya is a word whose intended meaning is hidden and is understood only by a qarīna, whether the word is literal or figurative. Kināya, too, cuts across ḥaqīqa and majāz. Its hidden-ness is in use (not in wazʿ as with mushtarak). Examples: a mahjūra ḥaqīqa (lā yaḍaʿu qadamahu fī dār Fulān) is kināya; a mustaʿmala ḥaqīqa is ṣarīḥ; a mutaʿāraf majāz is ṣarīḥ, unmutaʿāraf is kināya; pronouns are kināya. Its ruling: no action without niyya or a substitute (the state of anger, the talk of ṭalāq). The sharḥ then treats the bāʾin-type words in ṭalāq (anti bāʾin, battah, ḥarām…), the three rajʿī words (iʿtaddī, istabriʾī raḥimaki, anti wāḥida), and the rule that ḥudūd and kaffārāt do not rest on kināya.
What is kināya and why does it also cross ḥaqīqa/majāz?
وأما الكناية فما استتر المراد به ولا يفهم إلا بقرينة حقيقة كان أو مجازا
Kināya is what hides its meaning: the intended meaning is not understood except by a qarīna, whether the word is literal or figurative. Its istitār is in use (the speaker uses it to conceal), unlike the mushtarak whose hiddenness is by wazʿ. Khafī and mushkil need not be excluded in the definition because their hidden-ness is caused by something else; for the ḥāshiya they in fact enter kināya (Baḥr al-ʿUlūm), and one division can enter another.tap to reveal
How do ṣarīḥ and kināya combine with ḥaqīqa and majāz?
A ḥaqīqa mahjūra is kināya (the meaning is understood only by a qarīna since people have left it); a ḥaqīqa mustaʿmala is ṣarīḥ; a majāz mutaʿāraf is ṣarīḥ (lā yaḍaʿu qadamahu in 'the house of so and so' as 'enter' once the figurative sense is common); a majāz that is not mutaʿāraf is kināya (rāʾaytu asadan, for the brave man). Qāḍī Abū Zayd: any word with several possibilities is kināya, so a majāz before it becomes mutaʿāraf is kināya.tap to reveal
What is the ruling of kināya?
وحكمها أن لا يجب العمل بها إلا بالنية
No action is required on it without niyya (of the speaker), since its meaning is hidden; or something standing in for the niyya: the state of anger or the discussion of ṭalāq. 'Niyya' in the sharḥ is thus wider than intention: it includes a state or qarīna. For the hearer the ruling follows whichever indicator he can see, since a niyya is hidden.tap to reveal

Ishārat al-naṣṣ

Ishāra is what is established by the wording in language but was not intended and not what the text was set forth for, and is not evident from every side. Example: 'wa-ʿalā al-mawlūd lahu rizquhunna wa-kiswatuhunna' is ʿibāra for the father's duty to maintain the mothers, and ishāra that the child belongs to the father (lām al-ikhtiṣāṣ) and that he may take from the son's property when needed. ʿIbāra and ishāra are both qaṭʿī in force, but ʿibāra wins in a clash (the ḥadīth of the deficiency of women; the 15-day view of ḥayḍ). Ishāra, like ʿibāra, can be general or specified.
What is ishārat al-naṣṣ, and what does each qualification exclude?
ما ثبت بنظمه لغة لكنه غير مقصود ولا سيق له النص وليس بظاهر من كل وجه
Ishāra is what is established by the wording (naẓm) in language, yet unintended and not what the text was set forth for, and not apparent from every side. 'bi-naẓmihi' covers both ʿibāra and ishāra but excludes dalāla (it is established by the meaning, not the wording). 'lughatan' excludes the muqtaḍā (it is established in sharʿ or reason, not language). 'Not intended and not set forth for' excludes ʿibāra. 'Not apparent from every side' is added for emphasis: like a man who looks straight at someone and also sees, unattended, the one at his side. The first is ʿibāra, the second ishāra.tap to reveal
What does 'wa-ʿalā al-mawlūd lahu rizquhunna wa-kiswatuhunna bi-l-maʿrūf' establish by ʿibāra and by ishāra?
ʿIbāra: the father's duty to maintain and clothe the mothers (whether as wives, or as nursing mothers if divorced and after their ʿidda). Ishāra: (1) the child is attributed to the father with the lām of ikhtiṣāṣ ('lahu'), so the lineage follows him (even if the mother is non-Arab, the child is Qurashī in kafāʾa and imāma, per al-Qārī); (2) the father has a right of taking from the son's property in need (the child belongs to the father by lām al-milk; the full ownership of the child is voided by ijmāʿ, but its effect remains as a right of tamalluk in need); (3) nobody shares the father in the duty to maintain, as nobody shares the attribution.tap to reveal
How do ʿibāra and ishāra compare in force?
Both give a qaṭʿī ruling and are equal in establishing it (the ruling is certain, not necessarily wājib). But when they clash, ʿibāra is preferred, since what ʿibāra shows is the aim of the speech, while ishāra's is not what it was set forth for.tap to reveal

Dalālat al-naṣṣ

Dalāla is what is established by the meaning of the naṣṣ in language, not by ijtihād. The ruling is extended from what the text states to what it does not state because the same ground (manāṭ) is found. Its famous example: 'falā taqul lahumā uff' shows the prohibition of beating and insulting parents. It is not qiyās (al-Rāzī called it qiyās jalī; the sharḥ gives four reasons: qiyās is ẓannī, known only to the mujtahid, dalāla preceded qiyās, and deniers of qiyās accept dalāla). Ḥudūd and kaffārāt may rest on it, with examples: ḥadd of zinā for rajm on others than Māʿiz, ḥadd of qaṭʿ al-ṭarīq, the kaffāra for a woman and for eating/drinking in Ramaḍān. It has no generality or specification. At clash, ishāra is stronger, e.g., kaffāra for deliberate killing.
What is dalālat al-naṣṣ and what does each qualification exclude?
وأما الثابت بدلالة النص فما ثبت بمعنى النص لغة لا اجتهادا
It is what is established by the maʿnā of the naṣṣ in language, not by ijtihād. 'Maʿnā al-naṣṣ' excludes ʿibāra and ishāra (they are by the wording). By 'maʿnā' is meant its entailed meaning (maʿnāhu al-iltizāmī), not the literal one (e.g. pain from 'uff'). 'Lughatan' excludes iqtiḍāʾ and ḥadhf (they are by sharʿ or reason, ḥadhf by language per another view). 'Lā ijtihādan' makes it clear that it is not qiyās.tap to reveal
Is dalālat al-naṣṣ the same as qiyās?
No. Imām al-Rāzī said it is qiyās jalī (clear qiyās, with an aṣl, a far ʿ, and a common ʿilla). The sharḥ answers with four reasons: (1) qiyās is ẓannī while dalāla is qaṭʿī; (2) qiyās is known only to the mujtahid, dalāla by every speaker of the language; (3) dalāla was valid before qiyās was introduced; (4) those who deny qiyās do not deny dalāla.tap to reveal
What are the examples of rulings by dalāla?
(1) Beating/insulting parents is forbidden from 'lā taqul lahumā uff' (the literal sense is a word of annoyance; its entailed sense is hurting). (2) Ḥadd for liwāṭ according to the two, from the ḥadd of zinā: the meaning of zinā that necessitates ḥadd (satisfying lust by spilling semen in a prohibited, desired place) exists in liwāṭ. (3) The kaffāra on a woman in Ramaḍān intercourse from the text on the man: the common meaning is the offence against the fast. (4) The kaffāra for eating and drinking in Ramaḍān from the text about intercourse: even more so, as patience for them is harder and the urge greater.tap to reveal

Iqtiḍāʾ al-naṣṣ (al-muqtaḍī) and its difference from al-maḥdhūf

Iqtiḍāʾ is what the text cannot work without presupposing first: the ruling is attached to the naṣṣ through the muqtaḍā that makes it valid. Mark: the stated thing becomes valid by it and is not altered when it appears; unlike the maḥdhūf, where the sentence loses its previous form. Objections (ḍarabnā bi-ʿaṣāka al-ḥajar; baʿ ʿabdaka ʿannī) show the line is thin, so some say muqtaḍā is sharʿī and maḥdhūf is lughawī. Examples: 'aʿtiq ʿabdaka ʿannī bi-alf', 'ghusl with water' vs dalāla; no ʿumūm in the muqtaḍā (so 'in akaltu' with niyyat ṭaʿām min ṭaʿām is not accepted); 'anti ṭāliq' with niyya of three is not valid (but 'anti bāʾin' and 'ṭalliqī nafsaki' are).
What is iqtiḍāʾ al-naṣṣ?
وأما الثابت باقتضاء النص فما لا يعمل النص إلا بشرط تقدمه
What the naṣṣ cannot be put into effect without something coming before it: that previous thing (the muqtaḍā) is required by the text for the soundness of what it says, and the ruling becomes attached to the text through that muqtaḍā. The wording of the matn has two readings: the thābit bi-iqtiḍāʾ is the muqtaḍā itself (ism al-mafʿūl), or it is the ruling established by it. The sharḥ prefers the first with 'taqaddumih' (idāfa) and notes the other reading.tap to reveal
What is the mark of the muqtaḍā and how does it differ from the maḥdhūf?
وعلامته أن يصح به المذكور ولا يلغى عند ظهوره بخلاف المحذوف
The mark of the muqtaḍā: the stated is made sound by it, and when it is made explicit the rest of the sentence is not changed in form or meaning. E.g. 'in akaltu fa-ʿabdī ḥurr' with the muqtaḍā 'ṭaʿāman' added: 'in akaltu ṭaʿāman' changes nothing. The maḥdhūf is not so: the sentence changes its form when it is added, as in 'wa-sʾal al-qarya': when 'ahl' is made explicit, 'al-qarya' changes from naṣb to jarr.tap to reveal
How strong is the ruling by iqtiḍāʾ and how does it compare with dalāla?
والثابت منه كالثابت بدلالة النص إلا عند المعارضة
Like dalāla it is qaṭʿī, and they are equal in establishing the ruling, but in a clash dalāla is stronger. Iqtiḍāʾ is by sharʿ for need only (ḍarūrī), dalāla by language (established from every side).tap to reveal

Al-wujūh al-fāsida (1): mafhūm al-laqab (naming a thing does not negate the others)

After the four ways of reaching the intended meaning, Mullā Jīwan adds a chapter on 'false' ways (wujūh fāsida) that some (Shāfiʿī-leaning uṣūlīs, some Ashʿarīs and Ḥanbalīs) use to derive rulings, and that the Ḥanafīs reject. First: a ruling attached to a name (ʿalam or jins-noun, 'laqab') does not negate it for others. The sharḥ sets out mafhūm muwāfaqa vs mukhālafa, the conditions the other side sets, the ḥadīth 'al-māʾ min al-māʾ', and replies that naming merely puts the thing under the ruling.
What are mafhūm muwāfaqa and mukhālafa, and what do the opponents require for mukhālafa?
Muwāfaqa: the silent case is understood to follow the stated ruling (the same as dalālat al-naṣṣ). Mukhālafa: the silent case is understood to have the opposite ruling. Its types: from a name (laqab), from sharṭ or waṣf, from number, ghāya and ḥaṣr. Its proponents require: no clear priority of the silent case over the stated, no conformity to custom, no answer to a question or an event, no use for explanation, praise or blame, and no other benefit. When these are met, the ruling is negated for what is not named.tap to reveal
Does ruling on a name ('ʿalam') negate it for others?
Not for us. The name is the subject (maḥkūm ʿalayhi), a part of the sentence, and is mentioned out of necessity; it is not mentioned to negate others. 'Muḥammad rasūl Allāh' would then imply that no other is a messenger, which is unbelief and falsehood; and 'Zayd is existent' would imply that nobody else exists. The silent case is not covered by the text, so the text neither proves nor negates it: 'Zayd came' is silent about ʿAmr. The benefit of naming is that the mujtahids reflect and extend the ruling by qiyās and reach the rank of ijtihād.tap to reveal
How do the Ḥanafīs answer the use of 'al-māʾ min al-māʾ' to show that no ghusl is due without ejaculation (iksāl)?
The opponents say the Anṣār understood from the ḥadīth 'water is from water' that there is no ghusl for iksāl (penetration without emission), as they were Arabic speakers, so a ruling on 'water' negates others. We answer: the Anṣār's understanding was not by mafhūm of laqab but because the lām in 'al-māʾ' is lām al-istighrāq (when there is no ʿahd): every ghusl in matters of lust comes from 'water'. For us it is so: water (i.e. emission) appears in two ways, either as seen (ʿiyānan) or by indication (dalāla): the meeting of the two circumcised parts stands in for it because it causes emission and the emission is so rare it may go unnoticed; so we required ghusl by precaution. Further reply in the ḥāshiya: the ḥadīth was abrogated (Muḥyī al-Sunna; Abū Dāwūd on Ubayy: it was a concession at the start of Islam).tap to reveal

Al-wujūh al-fāsida (2): mafhūm al-waṣf wa-l-sharṭ and the effect of taʿlīq

Second false way: attaching a ruling to a described subject or to a condition negates it for the rest. Al-Shāfiʿī's examples: nikāḥ of a believing slave woman only when one lacks the means to marry a free woman (Q 4:25). The Ḥanafīs reply with the 'taʿlīq' discussion: the muʿallaq on a sharṭ is not an existing sabab that is prevented from showing its ruling (the Shāfiʿī) but a sabab not yet formed (Ḥanafī), so the lack of the ruling before the sharṭ is a lack of sabab, not a sharʿī negation that can be extended. The famous consequences: taʿlīq al-ṭalāq bi-l-milk (his 'in nakaḥtuki fa-anti ṭāliq' is void) and early takfīr before ḥinth.
What does al-Shāfiʿī infer from a described subject or a sharṭ?
That the ruling is negated where the description or condition is missing. 'Waman lam yastaṭiʿ minkum ṭawlan an yankiḥa al-muḥṣanāt al-muʾmināt fa-min mā malakat aymānukum min fatayātikum al-muʾmināt' (Q 4:25) shows, for him, that nikāḥ of a slave woman is not allowed to one who can afford a free woman (lack of the condition), nor a Kitābī slave woman (lack of the description 'believing'). Our answer: this is a specification of the general 'fa-nkiḥū mā ṭāba lakum' (Q 4:3), which allows nikāḥ of a slave woman with or without ṭawl; to specify the general by mukhālafa (a weaker proof than the manṭūq) is not reasonable, so the ruling for the unmentioned is left to another proof.tap to reveal
What are the two bases of al-Shāfiʿī's view on the effect of sharṭ and waṣf?
(1) The waṣf is attached to the sharṭ: 'anti ṭāliq rākiba' is as 'in kunti rākiba'. Just as ṭalāq depends on the sharṭ, so does the ruling depend on the description. (2) The taʿlīq by sharṭ prevents the ruling, not the sabab: in 'in dakhalti al-dār fa-anti ṭāliq' the sabab 'anti ṭāliq' exists in words, the ruling (falling) is prevented until entry. So the lack of the ruling is a sharʿī lack (not original) and extends to others. Likened to hanging a lantern with a rope: it does not remove the weight (sabab of falling) but prevents the falling.tap to reveal
If a man says to a stranger woman 'in nakaḥtuki fa-anti ṭāliq' (or 'in malaktuka fa-anta ḥurr'), is it valid?
Al-Shāfiʿī: void. The effect of taʿlīq is only to delay the ruling, not to stop the sabab; here the ruling 'ṭalāq' is already impossible without the maḥall (she is not yet his wife), so the taʿlīq does nothing; and the words 'anti ṭāliq' have no sabab effect there. It is as if he had said 'anti ṭāliq' to an alien woman: void. We (Ḥanafīs): taʿlīq is a statement that is valid, becomes sabab only when the sharṭ occurs: when he marries her, it falls (see next). The ḥāshiya (al-Sunbulī): the muʿallaq is a sabab only at the sharṭ, so if the maḥall is found then, ṭalāq falls.tap to reveal

Al-wujūh al-fāsida (3-4): ḥaml al-muṭlaq ʿalā al-muqayyad; al-qirān fī al-naẓm

Third false way: a muṭlaq text is carried onto a muqayyad text (al-Shāfiʿī). The five cases (same or different ḥukm, same or different ḥādītha, plus sabab/sharṭ) show where there is agreement (same ḥukm, same ḥādītha: carried; different ḥukm and ḥādītha: not carried) and where Ḥanafīs differ (the rest). Kaffāra of ẓihār vs qatl (raqaba muʾmina) and fasting three days (tatābuʿ in Ibn Masʿūd's qirāʾa) are the examples. Fourth false way: qirān fī al-naẓm: joining two sentences with wāw means equal ruling (Mālik): so zakāt is not due on the child as ṣalāt is not. Ḥanafīs: wāw joins sentences without sharing, though joining an incomplete sentence to a complete one needs sharing.
What is the rule of carrying muṭlaq onto muqayyad?
When a text is muṭlaq (the thing without a qualifier) and another is muqayyad (with the qualifier), the muṭlaq is carried onto the muqayyad according to al-Shāfiʿī, even if they speak of two events with a single ruling. Reasoning: the muṭlaq is silent and ambiguous, while the muqayyad is explicit; so the muṭlaq is explained by it (al-Qamar disputes that the muṭlaq is silent: it states the ruling of the whole). The qualifier is not just any added description: it is like a sharṭ and it negates the ruling when absent.tap to reveal
What are the five combinations and who carries?
(1) same ḥukm, same ḥādītha: carried (agreed), since muṭlaq and muqayyad cannot both hold: e.g. fasting three days in the kaffāra of oath: 'fa-ṣiyām thalāthat ayyām' with Ibn Masʿūd's reading 'mutatābiʿāt'; also the Prophet's order to the Aʿrābī: 'ṣum shahrayn' and 'shahrayn mutatābiʿayn'. (2) different ḥukm, same ḥādītha: not carried (Ḥanafīs); carried (Shāfiʿī): kaffāra of ẓihār has three rulings: taḥrīr and ṣiyām qualified 'min qabl an yatamāssā' and the feeding not so qualified; the Shāfiʿīs qualify the feeding. (3) one ḥukm, two ḥādithas: not carried (Ḥanafīs); carried (Shāfiʿī): qatl: 'taḥrīr raqaba muʾmina' vs yamīn/ẓihār: 'raqaba'. (4) different ḥukm and ḥādītha: not carried (agreed): tatābuʿ in fasting of qatl and the unqualified feeding of ẓihār. (5) The Ḥanafī-Shāfiʿī dispute in the others is detailed in the long books.tap to reveal
Why do the Ḥanafīs refuse the carrying across two events?

In one ḥukm and one ḥādītha both cannot hold together (two opposite qualities), so the muṭlaq must be carried (and not the reverse).tap to reveal

Al-aḥkām al-mashrūʿa: ʿazīma, its four kinds, and al-farīḍa

Having finished the divisions of the Book, Mullā Jīwan (following Fakhr al-Islām) adds the rulings the Book establishes, though al-Tawḍīḥ places them after qiyās. The mashrūʿāt are ʿazīma (the original ruling, not tied to special circumstances) and rukhṣa. ʿAzīma has four types by two cuts: whether leaving it is punished: farīḍa, wājib; whether its leaver is blamed: sunna, nafl. Farīḍa: what allows no addition or subtraction, established by a proof with no doubt in it; ruling: obligation in knowledge (certain), in heart (taṣdīq), and in body; its denier is a kāfir and its deliberate leaver without excuse a fāsiq.
What is ʿazīma, and what are its four kinds?
ʿAzīma is the name of what is original among the legislated rulings, not tied to circumstances: it is not prescribed because of an excuse (as breaking the fast was prescribed because of illness). Al-wājib, al-sunna and al-nafl (Nūr al-Anwār's additions to Shāshī)

Wājib is what is established by a proof with doubt in it (the specified general, the mujmal, khabar al-wāḥid): its ruling is binding in practice but not in knowledge; its leaver is a fāsiq only if he belittles khabar al-wāḥid, not if he has an interpretation. Sunna: the followed way in religion: asked of the person with no farḍ or wujūb. Two types: sunnat al-hudā (jamāʿa, adhān, iqāma) whose leaver is blamed and who may be fought if a town gives it up (Abū Yūsuf: disciplined); and zawāʾid (the Prophet's dress and sitting), no blame. Al-rukhṣa and its four kinds

Rukhṣa is not defined as one thing; it is divided into four kinds by how the ʿazīma stands: two true rukhṣas (the ʿazīma remains in force) and two figurative (the ʿazīma has fallen): (1) a ḥarām permitted while the sabab and the ḥukm remain (ikrāh to utter kufr, break the fast, destroy another's property; the fear-stricken leaving al-amr bi-l-maʿrūf; the muḥrim forced to hunt; the starving eating another's food): ʿazīma is better, and one dies a martyr. (2) permitted with the sabab but the ḥukm delayed (the traveller's fast): fasting is better unless it weakens. (3) What was lifted from us of previous burdens (iṣr, aghlāl): figurative. (4) What fell but remains legislated generally (qaṣr in travel, lifting ḥurma of khamr/mayta for the muḍṭarr, washing the foot while on the khuff).
Why is rukhṣa not defined once and why are there four kinds?
Because it is not a single concept (not mushtarak maʿnawī): two kinds are true, two figurative, so there is no definition covering all. The division is by what the word is used for: the truer of the true (ʿazīma and its ḥukm both present), the lesser true (ʿazīma present from one side), the fuller figurative (ʿazīma gone, no part left in any matter), the less figurative (ʿazīma remains in other matters).tap to reveal
What is the first and truest rukhṣa?
What is treated as permitted while the cause of the prohibition and its ruling are still present: the ḥarām stands and is not removed; it is only that one is not taken to account. Examples: (1) one forced to utter kufr under threat of death/limb, as long as the heart is at rest in īmān: the ḥurma of kufr exists yet he is excused as his own right (to life) is lost both in form (destroying the building) and in meaning (loss of soul), whereas Allāh's right (belief) is not lost; (2) one forced to break the fast in Ramaḍān; (3) one forced to destroy another's property (the owner's right remains through compensation); (4) a fearful person leaving al-amr bi-l-maʿrūf to an unjust ruler (if disliking it in heart); (5) a muḥrim forced to hunt (he pays jazāʾ); (6) one who eats another's food to save his life (with compensation later).tap to reveal

The asbāb are legal ʿilal to which the rulings are ascribed in appearance, though the real effect belongs to Allāh. The last group of asbāb: ʿuqūbāt and ḥudūd (ascribed to their crimes: qatl, zinā, sariqa) and kaffārāt, whose sabab is something 'dāʾir bayna al-ḥaẓr wa-l-ibāḥa' (a mixture of ḥaẓr and permissibility): because kaffāra is both worship and punishment. The closing rule: ascription (iḍāfa) shows sababiyya because the sabab is what the ruling is attached to and arises through it. A sharṭ is ascribed only figuratively (ṣadaqat al-fiṭr: fiṭr is the sharṭ, the head the sabab; ḥajj: Islam the sharṭ, the Bayt the sabab). This closes the Manār's Book; the Sunna begins with the next bāb.

The very crimes they are ascribed to: killing (qiṣāṣ), zinā (rajm or jald), theft (cutting the hand; 'ḥadd al-sariqa'), drinking khamr and qadhf for their ḥudūd. ʿUqūba is wider than ḥadd, as it covers qiṣāṣ also: ḥadd is a penalty fixed for Allāh's right; qiṣāṣ is mostly the servant's right.tap to reveal
What is the sabab of the kaffārāt, and why is it 'between ḥaẓr and ibāḥa'?
وأمر دائر بين الحظر والإباحة
An act partly forbidden and partly permitted. The kaffāra is both worship (done by fast, freeing, charity) and punishment (an atonement for a forbidden act, that deters), so its sabab must combine the two: pure ḥaẓr would give a punishment only; pure ibāḥa would give a worship only. Examples: killing by mistake (permitted as aiming at game, forbidden for missing caution: freeing a believing slave, then two months' fasting); ḥinth in an oath (breaking an oath is forbidden; the oath itself was needed and allowed); ẓihār (a rebuke that is permitted as discipline, but an evil, false statement, a major sin); deliberately breaking the fast in Ramaḍān (eating is permitted in itself, but it is a crime against the fast). Ascription (iḍāfa): the sabab is what the ruling is ascribed to and attached to (taʿalluquh bihi): without it the ruling does not exist and it recurs when it recurs ('ṣalāt al-Ẓuhr', 'ṣawm Ramaḍān', 'zakāt al-māl').

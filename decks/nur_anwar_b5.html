Taʿrīf al-qiyās: definition (Nūr al-Anwār's refinement of Shāshī)

Volume 2 opens at Bāb al-Qiyās. Mullā Jīwan defines qiyās lughatan as taqdīr and sharʿan as taqdīr al-farʿ bi-l-aṣl fī al-ḥukm wa-l-ʿilla, choosing it because it stays closest to the lexical meaning with the least change. He answers two objections (the definition seems not to cover qiyās between two non-existents, and it seems circular), rejects the definition 'taʿdiyat al-ḥukm' (a ḥukm is an attribute and attributes cannot move), and records the alternative 'ibānat mithl ḥukm…', stressing that qiyās reveals (muẓhir) and does not establish (muthbit), and that what crosses over is the mithl of the ruling, not its ʿayn. Shāshī only said what qiyās is; this section adds the defence of the wording.
How does Mullā Jīwan define sharʿī qiyās and why that wording?
تقدير الفرع بالأصل في الحكم والعلة
Qiyās is taqdīr al-farʿ bi-l-aṣl fī al-ḥukm wa-l-ʿilla. He prefers it because it is nearest to the lexical meaning (taqdīr) with the least alteration (qillat al-taghyīr).tap to reveal
Does qiyās establish a ruling?
القياس مظهر لا مثبت
No: it is muẓhir lā muthbit; the Establisher in truth is Allāh. And what is shown is the mithl al-ḥukm not its ʿayn, otherwise the aṣl would be left without any ruling (bāṭil).tap to reveal
1 · Why?

Why does Mullā Jīwan insist on 'mithl' al-ḥukm?

Because a ḥukm is a waṣf of the maḥall; if the same ruling migrated to the farʿ, the aṣl would be left empty. What exists in the farʿ is a mithl of the aṣl's ruling, produced by the shared ʿilla.explain it in your own words — then tap

Ḥujjiyyat al-qiyās: the deniers, the replies and the proofs (naql and maʿqūl)

Shāshī brought ḥadīth Muʿādh, the Khathʿamiyya and Ibn Masʿūd. Nūr al-Anwār first states who rejects qiyās and their three arguments with the three replies, then gives the two proofs: naql (Ḥashr 2 'fa-ʿtabirū' and ḥadīth Muʿādh) and maʿqūl, where it explains that the verse proves qiyās by dalālat al-naṣṣ (not ʿibārat al-naṣṣ, which was about ittiʿāẓ), and by analogy with taʿdiya in the lexical istiʿāra (by ijmāʿ). The ḥawāshī make clear this is not circular.
What is the naql proof (Ḥashr 2)?
'Fa-ʿtabirū yā ulī al-abṣār': iʿtibār is radd al-shayʾ ilā naẓīrih, as if to say 'make qiyās of the thing on its likeness'. It covers both the qiyās of the later wrongdoers on the earlier punished ones and qiyās of sharʿī furūʿ on uṣūl, because al-ʿibra bi-ʿumūm al-lafẓ lā bi-khuṣūṣ al-sabab (al-Bushrā ḥāshiya).tap to reveal
What does Mullā Jīwan add on ḥadīth Muʿādh?
He notes it is well known; the ḥāshiya says the uṣūlīs treat it as mashhūr and al-Ghazālī says the Umma received it with acceptance. Had qiyās not been a ḥujja the Prophet would have denied it and not praised Allāh for it. It does not contradict 'mā farraṭnā fī al-kitāb min shayʾ' because not finding a ruling does not mean it is not in the Book.tap to reveal

Worked example: the six items of ribā, ḥukm al-naṣṣ, its ʿilla and the extension to rice

The Manār illustrates the whole procedure on the ḥadīth of the six items. The command 'mithlan bi-mithlin' is for wujūb al-taswiya (the order is ṣarf to the ḥāl: bi-shart al-tamāthul), the ʿilla is qadr (kayl/wazn) plus jins, the quality of jawda is dropped by the naṣṣ 'jayyiduhā wa-radīʾuhā sawāʾ', so rice and the rest of the kaylīs/wazanīs take the same ruling even if not edible. The same section gives the Banū Naḍīr episode as the model of taʾammul: expulsion is a punishment like killing, and kufr is its cause.
What does the ḥadīth of the six items enjoin?
The ruling is wujūb al-taswiya (equality in qadr), not wujūb al-bayʿ: the amr's ījāb is directed to the ḥāl, 'sell wheat for wheat in the state of being equal'. Nothing less than half a ṣāʿ has a legal qadr, so a handful for two handfuls is permitted.tap to reveal
What is the ʿilla of the naṣṣ?
Qadr and jins: qadr completes ṣūrī sameness and jins maʿnawī sameness. Jins is the madlūl of 'al-ḥinṭa bi-l-ḥinṭa' and qadr of 'mithlan bi-mithlin' (mentioned in another narration 'kaylan bi-kaylin wa-waznan bi-waznin').tap to reveal
3 · Why?

Why does the Manār choose ribā to illustrate qiyās?

Because the ḥukm (taswiya), the ʿilla (qadr+jins) and the farʿ (rice, etc.) are each visible, so it shows the three things a qiyās needs: a naṣṣ with ḥukm, an ʿilla deduced from it, and a farʿ without a naṣṣ.explain it in your own words — then tap

Is every naṣṣ ma'lūl? Fakhr al-Islam's three requirements and the dissent

Before qiyās can proceed Mullā Jīwan follows Fakhr al-Islam: (1) in principle nuṣūṣ are ma'lūla (the Sharʿ is built on benefit to the servants) though some are qāṣira or purely ta'abbudī; (2) a separate dalīl is needed that this naṣṣ is ma'lūl at present; (3) a dalīl that distinguishes the true ʿilla from other attributes (dalālat al-tamyīz). Once all three are together qiyās is a ḥujja. The ḥāshiya (al-Qamar, al-Sunbulī) says the second requirement is not necessary and that the Companions made qiyās without it, and mentions two other positions (maṣdar al-ṭarad: aṣl is not taʿlīl; or aṣl is taʿlīl without need to distinguish).
Is every naṣṣ ma'lūl?
The aṣl is that nuṣūṣ are ma'lūla by an ʿilla that also exists in the farʿ, because the Sharʿ is built on benefit and repelling harm for the servants. It is possible that a naṣṣ is not ma'lūl at all (ta'abbud: acting by the bare command) or ma'lūl by a qāṣira ʿilla, but this is the exception.tap to reveal
What three things are needed before one can say the qiyās is a ḥujja (Fakhr al-Islam)?
(1) The aṣl in each naṣṣ is that it is ma'lūl; (2) a distinct dalīl that this particular naṣṣ is ma'lūl at present, regardless of the general aṣl (the word 'shāhid' means it can witness for the ruling of the farʿ); (3) a dalīl that separates the true ʿilla from other attributes. When these three are met, qiyās is a ḥujja.tap to reveal
What does the al-Bushrā ḥāshiya say about the second requirement?
Al-Bushrā ḥāshiya: it is not necessary. Once a naṣṣ states a ruling the mujtahid is entitled to try to extract the ʿilla by proof; if he finds it he acts on it, if not he does not. The Companions also made qiyās at the start without proving beforehand that the naṣṣ is ma'lūl. The ḥāshiya names two other opinions (the aṣl in nuṣūṣ is non-taʿlīl; or taʿlīl is the aṣl and no tamyīz is needed except on conflict, which it calls clearly wrong, attributed to aṣḥāb al-ṭard) and says Mullā Jīwan follows Fakhr al-Islam.tap to reveal
4 · Why?

Why does Fakhr al-Islam require a separate dalīl that the naṣṣ is ma'lūl?

Because some nuṣūṣ are known not to be ma'lūl (ta'abbudī), so one cannot assume; the al-Qamar note answers that the proof of the ʿilla itself already shows the naṣṣ is ma'lūl.explain it in your own words — then tap

Shurūṭ al-qiyās (1–2): the aṣl must not be singled out (Khuzayma) nor an exception to qiyās (the forgetful faster)

Mullā Jīwan lists the conditions of qiyās after proving its ḥujjiyya. Shāshī's five (qahqaha, ḥajj with a maḥram, niyya, nabīdh, khamr…) are inherited. Nūr al-Anwār adds the detailed first two conditions: the aṣl must not be exclusive in its ruling by another naṣṣ (the testimony of Khuzayma b. Thābit, which the Prophet ﷺ made equal to that of two men) and it must not be ma'dūl bihi ʿan sunan al-qiyās (the eating of the forgetful person while fasting). On the second it gives the Ḥanafī reasons why the mistaken (khāṭiʾ) and the compelled (mukrah) cannot be analogized to the forgetful, as al-Shāfiʿī did.
What is the second condition and its example?
أطعمك الله وسقاك
The aṣl must not be ma'dūl bihi ʿan sunan al-qiyās. Qiyās would say that eating while fasting breaks the fast since the rukn (imsāk) is lost; but for the forgetful person the Prophet ﷺ said 'innamā aṭʿamaka Allāh wa-saqāk' so he is told to complete his fast. This is against qiyās, therefore no qiyās is made on it.tap to reveal
Why are the khāṭiʾ and the mukrah not analogized to the nāsī?
In the nāsī's case the lack of ʿamd and ikhtiyār is complete and the act is attributed to the Lawgiver (Allāh fed him). The khāṭiʾ remembers the fast but is careless (e.g. water passes the throat during rinsing) and the mukrah is ʿāmid and makes his choice though not rāḍi. There is no shared ʿilla, so both fasts break and qaḍāʾ is due. Shāfiʿī: made them like the forgetful (so no qaḍāʾ).tap to reveal

Shurūṭ al-qiyās (3): taʿdiyat al-ḥukm — shar‘ī, ʿaynuhu, with a nazīr and no naṣṣ in the farʿ

The third condition 'an yataʿaddā al-ḥukm al-sharʿī al-thābit bi-l-naṣṣ biʿaynihi ilā farʿ huwa naẓīr wa-lā naṣṣ fīhi' is one in name but contains four (al-Bushrā ḥāshiya says six) conditions. Mullā Jīwan distributes the examples: no qiyās in language (liwāṭ as zinā; naming every mind-clouding drink khamr), no ta'diya with change (the Dhimmī's ẓihār as the Muslim's), the farʿ must be a nazīr and not lower, and no naṣṣ in the farʿ (raqaba muʾmina in kaffārat al-yamīn and al-ẓihār).
What does the third condition contain?
That the sharʿī ruling established in the aṣl by naṣṣ (Book, Sunna or ijmāʿ) pass on exactly (biʿaynihi, with no change) to a farʿ which is its nazīr (not lower) and where there is no naṣṣ. It is one by name but includes four conditions: ḥukm sharʿī not lughawī; taʿdiya without taghyīr; farʿ a nazīr; no naṣṣ in the farʿ (al-Bushrā ḥāshiya adds that the aṣl's ruling must not itself be a farʿ).tap to reveal

Why must the farʿ have no naṣṣ?

Because if a naṣṣ already rules the farʿ in the opposite direction, qiyās would nullify it; if in the same direction, the naṣṣ makes qiyās redundant (most of our authorities) — though some allowed qiyās there as tanbīh.explain it in your own words — then tap

The fourth condition: the naṣṣ's ruling must stay as before — the ribā 'qalīl' and the sheep-for-value zakāt objections

The fourth condition is that the aṣl's ruling should not change by the taʿlīl. Two famous objections against the Ḥanafī taʿlīl are replied to here: (1) taʿlīl of ribā by qadr and jins specifies 'qalīl' (a handful) from the naṣṣ; (2) taʿlīl of 'fī khamsin min al-ibil shāt' by 'māl ṣāliḥ li-l-ḥawāʾij' cancels the 'sheep' in zakāt. The answer in both is that the change comes by the naṣṣ and its dalāla, not by the taʿlīl, which the ḥāshiya also supports with the verse of rizq and the lām of 'li-l-fuqarāʾ' (lām al-ʿāqiba, not tamlīk).
How does Mullā Jīwan answer the objection that taʿlīl by qadr and jins restricts the qalīl (a handful)?
The naṣṣ is 'lā tabīʿū al-ṭaʿām bi-l-ṭaʿām illā sawāʾan bi-sawāʾ'. Shāfiʿī reads the mustathnā as 'ṭaʿāman musāwiyan' (so everything else, including handful by handful, is forbidden). We read the mustathnā minhu as a general of aḥwāl (sawāʾ, mufāḍala, mujāzafa, all states of the kathīr) and the qalīl is not addressed, so it stays in the original ibāḥa. The restriction to the kathīr is therefore by the naṣṣ together with taʿlīl, not by taʿlīl.tap to reveal
How is the zakāt of a sheep given in value without cancelling 'shāt'?
The naṣṣ made the sheep the measure (ʿiyār) of the amount due. The faqīr's right dropped from the form of the sheep by the naṣṣ: the verses on Allāh's providing the rizq of all (Hūd 6) and the order of taking from the wealthy and giving to the poor. Allāh promised the livelihood of the poor and ordered the wealthy to redeem those promises from the fixed amount; since the poor's needs are many (bread, condiment, wood, clothes) and a sheep cannot meet them all, the order carries an implied permission to exchange the sheep for its value, which is established by necessity of the naṣṣ. The ḥāshiya adds a second reply: paying the value of the named property is itself established in the Sharʿ, so it is the Lawgiver who permitted it.tap to reveal

Rukn al-qiyās: what is the pillar and what is it a sign of

Mullā Jīwan says the rukn of qiyās is 'the meaning that was made a sign (ʿalam) of the ruling of the naṣṣ', i.e. the ʿilla, named a pillar because the whole qiyās revolves on it. The ʿilal of the Sharʿ are amārāt and muʿarrifāt, not mūjibāt. The ḥāshiya explains the two Ḥanafī views: the ʿilla is a sign of the ruling in the farʿ only (the Iraqis, Abū Zayd, Fakhr al-Islam and the two Shaykhs) or of the ruling in both aṣl and farʿ (Samarqand). The four arkān of qiyās are the aṣl, the farʿ, the ʿilla and the ḥukm; the qāʾis is not a rukn.
What is the rukn of qiyās?
ما جعل علما
The meaning (al-maʿnā al-jāmiʿ, called ʿilla) that has been made an ʿalam on the ḥukm of the naṣṣ, whether it was made so by the Qurʾān, Sunna, ijmāʿ or istinbāṭ. It is called rukn because the qiyās revolves around it and has no existence without it.tap to reveal
8 · Why?

Why is the ʿilla called the rukn although the aṣl and farʿ also make up the qiyās?

Because, as the ḥāshiya says, as long as the ʿilla is not realised there is no aṣl, no farʿ and no ruling in qiyās at all.explain it in your own words — then tap

ʿIlla al-qiyās: what may serve as an ʿilla (waṣf, ism, ḥukm; lāzim/ʿāriḍ; jalī/khafī; fard/ʿadad)

Mullā Jīwan, following Fakhr al-Islam, says that the shared meaning may be a waṣf (lāzim like thamaniyya or ʿāriḍ like the bursting of blood; jalī or khafī; single or compound), an ism (the word 'dam'), or a ḥukm (as in the Khathʿamiyya's 'dayn Allāh aḥaqq': the ḥukm of dayn). It may be in the naṣṣ or outside it but thābit bihi. The ḥāshiya objects that the true ʿilla is always a waṣf (the 'dam' example is really the 'infijār'), and says that a composite ʿilla is not an objection to the unity of the ʿilla because ʿilliyya is an iʿtibārī waṣf.

Al-istiḥsān: the dalīl that leaves the clear qiyās, its four kinds and when qiyās outranks it

Nūr al-Anwār's treatment of istiḥsān has no counterpart in Shāshī. Ḥukm al-ijtihād: the mujtahid errs and is right; the four-madhāhib and the taṣwīb debate

The Manār says the ḥukm of ijtihād is 'iṣāba bi-ghalabat al-raʾy', i.e. the mujtahid errs and is right and the right is one in the place of khilāf though not known for certain, so the four madhāhib are recognised. Mullā Jīwan gives the basis, the athar of Ibn Masʿūd on the mufawwiḍa (ijmāʿ that ijtihād may err), then the Muʿtazila claim that every mujtahid is right (ḥaqq muta'addid) and the answer, the Abū Ḥanīfa narration read in the sense of ʿamal, the limit to naqliyyāt not ʿaqliyyāt with the fate of the khāṭiʾ in creed, and the position preferred (musīb ibtidāʾan mukhṭiʾ intihāʾan) with Dāwūd and Sulaymān. The ḥāshiya (al-Qamar) gives several readings of the formula and criticises some.
What is the ḥukm of ijtihād?
Iṣāba bi-ghalabat al-raʾy, not yaqīn: the mujtahid errs and is right. Allāh has in each disputed question one ruling; whoever hits it is right and who misses errs, though the one is not known with certainty. So we say the four madhāhib are ḥaqq. The basis is Ibn Masʿūd's reply on the mufawwiḍa (a wife married with no ṣadāq whose husband died before consummation): 'I give my opinion; if right, it is from Allāh; if wrong, from me and Satan: the mahr of her peers, no less and no more'. It was in front of the Ṣaḥāba without objection, so ijmāʿ that ijtihād may err.tap to reveal
What do the Muʿtazila (and some Ashʿarīs) say and what is the reply?
Every mujtahid is right and the ḥaqq in the place of khilāf is multiple, in Allāh's knowledge too; rejected, for one considers a thing ḥarām and another ḥalāl, how could both be true in reality? The ḥāshiya notes they can say there's no fixed ḥukm before ijtihād and answer that a ḥukm for each person; our reply: contradictory rulings for two persons are impossible in the Sharīʿa of our Prophet who was sent to all and called all to the same rulings, and if a mujtahid changes his ijtihād the first must remain true (contradiction) or be abrogated by ijtihād (not allowed).tap to reveal

No, not the ʿilla mustanbaṭa. If it were permitted, any mujtahid facing a naqḍ could say 'it was a right ʿilla but a mānīʿ prevented the ḥukm', and every mujtahid would be right in istinbāṭ, leading to taṣwīb of all. The ḥāshiya counters that there are many ways of replying to a naqḍ and says that this matter has little fruit; the more apparent of Shāfiʿī's two views is also non-specification.tap to reveal
How does the school answer the case where the ḥukm is absent (e.g. the forgetful faster)?

What are the five mawāniʿ of the ḥukm?

(2) A mānīʿ preventing tamām of the ʿilla: selling another's slave without permission: it is concluded but not complete without the owner's consent. (3) Preventing the start of the ḥukm: khiyār al-sharṭ: the sale is an ʿilla but delays ownership. (4) Preventing tamām of the ḥukm: khiyār al-ruʾya: ownership established but incomplete. (5) Preventing luzūm: khiyār al-ʿayb: the buyer may return it. The first two are not really takhṣīṣ since the ʿilla does not exist, which is why Ṣāḥib al-Tawḍīḥ counts the causes of absent ḥukm as five.tap to reveal

Adab al-munāẓara: defending a qiyās — qawl bi-l-mūjib, the four kinds of mumānaʿa and fasād al-waḍʿ

With the rukn, shart and ḥukm of qiyās finished, Mullā Jīwan turns to dafʿ (repelling a qiyās), the part of uṣūl from which the science of munāẓara was taken. ʿIlal are ṭardiyya (the Shāfiʿī ones, which we repel in a way that forces them to speak of taʾthīr) or muʾaththira (ours, which the Shāfiʿī repels and we reply to). Four ways to repel a ṭardiyya: qawl bi-mūjib al-ʿilla, mumānaʿa, fasād al-waḍʿ and munāqaḍa; for the muʾaththira, after mumānaʿa, only muʿāraḍa remains. This section gives qawl bi-l-mūjib, the four kinds of mumānaʿa (in the waṣf itself, in its ṣalāḥ for the ḥukm, in the ḥukm, in nisbat al-ḥukm ilā al-waṣf) and fasād al-waḍʿ, the strongest, with Shāshī's examples (niyya of Ramaḍān, tathlīth, Islam and farqa) re-used.
What is qawl bi-l-mūjib and how does Ḥanafī reply to it?
It is accepting what the opponent's ʿilla yields while the dispute over the ḥukm remains. The Shāfiʿī argues that Ramaḍān fasting is fard so needs a niyya of taʿyīn. We say we also require taʿyīn, but taʿyīn is of two kinds: by the servant (qaṣdī) and by the Lawgiver ('idhā inslakha Shaʿbān fa-lā ṣawm illā ʿan Ramaḍān'); the first is in qaḍāʾ and kaffāra because their time is open, not in Ramaḍān which is mutaʿayyan like the one alone in a place. The ḥāshiya notes the discipline's people did not mention this: if the opponent says only qaṣdī taʿyīn counts, the qawl bi-l-mūjib becomes a mumānaʿa.tap to reveal
What are the four kinds of mumānaʿa?
By induction: (1) in the waṣf itself ('we do not concede this waṣf exists as you say'); (2) in its ṣalāḥ for the ḥukm while existent ('no athar is shown for it'); (3) in the ḥukm ('we do not concede the ḥukm is as you say'); (4) in the nisbat al-ḥukm ilā al-waṣf ('the ḥukm is attributed to another waṣf'). The ḥāshiya notes the discipline's people treat the first as 'mumānaʿa in the waṣf' and the second as 'in its ṣalāḥ'.tap to reveal
Example of mumānaʿa in the ḥukm and in nisbat al-ḥukm (mash al-raʾs)?

Al-munāqaḍa (naqḍ): takhalluf al-ḥukm ʿan al-waṣf and the four ways of repelling it

The Manār defines munāqaḍa as the ḥukm lagging behind the waṣf claimed to be the ʿilla. Taʾthīr by Book, Sunna and ijmāʿ makes naqḍ and fasād al-waḍʿ unlikely (the ḥāshiya: objections come according to the claim and the proof of athar). Mullā Jīwan gives the Shāfiʿī example (wuḍūʾ and tayammum are both ṭahāra so niyya; naqḍ by washing the garment or body) and the answer by taʾthīr (the ghusl of a garment is a ṭahāra ḥaqīqiyya, ratio found; wuḍūʾ is not ratio-based, so like tayammum it needs niyya). A mustadill who has been hit by naqḍ must answer by four means: dafʿ bi-l-waṣf, bi-l-maʿnā, bi-l-ḥukm, bi-l-gharaḍ, shown with the examples of ḥadath from khurūj al-najis and the sāʾil.
How does Mullā Jīwan answer the naqḍ of washing garments and body on 'wuḍūʾ and tayammum are ṭahāratān, so both need niyya'?
By taʾthīr: ghusl of a garment or body is ṭahāra ḥaqīqiyya, removing an impure thing (maʿqūl: the aim is to remove najāsa) with no ta'abbud. Ghusl in wuḍūʾ is a ṭahāra ḥukmiyya, unreasoned, like tayammum, so niyya is needed as for any ʿibāda. The impurity of the whole body at khurūj of bawl and manī is a ratio (the whole body is impure by them); only for the sake of ḥaraj do the four limbs suffice for bawl; water is ṭahūr by nature, hence no niyya; dirt is mulawwith, so niyya makes it a means.tap to reveal
What are the four ways to repel a naqḍ?

(2) bi-l-maʿnā: the meaning on account of which the waṣf is an ʿilla is absent there: khurūj requires washing that place; with bādin that is not there. (3) bi-l-ḥukm: the ḥukm is present but delayed: for the sāʾil bawl, ḥadath exists but its ḥukm is delayed to the end of the time as ʿafw (this holds for those who allow takhṣīṣ). (4) bi-l-gharaḍ: the aim of the taʿlīl (taswiya with bawl) is achieved: since bawl once it persists is ʿafw during the time, so is blood.tap to reveal

Al-muʿāraḍa (1): qalb — making the ʿilla a ḥukm and the ḥukm the ʿilla, and making the waṣf a witness against the opponent

Muʿāraḍa is establishing a dalīl against the very ḥukm the opponent proved. If the opponent's own dalīl is used it is the first kind (qalb), which is both muʿāraḍa (it proves the opposite) and munāqaḍa (it shows his dalīl does not prove his claim). Mullā Jīwan gives the two forms: qalb al-ʿilla ḥukman wa-l-ḥukm ʿilla (the jald/rajm of the disbelievers), and qalb al-waṣf shāhidan ʿalā al-khaṣm baʿd an kāna shāhidan lahu (the ramaḍān taʿyīn), and notes that a third form, the nafl and shuruʿ example, is weak and merely a form of ʿaks which can serve in tarjīḥ. The ḥāshiya discusses how a mustadill can protect himself by presenting the speech as istidlāl.
What is muʿāraḍa and what are the two kinds?
Iqāmat al-dalīl ʿalā khilāf mā aqāma ʿalayhi al-khaṣm dalīlan. If the same dalīl is used it is the first kind: muʿāraḍa with munāqaḍa = qalb; otherwise it is the second: muʿāraḍa khāliṣa (bi-l-ghayr).tap to reveal
What is qalb al-ʿilla ḥukman wa-l-ḥukm ʿilla (Shāfiʿī: the kuffār)?
Shāfiʿī: the kāfir is a jins whose bikr is flogged a hundred, so his thayyib is stoned like Muslims (Islam is not a condition of iḥṣān). We say Islam is a shart of iḥṣān and the kuffār only get jald, bikr or thayyib. We reverse: the Muslims' flogging of a hundred is not the ʿilla for the stoning of the thayyib; stoning is rather the ʿilla for the flogging there. It is muʿāraḍa (it proves the opposite of his claim) and carries munāqaḍa (his proof cannot be a cause). Ibn al-Malik (in the ḥāshiya) explains the Shāfiʿī argument: a hundred lashes is the limit of the bikr's ḥadd and rajm that of the thayyib's, and where the lesser gift of the blessing demands a hundred, the fuller blessing demands more, which is nothing but rajm.tap to reveal
What is qalb al-waṣf shāhidan ʿalā al-khaṣm?
Like a wall, the outer side is to you and the back to him; if you turn it his face is to you. Shāfiʿī: Ramaḍān fast is a fard so niyyat taʿyīn is needed like qaḍāʾ. We turn the same fardiyya as proof of no need for taʿyīn: since a fast of fard like qaḍāʾ after shuruʿ needs no new taʿyīn once fixed. Difference: qaḍāʾ becomes fixed only after shuruʿ, Ramaḍān is fixed by the Lawgiver before it, so the servant needs no taʿyīn.tap to reveal

Al-muʿāraḍa al-khāliṣa (muʿāraḍa bi-l-ghayr): five kinds against the farʿ's ḥukm, three kinds against the aṣl's ʿilla (mufāraqa)

(B) muʿāraḍa in the ʿilla of the aṣl (mufāraqa) is invalid in all three forms (qāṣira, extended to the agreed, to the disputed farʿ). But 'every sound speech is sound in itself' so the questioner should present it as mumānaʿa (the pledged slave freed by the rāhin).
What are the five kinds of muʿāraḍa in the ḥukm of the farʿ?
(1) Opposite ḥukm with no addition: Shāfiʿī: mash is a rukn so tathlīth sunna like washing; we: mash al-raʾs is mash so no tathlīth like mash al-khuff. (2) Opposite with explanatory addition: 'ruknan fa-lā yusannu tathlīthuh baʿda ikmālih'. (3) Opposite with taghyīr: the orphan girl: we say she is under wilāya for nikāḥ like māl (the wilāya of the father is also over the child); Shāfiʿī: the brother has no wilāya on māl so none on nikāḥ; this nafy is not what we affirmed, since we proved only mutlaq wilāya and he denies the brother's, but it contains muʿāraḍa since no one distinguishes brother from others. (4) Kāfir buys a Muslim slave: we say as he can sell so buy; Shāfiʿī: since ibtidāʾ and baqāʾ of ownership must be equal and he cannot keep it, he cannot begin it: the sale is valid, buying not. Dafʿ al-muʿāraḍa: tarjīḥ — what counts, what does not, and when two tarjīḥāt conflict

When a muʿāraḍa cannot be repelled by mumānaʿa, qalb or the like, only tarjīḥ remains, and if the mustadill cannot give it he is broken off (munqaṭiʿ) and the questioner may counter with another tarjīḥ. Mullā Jīwan defines tarjīḥ as the superiority of one of two equals in waṣf, not in dhāt, so a just witness outweighs a fāsiq but four do not outweigh two, and one qiyās is not preferred over another by a third that agrees. The four recognised wujūh of tarjīḥ are quwwat al-athar (istiḥsān), quwwat al-thabāt ʿalā al-ḥukm (taʿyīn vs fardiyya), kathrat al-uṣūl (mash) and the ʿaks (ṭard + inʿikās). If two kinds of tarjīḥ conflict, rujḥān in the dhāt outranks rujḥān in the ḥāl, shown by the case of the roasted stolen sheep.
What is tarjīḥ and what is not tarjīḥ?
Faḍl aḥad al-mithlayn ʿalā al-ākhar waṣfan: a superiority in the dalīl's own quality, not an extra dhāt. So the testimony of the just outweighs the fāsiq, but not four witnesses over two; one qiyās is not preferred over another by a third which agrees with it; a ḥadīth by a ḥadīth; an āya by another āya. The ḥāshiya adds that some say two ḥadīths backing each other (with tawīl closed) outweigh one, which is really by the strength of the dalīl.tap to reveal
What are the four wujūh of tarjīḥ?
(1) Quwwat al-athar (istiḥsān over qiyās: ṭahārat sūr sibāʿ al-ṭayr); (2) quwwat al-thabāt ʿalā ḥukmih: Ramaḍān's taʿyīn from the Shāriʿ is more ubiquitous than the Shāfiʿī's fardiyya since taʿyīn extends to wadīʿa (any return suffices), ghaṣb and bayʿ fāsid, whereas fardiyya is only in ṣawm and ṣalāt (ḥajj and zakāt need no taʿyīn); (3) kathrat al-uṣūl: our 'mash so no tathlīth' has aṣls (khuff, jabīra, tayammum) against Shāfiʿī's single ghusl; (4) al-ʿaks: ours is muṭṭarid and munʿakis; the other is not (madmada is sunna but not rukn).tap to reveal
When two kinds of tarjīḥ conflict, which prevails, and what is the case?
Rujḥān fī al-dhāt is stronger than in the ḥāl since the ḥāl is attached to the dhāt, and what follows has no appearance against what is followed. Case: a man usurps another's sheep, slaughters, cooks and roasts it: for us the owner's right cuts over to the value and the ghāṣib pays the value, since the ṣanʿa is complete in itself and the ʿayn is perishing from one side; so the ṣanʿa is like the dhāt and the ʿayn like the waṣf. Shāfiʿī: the sheep is the original and the cooking only a waṣf, so the owner takes the meat and the nuqṣān. The ḥāshiya compares it to the nephew of the brother preceding the uncle in ʿaṣūba (rujḥān in dhāt of qarāba vs in ḥāl).tap to reveal

Invalid tarjīḥāt (ghalabat al-ashbāh, ʿumūm, qillat al-awṣāf) and intiqāl al-muʿallil

Three tarjīḥāt are invalid for us: by the number of resemblances (the Shāfiʿī reasoning for the brother), by generality of the waṣf (ṭuʿm rather than qadr and jins) and by fewness of awṣāf (ṭuʿm alone or thamaniyya alone), because tarjīḥ is by taʾthīr; one with two parts can be stronger than one with a single part. After this Mullā Jīwan takes the intiqāl (moving from one ʿilla to another): four kinds, three sound and the fourth (new ʿilla for the same ḥukm) not sound since ʿilal are endless, then deals with the Ibrāhīm–Namrūd objection and closes the Bāb al-Qiyās with a transition to Faṣl fī al-Aḥkām.
Why is tarjīḥ by ghalabat al-ashbāh, ʿumūm or qillat al-awṣāf invalid, and what is the Shāfiʿī example?
Tarjīḥ is by the strength of the effect, not by the number of resemblances (each is as an ʿilla; many ʿilal outweigh one by quantity only, which is the invalid tarjīḥ of one qiyās by another), nor by generality (ṭuʿm covers a handful and a kayl but qadr+jins only the latter; we do not accept that ʿumūm outweighs khuṣūṣ since the Shāfiʿī himself prefers the khāṣṣ naṣṣ to the ʿāmm), nor by fewness (a ʿilla of two parts can be stronger than one of one part). After the Book, Sunna, ijmāʿ and qiyās the Manār turns to what they establish: al-aḥkām (the maḥkūm bihi, i.e. the acts of the mukallaf) and what the aḥkām depend on (sabab, ʿilla, sharṭ, ʿalāma). The ḥāshiya notes the old division is loose: the aḥkām are the attributes of the mukallaf's act (wujūb, ḥurma, fardiyya, ʿazīma…), already treated after the Book. Mullā Jīwan gives four classes (ḥuqūq Allāh khāliṣa; ḥuqūq al-ʿibād khāliṣa; both with Allāh's right dominant, e.g. ḥadd al-qadhf; both with the servant's dominant, e.g. qiṣāṣ), then the eight kinds of ḥuqūq Allāh: ʿibādāt khāliṣa (asl, laḥiq, zāʾid), ʿuqūbāt kāmila, ʿuqūbāt qāṣira, ḥuqūq dāʾira (kaffārāt), ʿibāda fīhā maʿnā al-muʾna (ṣadaqat al-fiṭr), muʾna fīhā maʿnā al-ʿibāda (ʿushr), muʾna fīhā maʿnā al-ʿuqūba (kharāj) and ḥaqq qāʾim bi-nafsih (khums).
What are the four classes of ḥukm in terms of ḥaqq Allāh and ḥaqq al-ʿabd?
(1) ḥuqūq Allāh khāliṣa: its benefit is universal (the sanctity of the Bayt, the ḥurma of zinā), ascribed to Allāh out of respect; (2) ḥuqūq al-ʿibād khāliṣa: with particular maṣlaḥa such as the property of another (it can be permitted by the owner's permission, unlike zinā); (3) both, with Allāh's right dominant, e.g. ḥadd al-qadhf (a jazāʾ for violating the honour of the chaste, which also removes the shame of the slandered), so no inheritance or ʿafw applies, whereas Shāfiʿī: the servant's right is dominant so they apply; (4) both with the servant's right dominant: qiṣāṣ (Allāh's right is to purge the world of corruption; the servant's is the assault on his own soul), so inheritance, composition and pardon apply.tap to reveal

Al-aṣl and al-khalaf: īmān, tayammum, the imām mutayammim and the conditions of khilāfa

Ḥuqūq Allāh and ḥuqūq al-ʿibād are each divisible into aṣl and khalaf (a substitute which stands when the aṣl is impossible). Īmān's aṣl is taṣdīq and iqrār together, but iqrār alone is khalaf in ḥukm al-dunyā; the parent's ada' stands for the child's; tabʿiyyat al-dār comes after. Ṭahāra by water has tayammum as khalaf; the khalaf is mutlaq for us (it removes ḥadath), ḍarūrī for Shāfiʿī; and between the water and dust the khilāfa is (Abū Ḥanīfa and Abū Yūsuf) between muʾaththirayn, whereas for Muḥammad and Zufar it is between wuḍūʾ and tayammum, hence imāmat al-mutayammim. A khalaf is established only by naṣṣ or dalālatuh, never by raʾy, and requires that the aṣl be absent in the ḥāl but possible, which is why no kaffāra is due in yamīn al-ghamūs but is due in the oath to touch the sky.
What is īmān's aṣl and khalaf in ḥukm?
The aṣl of īmān is taṣdīq and iqrār together (as in Abū Ḥanīfa's Fiqh Akbar); the iqrār alone becomes the khalaf of the taṣdīq in the ḥukm of the dunyā (a compelled person's iqrār stands for both so his blood and property are protected, he is prayed over); the child's īmān is stood in by one parent's ada': he inherits, is prayed over etc.; then tabaʿiyyat al-dār for a captive child without parents. These are not khalaf ʿan khalaf: each is a khalaf of the child's own adāʾ, the second following the first.tap to reveal
What is the status of tayammum for us and for Shāfiʿī?
For us tayammum is a mutlaq khalaf: it removes the ḥadath until water is found and makes ṣalāt permissible, supported by 'jiʿilat lī al-arḍ masjidan wa-ṭahūran'. Shāfiʿī: ḍarūrī (it does not remove the ḥadath originally but makes ṣalāt permissible out of need), so two obligatory prayers may not be prayed with one tayammum, and one must repeat it for each obligatory prayer, since need is measured by its measure. We say the dust is a purifier when water is unavailable, not a polluter.tap to reveal
Is the khilāfa between the water and dust or between wuḍūʾ and tayammum, and what follows?
Abū Ḥanīfa and Abū Yūsuf: between muʾaththirayn, water and dust (Nisāʾ 43: 'fa-lam tajidū māʾan fa-tayammamū'). Muḥammad and Zufar: between wuḍūʾ and tayammum, the two products. Result: imāmat al-mutayammim li-l-mutawaḍḍiʾīn is jāʾiz for the Shaykhayn (the two are equal in removing ḥadath, provided the mutawaḍḍiʾ does not find water, as the ḥāshiya adds), but not for Muḥammad and Zufar, since the mutayammim as a khalaf is weaker (building the strong on the weak is not valid). The ḥāshiya adds that most books ascribe to Zufar the permission when the mutawaḍḍiʾ finds water.tap to reveal

Al-sabab and its kinds: haqīqī, sabab fīhi maʿnā al-ʿilla, majāzī (yamīn, taʿlīq) and the Ḥanafī, Shāfiʿī and Zufar positions

The second thing al-aḥkām depend on is a fourfold set (sabab, ʿilla, sharṭ, ʿalāma), by istiqrāʾ. Shāshī separated sabab from ʿilla (idāfa, dalāla, sikkīn, siyāq). Nūr al-Anwār defines the sabab ḥaqīqī as a tarīq mufḍin ilā al-ḥukm without attributing wujūb or wujūd to it and without the sense of ʿilla (e.g. a man pointing to another's property or person for theft or killing: no ḍamān on him since the intervening ʿilla, the doer's free choice, is not his; the mutaʾakhkhirūn gave ḍamān in sayāʿa to a tyrant for the corruption of the age), the sabab fīhi maʿnā al-ʿilla (sāʾiq/qāʾid of a beast: ʿilla al-ʿilla: diya/value are due but no kaffāra, qiṣāṣ or inheritance loss) and the sabab majāzī (yamīn bi-llāh, yamīn bi-l-ṭalāq wa-l-ʿitāq): since the birr is the aim and prevents ḥinth. Shāfiʿī calls it ḥaqīqī in the ḥāl (ifrāṭ), Zufar a pure majāz (tafrīṭ) and we hold the middle: it has shubhat al-ḥaqīqa, so tanjīz cancels the taʿlīq.
What are the four things on which aḥkām depend and why four?
Sabab, ʿilla, sharṭ, ʿalāma by istiqrāʾ. The ḥāshiya gives a proof: if it is mūʾaththir in the ḥukm with its wujūd apparent, it is ʿilla; if not, then if the ḥukm exists with it, it is sharṭ; if not, then if it is a sign of the ḥukm's wujūd it is ʿalāma; otherwise sabab.tap to reveal
What is the exact definition of sabab ḥaqīqī and what do its qayds exclude?
Ṭarīq mufḍin ilā al-ḥukm fī al-jumla, without wujūb being attributed to it (excluding ʿilla), nor wujūd (excluding sharṭ), nor any maʿnā al-ʿilla in it (excluding the sabab with shubhat al-ʿilla); ṭarīq excludes ʿalāma (which is only a sign on the road). If a ʿilla were attributed to it, the ḥukm would be attributed to that ʿilla, making the sabab ʿilla al-ʿilla.tap to reveal

The later jurists nevertheless ruled ḍamān on the informer to a tyrant, to protect rights from the plague of informers. Al-ʿilla al-sharʿiyya and its seven kinds (ism, maʿnā, ḥukm), with the examples of Mullā Jīwan

The third thing on which aḥkām depend is the ʿilla: what has the wujūb of the ḥukm attributed to it directly (without wāsiṭa), both the waḍʿiyya (bayʿ, nikāḥ) and the mustanbaṭa. A true sharʿī ʿilla is complete by three attributes: ism (it is laid down for the ḥukm and the ḥukm is attributed to it directly), maʿnā (it is effective) and ḥukm (the ḥukm comes without delay). By the logical division there are seven kinds; Mullā Jīwan gives seven examples: (1) ism, maʿnā and ḥukm: absolute bayʿ for ownership; (2) ism only: the muʿallaq ījāb; (3) ism and maʿnā but not ḥukm: bayʿ with khiyār, bayʿ mawqūf, ījāb muḍāf, niṣāb before ḥawl, ʿaqd al-ijāra; (4) ʿilla in the sphere of asbāb: purchase of a relative, maraḍ al-mawt, tazkiya; (5) one of two parts of an ʿilla (qadr or jins): shubhat al-ʿilla; (6) maʿnā and ḥukm without ism: the last part of a compound ʿilla (qarāba + milk); (7) ism and ḥukm without maʿnā: safar for rukhṣa, nawm for ḥadath.
What is the sharʿī ʿilla according to Mullā Jīwan?
Mā yuḍāf ilayhi wujūb al-ḥukm ibtidāʾan (without a wāsiṭa). This excludes the sabab, ʿalāma and ʿilla al-ʿilla. It covers ʿilal waḍʿiyya (bayʿ, nikāḥ) and the mustanbaṭa by ijtihād.tap to reveal
What is the first kind (complete)?
Absolute (without khiyār) bayʿ is an ʿilla ismān: it is laid down for the milk; maʿnā: it is effective in it and was legislated for it; ḥukman: ownership occurs at once. Likewise nikāḥ is ʿilla for ḥill and qatl for qiṣāṣ.tap to reveal
What is the second kind (ism only)?
The ījāb muʿallaq bi-sharṭ, as 'anta ṭāliq in dakhalti al-dār': 'anta ṭāliq' is an ʿilla ismān for the ṭalāq (laid down for it, and the ḥukm is attributed to it when the shart is found), not ḥukman (the ḥukm is delayed to the shart) and not maʿnā (no effect before the shart). Al-sharṭ (five kinds) and al-ʿalāma: dukhūl al-dār, ḥafr al-biʾr, ḥall al-qayd, the two shurūṭ, and iḥṣān

The third and fourth things aḥkām depend on. Sharṭ is what the wujūd (not wujūb) of the ḥukm depends on and is external to its nature. Mullā Jīwan's five kinds: (1) sharṭ maḥḍ (dukhūl al-dār), (2) a sharṭ in the ḥukm of the ʿilla (ḥafr al-biʾr, shaqq al-ziqq: the ʿilla is a natural thing, so the ḥukm is attributed to the sharṭ), (3) a sharṭ with the ḥukm of the asbāb (ḥall qayd al-ʿabd, with the disagreement over fatḥ bāb al-qafaṣ), (4) a sharṭ ismān wa-ḥukman: the last of two shurūṭ (dukhūl al-dārayn), and (5) sharṭ like ʿalāma khāliṣa (iḥṣān for rajm). The sharṭ is known by ṣīgha (words of condition) or dalāla (e.g. al-marʾa allatī atazawwajuhā ṭāliq), and ʿalāma is what indicates the wujūd without wujūb or wujūd attached; the ḥāshiya explains the ḍamān of shuhūd al-sharṭ against shuhūd al-iḥṣān.
What is the sharṭ and what is it different from?
Mā yataʿallaq bihi al-wujūd dūn al-wujūb: so it is different from ʿilla (wujūb is attributed to it), from sabab (it leads to the ḥukm) and from the juzʾ (the ḥāshiya adds 'and external to its mahiyya'). Faṣl fī bayān al-ahliyya: ʿaql, the Ashʿarī/Muʿtazilī/Māturīdī positions, and ahliyyat al-wujūb and al-adāʾ with the six classes of acts

Having finished the things on which aḥkām depend (sabab, ʿilla, sharṭ, ʿalāma) the book moves to the ahliyya of the maḥkūm ʿalayh, the mukallaf. ʿAql comes first since there is no ahliyya without it. The Sharʿ replaced the exact moment of ʿaql's balance by bulūgh. The positions: Ashʿarīs (no ʿibra for ʿaql in knowing sharʿī aḥkām: only samʿ), Muʿtazila (ʿaql is a mūjiba ʿilla for the good and a muḥarrima for the evil) and the Māturīdī/Ḥanafī (ʿaql is muʿarrif, not mūjib; the one the daʿwa has not reached is not bound by bare ʿaql, but if he had time to reflect and did not, he is not excused). Ahliyya is of two kinds: wujūb (built on the dhimma, from the covenant of alast) and adāʾ (qāṣira then kāmila, built on ʿaql and body). The six classes of acts of the ṣabī follow (īmān, kufr, ʿibādāt, nafʿ maḥḍ, ḍarar maḥḍ, dāʾir).
What is the role of ʿaql in ahliyya?
ʿAql is considered for establishing ahliyya because the khiṭāb is not understood without it, and addressing one who does not understand is ugly. Since its exact balance is hidden, the Lawgiver put bulūgh (the age when ʿaql is generally balanced) in its place; ʿaql differs in strength among people (prophets, awliyāʾ, ʿulamāʾ, ḥukamāʾ, then the common).tap to reveal
What do the Ashʿarīs and the Muʿtazila say of ʿaql and how do the Ḥanafīs answer?
Ashʿarīs: in the knowledge of sharʿī rulings only samʿ matters, so there is no accountability before the Message ('wa-mā kunnā muʿadhdhibīn ḥattā nabʿatha rasūlan', Isrāʾ 15), and the beliefs the intellect finds good need not be accepted. The Muʿtazila: ʿaql is an ʿilla mūjiba for what it finds good (shukr al-munʿim) and muḥarrima for the ugly; the ʿaql is above the sharʿī ʿilal since they are only signs, so they deny what the ʿaql cannot imagine (ruʾya, ʿadhāb al-qabr, mīzān, ṣirāṭ), arguing that Ibrāhīm said 'I see you and your people in clear error' before any wahy. Is one whom the daʿwa has not reached excused?

Ashʿarīs: excused whether he was heedless until he died or held shirk since the daʿwa did not reach him; so if one kills him he owes diya (his kufr is pardoned); for us no ḍamān, though killing before the daʿwa is ḥarām.tap to reveal

Al-ʿawāriḍ al-samāwiyya (1): ṣighar, junūn, ʿatah, nisyān, nawm and ighmāʾ

Al-umūr al-muʿtariḍa ʿalā al-ahliyya are of two kinds: samāwī (eleven: ṣighar, junūn, ʿatah, nisyān, nawm, ighmāʾ, riqq, maraḍ, ḥayḍ, nifās, mawt) and muktasab (seven: jahl, sukr, hazl, safah, safar, khaṭaʾ, ikrāh). The ṣighar is in its first state like junūn, therefore the child is not subject to wilāya of his own and his guardian's faith counts; it is ʿudhr so what could be dropped of the ʿuhda from a baligh is dropped, but the farḍiyyat al-īmān is not dropped and a child who kills his muwarrith is not deprived of inheritance (kufr and riqq only because of loss of ahliyya). Junūn removes the ʿibādāt (and ḍamān holds); if it is not prolonged it is like sleep and qaḍāʾ is due; the limit of prolongation is by prayer (more than day and night; Muḥammad: six prayers) or by hours (the Shaykhayn), the whole of Ramaḍān for fasting, most of the year for zakāt; ʿatah is like a ṣabī with ʿaql. Nisyān is excused only where it is ghālib and no reminder exists; sleep is ʿajz and postpones the khiṭāb; ighmāʾ is stronger than sleep.
What does ṣighar do to the ahliyya?
In its first state it is like junūn (the child cannot yet perform), so his īmān is by tabaʿiyya and he is not offered Islām: it is delayed until he reasons. It is ʿudhr: what could be forgiven from a bāligh of the ʿuhda (ʿibādāt, ḥudūd, kaffārāt) is dropped. But the farḍiyyat al-īmān is not dropped, since it does not accept dropping: so if performed it is farḍ and brings all its rulings (separation from the idolatrous wife, inheritance cut from the infidel). In short: the ʿuhda of whatever can be pardoned falls, and he may do whatever carries no ʿuhda (accepting a gift or ṣadaqa and the like).tap to reveal
Does a child who kills his muwarrith lose the inheritance?
No (for us): the deprivation is an ʿuqūba and a ʿuhda which a child does not deserve, so he is as one whose muwarrith died naturally. Shāfiʿī: yes. The objection that kufr and riqq also deprive is answered: those deprive not as jazāʾ but because of the absence of ahliyya of inheritance: riqq is opposed to milk, and kufr to the wilāya over a Muslim.tap to reveal
What is the ruling of junūn?

If it does not extend, it is like sleep and the ʿibādāt are due by qaḍāʾ; and it is not considered when short, to avoid ḥaraj. For the ʿāriḍī (after bulūgh with ʿaql) it is like sleep; for the aṣlī (from childhood) Abū Yūsuf: like ṣabā (even a short period drops), Muḥammad: like ʿāriḍī (or the opposite, another view, as the ḥāshiya notes).tap to reveal

Al-riqq: ʿajz ḥukmī, jazāʾ al-kufr, what it removes (milk al-māl, kamāl al-ḥāl) and what it leaves (ʿiṣma, dam, nikāḥ)

Riqq is a ʿajz ḥukmī (by the Sharʿ), a recompense for kufr: since the kuffār refused to serve Allāh He made them servants of His servants; in its beginning only; once established it remains as a ḥukmī matter even if the person becomes Muslim. It is a quality that is not divisible (it is Allāh's right), though milk is, and Abū Ḥanīfa differs from the two on the divisibility of iʿtāq. It negates milk al-māl (the slave and the mukātab as to the raqaba own nothing), hence ḥajj al-Islām does not fulfil a fard for him, but leaves milk al-nikāḥ (subject to the owner's permission) and milk al-dam, and it negates the kamāl al-ḥāl: the dhimma is naqiṣa, no wilāya, half the number of wives. It does not affect ʿiṣmat al-dam (hence the free is killed for a slave, Shāfiʿī dissenting), and the ʿabd ma'dhūn's amān and iqrār are valid.
Is riqq/ʿitq divisible?
Riqq is not (it is Allāh's right, a quality of kufr that cannot be divided in thubūt or zawāl), so a man cannot be partly slave and partly free; milk is divisible (two men may own one slave). ʿItq: the Shaykhayn say it is a ḥukmī power opposed to the ḥukmī weakness of riqq and so cannot be divided either, hence freeing part frees the whole (otherwise the athar, ʿitq, would be without the muʾaththir, iʿtāq, or the reverse, or ʿitq itself would be divided, which is agreed to be impossible). Abū Ḥanīfa: iʿtāq is izālat al-milk, which is divisible, and the muʿtiq acts only in what is purely his, his milk, not in riqq or ʿitq (Allāh's right): freeing part removes his milk in that part and leaves the rest ruined for ownership, like a mukātab (al-Bushrā ḥāshiya).tap to reveal
What does riqq negate of ownership?
It negates mālikiyyat al-māl: the slave's milk is his owner's, the mukātab owns nothing in his raqaba, the māʾdhūn owns nothing. The ḥāshiya notes that the usual proof (one cannot be both owner and owned in one respect) is contested and that the Taḥqīq prefers to rest the ruling on ijmāʿ. It does not negate mālikiyyat ghayr al-māl (nikāḥ, dam), since the slave needs the sexual satisfaction (farḍ) and the only legitimate way is nikāḥ, which is with his owner's permission; and he is owner of his dam as it is needed for life.tap to reveal

Al-maraḍ, al-ḥayḍ wa-l-nifās and al-mawt: what they remove and what remains

The last four samāwī ʿawāriḍ. Maraḍ is a state of the body in which the balance of the temperament is lost; it does not negate ahliyya (ṣiḥḥat nikāḥ and ṭalāq) but is a ʿajz in power so it lightens (sitting prayer, lying prayer), and since it is a sabab of death it attaches the rights of the heirs and creditors to the property; the ḥajr appears only if the death follows, retroactively from the start of the illness (hence hiba and waṣiyya are valid at once and revoked as needed; a ʿitq becomes saʿāya if the property is insufficient). Ḥayḍ and nifās do not negate ahliyya of wujūb or adāʾ: ṭahāra from them is a condition for prayer and fasting, so the adāʾ is lost; qaḍāʾ of fast but not of prayer by naṣṣ and for ḥaraj. Mawt is a total ʿajz, so takālīf cease; ʿibādāt (zakāt, ḥajj) lapse; only the sin remains; his rights: tajhīz, debts, waṣāyā (third), mīrāth; and the grave is as the cradle: he lives in the hereafter.
What is the effect of maraḍ on the ahliyya and the property?
It does not negate ahliyya: his nikāḥ, ṭalāq and ʿibādāt are valid but made lighter (sitting if he cannot stand, lying if he cannot sit). As a sabab of death, it attaches the right of the creditors to the whole property and of the heirs to a third beyond it, so he is restrained in the amount of the debt and the heirs' share, but only if death follows from that illness: then he is deemed restrained from the beginning of the illness (the illness that kills is the cause of the ḥajr, not any illness). A nikāḥ with mahr al-mithl is valid (necessities: they have a right only in what remains); hiba and waṣiyya are effective at once as nobody is yet harmed and are cancelled to the extent required if he dies.tap to reveal
What happens to a sick person's ʿitq?
If the property is enough for the debts or the slave is within a third, the ʿitq takes effect at once. Otherwise he is free but works to pay his value to the creditors and heirs (saʿāya). Unlike the rāhin who frees the pledged slave: that is effective because the murtahin's right is only in the yad (hand), not the raqaba, and the validity of ʿitq is built on the milk al-raqaba which remains his.tap to reveal
What do ḥayḍ and nifās do?
They do not remove ahliyyat al-wujūb or al-adāʾ (dhimma, discernment, bodily ability remain), so in principle prayer and fast should not drop; but ṭahāra from them is a condition of ṣalāt and an established condition for ṣawm (naṣṣ): when the condition is lost the adāʾ is lost, so no wujūb remains and no qaḍāʾ (for ṣalāt). The naṣṣ forbids fasting during ḥayḍ and nifās, and qaḍāʾ is due for ṣawm by naṣṣ ('we were ordered to make up the fast and not the prayer', ʿĀʾisha), not extended to prayer. Al-umūr al-muktasaba (1): jahl (its kinds and when it is an excuse) and sukr

The muktasaba are what the servant has a hand in bringing about. Jahl, though original in man, is counted among them because he could remove it by learning. It has three groups by effect: (1) bāṭil, no excuse in the hereafter (the kāfir after clear proofs; the follower of hawā in the sifāt and the aḥkām of the hereafter; the rebel against the true Imām; one who contradicts Book or mashhūr Sunna in his ijtihād, e.g. Shāfiʿī on matrūk al-tasmiya ʿamdan, Dāwūd on selling umm walad); (2) in a place of valid ijtihād or shubha: excuse and a shubha that wards off ḥadd and kaffāra (the cupped man, the father's slave girl); (3) the Muslim in dār al-ḥarb who did not emigrate (no qaḍāʾ of prayer and fast), the shafīʿ who does not know of the sale, the married slave girl who is freed, the virgin married by a non-father walī, the wakīl and māʾdhūn who do not know of the authorisation or its withdrawal. Sukr from a permitted cause is like ighmāʾ; from a forbidden cause it does not negate the khiṭāb, and its words and acts bind except ridda and iqrār of the pure ḥudūd of Allāh.
What kinds of jahl are not excuses?
(1) The kāfir's after the evidences of tawḥīd and risāla are clear: no excuse in the hereafter, though in the dunyā it wards off killing if he accepts the dhimma; (2) the ṣāḥib hawā in Allāh's attributes and the rulings of the hereafter (the Muʿtazila, in denying the ṣifāt, ʿadhāb al-qabr, ruʾya and shafāʿa): he is not a kāfir but a fāsiq/innovator, and we argue with him; (3) the bāghī against the true Imām who relies on a false proof: if without manaʿa he guarantees the property and lives of the just; with manaʿa he is not seized after tawba, as the ahl al-ḥarb after Islam; (4) one whose ijtihād contradicts the Book or a mashhūr Sunna: Shāfiʿī on the permission of the meat slaughtered with tasmiya deliberately omitted (against 'wa-lā taʾkulū mimmā lam yudhkar ismu Allāh ʿalayhi'); Dāwūd al-Ẓāhirī on selling umm walad (against 'umm walad hiya muʿtaqa ʿan dubur minhu'); Shāfiʿī on judging by one witness and an oath (against 'al-bayyina ʿalā al-muddaʿī wa-l-yamīn ʿalā man ankar'). The ḥāshiya remarks that the examples are not all strictly against a qaṭʿī text and that the author's phrase on predecessors is only transmitted as they said it.tap to reveal
When is jahl an excuse and a shubha?
In a place of sound ijtihād or shubha: it is an excuse and a shubha warding off ḥadd and kaffāra. The cupped fasting man who then ate deliberately believing cupping breaks the fast: no kaffāra, since al-Awzāʿī holds 'afṭara al-ḥājim wa-l-maḥjūm'. Shaykh al-Islām (Khwāhir Zāda): only if he asked a jurist, or the ḥadīth did not reach him; if he did not ask a faqīh and no ḥadīth reached him, the kaffāra is due as his supposition was in the wrong place. The father who has intercourse with his son's slave girl: no ḥadd (the shubha of 'anta wa-mālu-ka li-abīka' gives him a milk ḥukmī in her before the act; he pays her price, the child's nasab holds) whether he thought it lawful or not; for the brother's slave girl ḥadd falls as the properties are separate.tap to reveal
Which ignorances are excuses in practice?
(1) A Muslim in dār al-ḥarb who has not emigrated: if he did not pray or fast for a period before the call reached him, no qaḍāʾ, since dār al-ḥarb is not a place where the rulings of Islam are well known; not so a dhimmī who becomes Muslim in dār al-Islām, who may ask. (2) The shafīʿ who does not know of the sale: his silence is an excuse and does not cancel shufʿa; after he knows it does. (3) The married slave girl who is freed and does not know of the freedom or of her option. (4) The virgin whom a walī other than father/grandfather married and who knows not: the option on bulūgh remains until she learns, but ignorance of the Sharīʿa after learning of the nikāḥ is no excuse since the land is dār al-Islām. Al-hazl: taʿrīf, shart, and the eight situations of mawāḍaʿa in sale, nikāḥ, khulʿ and iqrār

Hazl is using a word without intending its real or its metaphorical meaning (la'ib maḥḍ); it is distinct from taljiʾa (pretending under pressure). It does not negate ahliyya but it negates riḍā with the ḥukm (hence it spoils a sale, while the khiyār al-sharṭ does not), and it must be explicit in speech before the contract, but need not be repeated in the contract. Mullā Jīwan gives the cases: hazl in the sale (aṣl al-bayʿ, qadr al-thaman, jins al-thaman), each with four cases: they agree on bināʾ (building on the mawāḍaʿa), iʿrāḍ (turning away from it), neither, or they differ. The nikāḥ, ṭalāq, ʿitāq, yamīn, khulʿ, ṣulḥ and ibrāʾ are treated separately, with the ḥadīth 'thalāth jiddahunna jidd'. Ridda by hazl is kufr.
What is hazl and what is its general effect?
Hazl is that the lafẓ is used for neither its real meaning nor its metaphorical one: laʿib maḥḍ. It does not affect ahliyya since the mind and body are sound; it negates the speaker's riḍā with the ḥukm, though not his ikhtiyār (he chose the sabab): so it spoils those contracts that need riḍā and are open to faskh (sale), but makes no difference to those that do not (ṭalāq, ʿitāq, nikāḥ). It must be explicit by tongue before the ʿaqd (his statements are binding, dalālat al-ḥāl is not enough), but not repeated in the ʿaqd as the khiyār must be, since the hazl's aim (that people think it a sale) would be lost by mentioning it; the khiyār's aim (to tell them it is not definitive) is realised only by mentioning it.tap to reveal
What is the ruling if they agreed to hazl in the very sale?
If they then agree to build on that mawāḍaʿa, the sale is fāsid and gives no milk (hazl is stronger than khiyār al-sharṭ, which only delays milk in a ṣaḥīḥ sale; and if they agree on iʿrāḍ the sale is ṣaḥīḥ and lāzim; if neither occurred to them or they differ (one says bināʾ, one iʿrāḍ), Abū Ḥanīfa: sound (ṣiḥḥa is the aṣl in contracts, so it prevails unless a mughayyir exists; the claimant of iʿrāḍ relies on the aṣl); the Ṣāḥibayn: invalid (the mawāḍaʿa is the zāhir and it is more probable that they built on it).tap to reveal
What is the effect of hazl in nikāḥ, ṭalāq and ʿitāq?
They are valid and the hazl void: 'thalāth jiddahunna jidd wa-hazluhunna jidd: al-nikāḥ wa-l-ṭalāq wa-l-yamīn' (other wording: al-ʿitāq; the ḥāshiya: al-Tirmidhī's wording is 'al-nikāḥ wa-l-ṭalāq wa-l-rajʿa'; the matn's wording is not found in the books, per al-Maẓharī). Al-umūr al-muktasaba (3): safah, safar and khaṭaʾ

Three more muktasaba. Safah is acting contrary to the requirement of the Sharʿ though its aṣl is legitimate, i.e. isrāf and tabdhīr; it does not damage ahliyya, so he is still addressed by all rulings, but his money is withheld from him: according to the verse 'lā tuʾtū al-sufahāʾ amwālakum' (with its two readings) and 'fa-in ānastum minhum rushdan'. Abū Ḥanīfa: it is given to him at twenty-five even without rushd, and there is no ḥajr on a free sane adult; the Ṣāḥibayn: no money until rushd and ḥajr in acts that hazl voids (the fatwā follows them). Safar is leaving the place of residence to travel (three days at least), does not negate ahliyya but is by itself a cause of lightening: qaṣr of four-rakʿa prayers, postponement of the fast; it is not a cause for dropping, and a fast begun at home is not broken for travel since safar is the servant's choice, while illness is not. Khaṭaʾ is the opposite of ṣawāb: an act with no full intention; it is an ʿudhr for Allāh's rights where it arises from ijtihād (shubha), not for the servants' rights (ḍamān and diya are due).
What is safah and its ruling on ahliyya and property?
Safah is acting against the requirement of the Sharʿ though its basis is legitimate (isrāf, tabdhīr). It does not harm the ahliyya or lift any ruling from him (he is mukallaf with all rulings); but his property is withheld by agreement from the start by 'wa-lā tuʾtū al-sufahāʾ amwālakum' (two meanings: do not give your wards from among wives and children the wealth Allāh made a livelihood for you, as they waste it and then you need their maintenance; or, 'your' being 'their' and added to those who manage it). The next verse 'fa-in ānastum minhum rushdan fa-ʿdafaʿū ilayhim amwālahum' ties the handing over to rushd, hence the Ṣāḥibayn: it is not handed over until rushd is seen. Abū Ḥanīfa: if he reaches twenty-five he is given his property even without rushd, since in that period he could be a father (twelve years for bulūgh and six months for pregnancy) and doubled a grandfather, and withholding no longer helps.tap to reveal
May a spendthrift be placed under ḥajr?
Abū Ḥanīfa: no ḥajr at all, whether in acts that accept no hazl (nikāḥ, ʿitāq) or in those that do (sale, ijāra), since ḥajr on a free sane adult is not legislated. Al-ikrāh: its three kinds, effect on khiṭāb, aqwāl vs afʿāl, four ḥurmāt, and the colophon of the book

The last topic of the whole work. Ikrāh is making a man do what he dislikes and would not do of himself. It has three kinds: mulji' (threat of death or loss of limb): it removes riḍā and spoils ikhtiyār; ghayr mulji' (the qayd, long ḥabs or a beating that does not threaten life): it removes riḍā only; and the threat of imprisoning father or son (or the like), after which riḍā and ikhtiyār both remain. It does not negate khiṭāb or ahliyya (ʿaql and bulūgh remain, and the mukrah is tested as in choice). It is of four rulings in the act: farḍ (eating carrion), ḥaẓr (zinā, killing an innocent), ibāḥa (breaking the fast) and rukhṣa (word of kufr with a firm heart). Aqwāl: those that accept no faskh and do not depend on riḍā are binding (ṭalāq, ʿitāq, nikāḥ, rajʿa, tadbīr, ʿafw, yamīn, nadhr, ẓihār, īlāʾ, fayʾ, Islām); sale is concluded but fāsid and may be ratified; all iqrārs are void. Afʿāl: eating and waṭʾ fall on the mukrah; killing and destroying property can be attributed to the mukrih (qiṣāṣ on him for Abū Ḥanīfa; on the mukrah for Muḥammad and Zufar; both for Shāfiʿī). The book closes with the ḥurma types, the Sharʿ's promise of shahāda if one endures in the third and fourth, and the author's colophon: finished 7 Jumādā al-ūlā 1105 AH in the Ḥaram of Madīna by Shaykh Aḥmad known as Shaykh Jīwan b. Abī Saʿīd (al-Ṣiddīqī al-Ḥanafī al-Amīthawī), who lived twenty-five years after and died in Delhi in 1130.
What are the three kinds of ikrāh?
(1) Mulji': by something that threatens his life or a limb (kill you, cut your hand): riḍā is lost and ikhtiyār spoiled, certainly. (2) Not mulji': by qayd, long ḥabs or a beating that does not threaten life: riḍā is lost but ikhtiyār remains. (3) By a threat to imprison his father, son or wife: neither riḍā nor ikhtiyār is lost. The ikrāh in all of them does not negate khiṭāb or ahliyya (ʿaql and bulūgh remain, and he is tried as in choice).tap to reveal
What are the four rulings of acting under ikrāh?
(1) Farḍ: eating carrion or pork under mulji' ikrāh: he must, and if he endures until death he is punished (throwing himself into destruction); (2) ḥaẓr: zinā and killing a protected life: forbidden even under mulji' ikrāh (one who endures is rewarded; there is no rukhṣa in killing another since both lives are equally protected, and zinā is a form of killing: the child lost and his lineage and maintenance; if the woman is forced, she has the rukhṣa, as there is no killing of a child in her yielding); (3) ibāḥa: breaking the fast: permitted (farḍ for a traveller, rukhṣa for a resident, and no case equals doing and not doing in sin and reward); (4) rukhṣa: uttering kufr while the heart is firm in belief. The difference of rukhṣa and ibāḥa: in ibāḥa the ḥurma is lifted; in rukhṣa only the sin is lifted (some say there is no need for ibāḥa as it falls in farḍ or rukhṣa).tap to reveal
What of words spoken under ikrāh?
The mukrah cannot be an instrument of another in words (no one speaks with another's tongue), so the effect is on him: the words that do not accept faskh and do not depend on riḍā are not voided by ikrāh and are binding on the one forced: ṭalāq, ʿitāq, nikāḥ, rajʿa, tadbīr, ʿafw ʿan dam ʿamd, yamīn, nadhr, ẓihār, īlāʾ, fayʾ qawlī, Islām (they are not subject to faskh nor hazl-like): they are counted, except where something changing (istithnāʾ, taʿlīq) is joined. Those that accept faskh and depend on riḍā (bayʿ, ijāra): the ʿaqd concludes but is fāsid as riḍā is absent; if he ratifies after the ikrāh ends it is valid since the cause of fasād is removed. All iqrārs are void: their validity rests on the muṣaddaq, and ikrāh shows the speaker was warding off the sword, not reporting; it cannot be taken as majāz since the evidence of lying (ikrāh) is present.tap to reveal

Zanna and her sisters

Zanna wa akhawatuha is the third group of verbs that enter the mubtada' and khabar (after kana and kada/inna families). They put both in the accusative as two maf'ul. They split into af'al al-qulub (certainty: ra'a, 'alima, wajada, dara, ta'allam; probability: khala, zanna, hasiba, za'ama, 'adda, haja, ja'ala, hab) and af'al al-tahwil (sayyara and sisters). The bab then treats ta'liq and ilgha', dropping the maf'ul, and the special rule by which qawl acts like zanna.
What do zanna and her sisters govern?
انصب بفعل القلب جزئي ابتدا ... أعني رأى خال علمت وجدا
They put both parts of the mubtada'/khabar in the accusative as two maf'ul; the first is the original mubtada', the second the khabar.tap to reveal
Which are the yaqin verbs and which the rujhan verbs?
ظن حسبت وزعمت مع عد ... حجا درى وجعل اللذ كاعتقد
Yaqin: ra'a, 'alima, wajada, dara, ta'allam. Rujhan: khala, zanna, hasiba, za'ama, 'adda, haja, ja'ala (in the sense of i'taqada), hab. Ja'ala meaning sayyara belongs to tahwil.tap to reveal
Which verbs are the tahwil sisters?
وهب تعلم والتي كصيرا ... أيضا بها انصب مبتدا وخبرا
Sayyara and its like (ja'ala, wahaba, takhidha, ittakhidha, taraka, radda) also take two objects, originally mubtada' and khabar.tap to reveal

A'lama and Ara (three-object verbs)

This short bab covers the verbs that take three objects. A'lama and ara are the hamza-causative of 'alima and ra'a: the hamza adds the former fa'il as a new first object, so two objects become three. The second and third behave like zanna's objects (mubtada'/khabar origin, ilgha', ta'liq, dropping). Five more verbs follow: nabba'a, akhbara, haddatha, anba'a, khabbara.
How do a'lama and ara get three objects?
إلى ثلاثة رأى وعلما ... عدوا إذا صارا أرى وأعلما
Ra'a and 'alima take two objects; the hamza adds a third (the original fa'il).tap to reveal
Do the 2nd and 3rd objects follow zanna's rules?
وما لمفعولي علمت مطلقا ... للثان والثالث أيضا حققا
Yes: they are originally mubtada'/khabar and admit ilgha', ta'liq and dropping with evidence.tap to reveal
What if the base verb took only one object?
وإن تعديا لواحد بلا ... همز فلاثنين به توصلا
With hamza it takes two; the second works like the second of kasa/a'ta: not predicable of the first, and both or either can be dropped without clue.tap to reveal

The Fa'il (agent)

The fa'il is the noun to which a verb (or verb-like word) is attributed in the active pattern; it is marfu'. Ibn Aqil treats: the definition; its rank after its verb (Basra vs Kufa on fronting); the verb stays singular with a dual/plural fa'il (lughat akaluni al-baraghith); deleting the verb; the ta' of ta'nith (obligatory, preferred, optional); and word order of fa'il and maf'ul (obligatory and permitted fronting and delaying, mahsur bi-illa/innama, and the idiom khafa rabbahu 'Umar).
What is the fa'il and its i'rab?
الفاعل الذي كمرفوعي "أتى ... زيد" "منيرا وجهه" "نعم الفتى (١) "
The noun attributed to a verb or verb-like word on the active pattern; marfu'. It may be sarih (قام زيد) or mu'awwal (يعجبني أن تقوم).tap to reveal
Where does the fa'il stand?
وبعد فعل فاعل فإن ظهر ... فهو وإلا فضمير استتر
After its raiser; if no overt noun it is a hidden pronoun. Fronting is not allowed: زيد قام has a hidden fa'il in the khabar.tap to reveal
Does the verb agree in number with a dual/plural fa'il?
وجرد الفعل إذا ما أسندا ... لاثنين أو جمع كفاز الشهدا
No: when the fa'il is an overt dual/plural the verb is kept bare (قام الزيدان). Rare dialect: وقد يقال سعدا وسعدوا.tap to reveal

Na'ib al-Fa'il (passive subject)

When the fa'il is dropped, the maf'ul bihi takes its place with all its rulings: marfu', placed after the verb, and cannot be dropped. The verb takes a changed vowel pattern (majhul). If there is no maf'ul bihi, a suitable zarf, masdar or jarr-majrur may stand in. The bab then deals with which one to prefer when several exist (Basra vs Kufa vs Akhfash), and with two- and three-object verbs.
What takes the fa'il's place?
ينوب مفعول به عن فاعل ... فيما له كنيل خير نائل
The maf'ul bihi, getting the fa'il's rulings: raf', adjacency to the verb, no deletion.tap to reveal
How is the verb vowelled?
فأول الفعل اضممن والمتصل ... بالآخر اكسر في مضي كوصل
Damm the first letter and kasr the letter before the last in the past (wusila); fatha before last in the present (yuntaha).tap to reveal
If there is no maf'ul bihi, what stands in?
وقابل من ظرف أو من مصدر ... أو حرف جر بنيابة حري
A suitable zarf, masdar or jarr-majrur, provided it is capable and informative: سير يوم الجمعة, ضرب ضرب شديد, مر بزيد. Not عندك, not uninformative ones.tap to reveal

Ishtighal (verb preoccupied with a pronoun)

Ishtighal is when a noun comes first and a verb after it works on the noun's pronoun (zaydan darabtuhu) or on someone attached to it (zaydan darabtu ghulamahu). The noun can then be accusative by a hidden verb that mirrors the shown one. The grammarians sort cases into five: obligatory nasb, obligatory raf', nasb preferred, raf' preferred, and equal. Ibn Aqil then extends this to the participle and to tabi's that bring in the connection.
What is ishtighal and what is the ruling?
إن مضمر اسم سابق فعلا شغل ... عنه: بنصب لفظه أو المحل
If a pronoun of an earlier noun occupies a verb that would otherwise act on the noun, the noun may be accusative; the pronoun may be accusative in form or place (mahall).tap to reveal
What puts the fronted noun in nasb?
فالسابق انصبه بفعل أضمرا ... حتما موافق لما قد أظهرا
A hidden verb that must be dropped, matching the shown verb in meaning.tap to reveal
When is nasb obligatory?
والنصب حتم إن تلا السابق ... ما يختص بالفعل: كإن وحيثما
When the noun follows an instrument that takes only a verb (conditionals): إن زيدا أكرمته أكرمك.tap to reveal

Ta'addi and Luzum (transitivity)

This bab defines the transitive (muta'addi) and intransitive (lazim) verb, gives the sign of transitivity (the object-pronoun ha' that does not refer to the masdar), and lists what forces a verb to be lazim (character traits, iq'ansasa and ifa'alla patterns, cleanliness, dirt, bodily states, mutawa'a). It then covers dropping the jarr particle (qiyas with an/anna, sama' elsewhere), the order of two objects, and dropping the fadla.
What marks a verb as transitive?
علامة الفعل المعدى أن تصل ... ها غير مصدر به نحو عمل
That a ha' (referring to something other than the masdar) can attach to it: الباب أغلقته. A masdar-ha' attaches to both kinds and is not a sign.tap to reveal
How does a transitive verb act?
فانصب به مفعوله إن لم ينب ... عن فاعل نحو تدبرت الكتب
It puts its object in nasb unless the object is raised as na'ib fa'il. Rarely raf' of the object and nasb of the fa'il is heard (خرق الثوب المسمار), by sama'.tap to reveal
Which verbs are always intransitive?
ولازم غير المعد ى وحتم ... لزوم أفعال السجايا كنهم
Character verbs (sharufa, karuma, nahima), verbs on ifa'alla (iqsha'arra) or if'anlala (iq'ansasa), verbs of cleanliness (tahura), dirt (danisa), bodily accident (marida, ihmarra), and mutawa' of a one-object verb (madadtuhu fa-imtadda).tap to reveal

Tanazu' (competing operators)

Tanazu' is when two operators (before the noun) both look for one noun, as in darabtu wa-akramtu Zaydan. Both may act on the noun. Basra prefers the nearer second, Kufa the earlier first. Whichever is not given the overt noun acts on its pronoun, which is obligatory if it needs a fa'il or in zanna's object, with details for other objects.
What is tanazu' and what is allowed?
إن عاملان اقتضيا في اسم عمل ... قبل فللواحد منهما العمل
If two operators before an overt noun both require it, either may act on it and the other acts on its pronoun.tap to reveal
Which operator is better?
والثان أولى عند أهل البصره ... واختار عكسا غيرهم ذا أسره
Basra: the second, due to proximity. Kufa: the first, due to precedence.tap to reveal
What of the neglected operator?
وأعمل المهمل في ضمير ما ... تنازعاه والتزم ما التزما
It acts on the pronoun of the noun, which must be kept if the demand is something that cannot be dropped, such as a fa'il: يحسنان ويسيء ابناك / يحسن ويسيئان ابناك.tap to reveal

Al-Maf'ul al-Mutlaq (absolute object)

The masdar names the event (hadath) part of the verb's meaning without time. The maf'ul mutlaq is the masdar in nasb that stresses its own verb, or describes its kind or count. Ibn Aqil covers its raiser (a masdar, verb or participle), its three uses, the nouns that stand in for it (kull, ba'd, synonym, pronoun, demonstrative, count), dual and plural, and when its verb is dropped (obligatory and not).
What is the masdar?
المصدر اسم ما سوى الزمان من ... مدلولي الفعل كأمن من أمن
The name of the hadath, the one of the verb's two meanings (event, time) that is not time.tap to reveal
What raises the maf'ul mutlaq?
بمثله أو فعل أو وصف نصب ... وكونه أصلا لهذين انتخب
A masdar, a verb or a participle. Chosen: the masdar is the origin of both verb and participle.tap to reveal
What are its uses?
توكيدا أو نوعا يبين أو عدد ... كسرت سيرتين سير ذي رشد
Emphasis (ضربت ضربا), kind (سرت سير ذي رشد), number (ضربت ضربتين).tap to reveal

Al-Maf'ul Lahu (object of reason)

The maf'ul lahu is a masdar that states the reason for the action, and shares time and doer with its verb. It is accusative; it may also be governed by a lam of reason, even when it meets the conditions. Un-annexed bare forms are mostly accusative, forms with al- mostly jarr, and annexed ones either way.
What is it and its conditions?
ينصب مفعولا له المصدر إن ... أبان تعليلا ك جد شكرا ودن
A masdar showing reason is accusative: جد شكرا. It must also share time and doer with its raiser.tap to reveal
What if a condition is missing?
وهو بما يعمل فيه متحد ... وقتا وفاعلا وإن شرط فقد
If any condition is missing, jarr it with a particle of reason (lam): جئتك للسمن.tap to reveal
May a satisfying maf'ul lahu take jarr?
فاجرره بالحرف وليس يمتنع ... مع الشروط: كلزهد ذا قنع
Yes, it is not forbidden: لزهد ذا قنع.tap to reveal

Only then is it clear that the masdar is the reason, taking the verb directly; otherwise a particle is needed to link them.explain it in your own words — then tap

Al-Maf'ul Fihi (Zarf)

The zarf is a time or place noun that carries the meaning of 'fi' regularly. Al-Istithna' (exception)

Istithna' excludes something from a ruling by illa or its like. Ibn Aqil treats illa in positive speech (nasb), after negation/prohibition/question (ittiba' preferred for the connected; nasb for the disconnected, with Tamim allowing badal), the fronted mustathna, tafrigh (emptying), repetition of illa (tawkid vs non-tawkid), ghayr and siwa (take the i'rab of the noun after illa), and the verb-particle words laysa, la yakun, khala, 'ada, hasha (with and without ma).

Al-Hal (circumstantial accusative)

The hal is a fadla participle in nasb that shows the state of its owner at the time of the action. Ibn Aqil covers: the definition; usually mutaqila and mushtaqqa but not necessarily (jamid in price, mutual, simile); indefiniteness (and the apparent definites); a masdar as hal; the owner of the hal is usually definite unless it is specified, after negation, or the hal is fronted; fronting the hal over its owner and its raiser; its dual; muakkida; the sentence as hal with waw and/or pronoun; and dropping its raiser.

الحال وصف فضلة منتصب ... مفهم في حال كفردا أذهب

Al-Tamyiz (specification)

The tamyiz is an indefinite noun carrying the meaning of 'min' that clarifies an ambiguity in what precedes. It has two kinds: clarifying an ambiguous thing (after measures: area, volume, weight, numbers) and clarifying an ambiguous relation (tab Zaydun nafsan). The bab fixes when the tamyiz after a measure may be jarr by idafa or must be nasb; the nasb after af'al al-tafdil when it is the fa'il in meaning; after ta'ajjub; jarr by min; and placing tamyiz before its raiser.
What is the tamyiz?
اسم بمعنى من مبين نكره ... ينصب تمييزا بما قد فسره
An indefinite noun with meaning of min, clarifying; nasb by what it explains: شبر أرضا, قفيز برا, منوين عسلا وتمرا.tap to reveal
Jarr or nasb after a measure?
وبعد ذي وشبهها اجرره إذا ... أضفتها كـ "مد حنطة غذا "
After measures, jarr by idafa is allowed if the measure is not annexed to another word (مد حنطة).tap to reveal
When is nasb obligatory?
والنصب بعد ما أضيف وجبا ... إن كان مثل "ملء الأرض ذهبا "
If the measure is annexed to something else: ملء الأرض ذهبا.tap to reveal

Huruf al-Jarr (prepositions)

Ibn Malik lists twenty jarr particles, all specific to nouns. Ibn Aqil sorts them: those that govern only overt nouns (hatta, kaf, waw, ta', rubba, mudh, mundhu), their special limits (mudh/mundhu with times; rubba with indefinites; waw and ta' for oath), and then the meanings of min, ila, hatta, lam, ba', fi, 'ala, 'an, kaf. Al-Idafa (annexation)

Idafa joins two nouns so that the second is jarr and the first loses its nun or tanwin and takes specification or definiteness from it. Ibn Aqil teaches: the hidden meaning of the idafa (lam, min, fi), true (mahda/ma'nawiyya) versus untrue (ghayr mahda/lafziyya) idafa, al- with the mudaf, banning idafa of something to itself, a mudaf gaining gender, nouns always annexed (some only to pronouns), nouns annexed to sentences (hayth, idh, hin), kila/kilta, ayy, ladun, ma'a, ghayr, qabl, ba'd (their four states), deleting the mudaf or the mudaf ilayh, and separating the two.
What changes in an idafa?
نونا تلي الإعراب أو تنوينا ... مما تضيف أحذف كطور سينا

والثاني أجرر وأنو من أو في إذا ... لم يصلح إلا ذاك واللام خذا

Mudaf to Ya' al-Mutakallim

When a noun is annexed to the speaker's ya', its last letter takes kasra if it is sound, or like sound (dalw, zaby). The ya' takes fatha with the maqsur, the mangus, the dual, and the sound masculine plural, where the preceding ya' or waw is merged into it. I'mal al-Masdar (action of the verbal noun)

The masdar acts like its verb in two cases: replacing the verb (darban Zaydan), or when it can be replaced by an + verb or ma + verb. In the latter case it may be annexed, tanwin-ed, or with al-, the annexed form being the commonest. Ism al-masdar (e.g. 'ata') may act, rarely. The masdar annexed to the fa'il takes a maf'ul in nasb, and annexed to the maf'ul takes a fa'il in raf'. Tabi's follow the lafz or the mahall.
When does the masdar act?
بفعله المصدر ألحق في العمل ... مضافا أو مجردا أو مع أل

إن كان فعل مع أن أو ما يحل ... محله ولأسم مصدر عمل
An + verb for past and future (ضربك زيدا أمس = أن ضربت), ma + verb for present.tap to reveal

I'mal Ism al-Fa'il (action of the active participle)

The active participle acts like its verb (raf' of a fa'il, nasb of a maf'ul) when it refers to the present or future, and when it relies on something before it (question, nida', negation, na't, hal, or khabar). If it follows al- as a relative it acts in all times. The intensive forms fa''al, mif'al, fa'ul, fa'il, fa'il act as well. The dual and plural follow. The participle may be annexed to its maf'ul. The passive participle is treated the same, acting like the passive verb, and may be annexed to its na'ib.

Al-Sifa al-Mushabbaha (resembling participle)

The sifa mushabbaha is an adjective of a fixed quality that acts like ism al-fa'il by taking a fa'il related to its owner. Its sign is that the fa'il can be put in jarr by it (hasan al-wajh). It is made only from lazim verbs and refers to the present. It acts like the transitive participle, but its ma'mul cannot be fronted, and it works only on a sababi (not a stranger). Its ma'mul has six forms with al-, annexed, pronoun, etc., giving 36 images; jarr is banned with al- when the ma'mul is bare or annexed to bare.

Al-Ta'ajjub (wonder)

Ta'ajjub has two patterns: ma af'alahu (ma is an indefinite complete noun, af'ala a past verb with a hidden fa'il, and the thing wondered at is its maf'ul) and af'il bihi (an imperative in form, with a ba'-governed fa'il, meaning wonder). Ibn Aqil covers dropping the muta'ajjab minhu, the verbs being fixed (non-conjugable), the conditions for forming them (triliteral, conjugable, gradable, complete, positive, not passive, not for colour/defect), the forms for the rest (ashadd, ashdid with the masdar), shudhudh, and the ban on fronting or separating the muta'ajjab minhu.

Ni'ma, Bi'sa and their kin (verbs of praise and blame)

Ni'ma (praise) and bi'sa (blame) are non-conjugable verbs (jumhur; some Kufans, including Farra', call them nouns). Their fa'il is either a noun with al-, or annexed to a noun with al-, or a hidden pronoun explained by a tamyiz (ni'ma qawman ma'shiruhu). The mamdun/madhmum (makhsus) follows as a mubtada' or khabar of a dropped mubtada'. Ibn Aqil covers whether tamyiz and overt fa'il may be combined, ma after ni'ma, the makhsus and its fronting, sa'a and the fa'ula verbs used in the same sense, and habbadha (hubba dha) with its i'rab and the ruling for dha.

Af'al al-Tafdil (comparative/superlative)

Af'al al-tafdil is built on the pattern af'al from verbs from which ta'ajjub can be built, and by the same conditions; otherwise it uses ashadd etc. It has three states: bare, annexed, and with al-. Bare: needs 'min' (shown or understood) and stays masculine singular; annexed to an indefinite: the same; with al-: agrees; annexed to a definite: two ways (as bare or agreeing). Min and its object come right after unless the object is an interrogative (mimman anta khayr). It raises a hidden pronoun, and an overt noun only rarely, unless it replaces a verb (the kuhl case).
From which verbs is it built?
صغ من مصوغ منه للتعجب ... أفعل للتفضيل وأب اللذ أبى
From those from which ta'ajjub is built; not from those it refuses: no non-triliteral, non-conjugable, ungradable, incomplete, negated, af'al-adjective, passive.tap to reveal
If conditions fail?
وما به إلى تعجب وصل ... لمانع به إلى التفضيل صل
What serves ta'ajjub (ashadd + masdar) also serves tafdil (أشد استخراجا).tap to reveal

وأفعل التفضيل صله أبدا ... تقديرا أو لفظا بمن إن جردا

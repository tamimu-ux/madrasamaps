Chapter on the Invalid (Fāsid) Sale

This chapter lists the sales the Ḥanafī school classes as fāsid: sales whose counter-value is unlawful or unowned, whose object is uncertain or undeliverable, or which carry a stipulation the contract does not call for. It then states the chapter's key consequence: a fāsid sale, once the buyer takes possession with the seller's permission, still transfers ownership (for the item's value) but must be undone. The closing paragraphs separate fāsid from merely makrūh sales (najsh, outbidding, meeting caravans, selling for a bedouin, selling at the Jumuʿa adhān), which are valid despite being prohibited. This chapter follows the khiyār chapters and gives the rules needed before the next chapters (iqāla, murābaḥa, ribā).
When is a sale fāsid because of the counter-value?
إذا كان أحد العوضين أو كلاهما محرما فالبيع فاسد
If either counter-value (or both) is unlawful, the sale is fāsid; so too when the item is not owned property, such as a free person.tap to reveal
May fish still in the sea or birds in the air be sold?
ولا يجوز بيع السمك في الماء ولا بيع الطير في الهواء
Invalid: the seller neither possesses nor can deliver them.tap to reveal
Can unborn young, or milk still in the udder, be sold?
ولا يجوز بيع الحمل ولا النتاج ولا بيع اللبن في الضرع
Not valid: the existence and quantity are uncertain.tap to reveal

Under the chapter, the extra stipulation (a loan) benefits the seller and is not required by the sale, so the sale is fāsid. If Bilal already took the laptop with Hamza's permission, he owns it but owes its value, and either may rescind.discuss first — then tap

Chapter on Iqāla (Mutual Cancellation of a Sale)

Iqāla is the parties' mutual agreement to undo a completed sale. Qudūrī states that it is valid in a sale at the original price: any condition for a smaller or larger price is void and the original price is returned. He gives Abū Ḥanīfa's classification: it is a rescission (faskh) for the two parties but counts as a new sale for third parties. Finally, the perishing of the price does not block iqāla but the perishing of the item does, and if only part of the item perished, iqāla remains valid for the rest. The chapter links back to the khiyār chapters (another way of undoing a sale) and forward to murābaḥa (which depends on the original price).
At what price must iqāla be made?
الإقالة: جائزة في البيع بمثل الثمن الأول
Iqāla is valid in a sale, at the same price as the original.tap to reveal
What if the parties agree to iqāla for a lower or higher price?
فإن شرط أقل منه أو أكثر فالشرط باطل ويرد مثل الثمن الأول
The condition is void; the original price is returned, and the iqāla stands.tap to reveal
Does the perishing of the price block iqāla?
وهلاك الثمن لا يمنع صحة الإقالة وهلاك المبيع يمنع منها
Perishing of the price does not block iqāla; perishing of the item does.tap to reveal

Chapter on Murābaḥa and Tawliya (Sale at Cost-plus and at Cost)

These are 'trust sales' (buyūʿ al-amāna): the seller discloses what the item cost him and the buyer relies on his honesty. Murābaḥa adds a profit; tawliya passes the item at cost. Because the buyer trusts the stated cost, Qudūrī specifies what may be added to the cost (finishing, dyeing, transport) and how to word it. He then gives the effect of a discovered deception (khiyāna): Abū Ḥanīfa gives the buyer a choice in murābaḥa, and a price reduction in tawliya, with Abū Yūsuf and Muḥammad differing. The chapter ends with general sale rules: the ban on reselling movables before possession, resale of measured goods, increase/decrease of price, and deferral of debts.
What are murābaḥa and tawliya?
المرابحة: نقل ما ملكه بالعقد الأول بالثمن الأول
Murābaḥa transfers what was acquired in the first contract at the first price, plus a profit; tawliya transfers it at the same price without profit.tap to reveal
Which costs can the seller add to the base price?
ويجوز أن يضيف إلى رأس المال أجرة القصار والصباغ والطراز والفتل وأجرة حمل الطعام ولكن يقول: قام علي بكذا ولا يقول: اشتريته بكذا
He may add fuller's, dyer's, embroidery and twisting wages, and food transport; but he says 'it stood me at X', not 'I bought it for X'.tap to reveal
What if the buyer finds the seller lied about the cost?
فإن اطلع المشتري على خيانة في المرابحة فهو بالخيار عند أبي حنيفة: إن شاء أخذه بجميع الثمن وإن شاء رده
In murābaḥa (Abū Ḥanīfa) the buyer may take it at the full price or return it; in tawliya the buyer removes the overstated amount from the price.tap to reveal

Chapter on Ribā (Usury / Unlawful Excess)

Ribā al-faḍl is the prohibited excess in exchanging like for like. Qudūrī gives the Ḥanafī theory: the ʿilla (effective cause) is qadr (measure by volume or weight) together with jins (same genus). If both are present, excess and deferral are both forbidden; if neither, both are allowed; if only one, excess is allowed but deferral is forbidden. Which items count as measured or weighed is fixed by the text of the ḥadīth (wheat, barley, dates, salt by volume; gold and silver by weight) and, for everything else, by custom. The rest of the chapter applies the theory to concrete exchanges: wheat for flour, meat for animals, fresh for dried fruit, oil for seeds, bread, and the (controversial) exceptions for master/slave and Muslim/ḥarbī in dār al-ḥarb.
What is the ʿilla of ribā in a measured or weighed item?
فالعلة فيه الكيل مع الجنس
The ʿilla is qadr (volume) with jins, or weight with jins.tap to reveal
How may a measured item be sold for its own genus?
فإذا بيع المكيل أو الموزون بجنسه مثلا بمثل جاز البيع وإن تفاضلا لم يجز
Like for like is valid; if there is any excess, the sale is invalid.tap to reveal
Does quality matter when exchanging the same genus?
ولا يجوز: بيع الجيد بالرديء
Good for poor is not valid except like for like in quantity; quality does not compensate for excess.tap to reveal

Chapter on Salam (Advance-Payment Sale)

Salam is a sale where the price is paid in full at the contract and the goods, described precisely, are delivered at a fixed later date. It is an exception to the rule against selling what one does not have, permitted by the ḥadīth of Ibn ʿAbbās for the needs of farmers and merchants. Qudūrī lists what can and cannot be the subject of salam, the seven conditions according to Abū Ḥanīfa (with the Ṣāḥibayn's difference about the place and the capital), the need for the goods to exist throughout the period, and the two key controls: capital must be received in the session, and neither capital nor the goods can be dealt with before receipt. The final paragraphs (p. 89) append miscellaneous rules on what may be sold (dogs, predators, silkworms, bees) and on dhimmīs' trade in wine and pork.
What can salam be made in?
السلم جائز في المكيلات والموزونات والمعدودات التي لا تتفاوت كالجوز والبيض وفي المذروعات
Salam is valid in measured, weighed, uniform counted goods (walnuts, eggs), and goods measured by length.tap to reveal
Can salam be made in animals, hides by count, firewood in bundles?
ولا يجوز السلم في الحيوان ولا في أطرافه ولا في الجلود عددا ولا في الحطب حزما ولا في الرطبة جرزا
No: these differ too much in size and quality to be described exactly.tap to reveal
Must the goods exist from the contract to the due date?
ولا يجوز السلم حتى يكون المسلم فيه موجودا من حين العقد إلى حين المحل
Yes: they must be available (in the market) from the contract until the due date.tap to reveal

Chapter on Ṣarf (Exchange of Currency / Gold and Silver)

Ṣarf is the sale of a 'price-type' item (gold, silver, or currency) for another price-type item. Qudūrī gives the main rules: same genus requires exact equality, whatever the quality or workmanship, and exchange of both sides before parting; different genus allows unequal amounts but still requires both sides to be taken before parting. A contract is void if the parties separate without full receipt. The chapter then covers composite cases (a sword with a silver ornament; a silver vessel part-paid or part-claimed), mixed exchanges (two dirhams and a dinar for two dinars and a dirham), the status of coins of mixed metal (ghalaba), and the effect of a coin ceasing to be used (kasād) on sales priced in it.
What is ṣarf?
الصرف هو: البيع إذا كان كل واحد من العوضين من جنس الأثمان
Ṣarf is a sale in which both counter-values are of the genus of price-media.tap to reveal
Same genus: how?
فإن باع فضة بفضة أو ذهبا بذهب لم يجز إلا مثلا بمثل وإن اختلفا في الجودة والصياغة
Silver for silver or gold for gold must be equal; quality and workmanship make no difference.tap to reveal
Must both sides be received?
ولا بد من قبض العوضين
Receipt of both counter-values before parting is a must (the source misprints 'الافتراق').tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Rahn (Pledge / Collateral)

Rahn is holding an item as security for a debt so that the creditor may be paid from it if the debtor fails. Qudūrī starts with the contract's formation (offer and acceptance, completed by the creditor's possession of a clearly separated item) and then the debt it can secure (a guaranteed debt only, never a trust like a deposit). He gives the Ḥanafī rule of liability: the pledge is guaranteed to the lesser of its value and the debt. What can and cannot be pledged (not a share, not unharvested fruit apart from the tree), who holds the pledge (the creditor, his dependants, or an agreed neutral person) and what each party may do with it (sell, free a slave, damage it) fill most of the chapter. It closes with expenses, growth, increase of the pledge or the debt, two creditors, a sale conditional on a pledge, and the debtor's death.
How is rahn formed?
فإذا قبض المرتهن الرهن محوزا مفرغا مميزا
The contract is by offer and acceptance and is completed by the pledgee's possession, with the item gathered, emptied and separated; before possession the pledger may choose to hand it over or retract. After possession it comes into the pledgee's guarantee.tap to reveal
What debt can rahn secure?
ولا يصح الرهن إلا بدين مضمون
Only a guaranteed debt; not trusts (deposits, mudāraba capital, partnership funds).tap to reveal
How does the pledgee bear loss if it perishes?
وهو مضمون بالأقل من قيمته ومن الدين
The pledge is guaranteed at the lesser of its value and the debt. If equal, the debt is considered paid; if the value is more, the excess is a trust; if less, that much of the debt drops and he claims the rest.tap to reveal

The pledge is guaranteed to the lesser of value and debt, here 600. Rana's debt is treated as paid; the extra 400 was a trust, so the pawnbroker owes it nothing. (Study aid.)discuss first — then tap

Kitāb al-Ḥajr (Legal Restriction / Interdiction)

Ḥajr is the legal restriction of a person's dealings with his own property. Qudūrī lists three causes that restrict by themselves (minority, slavery, insanity) and explains that they restrict words (contracts, acknowledgments, divorce) and not acts (a minor who destroys property pays for it). He then enters the great disputes: whether an adult free person who is spendthrift (safīh) may be interdicted (not per Abū Ḥanīfa; yes per the Ṣāḥibayn), the age of puberty (18/17 per Abū Ḥanīfa; 15 per the Ṣāḥibayn), and whether a court may interdict an insolvent debtor (Abū Ḥanīfa: no, but imprisonment; Ṣāḥibayn: yes, and distribute his property). It ends with the procedure of imprisonment, release and creditors' rights.
What are the causes of ḥajr?
الأسباب الموجبة للحجر ثلاثة: الصغر والرق والجنون
Three: minority, slavery, insanity.tap to reveal
What dealings are valid?
ولا يجوز تصرف الصغير إلا بإذن وليه ولا تصرف العبد إلا بإذن سيده ولا يجوز تصرف المجنون المغلوب على عقله بحال
A minor needs his guardian's permission, a slave his master's; the insane have no valid dealing at all.tap to reveal
A discerning minor, slave or insane who sells or buys while understanding the sale?
ومن باع من هؤلاء شيئا أو اشتراه وهو يعقل البيع ويقصده فالوالي بالخيار: إن شاء أجازه إذا كان فيه مصلحة وإن شاء فسخه
The guardian may ratify it if beneficial, or cancel.tap to reveal

Kitāb al-Iqrār (Acknowledgment / Admission of a Right)

Iqrār is a person's admission of a right owed to another. It binds the person making it and no one else, which governs every rule in the chapter. Qudūrī sets who may make it, the principle that an unspecified admission is binding and must be specified by the admitter, and then the minimum amounts that various vague phrases ('a great sum', 'many dirhams', 'such-and-such dirhams') bind. He covers the wording of exceptions (istithnāʾ), conditions, containers and accessories (a date-basket, an animal in a stable, a ring and its stone), the admission 'from X to Y', admissions in connection with a price, admissions to an unborn child, and finally admissions made in the illness of death, admissions of lineage, and who may be acknowledged as a relative.
Who may make a binding iqrār, and what if it is vague?
إذا أقر الحر البالغ العاقل بحق لزمه إقراره مجهولا كان ما أقر به أو معلوما
A free, adult, sane person's admission binds him, even if vague; he is told to specify.tap to reveal
'He has something against me'?
والقول فيه قوله مع يمينه إن ادعى المقر له أكثر من ذلك
He specifies something of value, and his word is accepted on oath if the claimant asks for more.tap to reveal
'ʿAlayya/qibalī' versus 'ʿindī/maʿī'?
وإن قال: عندي أو: معي فهو إقرار بأمانة في يده
'ʿAlayya' or 'qibalī' is an admission of debt; 'ʿindī' or 'maʿī' is an admission of a trust in his hand.tap to reveal

Quick quiz

Answer aloud, then tap the row

1May the goods be restricted to a particular village's crop or a particular palm's fruit?
No: the crop might fail, so delivery may be impossible.
2Can salam be made in animals, hides by count, firewood in bundles?
No: these differ too much in size and quality to be described exactly.
3What are murābaḥa and tawliya?
Murābaḥa transfers what was acquired in the first contract at the first price, plus a profit; tawliya transfers it at the same price without profit.
4What debt can rahn secure?
Only a guaranteed debt; not trusts (deposits, mudāraba capital, partnership funds).
5Can 'one of these two cloths' be sold without specifying which?
Invalid: the item sold is not specified.
6At what price must iqāla be made?
Iqāla is valid in a sale, at the same price as the original.
7Same genus: how?
Silver for silver or gold for gold must be equal; quality and workmanship make no difference.
8What if a free man and a slave are sold together, or a slaughtered sheep and a carcass?
The sale is void as to both (nothing valid).
9Can a debt be deferred after it falls due?
Every immediate debt becomes deferred if the creditor defers it, except a loan (qarḍ), whose deferral is not valid.
10Must the goods exist from the contract to the due date?
Yes: they must be available (in the market) from the contract until the due date.

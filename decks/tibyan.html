Ulūm al-Qurʾān: definition of the Qurʾān, its virtues and names

The opening chapter defines what 'ulūm al-Qurʾān' means (the investigations into the Book: its revelation, collection, arrangement, asbāb al-nuzūl, Makkī/Madanī, nāsikh/mansūkh, muḥkam/mutashābih and the rest) and states the purpose: to understand God's speech in light of the Prophet's explanation and the Companions' and Successors' tafsīr. It gives the standard uṣūlī definition of the Qurʾān, a short anthology of āyāt and aḥādīth on its merit, the etiquette of the student, and the Qurʾān's names and attributes with their Qurʾānic proofs. Fawz opens differently (with the five sciences of the Qurʾān); al-Tibyān is a textbook-style survey and so starts with the field itself.
What does 'ulūm al-Qurʾān' mean and what is its purpose?
It means the studies connected to the Qurʾān (revelation, collection, order, writing, asbāb, Makkī/Madanī, nāsikh/mansūkh, muḥkam/mutashābih etc.). Its purpose is to grasp God's speech through the Prophet's explanation, the Companions' and Tābiʿūn's tafsīr, and to know the mufassirūn's ways and conditions of tafsīr.tap to reveal
How is the Qurʾān defined?
هو كلام الله المعجز المنزّل على خاتم الأنبياء والمرسلين بواسطة الأمين جبريل
As God's inimitable speech, revealed to the Seal of the Prophets via Jibrīl, written in muṣḥafs, mutawātir, recited as worship, beginning with al-Fātiḥa and ending with al-Nās; it is a constitution, a guidance and a proof of the Prophet's truth until the Day of Judgement.tap to reveal
What evidence does the author give for the Qurʾān's merit and for adab with it?
Āyāt (Fāṭir 35:29, al-Aʿrāf 7:204, Muḥammad 47:24) plus aḥādīth: 'the best of you is who learns the Qurʾān and teaches it' (Bukhārī); the skilled reciter is with the noble angels and the stumbling reciter gets double reward (Bukhārī, Muslim); it will intercede (Tirmidhī); the believer who recites is like the citron (muttafaq ʿalayh); 'the Qurʾān is a proof for you or against you'.tap to reveal

Beginning of revelation; the first and last revealed

Describes how revelation began (17 Ramaḍān, age forty, cave of Ḥirāʾ, the embrace of Jibrīl and 'Iqraʾ', preceded by true dreams and love of seclusion) using the Bukhārī narration of ʿĀʾisha, and shows from the Qurʾān that Jibrīl is the 'Rūḥ al-Amīn'. It then fixes the first revealed (the opening of al-ʿAlaq) and the last revealed (2:281, 'wa-ttaqū yawman turjaʿūna fīhi ilā Allāh', nine nights before the Prophet's death), rejects 5:3 as the last, and summarises al-Suyūṭī's replies to two objections (the Muddaththir ḥadīth and the 'completion of religion' verse). Finally it gives the first verses on jihād, wine and foods. Fawz does not treat these topics; they are new.
When and how did revelation begin?
On 17 Ramaḍān when the Prophet was forty, in Ḥirāʾ; Jibrīl came, pressed him three times saying 'Iqraʾ', the Prophet answering 'mā anā bi-qāriʾ', then 'Iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika alladhī khalaq...' (96:1-5). Evidence: ʿĀʾisha's report in Bukhārī, Bāb Bad' al-Waḥy; and 2:185 for Ramaḍān.tap to reveal
What proves that the angel who brought the Qurʾān was Jibrīl?
Explicit verses: 26:193-195 (al-Rūḥ al-Amīn) and 16:102 (Rūḥ al-Quds), both identified as Jibrīl by agreement of the mufassirūn.tap to reveal
What was the first revealed part of the Qurʾān?
أول ما نزل من القرآن الكريم الآيات الأولى من سورة العلق
The first verses of Sūrat al-ʿAlaq ('Iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika...', 96:1-5), per the Bukhārī ḥadīth.tap to reveal

The two tanzīls and the wisdom of revelation in instalments (tanjīm)

Explains that the Qurʾān has two descents: first, all at once from the Preserved Tablet to Bayt al-ʿIzza in the lowest heaven on Laylat al-Qadr (proved by 44:3, 97:1, 2:185 and reports from Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Qurṭubī reports ijmāʿ), and second, in instalments over 23 years (17:106, 25:32). Earlier scriptures came down at once, a point the Jews and the mushrikūn raised against the Prophet. Six wisdoms follow: steadying the Prophet's heart, gentleness to him at the weight of revelation, gradual legislation (with the four stages of the prohibition of wine), ease of memorising and understanding for an unlettered umma, keeping pace with events, and pointing to the divine origin of the Book (al-Zarqānī on its coherence despite piecemeal revelation). This is new relative to Fawz, which has no such discussion.
How many tanzīls does the Qurʾān have?
Two: (1) from al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ to Bayt al-ʿIzza in the lowest heaven, all at once, in Laylat al-Qadr; (2) from the lowest heaven to the earth in instalments over 23 years, from the mission to the Prophet's death.tap to reveal
What are the proofs for the first tanzīl?
Three āyāt (44:1-3, 97:1-2, 2:185) and Ibn ʿAbbās reports (Ḥākim, Bayhaqī, Ṭabarānī) that the Qurʾān was separated from al-Dhikr and placed in Bayt al-ʿIzza, and that it was sent down in Ramaḍān to the lowest heaven all at once, then in portions; al-Suyūṭī says all are sound and cites al-Qurṭubī on ijmāʿ.tap to reveal
What is the wisdom of the first tanzīl?
To magnify the Qurʾān and the one it is revealed to, by announcing to the dwellers of the seven heavens that this is the last of the books on the Seal of the Messengers for the noblest umma, and that it has been brought near (al-Suyūṭī).tap to reveal

How the Prophet received the Qurʾān; lafẓ and maʿnā; is the Sunna waḥy?

Describes the chain of reception: God, Jibrīl (by hearing), the Prophet, the umma. The Prophet's haste to memorise is answered by 20:114 and 75:16-19 (God guarantees preservation); Jibrīl reviewed (mudārasa) the Qurʾān with him each Ramaḍān and twice in the final year. Quoting al-Bayhaqī, the author says Jibrīl took it by hearing. Following al-Zarqānī, he rejects the view that Jibrīl brought only the meanings (and the Prophet phrased them) or that the wording was Jibrīl's, as both would destroy iʿjāz. Quoting al-Suyūṭī from al-Juwaynī, he distinguishes two kinds of revealed speech: the Qurʾān (wording from God) and the Sunna (meaning from God, wording the Prophet's), which is why Sunna may be narrated by meaning but the Qurʾān may not. This is al-Tibyān's treatment of waḥy, which Fawz does not discuss.
How did the Qurʾān reach the Prophet?
God sent it via Jibrīl, whom He made trustworthy (81:19-21; 26:193-195); Jibrīl taught it to the Prophet, who conveyed it to the umma. Its reality (kalām and the sent-down) is God's: 27:6.tap to reveal
Why was the Prophet told not to hasten with revelation?
He used to strain to memorise and repeat with Jibrīl; God ordered him to be silent (20:114) and promised that collection and recitation are His responsibility (75:16-19), so it would be preserved in his chest.tap to reveal
How did Jibrīl receive the Qurʾān from God?
By hearing. Al-Bayhaqī reads 97:1 as 'We made the angel hear it and understand it, and sent it down as he heard it'; supported by the ḥadīth that when God speaks the revelation the heavens tremble, the angels fall prostrate, Jibrīl is first to raise his head and God speaks to him what He wills.tap to reveal

Asbāb al-nuzūl: importance, benefits, examples, how known, multiple causes, ʿumūm al-lafẓ

Al-Tibyān's chapter on asbāb al-nuzūl is a classic Suyūṭī-style treatment. It stresses the necessity of the subject (al-Wāḥidī, Ibn Daqīq al-ʿĪd, Ibn Taymiyya), lists compilers (ʿAlī b. al-Madīnī, al-Wāḥidī, Ibn Ḥajar, al-Suyūṭī's Lubāb al-Nuqūl), gives benefits and five worked examples (Marwān on 3:188, ʿUrwa and 2:158, the Ẓāhirīs on 65:4, al-Shāfiʿī on 6:145, Marwān on 46:17), defines sabab with its two types (ḥādītha and suʾāl), and stipulates that it is known only by sound transmission. It then gives six rules for conflicting reports and closes with the uṣūl debate whether the lesson is in the generality of the wording or the specificity of the cause. Fawz covers asbāb from a different angle (what 'nazalat fī kadhā' means, the Salaf's differences, tawjīh); here those points are inherited, and only what al-Ṣābūnī adds is mapped.
Is knowing asbāb al-nuzūl necessary for tafsīr, or only history?
Necessary: some verses cannot be understood without it; al-Wāḥidī says the verse's meaning cannot be known without its story; Ibn Daqīq al-ʿĪd: the sabab is a strong route to the meaning; Ibn Taymiyya: knowing the cause yields knowing the effect. The claim that it is mere history is called 'khāṭiʾ mardūd'.tap to reveal
What are the benefits of knowing the sabab?
(a) Knowing the wisdom behind legislation; (b) repelling the false impression of restriction where the wording seems restrictive; (c) specifying the ruling by the sabab (for those who say the sabab governs); (d) knowing who a verse was revealed about and identifying anything vague.tap to reveal
How does the sabab remove the impression that 6:145 limits forbidden foods to the four listed?
Al-Shāfiʿī: the unbelievers had declared lawful what God forbade and forbidden what God allowed; the verse answers them (in effect: nothing is permitted except what you forbade...) so its aim is to establish tahrīm, not to make everything else lawful; Imām al-Ḥaramayn: 'were it not for al-Shāfiʿī we would not allow opposing Mālik on the limitation of the forbidden'.tap to reveal

Revelation on seven aḥruf: evidence, wisdom, meaning, views, and replies

Al-Ṣābūnī proves that the Qurʾān was revealed on seven aḥruf with nine hadiths (Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿUmar and Hishām b. Ḥakīm, Ubayy b. Kaʿb, ʿUthmān's witnesses, ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ, Zayd b. Arqam and Abū Hurayra). He gives the wisdom (ease for an unlettered, multi-dialect umma, and uniting them on Qurayshī speech, per Ibn al-Jazarī), analyses the word ḥarf (lafẓ mushtarak), surveys the views, and prefers the seven aspects of difference (al-Rāzī, Abū al-Faḍl; supported by al-Zarqānī). He then discusses whether the aḥruf all remain in the ʿUthmānic muṣḥafs (three views, preferring that they contain all that the rasm can bear, and refuting al-Ṭabarī's one-ḥarf view in seven arguments) and answers two objections. This topic is wholly new relative to Fawz.
What proves the Qurʾān was revealed on seven aḥruf?
إن هذا القرآن أنزل على سبعة أحرف
Nine reports: Ibn ʿAbbās (Bukhārī, Muslim): 'Jibrīl taught me on one ḥarf; I kept asking him to increase until it reached seven'; ʿUmar and Hishām b. Ḥakīm; Ubayy b. Kaʿb (Muslim, with Muslim's addition from Ibn Shihāb that the seven concern the same matter, no difference over ḥalāl/ḥarām); ʿUthmān on the minbar with witnesses; Ubayy at Aḍāʾat Banī Ghifār; Tirmidhī's ḥadīth on the unlettered umma; ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ (Aḥmad): 'whichever you recite you are right, so do not dispute'; Zayd b. Arqam (ʿAlī: let each read as he was taught); Abū Hurayra: 'do not end mercy with punishment or punishment with mercy'.tap to reveal
What is the wisdom of seven aḥruf?
(1) Ease for the umma, especially the Arabs with differing dialects, from 'hawwin ʿalā ummatī' and 'my umma cannot bear it' (Ibn al-Jazarī adds the elderly, the woman, those who never read a book, and earlier prophets sent to particular peoples with one ḥarf); (2) unifying the umma on one speech, Qurayshī, which had gathered the choice usages of the tribes visiting Makka.tap to reveal
What does ḥarf mean in the ḥadīth?
ḥarf is a lafẓ mushtarak; by context it means wajh (aspect), so the ḥadīth means the Qurʾān was sent down with latitude: the reader may recite on any of seven aspects, one in place of another. The author explicitly says it does not mean a single ḥarf contains seven aspects.tap to reveal

Qirāʾāt: definition, origin, categories, conditions of acceptance, and the seven readers

Defines qirāʾa as a school of pronunciation of the Qurʾān taken by one of the imams and traced by isnād to the Prophet, explains how it arose (the Companion muqriʾūn, then the Tābiʿūn, and the unvowelled, undotted ʿUthmānic muṣḥafs that suited several readings), gives the categories (mutawātir, mashhūr, āḥād, shādhdh, mawḍūʿ, mudraj) and Ibn al-Jazarī's three conditions (Arabic, rasm, sound isnād), lists the seven, ten and fourteen, the first authors, when the seven became famous (200 AH) and were codified (Ibn Mujāhid, end of 3rd century), and then gives a short life of each of the seven readers with their two rāwīs, quoting the Shāṭibiyya. All of this is new relative to Fawz.
What is a qirāʾa?
A way of pronouncing the Qurʾān adopted by one of the imams of recitation that differs from others, each established by chains to the Prophet.tap to reveal
How did the qirāʾāt arise?
Reliance in the Qurʾān is on talaqqī, trustworthy from trustworthy, to the Prophet. The first muṣḥafs were unpointed and unvowelled, and a word's shape could carry several readings; if not, it was written one way in one muṣḥaf and another in another. The Companions had received different aḥruf from the Prophet and scattered; ʿUthmān sent with each muṣḥaf a reader mostly following its reading; the Tābiʿūn took from them; then specialised imams of recitation appeared. Differences are minor relative to what is agreed, and within the seven aḥruf.tap to reveal
What types of reading are there?
Mutawātir, mashhūr, āḥād, shādhdh, mawḍūʿ, mudraj (al-Itqān). Al-Bulqīnī: mutawātir = the seven; āḥād = the three completing the ten and Companions' readings; shādhdh = Tābiʿūn like al-Aʿmash. Al-Suyūṭī says this has issues and prefers Ibn al-Jazarī.tap to reveal

Naskh in the Qurʾān: definition, occurrence, kinds, abrogation by Sunna, naskh of reports

Opens with the wisdom of gradual rulings (the Prophets as physicians of hearts) and al-Qāsimī's argument that naskh is a natural law like human growth. Tafsīr and taʾwīl: why we explain the Qurʾān, definitions, difference, three divisions

Opens the tafsīr chapter by arguing that the Qurʾān's blessing lies in reflection and guidance, not in reciting its words (citing 38:29, 47:24, 54:17), and that tafsīr is the way to its treasures. Defines tafsīr lughatan (īḍāḥ, tabyīn; 25:33) and iṣṭilāḥan (al-Zarkashī's and a second definition: the science that investigates the Qurʾān as it indicates God's intent as far as human capacity), defines taʾwīl (from awl, 'return'), reports that the early scholars used the two as synonyms (al-Ṭabarī, Mujāhid) while later scholars distinguish them, and adopts the view that tafsīr is the clear surface meanings while taʾwīl is what the learned deduce of hidden meanings and subtle divine secrets (al-Ālūsī). Tafsīr bi-al-dirāya (bi-al-raʾy): meaning, praiseworthy and blameworthy, conditions of the mufassir, permissibility

Defines tafsīr bi-al-dirāya (also called bi-al-raʾy or bi-al-maʿqūl): tafsīr by ijtihād based on Arabic, its idiom and the necessary sciences, not mere opinion or whim. It reconciles the threatening ḥadīths ('man qāla fī al-Qurʾān bi-raʾyihi...') with al-Qurṭubī's two readings, splits raʾy into maḥmūd (accords with the lawgiver's aim and Arabic) and madhmūm (without knowledge, by whim, to support a corrupt school, or by claiming certainty about what God kept to Himself), and gives al-Zarkashī's four pillars. The conditions of the mufassir are the fifteen sciences of al-Suyūṭī (grouped here by examples: language, naḥw, ṣarf/ishtiqāq, balāgha, uṣūl al-fiqh, asbāb, nāsikh/mansūkh, qirāʾāt, ʿilm al-mawhiba) with Qurʾānic and anecdotal illustrations (the Bedouin and 9:3; French 'trousers' translation of 2:187; the black and white threads). It adds Muḥammad ʿAbduh's two ranks of tafsīr, Ibn ʿAbbās's four wujūh, and the debate on permissibility (the prohibitors' four arguments, the jumhūr's five, and replies). New relative to Fawz, which has no discussion of the conditions of tafsīr.
What does 'raʾy' mean in tafsīr bi-al-raʾy?
فالتفسير المحمود: ما كان موافقا لغرض الشارع
ijtihād based on sound principles, which anyone entering tafsīr must observe; not mere opinion, nor whim, nor whatever comes to mind. al-Qurṭubī: whoever says about the Qurʾān what occurs to him without proof from the uṣūl is mistaken and blameworthy; and 'man qāla fī al-Qurʾān bi-raʾyihi fa-ʿaṣāba fa-qad akhṭaʾa' (Abū Dāwūd from Jundab).tap to reveal
How are the threatening ḥadīths reconciled with raʾy-tafsīr?
Ibn ʿAbbās's two readings via al-Qurṭubī: (1) saying something obscure about which the Companions and Tābiʿūn gave no view exposes one to God's anger; (2) saying what he knows the truth is otherwise. Al-Qurṭubī prefers the second and takes Jundab's ḥadīth as 'raʾy = hawā'. Ibn ʿAṭiyya: the ḥadīth does not stop linguists from explaining language, grammarians naḥw, jurists rulings, each by ijtihād on the rules of the science.tap to reveal

The third division of tafsīr. Tafsīr ishārī is taʾwīl of the Qurʾān contrary to its surface for subtle allusions that appear to some learned or to the 'ārifūn by ilhām, while still compatible with the apparent meaning. Scholars differ (permitted as perfection of īmān; forbidden as deviation); the author sorts out ishārī from bāṭinī. Evidence for permission: ʿUmar and Ibn ʿAbbās on al-Naṣr (the sūra announces the Prophet's death), Abū Bakr's weeping at the khuṭba. Iʿjāz: the Qurʾān as the lasting miracle, definition, conditions of a miracle, tahaddī

Opens the iʿjāz chapter by presenting the Qurʾān as Muḥammad's everlasting, rational miracle (as against the sensory miracles of Mūsā and ʿĪsā, al-Zarqānī, Muḥammad al-Bannā). It defines iʿjāz and muʿjiza, states the aim (proving the messenger's truth, not shaming humanity), gives the three requirements for iʿjāz to be established (tahaddī, an existing motive to answer, no obstacle), describes the descending challenge (whole Qurʾān, ten sūras, one sūra) and its two kinds (general to all creation, specific to the Arabs; and total or partial), argues that the Arabs had every motive and no obstacle (al-Bāqillānī), gives al-Zarqānī's definition and illustration of a miracle (Mūsā's staff, ʿĪsā's healing) and the five conditions of a true miracle. Why is the Qurʾān the Prophet's lasting miracle and how does it differ from earlier miracles?
Earlier prophets' miracles were sensory and fitted their times (Mūsā: hand and staff in an age of magic; ʿĪsā: healing and raising the dead in an age of medicine); Muḥammad's is a rational, spiritual miracle that remains. Bukhārī: 'every prophet was given signs by which people believed, but what I was given is revelation'. Al-Zarqānī: Muḥammad's miracles in the Qurʾān alone are thousands and remain; others are limited and only the Qurʾān reports them. Muḥammad al-Bannā: earlier prophets came to particular peoples, ending with each; his message is final and universal, so its miracle comes after human reason matured.tap to reveal

The aspects (wujūh) of iʿjāz: naẓm, uslūb, jazāla, tashrīʿ, ghayb, ʿulūm, wafāʾ, taʾthīr...

Sets out the views on what makes the Qurʾān inimitable: all Arabs agree it is inimitable by itself (not by ṣarfa); scholars differ whether the aspect is its naẓm, its fāṣāḥa and balāgha, its freedom from contradiction and its hidden meanings, or its fawātiḥ and khawātīm. Ṣarfa refuted; attempts at opposition; objections to iʿjāz and replies

Treats the ṣarfa view (al-Naẓẓām, some Muʿtazila, al-Murtaḍā; Ibn Ḥazm's variant) and refutes it in five arguments (iʿjāz would lie in the ṣarfa, not the Qurʾān; it would be taʿjīz not iʿjāz; opponents would not have fought him; they would have announced the obstacle; later Arabists could have opposed it), and cites al-Qurṭubī's rejection (consensus that the Qurʾān is the miracle). How is the ṣarfa view refuted?

Are there non-Arabic words in the Qurʾān?

Short discussion. Scholars agree the Qurʾān is Arabic (26:195, 41:3, 12:2, 39:28) but differ on whether single words in it are foreign. First view (jumhūr: al-Bāqillānī, al-Ṭabarī): it is entirely Arabic; shared words result from tawārud al-lughāt. Second: some words are non-Arabic but few and do not remove its Arabic character, e.g. mishkāt (Ethiopic: niche), kifl (portion), qaswara (lion), qisṭās (Roman: scale), sijjīl (Persian), ghassāq (Turkish), yamm and ṭūr (Syriac: sea, mountain). Ibn ʿAṭiyya reconciles: they were foreign originally but the Arabs used and Arabised them, so they became Arabic. The author adopts the first view (also al-Qurṭubī's argument: if the Arabs did not know them God would not have addressed them, and if they knew them they are theirs) and gives five proofs. Fawz's nuzūl ʿarabī (rule g_naql) covers the Arabic language of revelation in general; this is the specific loanword debate.

Does the Qurʾān contain individual non-Arabic words?
First, the jumhūr led by al-Bāqillānī and al-Ṭabarī: no, every word is Arabic; apparent loans came through languages coinciding. Second, a group: a few words are foreign without affecting its Arabic character, like mishkāt, kifl and qaswara (Ethiopic), qisṭās (Roman), sijjīl (Persian), ghassāq (Turkish), yamm and ṭūr (Syriac); Ibn ʿAṭiyya: they were originally foreign but Arabised and used by the Arabs, so Arabic by that route. Translation of the Qurʾān: kinds, conditions, rulings

Concluding chapter. Defines tarjamat al-Qurʾān as transferring the Qurʾān to non-Arabic languages for those who cannot read Arabic, and divides it into ḥarfiyya (literal, word for word) and tafsīriyya (rendering the meaning). The author lists four conditions for any translation (translator knows both languages; knows the styles and traits of the languages; the translation's form can replace the original; it fully conveys meanings and intentions), plus two extra for literal translation (full corresponding vocabulary; similar pronouns and connectors). From this he rules literal translation not permissible, giving three reasons (Qurʾān may not be written in other letters; other languages lack equivalents; wording-only translation spoils the meaning) with examples: 17:29 (hand tied to the neck), 17:24 (wing of humility), 10:2, 54:14, 2:187 (the French 'trousers' incident, also mentioned in section tibyan_s12_raay). Translation of meaning is permitted, even obligatory to convey God's guidance, but it is not 'Qurʾān' but tafsīr (God made us worship by the Qurʾān's wording; the Sunna can be narrated by meaning but not the Qurʾān). The book ends. Fawz mentions its author's own Persian translation; the later book supplies the conditions and ruling.
What is tarjamat al-Qurʾān and how many kinds?
Transferring the Qurʾān to non-Arabic languages and printing it so non-Arabs can understand God's meaning. Two kinds: ḥarfiyya and tafsīriyya.tap to reveal
What conditions must a translation meet?
(1) the translator knows both languages; (2) he is versed in the styles and features of the languages; (3) the translation's form is sound so that it could replace the original; (4) it fully conveys all meanings and aims of the original. For literal translation add: (5) complete vocabulary in the target language equal to the source; (6) similar hidden pronouns and connectors that bind sentences.tap to reveal
Is literal translation permitted?

No, it is neither permitted nor sound: (1) the Qurʾān may not be written in non-Arabic letters, lest taḥrīf; (2) other languages lack words, pronouns and structures equivalent to Arabic; (3) sticking to words can spoil meaning and structure. Examples: 17:29 'wa-lā tajʿal yadaka maghlūlatan' would read as a ban on tying the hand to the neck; 17:24 'janāḥ al-dhull' is an istiʿāra makniyya; 10:2 'qadam ṣidq'; 54:14 'tajrī bi-aʿyuninā'; and 2:187 'hunna libās lakum', literal translation turning into nonsense.tap to reveal

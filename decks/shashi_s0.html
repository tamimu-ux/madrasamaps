Muqaddima · al-Khāṣṣ wa al-ʿĀmm

The four roots of fiqh and the first division of the Book: the khāṣṣ (a word coined for one known meaning) is definitive and is not set aside by a solitary report or analogy; the ʿāmm is definitive until partly specified, after which it becomes probable and may be further specified down to a third.
What is the definition of khass (specific) in usul al-fiqh?
فالخاص لفظ وضع لِمَعْنى مَعْلُوم أَو لمسمى مَعْلُوم على الِانْفِرَاد
Khass: a word coined for a known meaning or known denotatum in isolation. Three sub-levels: individual (Zayd), species (rajul/man), genus (insan/human being). Its dalala (signification) is qat'i (definitive).tap to reveal
What is the definition of 'amm (general) in usul al-fiqh?
وَالْعَام كل لفظ يَنْتَظِم جمعا من الْأَفْرَاد إِمَّا لفظا كَقَوْلِنَا مُسلمُونَ وَإِمَّا معنى كَقَوْلِنَا من وَمَا
'Amm: every word that encompasses a plurality of individuals — either in form (e.g. 'muslimun') or by meaning (e.g. 'man', 'ma'). The 'amm covers all individuals within its scope unless a specifier (mukhassis) excludes some.tap to reveal
What is the ruling when a khass (specific) from the Quran conflicts with khabar al-wahid or qiyas?
وَحكم الْخَاص من الْكتاب وجوب الْعَمَل بِهِ لَا محَالة فَإِن قابله خبر الْوَاحِد أَو الْقيَاس فَإِن أمكن الْجمع بَينهمَا بِدُونِ تَغْيِير فِي حكم الْخَاص يعْمل بهما وَإِلَّا يعْمل بِالْكتاب وَيتْرك مَا يُقَابله
Acting upon the khass from the Quran is obligatory without exception (la mahalah). If khabar al-wahid or qiyas contradicts it: (1) if reconciliation is possible without changing the khass's ruling, both are acted upon; (2) if irreconcilable, the Quran prevails and the opposing hadith/qiyas is abandoned. FIQH EXAMPLE: 'thalatha quru'' (Quran 2:228) = three periods, definitive. Shafi'i interpretation (= two full purities + part) would change the khass — so that reading is rejected.tap to reveal

al-Muṭlaq wa al-Muqayyad

The unrestricted word is applied as unrestricted: nothing is added to it by a solitary report or analogy; the report is acted on in a way that leaves the Book's ruling intact (niyya and tartīb in wuḍūʾ as sunna, exile as siyāsa, wuḍūʾ for ṭawāf as wājib).
Can a condition be added to a mutlaq (unrestricted) command in the Quran by means of khabar al-wahid or qiyas?
الْمُطلق من كتاب الله تَعَالَى إِذا أمكن الْعَمَل بِإِطْلَاقِهِ فَالزِّيَادَة عَلَيْهِ بِخَبَر الْوَاحِد وَالْقِيَاس لَا يجوز
No. When a mutlaq command can be acted upon in its absolute form, adding a condition to it via khabar al-wahid or qiyas is impermissible. The added element from hadith is instead classified at a lower level (wajib or sunnah), not as a condition for the Quranic fard. FIQH EXAMPLES: (1) Wudu': 'wash your faces' (absolute) → niyyah is sunnah from hadith, not fard. (2) Hadd al-zina: 100 lashes (Quran) → taghrib is siyasa from hadith, not hadd. (3) Tawaf: absolute → wudu' for tawaf is wajib from hadith (compensated by dam if left); not fard. (4) Ruku': absolute → ta'dil al-arkan is wajib from hadith, not fard.tap to reveal
When one Quranic text is absolute (mutlaq) and another is restricted (muqayyad) on the same topic, how are they handled?
الْمُطلق يجْرِي على إِطْلَاقه والمقيد على تَقْيِيده
Each runs independently: the absolute on its absoluteness and the restricted on its restriction. They are not brought together by qiyas. FIQH EXAMPLE: Kaffarat al-zihar/yamin: 'raqaba' (slave) = absolute, no faith condition. Kaffarat al-qatl: 'raqaba mu'mina' (believing slave) = restricted. By Hanafi rule, the restriction in qatl is not imported to zihar/yamin by qiyas.tap to reveal
2 · Case clinic · which bāb, rule, terms?

A man performs zihar (declares his wife 'like his mother's back'). He cannot fast and wishes to free a slave as kaffara. His only available slave is a non-Muslim. Is the kaffara valid?

Step 1 — Identify the Quranic text: kaffarat al-zihar requires 'tahrir raqaba' (freeing a slave) — Quran 58:3, using mutlaq (absolute) — no condition of faith. Step 2 — Potential restriction: kaffarat al-qatl (60:92) says 'raqaba mu'mina' (believing slave). Could this restriction be imported by qiyas? Step 3 — Apply principle: mutlaq and muqayyad run independently; restrictions are not imported from muqayyad to mutlaq by qiyas. Step 4 — Ruling: freeing the non-Muslim slave is valid kaffara for zihar (Hanafi). Note: Shafi'i disagrees (requires Muslim slave, by qiyas with qatl).discuss first — then tap

al-Mushtarak wa al-Muʾawwal

A word with several coined meanings: with no indicator one suspends and reflects; once one sense is preferred by dominant opinion it becomes muʾawwal (probable); if the speaker clarifies it, it becomes mufassar (certain). Example: qurūʾ.
What is the mushtarak and what is its ruling?
الْمُشْتَرك مَا وضع لمعنيين مُخْتَلفين أَو لمعان مُخْتَلفَة الْحَقَائِق
Mushtarak: a word coined for two or more meanings with different underlying realities. Ruling: it has no 'umum (generality). When one meaning is intended, the other(s) are necessarily dropped. When context fixes one meaning, that becomes the operative reading. FIQH EXAMPLE: Abu Hanifa — 'anta 'alayya mithl ummi': mushtarak between 'honor' (karama) and 'prohibition' (hurma) → dhihar requires intent (niyyah) to establish the hurma meaning.tap to reveal
Does a mushtarak (homonym) carry 'umum (generality covering all its meanings)?
لَا عُمُوم للمشترك أصلا فَيسْقط اعْتِبَار الصُّورَة لِاسْتِحَالَة الْجمع
No. The mushtarak has no 'umum at all. When one meaning is intended, the other is dropped because combining both meanings simultaneously is impossible. FIQH EXAMPLE: Quran 5:95 on jaza' al-sayd: 'mithl' = mushtarak between 'equivalent in form' and 'equivalent in value (qima)'. The text intends 'value' → obligation of equivalent in form (nazir) drops.tap to reveal
What is the ruling on mu'awwal (the interpreted sense of a mushtarak)?
حكم المؤول وجوب الْعَمَل بِهِ مَعَ احْتِمَال الْخَطَأ
Acting on the mu'awwal is obligatory with the acknowledgment of a possible error. FIQH EXAMPLE: When a price is stated absolutely in a sale without specifying currency, it is mu'awwal as referring to the dominant local currency (ghalib naqd al-balad) — acting on this interpretation is obligatory, though the scholar making the ta'wil may have erred. Related: if no dominant currency exists, the sale is fasid because ta'wil is impossible.tap to reveal
3 · Case clinic · which bāb, rule, terms?

A merchant in a city with two circulating currencies (Pakistani rupees and dollars) agrees to sell goods for 'ten' without specifying the currency. No dominant currency exists. Is the sale valid?

Step 1 — Identify the issue: the price ('thaman') is mushtarak — it could mean rupees or dollars. Step 2 — Apply mu'awwal rule: the principle is that an unspecified price is interpreted (ta'wil) as the dominant local currency (ghalib naqd al-balad). Step 3 — Apply limiting condition: if no dominant currency exists (both equally used), ta'wil is impossible. Step 4 — Ruling: the sale is fasid (defective) due to unresolvable ambiguity in the price, per al-Shashi's own example.discuss first — then tap

al-Ḥaqīqa wa al-Majāz

Literal vs figurative meaning: the three kinds of ḥaqīqa (impossible, abandoned, in use); majāz is taken in the first two by agreement; in the third, with a customary majāz, Abū Ḥanīfa prefers the ḥaqīqa while the Ṣāḥibayn prefer the general majāz (the wheat oath). Ḥaqīqa and majāz cannot both be meant by one word.
What is haqiqa and what is majaz in the terminology of usul al-fiqh?
كل لفظ وَضعه وَاضع اللُّغَة بِإِزَاءِ شَيْء فَهُوَ حَقِيقَة لَهُ وَلَو اسْتعْمل فِي غَيره يكون مجَازًا لَا حَقِيقَة
Haqiqa: every word coined by the language-originator (wadi' al-lugha) for a particular thing — that is its haqiqa. Majaz: when the same word is used for something other than what it was coined for. The two are mutually exclusive at any given time in a single word.tap to reveal
Can the literal (haqiqa) and metaphorical (majaz) meanings both be intended from a single word at the same time?
ثمَّ الْحَقِيقَة مَعَ الْمجَاز لَا يَجْتَمِعَانِ ارادة من لفظ وَاحِد فِي حَالَة وَاحِدَة
No — they cannot coexist as intended meanings of one word at one time. When the majaz is intended, the haqiqa reading is dropped, and vice versa. FIQH EXAMPLES: (1) Hadith: 'Do not sell a dirham for two dirhams, nor a sa' for two sa's' — 'sa'' here is majaz (for what enters into/is measured by the sa'); so the actual container itself drops out → selling one sa'-vessel for two is permitted. (2) Ayat al-mulamasa (Quran 4:43 and 5:6): 'mulamas' is intended as wiqā' (intercourse) → touching with the hand drops out as a trigger for wudu'.tap to reveal
What are the three types of haqiqa, and when is majaz used for each?
الْحَقِيقَة أَنْوَاع ثَلَاثَة متعذرة ومهجورة ومستعملة وَفِي الْقسمَيْنِ الْأَوَّلين يُصَار إِلَى الْمجَاز بالِاتِّفَاقِ
Three types: (1) Muta'adhdhira (impossible/impractical): majaz by consensus. E.g. 'I will not eat from this tree' → fruit of the tree. (2) Mahjura (abandoned by 'urf/custom): majaz by consensus. E.g. 'I will not set foot in X's house' → entering the house. (3) Musta'mala (in current use): if no mu'taraf majaz → haqiqa by consensus. If mu'taraf majaz exists → dispute (Abu Hanifa vs Sahibayn).tap to reveal
4 · Case clinic · which bāb, rule, terms?

A person swears: 'I will not eat from this bag of wheat.' He later eats bread that was baked from that same wheat. Has he breached his oath?

Step 1 — Identify word type: 'this wheat' (al-hinta) has a haqiqa (the grain itself) that is musta'mala (in active use) AND a mu'taraf majaz (what the wheat produces, including bread). Step 2 — Apply dispute: Abu Hanifa (khalaf fi haqq al-lafz) → haqiqa still operative; bread is not 'the wheat' → no breach. Sahibayn (khalaf fi haqq al-hukm) → 'umum al-majaz extends to wheat-products → breach. Step 3 — Hanafi ruling (following Abu Hanifa): no breach by eating bread from that wheat.discuss first — then tap

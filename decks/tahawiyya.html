al-ʿAqīda al-Ṭaḥāwiyya

The whole matn in order: tawḥīd and attributes, qadar, the Qurʾān as kalām Allāh, ruʾya, prophethood, isrāʾ/miʿrāj, ḥawḍ, shafāʿa, mīthāq, ʿarsh/kursī, angels/books, īmān, kabāʾir, imāma, ṣaḥāba, awliyāʾ, ashrāṭ al-sāʿa, the grave and ākhira, istiṭāʿa, afʿāl al-ʿibād, duʿāʾ, closing warnings. Explained from the Ḥanafī-Māturīdī reading; other positions stated neutrally in khilāf.

1 · Re-teach

Tawḥīd: oneness, no partner, no likeness

Al-Ṭaḥāwī opens by naming the book as the creed of Abū Ḥanīfa, Abū Yūsuf and Muḥammad al-Shaybānī, then lays the foundation: Allah is one, without partner, without likeness, without incapacity and without another god. These four negations are the skeleton of the rest of the creed: every later attribute is affirmed on the basis that nothing resembles Him. In the Māturīdī reading, tawḥīd means oneness in essence, attributes and acts, together with the denial of tashbīh (anthropomorphism) and taʿṭīl (stripping the attributes).
What is the first statement of the creed of ahl al-sunna?
نقول في توحيد الله معتقدين بتوفيق الله: إن الله واحدٌ لا شريك له
Allah is one with no partner, in His essence, His attributes and His acts.tap to reveal
Does anything resemble Allah?
ولا شيء مثله
No. Nothing is like Him in essence, attribute or act; any similarity is only in word.tap to reveal
Is anything beyond Allah's power?
ولا شيء يعجزه
Nothing can make Him incapable; His power covers every possible (mumkin) thing.tap to reveal

2 · Re-teach

Foundational attributes: eternity, subsistence, will, life

This stretch lists the attributes that follow from tawḥīd: Allah is eternal without beginning (qadīm), everlasting without end, never perishes, nothing occurs except what He wills, minds cannot grasp Him, He is unlike creation, living and self-subsisting. Qidam and baqāʾ are two of the classical sifāt salbiyya (negational attributes) in Ḥanafī-Māturīdī texts such as Sharḥ al-ʿAqāʾid; ḥayāt and qayyūmiyya belong to the real attributes. The statement that only what He wills occurs prepares the qadar discussion.

Divine acts: creating, providing, giving death, resurrecting

The matn affirms that Allah creates without need, provides without effort, gives death without fear and resurrects without difficulty. Each is an act (fiʿl) of Allah that shows complete power and independence. Al-Māturīdī grounds all such acts in a single eternal attribute, takwīn (bringing into being), which is the Ḥanafī-Māturīdī specificity; Ashʿarīs derive them from qudra and irāda.
Does Allah create out of need?

No; He creates and provides without any need or effort.tap to reveal

4 · Re-teach

Eternity of the attributes and the divine names

Ṭaḥāwī states that Allah has always had His attributes before creation, and that creating did not add to Him anything He lacked: He was Creator before creation as He is Giver of life before giving life. This is the key refutation of the view that attributes of act are newly originated in Allah. In the Ḥanafī-Māturīdī reading it is the basis for the eternity of takwīn: khāliqiyya precedes the created thing. Ashʿarīs reach a similar conclusion through the eternal qudra and irāda; Muʿtazilīs reject eternal attributes.
Were Allah's attributes ever absent before creation?
ما زال بصفاته قديمًا قبل خلقه
No; He was always described by all His attributes before creating.tap to reveal
Did creation increase Him in anything?
لم يزدد بكونهم شيئًا، لم يكن قبلهم من صفته
No; creation added nothing to Him.tap to reveal
Was He 'Creator' only after creating?
ليس بعد خلق الخلق استفاد اسم (الخالق)
No; He deserved the name Creator before He created.tap to reveal

5 · Re-teach

Qadar I: knowledge, decree, will, guidance and misguidance

Here begins the first treatment of qadar: Allah created creation with His knowledge, decreed their measures and fixed their lifespans, knew what they would do before creating them, and commanded obedience and forbade disobedience. Everything runs by His decree and His will is effective; creatures only will what He wills for them. He guides, preserves and grants wellbeing by grace and misguides, forsakes and tests by justice. All of them turn between His grace and His justice. The Ḥanafī-Māturīdī reading distinguishes will (irāda), command (amr), and approval (riḍā), and keeps human choice as real while placing creation in Allah's hand.
Did Allah know what people would do before they existed?
وعلم ما هم عاملون قبل أن يخلقهم
Yes; His knowledge of everything precedes creation.tap to reveal
If everything is decreed, why commands and prohibitions?
وأمرهم بطاعته، ونهاهم عن معصيته
Because the servant has a real, acquired choice (kasb); decree is not coercion.tap to reveal
What is the extent of qadar?
وكل شيء يجري بتقديره
Everything, good and bad, runs by His decree.tap to reveal

Closing qadar I: surrender and transcendence

Ṭaḥāwī closes the first qadar block: all creatures turn between His grace and justice; He is exalted above opposites and equals; none can reverse His decree, appeal His judgement or overcome His command. We believe in all this and are certain that every thing is from Him. This is the stance of taslīm (surrender) which Ṭaḥāwī will repeat throughout.

Prophethood of Muḥammad ﷺ and its finality

Ṭaḥāwī turns from qadar to nubuwwa: Muḥammad ﷺ is Allah's chosen servant, elected prophet and approved messenger; the seal of the prophets, imām of the godfearing, master of messengers and beloved of the Lord of the worlds. Every later claim to prophethood is deviation and desire (ghayy and hawā). He was sent to all jinn and all humankind with truth, guidance, light and radiance. The Ḥanafī-Māturīdī reading ties this to the creed that revelation is the binding source after reason has proved the Creator, and to the Qurʾānic khātam al-nabiyyīn.

No prophet or messenger, old or new, comes after him; his sharia is final.tap to reveal
What is the status of a claim to prophethood after him?

It is ghayy and hawā, and its claimant and its believers are outside Islam.tap to reveal

8 · Re-teach

The Qurʾān is the speech of Allah

Ṭaḥāwī affirms that the Qurʾān is the kalām of Allah; it began from Him as speech without kayfiyya, He sent it down upon His messenger as revelation, the believers confirmed it and are certain it is truly His speech, uncreated unlike the speech of creatures. Whoever hears it and claims it is human speech has disbelieved, as Allah censured the one who said 'this is only human speech' and threatened him with Saqar. Whoever describes Allah with a human attribute has disbelieved. The Ḥanafī-Māturīdī position distinguishes the eternal kalām nafsī (inner speech, an attribute of Allah) from the recited words and letters (ḥādith as acts of the reciter), a distinction first articulated by the Māturīdīs and Ashʿarīs against the Muʿtazila and the Ḥanābila.

No. As Allah's speech it is an eternal attribute; letters and sounds recited by us are created.tap to reveal

He disbelieves.tap to reveal
What of one who describes Allah with a human attribute?
ومن وصف الله بمعنى من معاني البشر، فقد كفر
He disbelieves; likeness (tashbīh) is rejected as strongly as denial.tap to reveal

9 · Re-teach

The vision of Allah, submission, and transcendence

Ṭaḥāwī affirms the vision of Allah (ruʾya) for the people of Paradise, without encompassing and without kayfiyya, as the Qurʾān says: 'Faces that day will be radiant, looking at their Lord'. Its meaning is as Allah willed and knew; whatever sound ḥadīth reports is as the Messenger said. We do not enter into this by ta'wīl of our opinions or by conjecture of our whims. Safety in religion lies in submission (taslīm); one who does not guard against negation (nafy) and likening (tashbīh) slips and misses tanzīh. Allah is described with oneness and singularity; He is far above limits, ends, pillars, organs and instruments; the six directions do not contain Him. Ḥanafī-Māturīdī: ruʾya is a rational possibility (the Māturīdīs prove it by reason and naql) and real, without direction or likeness; Muʿtazila deny it.
Will believers see Allah in the hereafter?
والرؤية حق لأهل الجنة، بغير إحاطة ولا كيفية
Yes, in Paradise, without encompassing and without kayfiyya.tap to reveal

As the Messenger said, with meaning as Allah intended.tap to reveal

Miʿrāj, the Pond, intercession and the primordial covenant

Ṭaḥāwī affirms that the Miʿrāj is true: the Prophet ﷺ was taken by night and ascended in person, while awake, to the heaven and then to wherever Allah willed, where Allah honoured him and revealed what He revealed. The Pond (ḥawḍ) with which Allah honoured him as relief for his Ummah is true; so is the intercession (shafāʿa) He stored up for them, as reported in the narrations; and the covenant (mīthāq) taken from Ādam and his offspring. The Ḥanafī-Māturīdī reading holds the Miʿrāj to be bodily and in wakefulness, established by mutawātir Isrāʾ and mashhūr ḥadīth; the ascent through the heavens is proved by ḥadīth, and denying the Isrāʾ to al-Aqṣā is kufr, whereas the details of the ascent are mashhūr.
Was the Miʿrāj physical and in wakefulness?
وعرج بشخصه في اليقظة إلى السماء
Yes, in person (body and spirit) and while awake.tap to reveal
Is the Pond real?
والحوض الذي أكرمه الله تعالى به -غياثًا لأمته- حق
Yes; true and literal, given to the Prophet.tap to reveal
Is intercession true?
والشفاعة التي ادخرها لهم حق، كما رُوي في الأخبار
Yes, stored up for the Ummah by Allah's permission.tap to reveal

Qadar II: prior knowledge, the Tablet and the Pen

Ṭaḥāwī returns to qadar. Allah knew from eternity the number of those who enter Paradise and Fire in one comprehensive count; it neither increases nor decreases; likewise their actions He knew they would do. 'Everyone is eased to what he was created for'; actions are by their endings. The felicitous is made so by Allah's decree and the wretched likewise. The root of qadar is Allah's secret; neither an angel nor a prophet has been told it. Deep probing is a path to abandonment, a ladder of deprivation, a degree of rebellion; Allah folded its knowledge from His creatures. Knowledge is of two types: existing in creation and absent from it; denying the first and claiming the second are both kufr. We believe in the Tablet, Pen and all it records; if all creatures gathered to alter what Allah wrote or to bring about what He did not write, they could not. The pen has dried. Ḥanafī-Māturīdī: Allah's knowledge does not compel; He knows what creatures will freely choose.

وقد علم الله تعالى فيما لم يزل عدد من يدخل الجنة، وعدد من يدخل النار جملة واحدة، فلا يزداد في ذلك العدد، ولا ينقص منه
No; He knows what each will do without forcing the act.tap to reveal
What does 'each is eased to what he was created for' mean?
وكلٌ ميسرٌ لما خُلق له
Each finds easy the path destined, yet remains accountable.tap to reveal
Is felicity and wretchedness by Allah's decree?
والسعيد من سعد بقضاء الله، والشقي من شقي بقضاء الله
Yes; decree precedes, yet the person does the act by his choice.tap to reveal

Ahl al-qibla and the reality of īmān

Ṭaḥāwī calls the people of our qibla Muslims and believers as long as they acknowledge what the Prophet ﷺ brought and affirm all he said. We do not plunge into Allah, nor argue in Allah's religion or the Qurʾān, which is the speech of the Lord of the worlds, brought down by the Trustworthy Spirit; we do not say it is created. The jamāʿa, the one religion, the middle path and the close

Ṭaḥāwī concludes: we hold the jamāʿa as right and correct, and division as deviation and punishment. Allah's religion in earth and heaven is one, the religion of Islam: 'The religion with Allah is Islam' and 'I have approved Islam for you as a religion'. It lies between ghuluw and taqṣīr, tashbīh and taʿṭīl, jabr and qadar, amn and yaʾs. This is our religion and belief outwardly and inwardly; we are free before Allah from whoever opposes what we have stated. We ask Allah to keep us firm on īmān, seal our lives with it and protect us from conflicting desires, scattered opinions and base schools such as Mushabbiha, Muʿtazila, Jahmiyya, Jabriyya and Qadariyya and others who opposed the Sunna and the Jamāʿa. The Ḥanafī-Māturīdī reading: this is the creed of Abū Ḥanīfa, Abū Yūsuf and Muḥammad (Ṭaḥāwī writes on their behalf); the path is the middle between extremes; the passages naming the groups show the main Muslim sects of the 4th century.

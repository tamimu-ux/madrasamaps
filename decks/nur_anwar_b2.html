ʿĀmm — the Manār's definition and what each clause excludes

Shāshī only gives a short working definition of ʿāmm. Nūr al-Anwār goes clause by clause through the Manār's definition (mā yatanāwal afrādan muttafiqat al-ḥudūd ʿalā sabīl al-shumūl), saying what 'yatanāwal afrādan' excludes (khāṣṣ, asmāʾ al-ʿadad, the mushtarak) and that the last two phrases explain the reality of ʿāmm rather than exclude anything. The al-Bushrā ḥāshiya records the disputes behind the wording: whether istighrāq is required (Fakhr al-Islām: no; the author of al-Tawḍīḥ: yes), whether 'agreement of ḥudūd' separates ʿāmm from mushtarak, and whether ʿumūm can describe meanings as well as words.
What does each clause of 'mā yatanāwal afrādan muttafiqat al-ḥudūd ʿalā sabīl al-shumūl' exclude?
ما يتناول أفرادا متفقة الحدود على سبيل الشمول
'Yatanāwal afrādan' excludes khāṣṣ al-ʿayn (one individual), khāṣṣ al-jins/nawʿ (a universal meaning, not a set of afrād), asmāʾ al-ʿadad (they cover ajzāʾ, not afrād) and the mushtarak (it covers maʿānī, not afrād). 'Muttafiqat al-ḥudūd ʿalā sabīl al-shumūl' is, for Mullā Jīwan, a statement of what the ʿāmm is (taḥqīq māhiyya), not a further exclusion.tap to reveal
Must istighrāq be part of the definition of ʿāmm?
لفظ وضع وضعا واحدا لكثير غير محصور مستغرق لجميع ما يصلح له
The Manār follows Fakhr al-Islām: istighrāq (covering every individual) is not required, ʿāmm is a word that covers (yatanāwal) a number of afrād, so a plural indefinite (rijāl) is ʿāmm. Others, the majority of the verifiers per the sharḥ, require istighrāq and define ʿāmm as 'a word set by one waḍʿ for a large unrestricted number, covering everything it can apply to'.tap to reveal
Is 'muttafiqat al-ḥudūd' a sound mark separating ʿāmm from mushtarak?
Mullā Jīwan: 'muttafiqat al-ḥudūd ʿalā sabīl al-shumūl' explains the reality of ʿāmm and is not an iḥtirāz. Others say it is an iḥtirāz from the mushtarak, whose afrād differ in definition. The ḥāshiya objects to Mullā Jīwan's phrasing: since the Manār itself defines the mushtarak as 'mā yatanāwal afrādan mukhtalifat al-ḥudūd', the correct statement is that the mushtarak is excluded by 'muttafiqat al-ḥudūd', unless one says that the ʿāmm is set for one meaning shared among its afrād, while the mushtarak is set for several meanings by several waḍʿs.tap to reveal

Ruling of the unspecified ʿāmm: qaṭʿī, equal to khāṣṣ

Shāshī only says an ʿāmm from which nothing has been excluded is qaṭʿī. Nūr al-Anwār unpacks the Manār's sentence ('wa-innahu yūjib al-ḥukm fīmā yatanāwaluh qaṭʿan'), assigning each word a rebuttal: 'yūjib' against those who make it mujmal and suspend (waqf), 'fīmā yatanāwaluh' against those who accept only the minimum, and 'qaṭʿan' against the Shāfiʿī view that it is ẓannī because almost every ʿāmm has been specified. It then derives the corollary that the ʿāmm is as strong as the khāṣṣ and may therefore abrogate it, with the ʿUraniyyīn/istanzihū hadith as the example.
Which opponent does each word of 'yūjib al-ḥukm fīmā yatanāwaluh qaṭʿan' answer?
وإنه يوجب الحكم فيما يتناوله قطعا
'Yūjib al-ḥukm' refutes those who call the ʿāmm mujmal (because plurals differ in number) and so suspend until a proof appears. 'Fīmā yatanāwaluh' refutes those who say it proves only the least certain portion (one for a singular, three for a plural) and suspend the rest. 'Qaṭʿan' refutes al-Shāfiʿī, for whom it is ẓannī, since every ʿāmm has had something specified from it, so acting on it is required but certain knowledge is not.tap to reveal
What is the Shāfiʿī reason for treating ʿāmm as ẓannī?
Almost every ʿāmm in the sharʿ has had something specified from it (mā min ʿāmm illā wa-qad khuṣṣa minhu al-baʿḍ, e.g. 'faqtulū al-mushrikīn' with the dhimmīs and others excluded). So in every ʿāmm there is the possibility that part of it is not intended, and what is certain is only the least number; the rest is proven by ẓann as in khabar al-wāḥid and qiyās, requiring ʿamal not ʿilm.tap to reveal
How do the Ḥanafīs answer the argument from kathrat al-takhṣīṣ?
The possibility of specification in a ʿāmm that has not been specified is an iḥtimāl that does not arise from a dalīl; such a possibility is not taken into account (and a conjectured khuṣūṣ does not undo the certain ṣīgha). Hence before takhṣīṣ the ʿāmm remains qaṭʿī.tap to reveal

Furūʿ on the unspecified ʿāmm: the ring, matrūk al-tasmiya, man dakhalahu kāna āminan

Nūr al-Anwār illustrates the principle 'ʿāmm is qaṭʿī and equals khāṣṣ, and a qaṭʿī ʿāmm is not specified by qiyās or khabar al-wāḥid' with three furūʿ. (1) A bequest of a ring to one man and then of its stone to another: khātam is ʿāmm over ḥalqa and faṣṣ, the faṣṣ is khāṣṣ, they are equal, so the stone is shared if the second bequest is disconnected but goes to the second alone if connected (bayān). (2) 'Wa-lā taʾkulū mimmā lam yudhkar ismu Allāhi ʿalayhi' and (3) 'wa-man dakhalahu kāna āminan': Mullā Jīwan states how the Shāfiʿīs are said to have specified these ʿāmm texts by qiyās and khabar al-wāḥid, and shows why the Ḥanafī position does not do so.
A man bequeaths a ring to X and then, separately, the stone of the same ring to Y. Who gets what?
وإذا أوصى بخاتم لإنسان ثم بالفص منه لآخر
If the second bequest is a separate statement: the ḥalqa goes to X alone, and the faṣṣ is shared between X and Y. If the second is connected (mawṣūl) to the first: it is a bayān and the faṣṣ goes to Y alone.tap to reveal
How does matrūk al-tasmiya illustrate the rule that ʿāmm is not specified by qiyās or khabar al-wāḥid?
ولا تأكلوا مما لم يذكر اسم الله عليه
The Ḥanafī statement: 'mā' is ʿāmm, covering both deliberate and forgetful omission, so by its ʿumūm no matrūk al-tasmiya may be eaten (Mālik's line). The Ḥanafīs allow the forgetful case on the basis of a hadith, treating the forgetful as one who has remembered. They claim Shāfiʿī also took the deliberate case out by qiyās on the forgetful and by khabar al-wāḥid ('the Muslim's name suffices him'), which is not permitted because a qaṭʿī ʿāmm may not be specified by a ẓannī proof.tap to reveal
Does 'wa-man dakhalahu kāna āminan' (3:97) protect an offender in the Ḥaram?
ومن دخله كان آمنا
Ḥanafī statement of Shāfiʿī: 'man' is ʿāmm and includes whoever enters, including a killer who killed before entering and one who cut limbs; Shāfiʿī specified those by qiyās on the killer inside and by khabar al-wāḥid ('the Ḥaram does not protect a sinner nor a fugitive after bloodshed'), so only safety from Hell would remain. Ḥanafī answer: we do not specify the ʿāmm either by qiyās or khabar al-wāḥid: the amān is for the dhāt (the person), limbs are in the position of property, so qiṣāṣ in limbs is carried out in the Ḥaram, but not the killing of one who entered after an offence against life.tap to reveal

Takhṣīṣ — definition, istiqlāl, muqārana, and what is not takhṣīṣ

Shāshī uses takhṣīṣ without defining it. Nūr al-Anwār gives the iṣṭilāḥī definition (qaṣr al-ʿāmm ʿalā baʿḍ musammayātihi bi-kalām mustaqill mawṣūl). The ḥāshiya explains that a mustaqill mukhaṣṣiṣ stands alone (unlike ghāya, sharṭ, istithnāʾ, ṣifa, badal al-baʿḍ), that muqārana separates takhṣīṣ from naskh and from the change of the ʿāmm from qaṭʿī to ẓannī, and that limits that come from ʿaql, ḥiss, ʿāda or an excess or deficiency in some afrād are not takhṣīṣ in the term and do not make the ʿāmm ẓannī.
How is takhṣīṣ defined in the usūlī term?
قصر العام على بعض مسمياته بكلام مستقل موصول
Takhṣīṣ, in the term, is restricting the ʿāmm to part of what it names (musammayāt) by an independent, connected speech. If the limit comes from ʿaql, ʿāda or the like, or from a non-independent element, it is not takhṣīṣ in the technical sense (though the word is often used loosely).tap to reveal
Must the mukhaṣṣiṣ be mustaqill, and is istithnāʾ/sharṭ/ṣifa/ghāya takhṣīṣ?
Those who require istiqlāl exclude istithnāʾ, sharṭ, ṣifa, ghāya and badal al-baʿḍ from takhṣīṣ proper. An independent mukhaṣṣiṣ is, for example, 'wa-man kāna marīḍan aw ʿalā safar…' after 'faman shahida minkum al-shahr falyaṣumhu'.tap to reveal
Why must the mukhaṣṣiṣ be connected (muqārin)?
Mullā Jīwan makes the mukhaṣṣiṣ connected (mawṣūl). The reasons given for the condition: (1) a delayed mukhaṣṣiṣ is naskh, not takhṣīṣ; (2) takhṣīṣ turns the ʿāmm from qaṭʿī to ẓannī, which is bayān taghyīr, and bayān taghyīr is valid only if connected; (3) a bare ʿāmm means the whole is meant, and delaying the khuṣūṣ would make the first statement false, which is unfit for the Lawgiver.tap to reveal

Ruling of the ʿāmm after takhṣīṣ: three schools and the khiyār-sale analogy

Shāshī only states that an ʿāmm from which something has been excluded can still be acted on. Nūr al-Anwār states the mukhtār: after takhṣīṣ, whether the exclusion is known or unknown, the ʿāmm is no longer qaṭʿī but remains a ḥujja. It then lays out three schools, each with an analogy from sales: the Manār's mukhtār (ẓannī yet ḥujja; resembles both istithnāʾ and nāsikh; like selling two slaves with khiyār on one), the school of Karkhī and ʿĪsā b. Abān (the ʿāmm loses its probative force; like selling a free man and a slave together) and a third school (it stays qaṭʿī as before; like selling two slaves of whom one dies before delivery); al-Tawḍīḥ adds a fourth. The ribā āya is the usūlī example of a ʿāmm whose exclusion is first unknown and then explained.
What is the status of a ʿāmm after takhṣīṣ, whether the exclusion is known or unknown?
فإن لحقه خصوص معلوم أو مجهول لا يبقى قطعيا لكنه لا يسقط الاحتجاج به
It stops being qaṭʿī but its probative value is not dropped: it is acted on like khabar al-wāḥid and qiyās. This is the mukhtār of the Manār, whether the exclusion is known or unknown. The mukhaṣṣiṣ resembles both istithnāʾ (the excluded did not enter) and the nāsikh (an independent sentence), so both likenesses count.tap to reveal
How does the mukhtār reason from the two likenesses?
If the exclusion is known: the likeness to istithnāʾ would keep the ʿāmm qaṭʿī (a known exception leaves the rest as it was), but the likeness to a nāsikh would drop the ḥujja entirely (a mustaqill proof accepts taʿlīl, so we do not know how much it removes). Combining the two: not qaṭʿī, but a valid ḥujja. If the exclusion is unknown, the effects reverse: istithnāʾ majhūl drops the ḥujja; a majhūl nāsikh falls by itself and leaves the ʿāmm qaṭʿī; so again the middle result — not qaṭʿī, but ḥujja.tap to reveal
How does the ribā ayah illustrate a ʿāmm with majhūl then maʿlūm khuṣūṣ?
وأحل الله البيع وحرم الربا
'Al-bayʿ' with lām al-jins is ʿāmm; ribā (lit. 'excess', faḍl) is taken out. Since a sale is lawful only for the sake of gain, it was not known which faḍl was meant, so the exclusion was majhūl. Then the Prophet explained: wheat for wheat, barley for barley, dates for dates, salt for salt, gold for gold, silver for silver, like for like, hand to hand; the excess is ribā. For those six it became maʿlūm; for everything else it stayed unknown. Hence ʿUmar's remark that the Prophet died without explaining the chapters of ribā, and the ʿilla fights: Abū Ḥanīfa — qadr and jins; Shāfiʿī — ṭuʿm and thamaniyya; Mālik — iqtiyāt and iddikhār.tap to reveal

Ṣiyagh al-ʿumūm (1): ʿāmm by ṣīgha and maʿnā, qawm/rahṭ, and man/mā

Having treated the ʿāmm's ruling, the Manār lists its formulae. This section covers the first division — ʿumūm by ṣīgha and meaning together (rijāl, nisāʾ) versus by meaning only (qawm, rahṭ) — and the particles man and mā: both are by wadʿ for ʿumūm (aṣluhumā al-ʿumūm) but accept khuṣūṣ through qarāʾin; man for those who think, mā for those who do not, each with majāzī uses. The furūʿ are two sets of oaths/manumission formulae: 'man shāʾa min ʿabīdī al-ʿitq' (everyone who wills is freed) and 'in kāna mā fī baṭnik ghulāman' (not freed if she bears a boy and a girl). None of this is in Shāshī, which has no section on the ṣiyagh al-ʿumūm.
Into what two kinds does the Manār divide ʿumūm by its source?
والعموم إما أن يكون بالصيغة والمعنى جميعا أو بالمعنى لا غير كرجال وقوم
(a) ʿumūm by ṣīgha and maʿnā together: a plural formula (rijāl, nisāʾ, jumūʿ munakkara/maʿrifa, qilla and kathra) whose meaning also covers all; (b) ʿumūm by meaning only: a word singular in form (can be dualised and pluralised: qawmān, aqwām) such as qawm and rahṭ. The reverse (a general ṣīgha with a non-general meaning) is not possible.tap to reveal

Repetition of a nakira and of a maʿrifa: the second is the same or different?

A rule of Qurʾānic and legal interpretation attached to ʿāmm: when a nakira is repeated as a maʿrifa the second is the first; when a maʿrifa is repeated as a maʿrifa, same again; when a nakira is repeated as a nakira the second differs from the first. The standard example is 'inna maʿa al-ʿusri yusrā' (94:5–6), read as one hardship and two eases. Nūr al-Anwār gives the iqrār example (a thousand stated twice) and the exceptions (6:155–156; 'wa-huwa alladhī fī al-samāʾ ilāh'). Fakhr al-Islām's reservation (the second sentence may be a taʾkīd) is recorded. This is not in Shāshī.
What are the rules for repeating a nakira or maʿrifa?
والنكرة إذا أعيدت معرفة كانت الثانية عين الأولى وإذا أعيدت نكرة كانت الثانية غير الأولى والمعرفة إذا أعيدت معرفة كانت الثانية عين الأولى
(1) Nakira then maʿrifa: the second is the first (the lām points back to it: 'arsalnā ilā Firʿawn rasūlan faʿaṣā Firʿawn al-rasūl'). (2) Nakira then nakira: the second is other than the first (otherwise the first would already be definite, with no need for a particle to point back). (3) Maʿrifa then maʿrifa: same, as the lām points to what was mentioned. Hence 'inna maʿa al-ʿusri yusrā, inna maʿa al-ʿusri yusrā': the ʿusr is one, yusr is two (so 'lan yaghliba ʿusr yusrayn', Ibn ʿAbbās).tap to reveal

Mushtarak — the Manār's definition, word by word

Shāshī gives the mushtarak in one rule. Nūr al-Anwār explains the Manār's definition word by word: it is a word that covers several afrād (meanings) of different ḥudūd, but only in turn (badalan), never all at once. 'Mukhtalifat al-ḥudūd' separates it from the ʿāmm, whose afrād agree in definition, and 'ʿalā sabīl al-badal' states that its meanings are taken one at a time (and guards against the Shāfiʿī claim of ʿumūm al-mushtarak, treated in the next section). The al-Bushrā ḥāshiya adds the status of the word shayʾ, which is a mushtarak maʿnawī or lafẓī depending on the angle.
How does the Manār define the mushtarak, and what does each phrase do?
وأما المشترك فما يتناول أفرادا مختلفة الحدود على سبيل البدل
A mushtarak is a word that covers afrād of different ḥudūd in turn (badalan). 'Afrād' means more than one referent (musammayāt), so even a word with just two meanings (qurʾ) is included, and the khāṣṣ is excluded. 'Mukhtalifat al-ḥudūd' excludes the ʿāmm, whose afrād agree in definition. 'ʿAlā sabīl al-badal' either just states the fact, or guards against al-Shāfiʿī's claim that the mushtarak can cover its meanings together (shumūlan), as the sharḥ notes it will come back to.tap to reveal

Ruling of the mushtarak: tawaqquf with taʾammul, qurūʾ, and no ʿumūm

Shāshī says the mushtarak is suspended until one meaning is preferred, and that it has no ʿumūm. Nūr al-Anwār adds how: tawaqquf in belief, together with taʾammul (a condition for acting) to prefer one aspect for ʿamal rather than certain knowledge. The standard example is qurūʾ, which the Hanafīs take as ḥayḍ and the Shāfiʿīs as ṭuhr, with three Hanafī arguments. Then comes the dispute over ʿumūm al-mushtarak: the Shāfiʿīs allow both meanings to be intended where they do not clash (the verse of ṣalāt on the Prophet), while the Hanafīs refuse and read that verse through a single general meaning (ʿināya bi-shaʾnihi), i.e. ʿumūm al-majāz.
What exactly is the ruling of the mushtarak?
وحكمه التوقف فيه بشرط التأمل ليترجح بعض وجوهه للعمل به
Suspend any decision on one particular meaning as a belief, but reflect (taʾammul) so that one aspect is preferred for acting on it. The taʾammul is a condition for ʿamal; it is not for reaching qaṭʿī ʿilm.tap to reveal
Why do the Hanafīs take qurūʾ as ḥayḍ and not ṭuhr?
كما تأملنا في القرء بعدة أوجه أحدها بصيغة ثلاث والثاني بكون أقل الجمع ثلاثا والثالث بمعنى الجمع والانتقال
Mullā Jīwan gives three ways of reflecting on qurʾ. (1) The number: 'thalātha qurūʾ' is a fixed number. If qurʾ were ṭuhr and the divorce fell in a ṭuhr that is counted, the ʿidda would be two ṭuhrs and part of a third, not three, which would void the word 'three'. (2) Qurūʾ is a plural and the least plural is three. (3) The lexical senses: qaraʾa means to gather and to move, and it is the blood that gathers and moves, which fits ḥayḍ and not ṭuhr.tap to reveal
Can a mushtarak be used for all its meanings together?

Al-Shāfiʿī, as reported, allows both to be intended when they do not conflict, e.g. 'inna Allāha wa-malāʾikatahu yuṣallūna ʿalā al-nabī', where ṣalāt is raḥma from Allah, istighfār from the angels. The Hanafī reply: the verse was sent to oblige believers to follow Allah and the angels, so it needs one general sense, 'concern for his dignity' (ʿināya bi-shaʾnihi), shown by Allah as raḥma, by the angels as istighfār, and by believers as duʿāʾ. That is ʿumūm al-majāz, not ʿumūm al-mushtarak.tap to reveal

Muʾawwal — definition, ghālib al-raʾy, ruling

Shāshī has a one-line rule for the muʾawwal. Nūr al-Anwār defines it as the mushtarak whose aspect has been preferred by ghālib al-raʾy, explains why a result of ijtihād is still counted among the divisions of the text (the ruling is afterwards attributed to the ṣīgha), and says what ghālib al-raʾy covers: any ẓannī proof (khabar al-wāḥid, qiyās) and reflection on the ṣīgha or the siyāq. It also notes that a khafī, mushkil or mujmal clarified by a ẓannī proof becomes muʾawwal too, though it then belongs to the divisions of bayān. The ruling: act on it, allowing that it may be wrong.
How does Nūr al-Anwār define the muʾawwal, and why is it among the divisions of naẓm?
وأما المؤول فما ترجح من المشترك بعض وجوهه بغالب الرأي
While none of its meanings is preferred the word stays a mushtarak; once a mujtahid's taʾwīl prefers one meaning, that same word becomes muʾawwal. Though it arises from the mujtahid's act, it counts among the divisions of the text because the ruling after taʾwīl is attributed to the ṣīgha, as if the text had come with that meaning. The Manār's 'min al-mushtarak' is a real restriction: a khafī, mushkil or mujmal whose obscurity is removed by a ẓannī proof also becomes muʾawwal, but it belongs to the divisions of bayān, not of the text.tap to reveal

The second taqsīm (clarity): mutadākhila or mutabāyina; al-Ẓāhir

Nūr al-Anwār now moves to the Manār's second division, by clarity of the meaning of the speech: ẓāhir, naṣṣ, mufassar, muḥkam. Shāshī already has all four; the additions here are the definition of ẓāhir word by word, its ruling (act on it qaṭʿan, so ḥudūd and kaffārāt can rest on it), and the question whether the four are mutually exclusive or nested, which turns on whether sawq (the speaker's purpose) is counted.
How does Nūr al-Anwār unpack the definition of ẓāhir?
وأما الظاهر فاسم لكلام ظهر المراد به للسامع بصيغته
Ẓāhir is a speech whose intended meaning is clear to the hearer from its ṣīgha. 'Bi-ṣīghatihi' means the hearer needs no ṭalab or taʾammul, as the concealed categories do, and nothing is added to the ṣīgha, such as sawq (the driving of the speech for that meaning), as in the naṣṣ. The hearer is one who knows the language. 'Kalām' is used because this second division is of the speech, while the first and the third divisions are of the single word (kalima). The 'ẓuhūr' in the definition is the lexical clearness, not the technical term, so the definition is not circular.tap to reveal
What is the ruling of ẓāhir?
وحكمه وجوب العمل بالذي ظهر منه على سبيل القطع واليقين حتى صح إثبات الحدود والكفارات بالظاهر
It is obligatory to act on the meaning that appears, as certain, so much so that ḥudūd and kaffārāt can be established by a ẓāhir. At most it allows majāz, and that possibility does not arise from a dalīl, so it is not taken into account.tap to reveal
Are ẓāhir and naṣṣ (and the other two) mutually exclusive, or nested?

The wording of most books (the earlier usage) is different: ẓāhir is wider, whether or not it has sawq, while naṣṣ certainly has sawq, and the same holds for each higher category.tap to reveal

Naṣṣ — definition, the extra clarity from sawq, ruling

Naṣṣ is the Manār's second clear category. Nūr al-Anwār defines it as a text that gains clarity over the ẓāhir through something from the speaker (sawq), not in the ṣīgha itself, and gives its ruling: act on it, allowing a taʾwīl that lies in the sphere of majāz. That taʾwīl can be a takhṣīṣ (if the naṣṣ is ʿāmm) or a majāz (if it is a ḥaqīqa); such possibilities do not arise from a dalīl and so do not harm qaṭʿiyya. The name comes from naṣaṣtu al-dābba, to draw out the most from a mount.
How is naṣṣ defined, and what is the 'increment' over ẓāhir?
وأما النص فما ازداد وضوحا على الظاهر بمعنى من المتكلم لا بنفس الصيغة
Naṣṣ is a speech that adds clarity over the ẓāhir through a meaning coming from the speaker, not from the ṣīgha itself. It lets one understand what the ẓāhir does not give, because the speaker drove that wording for that meaning (sawq), and not merely because of what the ṣīgha conveys. In the ḥāshiya's words, the 'meaning from the speaker' is his driving the speech for that meaning. The term is taken from naṣaṣtu al-dābba, 'I drew out of the mount more than its usual pace' (Fakhr al-Islām).tap to reveal
What is the ruling of naṣṣ?
وحكمه وجوب العمل بما وضح على احتمال تأويل هو في حيز المجاز
Act on the meaning that has been made clear, with the possibility of a taʾwīl that lies in the sphere of majāz. That taʾwīl can be a takhṣīṣ (if the naṣṣ is ʿāmm and accepts it) or a majāz (if it is a ḥaqīqa that could be metaphorical), so there is no need to say, as others do, 'taʾwīl or takhṣīṣ'. If even the naṣṣ admits this, the weaker ẓāhir has more reason to; yet such possibilities do not harm qaṭʿiyya.tap to reveal

Mufassar — definition, how the doubt is cut, ruling

Mufassar is the third clear category, above naṣṣ. Nūr al-Anwār defines it as what gains clarity over the naṣṣ in a way that leaves no room for taʾwīl or takhṣīṣ, whether by the Prophet's decisive bayān of a mujmal (by act or word) or by Allah adding a word that closes the door (as 'kulluhum ajmaʿūn' after the angels' prostration). Muḥkam — definition, li-ʿaynihi / li-ghayrihi, ruling, and the standard examples

Muḥkam is the highest clear category. Nūr al-Anwār defines it by its being shielded from naskh and tabdīl, not merely from khilāf, and divides it into muḥkam li-ʿaynihi (naturally unabrogable, like the verses of tawḥīd and the attributes) and li-ghayrihi (by the Prophet's death, or by a built-in tawqīt/taʾbīd). The word 'izdāda' is deliberately left out of its definition, to show that the muḥkam's rise above the mufassar is only the cutting of naskh. The section ends with the standard examples of all four categories.
How does Nūr al-Anwār define the muḥkam?
وأما المحكم فما أحكم المراد به عن احتمال النسخ والتبديل
The muḥkam is what has had its intended meaning made firm against the possibility of naskh and tabdīl, whether its immunity from naskh is in its own nature or from the Prophet's death. The Manār leaves 'izdāda' out of its definition, as it put it in the mufassar and naṣṣ, to point out that the muḥkam has risen above the mufassar only by one thing: closing naskh.tap to reveal

Li-ʿaynihi: the cutting of naskh is a quality in the text itself, as with the verses of tawḥīd and the attributes (it cannot be changed by reason). Ranking among the four and how conflicts are resolved (taʿāruḍ ṣūrī)

All four are qaṭʿī, so they differ not in epistemic status but only in conflict: the lower is left for the higher. Nūr al-Anwār states the rule (ẓāhir yields to naṣṣ, naṣṣ to mufassar, mufassar to muḥkam), explains that this is taʿāruḍ ṣūrī, not ḥaqīqī (real taʿāruḍ is the opposition of two equal ḥujjas, not of unequal ones), and gives four examples: 'wa-uḥilla lakum mā warāʾa dhālikum' versus 'fankiḥū… mathnā wa-thulātha wa-rubāʿ'; the hadith 'al-mustaḥāḍa tawaḍḍaʾ li-kull ṣalāt' versus 'li-waqt kull ṣalāt'; 'wa-ashhidū dhawayy ʿadl' versus 'wa-lā taqbalū lahum shahāda abadan'; and 'tazawwaja imraʾa ilā shahr' (mutʿa). Shāshī has the same rule with different examples.
When do the four differ in strength?
ويظهر التفاوت عند التعارض ليصير الأدنى متروكا بالأعلى
They do not differ in qaṭʿiyya/ẓanniyya (all are qaṭʿī); the difference shows only when they conflict: the lower is set aside by the higher. Ẓāhir yields to naṣṣ, naṣṣ to mufassar, mufassar to muḥkam.tap to reveal
What happens when 'wa-uḥilla lakum mā warāʾa dhālikum' meets 'fankiḥū… mathnā wa-thulātha wa-rubāʿ'?
The first is ẓāhir in making lawful everything beyond the forbidden relations without limiting the number, so it would permit more than four wives; the second is naṣṣ (sawq for the number) that bars going beyond four. The naṣṣ prevails.tap to reveal
How does the hadith of the mustaḥāḍa illustrate naṣṣ vs mufassar?
'Al-mustaḥāḍa tawaḍḍaʾ li-kull ṣalāt' is naṣṣ: a new wuḍūʾ for each prayer, whether ada or qaḍāʾ, farḍ or nafl. 'Li-waqt kull ṣalāt' is mufassar since it states the waqt explicitly and cannot be read otherwise: one wuḍūʾ suffices for each prayer-time, with whatever prayers she wishes. Conflict goes to the mufassar. Shāfiʿī acted on the first.tap to reveal

Khafī — the external cause, ṭalab, and the ṭarrār/nabbāsh case

The third division moves from clarity to concealment, the opposites of the four: khafī, mushkil, mujmal, mutashābih. Nūr al-Anwār defines khafī as what is hidden in meaning by a factor outside the ṣīgha and reached by ṭalab alone, and gives its ruling: look into it to see whether the concealment is a surplus or a deficiency of meaning compared with the ẓāhir. Mushkil — definition, its two causes, ruling, and the verse of 'annā shiʾtum'

Mushkil is more concealed than khafī: it has entered among its like and been lost among them, as a stranger changes his clothes and mixes with the crowd, so it needs both ṭalab and taʾammul. Nūr al-Anwār gives the ruling (first believe that what Allah meant is true, then seek and reflect until the meaning is clear) and two examples: 'annā shiʾtum' (whose two senses are resolved by reflecting on 'ḥarth', ruling out sodomy with one's wife) and 'qawārīra min fiḍḍa' (a rare, subtle metaphor).

Mujmal — definition, the three tasks (istifsār, ṭalab, taʾammul), and the ruling

Mujmal is more concealed than mushkil. Nūr al-Anwār defines it as a speech in which meanings have crowded together and the intention is obscured in a way that cannot be reached from the wording itself, but only by going back to the mujmil (istifsār), then ṭalab, then taʾammul. The crowding can be real (a mushtarak whose preference is blocked), by estimation (a rare word like halūʿ), or because the speaker's intention is not made clear. Its ruling: believe that what is meant is true and suspend until the explanation. A satisfying explanation (ṣalāt, zakāt) turns it into mufassar; an unsatisfying one (ribā) turns it into mushkil.
How does Nūr al-Anwār define mujmal, and what does 'crowding of meanings' mean?
وأما المجمل فما ازدحمت فيه المعاني واشتبه المراد به اشتباها لا يدرك بنفس العبارة بل بالرجوع إلى الاستفسار من المجمل ثم الطلب ثم التأمل
A mujmal is a speech in which meanings have crowded and the intended meaning is so obscured that it cannot be reached from the wording itself, only by going back to the mujmil (istifsār), then ṭalab, then taʾammul. 'Crowding' is the meanings gathering on a word with no preference for one, e.g. a mushtarak whose tarjīḥ is blocked, or it can be by estimation, as with a rare word like halūʿ. Khafī is reached by ṭalab only; mushkil by ṭalab then taʾammul; mujmal by three: istifsār (asking the one who spoke it), ṭalab (looking for its attributes), then taʾammul (fixing the exact meaning). Mutashābih — definition, ruling, the 'illā Allāh' waqf dispute, and its two kinds

The last of the four concealed categories is the mutashābih: what has cut off hope of knowing the intended meaning. Nūr al-Anwār gives its ruling (believe it is true before the Day of reaching it; for the Prophet it was known), the Hanafī reading that the waqf is at 'illā Allāh' against the Shāfiʿī and most Muʿtazila reading of 'wa-l-rāsikhūn' as a ʿaṭf, and calls this a largely verbal dispute (the Shāfiʿī 'knowing' is a ẓannī taʾwīl). The wisdom of sending down mutashābih is ibtilāʾ. Its two kinds are the disjointed letters and words whose lexical meaning is known but whose Divine intent is not (yad Allāh, istiwāʾ, naẓar).
How does Nūr al-Anwār define the mutashābih?
وأما المتشابه فهو اسم لما انقطع رجاء معرفة المراد منه ولا يرجى بدوه أصلا
The mutashābih is the name for what the hope of knowing its intended meaning has been cut off from, and no emergence of the meaning is ever hoped for. It is at the extreme of concealment, as the muḥkam is at the extreme of clarity, like a man lost from his city whose trace is cut and whose peers and neighbours have died. The cutting of hope may be accidental (a mujmal the Prophet died without explaining), essential (known from the Messenger that hope is cut), or because it is beyond understanding, as the question of qadar (the ḥāshiya).tap to reveal
What is the ruling of mutashābih?

Believe that what is meant by it is true, even though we do not know it until the Day of Resurrection, when it will be known for everyone in sha' Allāh. This is for the Umma; for the Prophet it was known, otherwise addressing would be pointless and would be like speaking Zanjī to an Arab. The ḥāshiya says this belief is a general one (ijmālī) before reaching the meaning and detailed afterwards.tap to reveal

Ḥaqīqa and majāz: definition, waḍʿ, ruling

The third taqsīm of the Manār is wujūh istiʿmāl al-naẓm, and its first two members are ḥaqīqa and majāz. Shāshī already gave their definitions; Nūr al-Anwār adds the precision. It explains that waḍʿ may be lughawī, sharʿī, ʿurfī khāṣṣ or ʿurfī ʿāmm, makes both definitions carry the qayd al-ḥaythiyya so that the same word (ṣalāt) is ḥaqīqa from one angle and majāz from another, and says the ḥaqīqa's ruling is the existence of what it was set for, khāṣṣ or ʿāmm. It also shows why earth-for-sky and hazl are outside majāz and how majāz bi-l-ziyāda still fits the definition.
How does Nūr al-Anwār sharpen the definitions of ḥaqīqa and majāz that Shāshī gave?
اسم لكل لفظ أريد به غير ما وضع له لأجل مناسبة بين المعنى الموضوع له وغير الموضوع له
Ḥaqīqa is every lafẓ used for what it was set for ('kull lafẓ' is like the genus; 'urīda bihi mā wuḍiʿa lahu' is the faṣl that removes the muhmal and the majāz). Waḍʿ is the assignment of the word to the meaning so it indicates it without a qarīna. If the assigner is the language-maker it is waḍʿ lughawī; if it is the Lawgiver it is waḍʿ sharʿī; if it is a specific group (the grammarians' naming of 'fiʿl') it is ʿurfī khāṣṣ; if it is a general custom without a known originator (dābba for four-legged animals) it is ʿurfī ʿāmm. The sign of ḥaqīqa; majāz does not compete with it; furūʿ of aymān and nikāḥ

The sign that separates ḥaqīqa from majāz is that the ḥaqīqa cannot be negated of what it names, while majāz can (nearest father vs grandfather, lion vs brave man). The governing principle is 'matā amkana al-ʿamal bihā saqaṭa al-majāz': what is borrowed does not compete with the original. Two furūʿ show it. In the oath verse the Ḥanafīs read 'ʿaqqadtum' as the contract of a future oath only, not as ʿazm, so a ghamūs oath has sin but no kaffāra, against the Shāfiʿī who adds kaffāra. In 'wa-lā tankiḥū' they keep nikāḥ as waṭʾ, so zinā creates ḥurmat al-muṣāhara; the Shāfiʿī reads ʿaqd.

The impossibility of combining ḥaqīqa and majāz; furūʿ

Shāshī already says both cannot be intended together. Nūr al-Anwār names the disagreement: the Shāfiʿī allows it where the two can be combined (lams, 'ṣubbū ʿalayhi dhanūban'); the Ḥanafīs deny it, citing either rational impossibility or the absence of usage, with the garment held at once as property and loan. Objections to the no-union rule: amān, setting the foot, yawm yaqdam, nadhr/yamīn

After the principle, Nūr al-Anwār answers four apparent counter-examples. A safety pledge for 'my sons and mawālī' protects grandsons not by intention but by the shubha of the wording; for 'my fathers and mothers' grandparents do not enter, because inclusion goes from asl to far ʿ. The oath not to 'place his foot in Fulān's house' breaks by ʿumūm al-majāz (entering, any occupation), not by union. 'Yawm yaqdam' means waqt; a ḍābiṭa on mumtadd verbs decides daytime vs time. The vow 'lillāhi ʿalayya ṣawm rajab' is a nadhr by wording and a yamīn by what it entails (taḥrīm al-ḥalāl), like buying a relative.

When a ḥarbī asks the Imām for safety for his sons and mawālī, grandsons and the freer's freed persons are also covered. The Manār answers that they are not in the intention (the ḥaqīqa is the intended one); rather the surface of the word is enough doubt (shubha) to protect blood, and shubha suffices to establish amān. So no union occurs.tap to reveal
Why does the same shubha not bring grandfathers into 'our fathers and mothers'?
بخلاف الاستئمان على الآباء والأمهات حيث لا يدخل الأجداد والجدات؛ لأن ذا بطريق التبعية، فيليق بالفروع دون الأصول

Grandchildren are furūʿ in wording and in descent and follow the named son; grandparents are uṣūl in descent, even if furūʿ in wording, so they cannot follow in wording. Maḥall al-kalām continued: khaṭaʾ and nisyān; taḥrīm al-aʿyān

More cases of the fifth qarīna. 'Rufiʿa ʿan ummatī al-khaṭaʾ wa-l-nisyān' cannot be literal; it means the sin is lifted, but worldly liability (people's rights, the invalidation of a fast or prayer by a mistaken act) stays. The same section states the final Ḥanafī claim, that the prohibition attached to things (mothers, wine) is a true ḥaqīqa and the stronger form of prohibition, against those who call it majāz for the act and some Muʿtazila who call it mujmal.
Does 'rufiʿa ʿan ummatī al-khaṭaʾ wa-l-nisyān' remove all consequences?
وقوله عليه السلام: «رفع عن أمتي الخطأ والنسيان»
Its ḥaqīqa, that mistakes and forgetting do not occur in the Umma, would be false, so it is read as majāz: the ukhrawī ḥukm, i.e. the sin, is lifted. In this world the liability remains in the rights of people, and in the invalidation of a fast by eating by mistake (e.g. water entering the throat while rinsing) and of prayer by speaking by mistake. So the hadith gives the Shāfiʿī no basis for keeping prayer or fast valid after those acts. The ḥāshiya adds that forgetful eating (not a mistake) does not break the fast, by the report 'man nasiya wa-huwa ṣāʾim fa-akala wa-shariba fa-l-yutimma ṣawmahu'.tap to reveal

Forgetfulness never drops the rights of people, since they are protected for their own need: he who destroys another's property forgetfully must pay. In pure rights of Allāh it may be an excuse if it did not come from his negligence; if it did, no.

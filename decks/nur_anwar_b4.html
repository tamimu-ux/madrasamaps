Bāb Aqsām al-Sunna: the definition of the Sunna

The Book is done, so the author turns to the Sunna, which comes after the Book in rank and before ijmāʿ and qiyās. In uṣūl, 'Sunna' here means the saying of the Messenger ﷺ (his acts and silence, and the acts and words of the Companions, are treated later in their own faṣl). The categories already met in the Book (khāṣṣ, ʿāmm, amr, nahy…) apply equally to the Sunna, so this bāb deals only with what is peculiar to it: how reports reach us and who may be trusted.
What does 'Sunna' mean in this chapter, and why is it placed after the Book?
السنة تطلق علی قول الرسول
Here it means the Prophet's own words (qawl al-Rasūl). It follows the Book in rank and precedes ijmāʿ and qiyās. Because the same classifications (khāṣṣ/ʿāmm, amr/nahy, etc.) occur in the Sunna, this bāb only treats what belongs uniquely to the Sunna: transmission and narrators.tap to reveal

First taqsīm (ittiṣāl): the mutawātir report

The Manār divides the Sunna four ways by how it reaches us, how its narrators stand, how the report was received and what 'wounds' it. The first division is by ittiṣāl (continuity from the Prophet ﷺ): either complete (mutawātir), or with a doubt in form (mashhūr), or with a doubt in form and meaning (āḥād). A mutawātir report is one narrated by so many that collusion in lying is unthinkable, and it yields certain knowledge.
What is a mutawātir report and is a fixed number required?
ولم يشترط فيه تعين عدد
It is a report passed on by a group whose collusion on falsehood the mind cannot imagine. No fixed number is required (the proposed figures of 7, 40, 70, 4, 10, 20 each have a verse or analogy behind them but none is a proof); whatever number produces necessary knowledge is the sign of tawātur. The condition must hold at beginning, middle and end alike.tap to reveal
What kinds of tawātur does the ḥāshiya mention?
Verbal tawātur (the same words, like the Qurʾān); tawātur of meaning, where many reports in different wording share one ruling (such as wiping over the khuffs, reported by seventy Companions); and silent tawātur, where each of a group reports and the rest stay silent in circumstances in which they would have denied it. Verbal tawātur of the Sunna is disputed: some said none exists, some cited 'innamā al-aʿmāl bi-l-niyyāt' or 'man kadhaba ʿalayya muta'ammidan'.tap to reveal
What does a mutawātir report yield, and who disagrees?
يوجب علم اليقين
It yields certain knowledge (ʿilm al-yaqīn), necessary like direct sight. Ḥanafīs and the majority hold this. Some Muʿtazila held it yields only a preponderant probability, and some held it yields acquired (istidlālī) knowledge.tap to reveal

Mashhūr and khabar al-wāḥid

Mashhūr is a report that began as āḥād in the Companions' generation, then spread so widely in the second and third generations that collusion on a lie is unthinkable. It yields ʿilm ṭumaʾnīna, and may add to the Book. Khabar al-wāḥid is anything below that, whether one narrator or more, and the number has no effect once it falls short of mashhūr. Its denier is not a kāfir but is declared astray (according to the sounder view).
How does the Manār define the mashhūr, and which centuries count?
It is a report that was āḥād in the Companions' generation and then reached the level of tawātur in the second generation (the Followers) and third (the Followers' followers). Spread after the three generations does not count, because most āḥād reports spread by then and would all become mashhūr.tap to reveal
What are the effects of the mashhūr report?
ولا يكفر جاحده بل يضلل على الأصح
It yields ʿilm ṭumaʾnīna, not certainty, so it is below mutawātir and above āḥād. It may add to or qualify the Book (for example the continuity of fasting in kaffārat al-yamīn, from the reading of Ibn Masʿūd), but cannot abrogate the wording of the Qurʾān. Its denier is not a kāfir but is in error, because he implicitly mistakes the second and third generations; he is declared misguided (yuḍallal), according to the sounder opinion.tap to reveal
What is khabar al-wāḥid and does the number matter?
وهو كل خبر يرويه الواحد أو الاثنان فصاعدا
It is every report narrated by one, two or more that has not reached mashhūr or tawātur. The Manār says this to refute those (like the Muʿtazilī al-Jubbāʾī) who distinguished between a report of two and of one, accepting two and not one. After it falls below mashhūr, the number has no weight, since all are equally mere probability.tap to reveal

Obligation to act on khabar al-wāḥid: the proofs

Khabar al-wāḥid obligates action (not certain belief). The Manār proves this from the Book (the verse of nafr, 'fa-law lā nafara…'), from the Sunna (the Prophet's acceptance of the reports of Barīra and Salmān, and his sending single envoys: ʿAlī, Muʿādh, Diḥya), from ijmāʿ of the Companions, and from reason. It then answers those who said that no action is valid without certain knowledge.
How does the verse of nafr prove that a solitary report obliges action?
The verse says that from every group a small party should go out to learn the religion and warn their people when they return. The warning is obligatory on a ṭāʾifa, which includes one or two (Ibn ʿAbbās), and the people are obliged to accept and act on it; hence the report of one or two obliges action. 'Laʿalla' (that they may beware) is taken as a demand, since expectation cannot apply to God, and a demand for caution means obligation.tap to reveal
What does the Sunna show about accepting the report of one person?
He ﷺ accepted Barīra's report that the food was ṣadaqa (and refused it) and Salmān's that it was a gift (and ate), and the ḥāshiya adds that he ﷺ asked whether food was ṣadaqa or hadiyya and acted on the answer. He also sent single people as his representatives: ʿAlī and Muʿādh to Yemen as judges, and Diḥya al-Kalbī to Heraclius with a letter calling to Islam. This would not have been done if a single report were not binding.tap to reveal
What are the ijmāʿ and rational arguments for acting on āḥād?
فالاجماع
Ijmāʿ: the Companions argued among themselves from solitary reports; Abū Bakr argued against the Anṣār with 'al-aʾimma min Quraysh' and they accepted without objection (the ḥāshiya notes an explanation that the Anṣār's 'a leader from us and from you' was an Arab custom). Reason: mutawātir and mashhūr reports do not exist for every new event; if solitary reports were rejected, the rulings would lie idle. The ḥāshiya adds that the report must come from a just narrator; the report of a sinner about the impurity of water is not acted on without deliberation.tap to reveal

Narrator's state (1): the narrator known for fiqh and ijtihād

For a khabar al-wāḥid, the narrator is either known (maʿrūf, for fiqh or for probity and memory) or unknown (majhūl, five kinds). If he is a known faqīh and mujtahid (the Rightly-Guided Caliphs and the 'ʿAbādila'), his report is a proof that overrides qiyās, even when it opposes it. Mālik is cited as dissenting (qiyās first if it opposes the report). The Ḥanafī reason: a report is certain in its source and doubtful only in its path, while qiyās is doubtful in both root and attribute.
What is the rule when a famous faqīh narrator's report opposes qiyās?
كان حديثه حجة يترك به القياس
His report is a proof and qiyās is left, even if it is contrary to the qiyās. If they agree, the ruling stands by the report and qiyās only supports it. This holds for the four caliphs and the ʿAbādila, and those joined to them.tap to reveal
What is the report of Abū Hurayra on carrying a bier, and why is it cited?
The report 'whoever carries a bier should make wuḍūʾ' was met with Ibn ʿAbbās asking whether we must make wuḍūʾ for carrying dry wood. It illustrates the rule that when the narrator is not a famous mujtahid, qiyās may be preferred. The ḥāshiya, however, remarks that this example is not apt, since Mullā Jīwan elsewhere places Abū Hurayra among those known for probity and memory, not fiqh, and that Ibn al-Humām affirms Abū Hurayra's fiqh.tap to reveal

Narrator's state (2): known for probity and memory but not fiqh (the muṣarrāt and other examples)

If the narrator is known only for probity and memory (Anas, Abū Hurayra in Mullā Jīwan's presentation), his report is accepted over qiyās unless it opposes qiyās in every respect, so that acting on it would close the door of opinion altogether. The reason is the fear of narrating by meaning and misunderstanding. Abū Hurayra's ḥadīth on the muṣarrāh is left for this reason, in the reading of the Manār's school. The ḥāshiya stresses that this is not a slight on the Companion and that the early imāms never made fiqh of the narrator a condition: the practice belongs to ʿĪsā b. Abān's later schema.
When may the report of a just, accurate but non-faqīh narrator be left for qiyās?
وإن عرف بالعدالة والضبط دون الفقه
If the report agrees with qiyās it is acted on. If it opposes it, it is not left except by necessity: that it opposes qiyās in every aspect, so that acting on it would block the door of opinion entirely in that matter. If it agrees with some qiyās, it is not left.tap to reveal
What is the muṣarrāh ḥadīth and why do the Ḥanafīs not act on it?
Abū Hurayra reports 'do not leave the camel or sheep unmilked; whoever buys it afterwards has the better of two options after milking it: keep it, or return it with a ṣāʿ of dates.' The ḥadīth is against qiyās in every respect, because compensation in sales and torts is by like for the fungible and by value for the non-fungible, and a ṣāʿ of dates is neither the milk nor its value, and does not vary with the quantity of milk. The ḥāshiya adds that it also conflicts with Qurʾān 42:40 ('a bad deed's recompense is a bad deed like it') and with the mashhūr ḥadīth 'al-kharāj bi-l-ḍamān'.tap to reveal
Did the early Ḥanafīs require the narrator's fiqh?
The ḥāshiya states that making the narrator's fiqh a condition for preferring his report to qiyās is a later innovation, attributed to ʿĪsā b. Abān. The early imāms did not, and Abū Ḥanīfa is quoted: what came to us from Allah and from the Messenger is on the head and eye. The author's own chosen view (in the Qamar ḥāshiya) is that the report of every just and accurate narrator precedes qiyās.tap to reveal

The unknown narrator (majhūl) and his five kinds

A narrator unknown in transmission (not in lineage, which the majority do not mind) falls into five categories according to how the early generations received him. The Ḥanafī rule: if the Salaf narrated from him, or disagreed about his report, or kept silent, he is treated as known and his report is accepted over qiyās only so long as it does not oppose it, like those known; if they rejected him with no acceptance, his report is munkar and refused; if his report did not appear among the Salaf, one may act on it but is not obliged.
How is the unknown narrator classified and treated?
If the Salaf narrated from him, or disagreed about him, or stayed silent after his report reached them, he becomes like a known narrator and his report is accepted as long as it does not oppose qiyās. If they rejected him, the report is munkar and refused. If his report did not appear among the Salaf (neither accepted nor refused), action is permitted but not obligatory. Unknown lineage alone does not harm acceptance in the view of most uṣūlīs.tap to reveal
What is the example of the report on which the Salaf differed?
Ibn Masʿūd was asked about a woman whose husband died before fixing her mahr or consummating; he said she gets the mahr of her peers, no more or less. Maʿqil b. Sinān testified that the Prophet ﷺ ruled the same for Barwaʿ bint Wāshiq. Ibn Masʿūd was delighted. ʿAlī rejected it, calling Maʿqil a bedouin who urinates on his heels. The Ḥanafīs act on it since trusted jurists such as ʿAlqama, Masrūq and al-Ḥasan narrated it (so he became like a known narrator) and since it agrees with the qiyās that death confirms the contract like consummation.tap to reveal
Why is Fāṭima bint Qays's report refused?
She reported that the Prophet ﷺ gave her neither lodging nor maintenance after her husband's third irrevocable divorce. ʿUmar rejected it before the Companions: we do not leave the Book of our Lord and the Sunna of our Prophet for the word of a woman; we do not know if she remembered or forgot. No one objected, so it is an ijmāʿ that the report is munkar (though a few, like Ibn ʿAbbās, accepted it). Others said ʿUmar meant the Book (Q 65:6 'do not expel them') and the qiyās on the pregnant mabtūta and the revocable divorcee.tap to reveal

Conditions of the narrator (1): ʿaql and ḍabṭ

The Manār lists four conditions for a narrator: ʿaql, ḍabṭ, ʿadāla and Islam. Perfect intellect is the intellect of the mature; the intellect of the child, the maʿtūh and the madman is deficient, because the Sharīʿa has not made them competent even for their own affairs, so a fortiori in the matter of religion. Maturity is required at the time of delivering the ḥadīth (adāʾ), not at the time of hearing (taḥammul). Ḍabṭ is hearing as it ought to be heard, understanding the meaning intended, retaining it with effort, guarding it by acting on it and by repeating it, until it is delivered to another.
What intellect is required of a narrator, and when must it be present?
العقل والضبط والعدالة والإسلام
The perfect intellect of the adult. A child's, a maʿtūh's and a madman's intellect is deficient, so their reports are not accepted. If the child heard before maturity but reports after it, his report is accepted, as his hearing is sound by tamyīz and his delivery is by a mature mind. The ḥāshiya adds the ijmāʿ that the reports of Ibn ʿAbbās and Ibn al-Zubayr, who heard as children, were accepted.tap to reveal
What does ḍabṭ require?
Four stages: to hear the speech fully as it should be heard; to understand its intended meaning, linguistic or legal; to retain it as far as human strength allows; and to hold firm to it by guarding its limits (acting on it) and by recalling it, remaining suspicious of one's own memory, until he delivers it to another who in turn passes it on.tap to reveal
Why is understanding of meaning not required for transmitting the Qurʾān?
Because the Qurʾān was established in the first place by the imams of guidance who transmitted it after perfect memorisation, its wording is itself a miracle, and it is protected from change and substitution ('We revealed the Reminder and We guard it', Q 15:9). So its wording can be passed on by someone who does not know its meaning. Rulings are tied to its words (e.g. it is forbidden for the junub and ḥāʾiḍ to recite), and so transmitting it by meaning is forbidden; translation is a different matter.tap to reveal

Conditions of the narrator (2): ʿadāla and Islam

ʿAdāla here is the perfect kind: the preponderance of religion and reason over desire, so that whoever commits a major sin or persists in a minor one loses it, while an occasional lapse into a minor sin does not. The imperfect (qāṣira) kind, presumed by the apparent practice of a sane Muslim, suffices for testimony in non-ḥudūd matters but not for narrating ḥadīth. Islam is tasdīq plus iqrār, and the Manār requires a summary explanation of the sharāʾiʿ.
What is ʿadāla for a narrator and what removes it?
The preponderance of religion and intellect over whim. A major sin or persistence in a minor one removes it; an occasional minor lapse does not, since avoiding all sins is a trait of prophets and is impossible for ordinary humans. The second division concerns interruption of the chain (inqiṭāʿ). Therefore the one who sends the report guarantees its soundness; the one who gives the chain delegates to others. Hidden inqiṭāʿ: presenting the report to the sources (ʿarḍ)

Hidden interruption is a flaw not in the narrator's transmission but in the report itself or its context. A report is hidden-interrupted if it conflicts with the Book, the well-known Sunna, an event of general notoriety (ʿumūm al-balwā), or if the early imams turned away from it. The Ḥanafī method is to present the report to these sources; a deficiency in the narrator is covered by the conditions already discussed. The Qamar ḥāshiya cautions that a ḥadīth with a sound chain is not dropped merely because it seems to oppose a general Qurʾānic text: it can specify it.

Third taqsīm: the subject-matter of the report (rights of Allah and rights of men)

The third division looks at what the report is about, since the conditions for accepting a solitary report change with its subject. The matter is either a right of Allah (acts of worship, penalties, and mixed forms) or a right of men, which is either pure imposition on another (debts, goods), no imposition at all (agency, muḍāraba, a messenger bringing a gift), or imposition in one respect only (dismissal of an agent, interdiction of a licensed slave). The Ḥanafī rule is calibrated to each: just narrator for the rights of Allah apart from ḥudūd, the full testimonial conditions for pure imposition, tamyīz alone for non-imposition, and one of the two testimonial halves for the mixed case (Abū Ḥanīfa).
Which rights of Allah are established by a solitary report?
Rights of Allah are worship, penalties, and in-between forms (ʿushr and kharāj, ṣadaqat al-fiṭr, kaffāra). For acts of worship a solitary report is a proof, provided the narrator has the four conditions. Some required a number because the Companions accepted ʿĀʾisha's ḥadīth 'when the two circumcised parts meet' on her own; some made it conditional on number elsewhere. Beliefs (iʿtiqādāt) are not established by solitary reports, since they rest on certainty.tap to reveal
Is a solitary report accepted for penalties?
خلافا للكرخي
Al-Karkhī: it is not accepted, and ḥudūd cannot be established by it, because its link to the Messenger contains doubt and ḥudūd are averted by doubt. The reply (ḥāshiya): the doubt that averts the ḥadd is a doubt in the existence of the cause (as in zinā or theft), not a doubt in the proof for the ruling; the ḥadd is established by the apparent text of the Book despite doubt in its indication. And the ḥudūd are established by testimony before the judge by a text against qiyās, so a report cannot be compared with that.tap to reveal
What is needed to report a right of men that is pure imposition?
All the narrator's conditions plus the testimonial ones: number (two men, or one man and two women in non-ḥudūd, four for zinā), the word 'ashhadu' (since it is an oath, 'aʿlamu' would not do), and walāya (so a slave's word is not accepted). Islam is required if the one against whom the claim is made is a Muslim.tap to reveal

Fourth taqsīm: the nature of the report and the ways of receiving and delivering it

The fourth division concerns the report itself: it is either certainly true (the Prophet's word, mutawātir), certainly false (Pharaoh's claim to lordship), equally likely either way (the sinner's report: suspend judgement, 'if a sinner brings you news, verify'), or leaning to truth (the just narrator who meets the conditions), which is the only kind that concerns the uṣūlī. For this fourth kind there are three stages (hearing, retention, delivery), each with a ʿazīma and a rukhṣa. Abū Ḥanīfa's strictness (he did not rely on writing without recall) is explained by the ḥāshiya as caution for the Sunna, and the Manār's rule on narrating by meaning is: a muḥkam text may be narrated by meaning by an expert in language, a ẓāhir only by a mujtahid faqīh, while mushkil, mushtarak, mujmal and jawāmiʿ al-kalim may not be narrated by meaning at all.
Into what four kinds does the Manār divide a report in itself?
(1) What is known to be true: the Prophet's report (his infallibility is proven by decisive evidence) and the mutawātir; (2) what is known to be false, like Pharaoh's claim to lordship, since a created, perishing thing cannot be a lord; (3) what could equally be either: a sinner's report, for which tawaqquf is required by the verse 'if a sinner brings you news, verify'; (4) what is preponderantly true: the report of a just narrator meeting the conditions, and this is the one the uṣūl discusses.tap to reveal
What are the ʿazīma forms of hearing a ḥadīth?
Four: (1) the shaykh reads or speaks to the student; (2) the student reads to the shaykh from memory or a book and the shaykh listens and affirms ('is it as I read?'): more cautious, says Abū Ḥanīfa in one report, because the one reading to himself is more careful; some call the shaykh's own reading better because it was the Prophet's way; (3) a letter written in the form of a book (with the chain and the words 'if my letter reaches you and you understand it, narrate it from me'); (4) a messenger sent with the ḥadīth. The first two are more perfect than the last two. In (3) and (4), the Imām requires proof that it is his letter, others accept a firm belief in the handwriting.tap to reveal
What is the rukhṣa in hearing, and what does Abū Ḥanīfa require in retention?
The rukhṣa is the ijāza, e.g. 'I permit you to narrate from me this book which so-and-so told me from so-and-so'; it needs no hearing, and a book without ijāza is not valid. The beneficiary should know what is in the book; in our time, an ijāza to one who has not read it is only an ijāza of blessing. Retention: the ʿazīma is to remember what was heard from hearing to delivery without relying on the book; hence Abū Ḥanīfa compiled no ḥadīth book and did not allow narration from a book alone, which became a cause of criticism by the narrow-minded who did not grasp his scrupulosity. The rukhṣa: if he sees his hearing written in his own hand and his session, then (Abū Ḥanīfa) it is no proof unless he remembers; Abū Yūsuf and Muḥammad, and al-Shāfiʿī, allow narration and obligate action upon it.tap to reveal

Ṭaʿn that attaches to a ḥadīth: denial by the source, the narrator's contrary practice, vague criticism

After the four taqsīmāt the Manār treats ṭaʿn, a criticism of the ḥadīth from the narrator's side or from outside. If the shaykh categorically denies having narrated it, the ḥadīth is dropped; if he only says he does not remember, it is not dropped. If the narrator himself acts contrary to what he narrated in a way that is certainly contrary, or refuses to act on it after narrating, it is a flaw, but only for a Companion and only when the ḥadīth is apparent and could not have been hidden from them. Vague criticism by ḥadīth imāms is not accepted, nor is accusation of tadlīs, irsāl, riding the mount, joking, youth or compiling much fiqh.
What is the effect of the shaykh's denial of having narrated the ḥadīth?
If he categorically denies ('you lied about me, I never narrated this to you') the ḥadīth falls by agreement, as each of source and student calls the other a liar, so one lied. If he merely hesitates ('I do not remember narrating it', 'I don't know it') then it is not dropped according to the Manār, because each is just and trustworthy and a man may narrate and later forget; the OCR of the continuation of the Manār's wording is garbled, so only the substance is recorded. The ḥāshiya's example of categorical denial is Ibn Jurayj's report from Sulaymān b. Mūsā from al-Zuhrī that al-Zuhrī did not know the ḥadīth 'any woman who marries without her walī's permission, her nikāḥ is void'.tap to reveal
When does the narrator's practice contrary to his ḥadīth invalidate it?
If after narrating he acts contrary to what is certainly its meaning, or refuses to act on it, the ḥadīth falls, since either he forgot (then he is heedless), or he knew it abrogated, or he disregarded it (then his probity is gone). If he acted before narrating, or the date of the contrary act is unknown, it does not wound, because the ḥadīth is a proof in itself and his earlier view may have been left for it. If a Companion interprets an ambiguous ḥadīth, one of two possible meanings, his interpretation does not bar the other (as Ibn ʿUmar read 'al-bayyiʿān bi-l-khiyār mā lam yatafarraqā' as parting of bodies; Ḥanafīs read parting by words, saying the ḥadīth fits both).tap to reveal
When does a Companion's contrary practice weaken a ḥadīth, and what is the example of raising the hands?

If the ḥadīth could be hidden from them (e.g. the ḥadīth of wuḍūʾ from laughter, which Abū Mūsā did not follow though the report was hidden), then it does not wound. Faṣl on taʿāruḍ: its rukn, shart and ruling between Book and Sunna

Apparent conflict arises between proofs only because we do not know which one abrogates the other; with Allah there is no real contradiction, since contradiction is a sign of incapacity. Taʿāruḍ is the confronting of two proofs on equal footing in essence and attribute; its conditions are sameness of subject, time and relation, with opposite rulings. The Manār orders the resort: between two verses, the Sunna; between two Sunnas, the Companions' sayings, then qiyās; failing all, taqrīr al-uṣūl (leave things as they were). The example for the last is the leftover of a donkey, where one does wuḍūʾ and also tayammum.
What is the rukn of taʿāruḍ and what conflicts are not true taʿāruḍ?
فركن المعارضة تقابل الحجتين على السواء
The confrontation of two proofs on equal terms, with no superiority of one in essence or attribute. So between muḥkam and mufassar, ʿibāra and ishāra, there is only an apparent conflict (the one is stronger in attribute); between mashhūr and āḥād, or between a khāṣṣ and an ʿāmm that has been specified, there is none at all (stronger in essence). The ḥāshiya adds: when two statements come from one speaker the second cancels the first.tap to reveal
What are the conditions of taʿāruḍ?
Unity of subject (a woman is lawful to her husband and unlawful to his father: no conflict), unity of time (wine was lawful then unlawful: no conflict) and opposition of rulings. The ḥāshiya adds unity of relation and equality in strength, and that the conflict must not be resolvable by combining them.tap to reveal
What is the ruling when two verses conflict, and what is the example?
من كان له إمام فقراءة الإمام قراءة له
Resort to the Sunna, because two verses that conflict cancel each other (they are the speech of one Speaker). Not to a third verse, if the third leads to the same conflict. The example: 'recite what is easy of the Qurʾān' (Q 73:20) generally obliges recitation on the follower, while 'when the Qurʾān is recited, listen and be silent' (Q 7:204) by its specific meaning denies it; both came regarding prayer, so they fall, and one turns to the ḥadīth 'whoever has an imām, the imām's recitation is his recitation'.tap to reveal

The affirming report outweighs the denying one: Ibn Abān vs al-Karkhī

When one report affirms and another denies, the affirmer is preferred (al-muthbit awlā min al-nāfī), because it carries added knowledge; al-Karkhī holds this, while ʿĪsā b. Abān says they conflict and tarjīḥ goes by the narrator's quality. The Manār reconciles by a principle: a denial counts as equal to the affirmation only where it is of a kind known by evidence, or where the narrator is known to have relied on evidence. Otherwise (denial based on the continuing original state) the affirmation wins. Three examples: Barīra's husband (free or slave), Maymūna's marriage in iḥrām, and purity of water or lawfulness of food.
What is the rule on an affirming and denying report, and who disagrees?
The affirming report is preferred to the denying one since it includes added knowledge. This is the view of al-Karkhī and the Ḥanafī companions of the Manār; ʿĪsā b. Abān (and the Muʿtazilī Qāḍī ʿAbd al-Jabbār) say the two conflict, being equal in the conditions of a sound report, so one turns to preference by the narrator's probity and accuracy. What is the Manār's principle for when a denial equals an affirmation?

Taʿāruḍ between two reports: preference by narrator, additions, and mutlaq/muqayyad

If the denial is of the type that rests on the original state, the affirming report of impurity or prohibition wins; if both rest on evidence, they conflict and the original state serves as a tie-breaker (it is no proof, but it can tip the scale). Between two reports, preference is not by the number of narrators nor by their being free or male, since probity and exactness are what count, and a few just narrators outweigh many sinners. Where one report has an extra word and the narrator is the same trusted man, the addition is taken. Where narrators differ, the two reports are two reports, as in the muṭlaq with different rulings.
What is the ruling when one report says water or food is impure or forbidden and another says pure or lawful?
The report of impurity or prohibition is preferred because it affirms; the denial rests on the original state. The ḥāshiya says: ask the basis of each report; if the first rests on the original state, impurity prevails; if both rest on evidence they conflict and istiṣḥāb (which is not a proof but can serve as a tie-breaker) gives purity; if unknown, impurity prevails as stronger. It also contrasts this with the donkey's leftover, where the conflict is between legal proofs and the doubt is allowed to become a ruling, whereas here a report establishes no ruling.tap to reveal
Does a greater number of narrators give preference between two reports?
No. Neither the number of narrators nor the freedom or sex of the narrator gives preference, as the Companions showed: ʿUmar and others preferred ʿĀʾisha's single report over the many narrators of 'innamā al-māʾ min al-māʾ'. Probity and exactness are what count: ʿĀʾisha was better than many men, Bilāl better than many free men, and a few just are better than many sinful. But where each report is of āḥād rank and one side has two narrators and the other one, some prefer the two. The ḥāshiya mentions that Muḥammad (in the water chapters of the Mabsūṭ, in kitāb al-istiḥsān) prefers two over one (e.g. one says water pure, two say impure) and Ḥanafīs left it by istiḥsān.tap to reveal

So the Ḥanafīs hold the mutual oath only when the goods exist, and say the other narration omitted the clause from carelessness (or because the clause is implied).tap to reveal

Faṣl on the kinds of bayān: taqrīr and tafsīr

Both the Book and the Sunna can be clarified. The Manār counts five kinds of bayān by induction: taqrīr, tafsīr, taghyīr, ḍarūra and tabdīl. Taqrīr confirms the apparent sense and removes the chance of metaphor or specification (the bird that flies with its wings; 'the angels all prostrated'). Tafsīr clarifies the mujmal or mushtarak (prayer and zakāt, explained by the Prophet's word and act; qurūʾ, explained by 'a slave woman's divorce is two and her ʿidda two ḥayḍs'). Both are valid whether joined or separated from the text; some theologians restrict separation to taqrīr and tafsīr proper.
How does the Manār illustrate taqrīr and tafsīr?
Taqrīr: 'no creature flies with its wings' (Q 6:38): 'ṭāʾir' could mean fast movement (as the courier is called a bird), so 'with its wings' cuts the metaphor and confirms the literal. 'So the angels prostrated, all of them together' (Q 15:30): 'angels' is plural and could be specific, so 'all of them together' removes specification and confirms generality. Tafsīr: the mujmal (the Prophet's word and act explained the pillars of prayer and amounts of zakāt) and the mushtarak (qurūʾ in Q 2:228 is explained by the ḥadīth 'a slave woman's divorce is two and her ʿidda is two ḥayḍs', which shows the free woman's ʿidda is three ḥayḍs, not three purities).tap to reveal
Must a tafsīr or taqrīr be joined to the text?
No: both are valid joined or separated. Some theologians (Ḥanbalīs, some Shāfiʿīs such as Abū Isḥāq al-Marwazī and Abū Bakr al-Ṣayrafī) and Muʿtazilīs (ʿAbd al-Jabbār, al-Jubbāʾī and his son) did not allow separated bayān of the mujmal and mushtarak, because the purpose of speech is to oblige action, which depends on understanding, which depends on bayān; so delay would impose the impossible. The Ḥanafī reply: the address before explanation has a benefit: a trial by believing in the truth of the meant sense in general while waiting for the explanation to act, and there is no harm; delaying bayān beyond the time of need is invalid, not delaying beyond the address. Support: 'then upon Us is its clarification' (Q 75:19), where 'thumma' indicates delay.tap to reveal

Bayān al-taghyīr (1): it must be joined; the delayed exception

Taghyīr changes the apparent meaning of the words to something else, as the condition (ta'līq bi-l-sharṭ) and exception do: 'you are divorced if you enter the house' turns an immediate divorce into a conditional one; 'he owes me a thousand except a hundred' changes a thousand to nine hundred. Because the condition and the exception are not independent speech, and because they are like a qarīna that must accompany what it explains, the Ḥanafīs require continuity (waṣl), with breathing, coughing or sneezing not counting as a break. The Prophet's ḥadīth on the oath (atone, then do the better thing) shows atonement, not a delayed 'God willing', is the way out of an oath.
Why must taghyīr be joined to the speech?
Because the condition and the exception are incomplete speech with no benefit without what precedes; because the bayān of taghyīr is a qarīna that diverts the word from its apparent meaning and a qarīna accompanies the word in use; and because if it were valid separately, trust in promise and threat would vanish. The Prophet's ḥadīth 'whoever swears an oath and sees something better, let him atone for his oath and do the better' made atonement the way out of an oath; if exception could come later, it would also be a way out ('God willing' later), cancelling oaths.tap to reveal
Does Ibn ʿAbbās hold that a delayed exception is valid, and how do the Ḥanafīs answer?
Ibn ʿAbbās is reported to allow 'God willing' even a year later (using the Prophet's saying 'I shall raid Quraysh', and after a pause 'God willing'; and Q 18:24 'and remember your Lord when you forget'); some reports say within a year, six months or a month. The Ḥanafīs reject the report's soundness; if sound, then his meaning was that someone who had intended the exception at the moment of speaking and declared it later is believed in conscience (diyāna), and this applies to an inner intention. The ḥāshiya says the Prophet's pause was from breathing or coughing, as the commentary in al-Talwīḥ says. The well-known story: when the Abbasid caliph al-Manṣūr asked Abū Ḥanīfa why he opposed his ancestor Ibn ʿAbbās, he answered: 'if that were valid, those who gave you the pledge could say 'God willing' later and your pledge would be broken,' and the caliph was silenced.tap to reveal

Takhṣīṣ as bayān taghyīr: the Shāfiʿī dispute and three objections

The Ḥanafīs hold that takhṣīṣ of an ʿāmm must be connected (mustaqill and muqārin, as in the base text) because it changes the ʿāmm from certainty to probability (taghyīr). Al-Shāfiʿī: the ʿāmm is probable anyway, so takhṣīṣ only confirms its probability (taqrīr) and may be joined or separated. A second takhṣīṣ of an already specified ʿāmm may be delayed by agreement. Three objections from the Qurʾān are answered: the cow of Banī Isrāʾīl (a muṭlaq qualified by a later text, which is a naskh), Nūḥ's family (the 'ahl' never included the disbelieving son), and ʿAbd Allāh b. al-Zibaʿrā's objection to 'you and what you worship besides God' (the 'mā' is for the non-rational).
Is takhṣīṣ of the ʿāmm a taghyīr or a taqrīr, and what follows?
For the Ḥanafīs takhṣīṣ is bayān taghyīr, since the ʿāmm is qaṭʿī and after specification it becomes ẓannī; so it is bound by the condition of continuity. For al-Shāfiʿī, the ʿāmm is already ẓannī (no ʿāmm but has been specified), so takhṣīṣ only confirms its probability (taqrīr) and is valid joined or separated. The ḥāshiya observes that one may still say it changes the ʿāmm from comprehending all individuals (what it was set for) to some, so it is still taghyīr. If the ʿāmm was first specified by a joined specifier, a second specification may be delayed by agreement.tap to reveal
The cow of Banī Isrāʾīl was specified later: is that a delayed takhṣīṣ?
No. The command 'slaughter a cow' (Q 2:67) uses an indefinite in an affirmative context, which is specific to one individual but absolute in attributes, so the later description (neither old nor young, yellow, etc.) is a qualification of an absolute, which in their case was a naskh of the absoluteness, and naskh is always delayed. So it is not a takhṣīṣ of an ʿāmm.tap to reveal
Was Nūḥ's family specified later (Q 11:40 'and your family except those against whom the word has gone')?
The ʿāmm 'ahl' never included the disbelieving son, because 'ahl' in the religious sense are the followers in faith, not those merely related by lineage; so there was no later takhṣīṣ. The ḥāshiya raises the problem of Nūḥ's own plea ('my son is of my family', Q 11:45) and answers that he thought his son a believer, since he was a hypocrite; or that the exception 'man sabaqa' is for the disbelievers whoever they are, with the son entering by his lack of following. It also says this is not takhṣīṣ, nor bayān of a mujmal, but a bayān of taqrīr that confirmed what Allah meant.tap to reveal

Istithnāʾ: its nature, the Shāfiʿī dispute, and its reference after several clauses

The Manār defines istithnāʾ as preventing the speech from covering what is excepted, in a way that the speaker is treated as having spoken only of the remainder. So 'a thousand except a hundred' means nine hundred. Al-Shāfiʿī: the exception cancels by muʿāraḍa, i.e. the earlier ruling is met with a contrary one and the two cancel. The Ḥanafī proof is 'he stayed among them a thousand years less fifty' (Q 29:14), which would be a lie as muʿāraḍa; and the linguists' two statements (istithnāʾ min al-nafy ithbāt, and 'istikhrāj') are harmonised by saying our reading is the ʿibāra and his the ishāra. The exception is muttaṣil (the real one) or munqaṭiʿ (a metaphor, 'but'). For a series of clauses joined by 'and', the exception goes to the last clause (Ḥanafī), to all of them (Shāfiʿī, like a condition).
How do the Ḥanafīs and al-Shāfiʿī explain the operation of istithnāʾ?
Ḥanafī: the exception prevents speaking about the excepted amount, so one has spoken only about the remainder, as if he said 'nine hundred'. Al-Shāfiʿī: the exception acts by muʿāraḍa: the speaker first gave a ruling on the whole including the excepted part, then the exception contradicts it for that part, and the two cancel, so the ruling remains on the remainder. The ḥāshiya says the difference appears in an exception of another genus, 'a thousand dirhams except a garment': for us it fails, for him it works by subtracting the garment's value; but the ḥāshiya notes that this too is open to objection.tap to reveal
What is al-Shāfiʿī's proof, and what is the Ḥanafī reply?
His proof: the linguists agree that an exception from a negation is an affirmation and from an affirmation a negation; and the Shahāda 'lā ilāha illā Allāh' is tawḥīd, which is both a negation and an affirmation. If it were only 'speaking about the remainder' it would deny others without affirming Allah. Ours: the verse 'he stayed among them a thousand years except fifty' (Q 29:14) is an account, and muʿāraḍa works only for establishing in the present, not for reports; if it worked by muʿāraḍa the verse would be false, which Allah is far above. And the linguists also say istithnāʾ is 'extraction' (istikhrāj) and speaking of the remainder; the two statements are harmonised by calling ours the ʿibāra (what the speech is made for) and his the ishāra.tap to reveal
What are the kinds of istithnāʾ?
Muttaṣil, the real exception, where the excepted can be extracted from the first part; and munqaṭiʿ, which cannot be extracted because it is not of the same genus, and which grammarians call munqaṭiʿ; calling it 'exception' is metaphorical, since it is an independent new sentence meaning 'but'. The example is Ibrāhīm's 'they are an enemy to me, except the Lord of the worlds' (Q 26:77), read either as 'but the Lord of the worlds is not my enemy' (new sentence) or, if his people worshipped Allah along with idols, as a muttaṣil exception.tap to reveal

Bayān al-ḍarūra: silence, circumstance, frequency of use, and ʿaṭf

Bayān al-ḍarūra is clarification obtained by what is not set for clarification, namely silence. It takes four forms: (1) silence that takes the place of speech (the share of the mother in 'wa-warithahu abawāhu fa-li-ummihi al-thuluth' implies the father gets the rest); (2) silence by the state of the speaker (the Lawgiver seeing people trade and not objecting; the Companions' silence over a gharūr's offspring, when ʿUmar fixed their value but said nothing of the usufruct, an ijmāʿ that the usufruct of the gharūr's children is not owed); (3) silence by necessity (the master silent as his slave trades is an authorisation, Zufar dissenting); and (4) by frequency of speech (a hundred and a dirham means all dirhams). The ḥāshiya concludes that the silent person is 'speaking by the tongue of his state'.
How does the silence of the Lawgiver or the Companions count as bayān?
If the Prophet ﷺ saw people doing a matter such as muḍāraba, partnership or selling in the market, and did not forbid it, it shows it is permitted; silence stands in place of a command of permission. The Companions' silence is the same provided that they can object and the doer is Muslim. The example: a slave woman claimed to be free and married a man, bore him children, then her master came; ʿUmar ruled that the master took the woman and the father ransomed the children with their value, and said nothing of the value of the usufruct of the children; since this was in the Companions' presence, it is ijmāʿ that the usufruct of the child of a gharūr is not guaranteed.tap to reveal
What is the effect of a master's silence when his slave trades?
For Abū Ḥanīfa it is an authorisation to trade (for later transactions), because otherwise people would be harmed by dealing with him and would be deceived, and averting deception is obligatory. Zufar: no authorisation: the silence could be satisfaction or extreme anger, and a doubtful matter is not a proof. The Ḥanafī reply: custom indicates that a man who does not accept his slave's dealing speaks out and disciplines him.tap to reveal
How can frequency of use make silence a clarification?
When a speaker omits the specifier because of the length of the speech or its frequent use: 'a hundred and ten dirhams' is understood as all dirhams, and 'a hundred and a dirham' means a hundred dirhams and a dirham, because the ʿaṭf makes the hundred explained by the dirham. This holds for things that are normally owed in the account as measured or weighed goods; it does not hold for 'a hundred and a garment', since a garment is not owed in the account except in salam and the hundred remains unspecified; the speaker is believed in its explanation.tap to reveal

Bayān al-tabdīl (naskh): its nature and permissibility

Tabdīl is naskh. The Manār's idea is that an absolute ruling known to Allah to be limited in time, but stated without a limit, appears to us to last; its later abrogation is a bayān of its true duration from Allah's side, and a tabdīl from ours. The example is killing: it is a clarification that the victim's term had come, and a tabdīl from the human side, who thought he would have lived on, so qiṣāṣ and diya are due. Naskh is permitted for all Muslims by the verse 'whatever verse We abrogate or cause to be forgotten…' against (some of) the Jews, who say it implies foolishness or ignorance; the reply is Allah's wisdom, seen in the changing treatment a physician gives a patient, and in the Sharīʿa itself (marriage of siblings in Ādam's law, later forbidden).
In what sense is naskh both a bayān and a tabdīl?
If an absolute ruling is known to Allah to end after a period but is stated without limit, it seems to us to continue; when the abrogating text comes, it is a pure bayān for Allah (it shows the period known to Him) but a tabdīl for us, since it replaces what we thought permanent. For example, if intoxicants were permitted without limit and later forbidden. Likewise killing is bayān of the victim's appointed term, and tabdīl for people who thought he would have lived, so qiṣāṣ and diya apply. The ḥāshiya objects that bayān concerns us, not Allah, to whom everything is clear, and notes that Shams al-Aʾimma did not count naskh among the kinds of bayān.tap to reveal
Is naskh possible, and how does the Manār answer those who deny it?
It is permitted by the verse 'whatever verse We abrogate or cause to be forgotten, We bring a better or a like one' (Q 2:106) and by agreement of Muslims. The Jews say it implies foolishness (changing one's mind) and ignorance of outcomes, which does not suit divinity; their aim is to keep the Sharīʿa of Mūsā permanent. The reply: Allah is wise and knows the needs of servants, so He rules each day as is suitable, as He creates a child and later causes him to die, and as a physician changes the patient's diet; this is not foolishness. The Jews' own Scripture bears naskh: in Ādam's law a brother could marry the twin sister of another, then it was forbidden in Nūḥ's. The ḥāshiya lists the three Jewish groups (one denies it by reason, one by revelation, one accepts it but limits Muḥammad's mission to the Arabs) and notes Abū Muslim al-Iṣfahānī among the Muʿtazila as an exception.tap to reveal

Conditions of naskh: the subject, no perpetuity, and taʿqīd al-qalb

What can be abrogated is a practical sharʿī ruling that could exist or not. It cannot be an obligatory-in-itself act like belief or a wrong-in-itself like disbelief, and it must not be linked to a limit or perpetuity: if a time was fixed, it ends by itself, not by naskh ('forgive and overlook until Allah brings His command'; the confinement of adulteresses until Allah makes a way). Reports (akhbār) are not abrogated, because truth in a report does not change with a new text. The condition is that the addressee has time to believe in it in his heart, not necessarily to do it: this is the Ḥanafī view against the Muʿtazila and others, with proof from the fifty prayers of the Isrāʾ reduced to five.
What rulings can be abrogated?
A sharʿī practical ruling that could either be or not be: not what is by nature obligatory like īmān or by nature forbidden like kufr (belief's obligation never changes in any religion), and not rational rulings such as God's unity. It must also not carry a time limit or perpetuity: with a time limit it ends by itself (e.g. 'stay in your homes three days', to the people of Ṣāliḥ; 'forgive and overlook until Allah brings His command', Q 2:109); with perpetuity, by explicit mention of 'forever' or by implication such as the laws that the Prophet died upholding, naskh is impossible. The ḥāshiya says some allow naskh of a ruling tied to 'forever' too, since both rulings are inshāʾ, but notes Fakhr al-Islām's view.tap to reveal
Must the addressee have time to perform the act before it is abrogated?
For us it is enough that he has time to believe in it with his heart (taʿqīd al-qalb); we do not require time to perform it. The Muʿtazila (and some Ḥanafī shaykhs, Shāfiʿīs and Ḥanbalīs) require time to act. Our proof: on the night of the Miʿrāj fifty daily prayers were commanded, then the excess over five abrogated within an hour, before anyone could perform them; only the Prophet had time to believe in them and, being imām of the Umma, his belief counts as theirs. The ḥāshiya raises the objection that the Umma had not been told, and that the Prophet had performed things on the Miʿrāj no one else could, so he may have been able to do them.tap to reveal

What can abrogate: qiyās, ijmāʿ, Book and Sunna

Qiyās cannot abrogate, nor can ijmāʿ: the Companions left opinion for the Book and Sunna (ʿAlī: had religion been by opinion the underside of the khuff would be wiped before the top), no one has authority to end a ruling of Allah, and opinion cannot know the end of a ruling's goodness. Fakhr al-Islām's statement that ijmāʿ may abrogate ijmāʿ is explained as a change in the maṣlaḥa; for the Muʿtazila who say ijmāʿ abrogated the share of the muʾallafa, the reply is that the ʿilla (weakness of Islam) ceased. Naskh by Book and Sunna works in all four combinations; al-Shāfiʿī allows only Book-by-Book and Sunna-by-Sunna, on the argument that critics would say the Prophet first contradicted God, and from 'We bring something better or like it'.
Can qiyās or ijmāʿ abrogate anything?
No. Qiyās abrogates none of the Book, Sunna, ijmāʿ or qiyās: the Companions abandoned opinion for the text (ʿAlī: 'were religion by opinion, the underside of the khuff would be wiped before the top; yet I saw the Messenger wipe the top'), no one has authority to end a ruling of Allah, and opinion cannot know when a ruling stops being good. Ibn Surayj (Shāfiʿī) allows abrogation of Book and Sunna by qiyās and al-Afṭasī allows it for qiyās derived from the Book or Sunna; the ḥāshiya notes that the attribute linking the branch is not known with certainty to be the ruling's meaning. As for a later qiyās, the mujtahid acts on it because he learns that the earlier was not sound, not by abrogation. Ijmāʿ abrogates nothing either, as it is a consensus of opinions, and cannot be abrogated by agreement on the ruling's permanence.tap to reveal
Can ijmāʿ abrogate, or be abrogated?
For the majority ijmāʿ neither abrogates nor is abrogated, since agreement of all without time limit indicates a ruling that is good in itself. Fakhr al-Islām says in the chapter on naskh that it cannot, and in the chapter on ijmāʿ that ijmāʿ may be abrogated by ijmāʿ; Mullā Jīwan reconciles: perhaps he meant that an ijmāʿ may rest on a maṣlaḥa and, when it changes, a new ijmāʿ may stand in its place. The ḥāshiya (al-Sunbulī) adds that Fakhr al-Islām makes an exception for the ijmāʿ of the Companions, which is stronger and cannot be abrogated by a later one. Some Muʿtazila: the Book can be abrogated by ijmāʿ, citing the share of the muʾallafa (those whose hearts are reconciled, Q 9:60) dropped by ijmāʿ under Abū Bakr. Reply: the ruling ended because its ʿilla (weakness of Islam) ended; or ʿUmar acted on a ḥadīth the Companions agreed on.tap to reveal
Which kinds of naskh between Book and Sunna are valid?
Four for the Ḥanafīs: Book by Book, Sunna by Sunna, Book by Sunna and Sunna by Book. Al-Shāfiʿī allows only the two same-kind cases. His arguments: if Book could be abrogated by Sunna, critics would say the Prophet first belied God; if Sunna by Book, that God contradicted His Messenger; and 'We bring one better than it or like it' implies the same kind (a Sunna is not better than or like the Qurʾān, which is Allah's speech and a miracle). The reply: such taunts cannot be avoided even in same-kind naskh and come from foolish people; and 'better' is in the welfare of servants, not in wording. The 'presentation' ḥadīth ('whatever comes to you from me, present it to the Book of Allah') is weak: the ḥāshiya cites al-Khaṭṭābī that the heretics made it and Fīrūzābādī that it is among the clearest forgeries; and the ḥadīth 'I was given the Qurʾān and its like with it'. Kinds of the mansūkh: wording, ruling, and a quality of the ruling (addition to the text)

The mansūkh in the Qurʾān is of four kinds: wording and ruling together (e.g. what was withdrawn from hearts in the Prophet's lifetime, such as parts of sūrat al-Aḥzāb that were once long), ruling without wording (the verses of non-fighting, 'to you your religion', abrogated by the verses of fighting), wording without ruling (the verse of stoning of the old man and woman; Ibn Masʿūd's 'three consecutive days'), and the abrogation of a quality (waṣf) of a ruling, namely its absoluteness, which is what an addition to the text is. For the Ḥanafīs, an addition to the Qurʾānic text is naskh and therefore needs a mutawātir or mashhūr report (wiping over the khuffs added to washing the feet); for al-Shāfiʿī it is a takhṣīṣ/bayān and a solitary report or qiyās suffices (banishment added to the lash, 'believing' added to the slave in the expiation of oath).
What are the kinds of mansūkh in the Qurʾān?
(1) Wording and ruling together, by 'making forget': e.g. sūrat al-Aḥzāb equalled sūrat al-Baqara in length but now has seventy-odd verses (the ḥāshiya notes different numbers in the sources). (2) Ruling without wording: 'to you your religion and to me mine' and about seventy (some say about 120) verses on not fighting, abrogated by the verses of fighting. (3) Wording without ruling: 'al-shaykh wa-l-shaykha idhā zanayā farjumūhumā' (ʿUmar attested it was recited), and Ibn Masʿūd's reading 'three consecutive days' in the expiation of oath and 'their right hands' for 'their hands' in the thief verse. (4) A quality of the ruling is abrogated, such as an addition to the text.tap to reveal
Is an addition to the text a naskh, and what follows?
For us it is naskh, since it lifts the absoluteness of the text. So wiping over the khuffs added to washing the feet (which the Book makes the duty, whether or not the khuffs are worn) is a naskh by the mashhūr ḥadīth (reported by nearly thirty Companions, mutawātir in meaning): washing is now only the duty for one not wearing khuffs. A solitary report cannot abrogate the certain Book, so an addition by one is refused. For al-Shāfiʿī it is bayān/takhṣīṣ, so a solitary report and qiyās may add: banishment for a year added to the hundred lashes ('al-bikr bi-l-bikr jald miʾa wa-taghrīb ʿām'), and the condition of belief in the slave freed in the expiation of oath and ẓihār, by analogy with the expiation of killing.tap to reveal

Faṣl on the Prophet's acts: slips, the kinds of act, and following them

After the Prophet's words, the Manār treats his acts. The acts he performed intentionally fall under four ranks (mubāḥ, mustaḥabb, wājib, farḍ), and the slip (zalla) is excluded since the aim of the chapter is what the Umma may follow. A slip is a lapse caused by pursuing a permitted act he intended, never persisted in (like one who stumbles on the road and falls, or Mūsā's blow that killed the Copt). The Manār's position on following: what we know of his acts and the rank on which he did them, we follow on that same rank until proof of specificity; where the rank is unknown, one acts on the lowest, ibāḥa. Others require tawaqquf (al-Daqqāq, al-Ghazālī) or following (Mālik, Ibn Surayj).
Which of the Prophet's acts do we follow?
His deliberate acts fall into four ranks and are followed; not followed are: acts done by slip (e.g. the salām after two rakʿas of Ẓuhr), natural acts (sleeping, eating, drinking), and those peculiar to him (marrying more than four; the ḥāshiya notes that the only ḥadīth that Ḍuḥā was wājib on him is a weak report from Ibn ʿAbbās). Wājib in the technical sense does not occur in respect of him, since his proofs were all certain, though it occurs in respect of us; the ḥāshiya offers two other ways of keeping the four-fold division valid (ijtihād with no persistence, or wājib meaning completion rather than pillar).tap to reveal
When an act's rank is unknown, what do we do?
The position adopted by the Manār (that of Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ al-Rāzī) is that what we know he did on a given rank (wājib, mandūb or mubāḥ), we do on that same rank until proof of specificity: what was wājib for him is wājib for us, mandūb mandūb, mubāḥ mubāḥ; and if we do not know the rank, we do it on the lowest level, ibāḥa, since he never did what was forbidden or disliked, so it must be at least permissible. Proof: 'say: if you love Allah, follow me' (Q 3:31). Others: tawaqquf (Abū Bakr al-Daqqāq, al-Ghazālī among the Shāfiʿīs), because following means matching the act and its rank, which is unknown. Mālik and Ibn Surayj: ittibāʿ is obligatory unless a proof shows it to be mubāḥ, on 'obey Allah and obey the Messenger' (Q 4:59).tap to reveal

Kinds of waḥy and the Prophet's ijtihād

The Manār turns to how the Prophet ﷺ made the rulings known by waḥy, which is either apparent or hidden. The apparent waḥy has three forms: what came by the tongue of the angel (Jibrīl) with certainty that the messenger was Jibrīl (the Qurʾān); what he was given by the angel's sign without words ('the Holy Spirit blew into my heart that no soul dies until it completes its provision'); and what Allah cast into his heart by inspiration (ilhām), a certain proof for him, unlike the saints' ilhām which is only for themselves. The hidden waḥy is ijtihād, by pondering the texts to derive the ʿilla and extend the ruling. Opponents say ijtihād is not waḥy and deny that he made it. The Ḥanafīs: he first waits for waḥy (up to three days or until the aim would be lost), then makes ijtihād; he is never left uncorrected on an error, unlike other mujtahids; the captives of Badr are the standard example.
What are the kinds of apparent waḥy?
Three: (1) by the tongue of the angel Jibrīl, after the Prophet's certain knowledge that the bearer is Jibrīl: the Qurʾān; (2) by the angel's sign without words, as in 'the Holy Spirit blew into my heart that no soul will die until it completes its sustenance, so fear Allah and be moderate in seeking'; (3) by ilhām, which Allah casts into his heart without doubt, by a light from Him. The saints share ilhām, but theirs may be right or wrong, while his can only be right. The ḥāshiya adds a fourth kind (what he heard directly from Allah without an angel, the ḥadīth qudsī) and denies the 'satanic verses' story as a forgery by the heretics.tap to reveal
Did the Prophet ﷺ make ijtihād, and was it waḥy?
Yes. The hidden waḥy is ijtihād, by deriving the ʿilla of the stated rulings and extending them to cases without text, as other mujtahids do. Opponents (Ashʿarīs and most Muʿtazila) say that he did not, since Q 53:3-4 says 'he does not speak from desire; it is only revelation revealed'; and ijtihād is not waḥy. The reply: the verse refers to the Qurʾān, not all he said; or even if general, his ijtihād is a hidden waḥy through the confirmation and the fact that he is not left on error. In the Ḥanafī view he is commanded to wait for waḥy first (up to three days, or until the goal is at risk); if none comes, that is permission to use opinion, by 'so take lesson, O people of insight' (Q 59:2), and who is more insightful than him?tap to reveal
What do the captives of Badr teach about the Prophet's ijtihād?
After Badr there were seventy captives (including al-ʿAbbās and ʿAqīl). He consulted the Companions: Abū Bakr advised ransom and release in hope they would become Muslim; ʿUmar advised having each Muslim kill his own relative. He preferred Abū Bakr's view, and after ransom was taken Q 8:67-69 was revealed ('it is not for a prophet to have captives until he has thoroughly subdued the land… had it not been for a decree from Allah that preceded…'). He wept and said 'had punishment come down only ʿUmar would have escaped'. So the truth was ʿUmar's opinion, and the Prophet erred in acting on Abū Bakr's, but he was not left in it: he was reproved. The ransom remained lawful ('eat of what you took as lawful and good'). This is the difference between a text revealed against an opinion (the opinion's ruling stands) and the opinion's contradiction with a text discovered beforehand (it is annulled).tap to reveal

Sharʿ man qablanā: the laws of previous prophets

The question is whether the laws of earlier prophets bind us. The Manār chooses a middle view: they bind us when Allah or His Messenger narrates them to us without rejecting them. If they are only found in the Torah and Gospel they do not bind us, because those books were altered, and no one can be sure they come from Allah. If Allah narrates a rule and then objects ('do not do the like') or the narration itself shows it was a punishment, they do not bind. This is a major principle of Abū Ḥanīfa from which many fiqh rulings branch. Examples that bind: the law of equal retaliation, 'life for life, eye for eye' (Q 5:45), the turn-taking in the water of Ṣāliḥ's she-camel (muhāyāt), and the prohibition of liwāṭ from Lūṭ's people. Examples that do not: the forbidden fat and claw-footed animals of the Jews, which Allah presents as a punishment for their wrongdoing.
Do the laws of earlier prophets bind us?
Some say they bind us absolutely, since every law established for a prophet stays until the Day of Judgement because it is among Allah's approved matters, unless a proof of abrogation comes (Q 6:90 'those are whom Allah guided, so follow their guidance'). This is the view of most Shāfiʿīs and some of our shaykhs. Others say they never bind, since each prophet's law ends with the sending of the next, unless it cannot be abrogated (Q 5:48 'for each of you We have made a law and a way'). The ḥāshiya answers the first that being approved does not mean permanent, and the second that the verse only shows that the first law is abrogated, not that it is abrogated entirely.tap to reveal
What is the Manār's own rule for earlier laws?
They bind us when Allah or His Messenger narrates them to us without rejection. If they merely exist in the Torah and Gospel, they do not bind us, because the Jews and Christians altered them and added what they desired, so we cannot be sure they come from Allah (and the report of one who has embraced Islam from them is likewise not taken). If Allah narrates a rule and then rejects it, explicitly ('do not do the like') or by implication (that it was punishment for their wrongdoing), then acting on it is forbidden. The ḥāshiya says that when they bind, it is as the law of our Prophet, not as the law of earlier prophets: once narrated without rejection, it becomes a part of our religion, supported by 'so follow their guidance' (Q 6:90) and 'follow the creed of Ibrāhīm' (Q 16:123).tap to reveal

Binding (narrated and not rejected): 'We prescribed for them in it: life for life, eye for eye, nose for nose, ear for ear, tooth for tooth, and wounds equal retaliation' (Q 5:45), so retaliation stands for us; the turn-taking in water ('tell them the water is shared between them and the she-camel', Q 54:28) which proves muhāyāt is lawful; and the prohibition of homosexuality from Lūṭ's people (Q 27:55 'do you come to men in lust instead of women'). Taqlīd of the Companion

Following a Companion (a mujtahid Companion) is obligatory and overrides qiyās (of the Followers and later), because his saying probably comes from hearing the Prophet ﷺ, and even if it is his own opinion, it is stronger than others' opinion since they witnessed the circumstances of revelation and the secrets of the Sharīʿa. One Companion's qiyās is not left for another's saying, since either may be heard. Al-Karkhī: following is obligatory only in what qiyās cannot reach; al-Shāfiʿī: no one is followed. Our scholars agree on following in what cannot be known by qiyās (the minimum of ḥayḍ, the sale of what one sold for less before payment); in what qiyās can reach they differ (ʿilm of the capital in salam; the guarantee of the joint hired worker).
What is the ruling on following a Companion, and who disagrees?
The Manār: following the Companion is obligatory and qiyās of those after him is left for it. Reasons: his saying probably derives from hearing the Prophet ﷺ (almost apparent in his case even if not attributed to him), and if it is his opinion, his opinion is stronger than that of others, since they saw the circumstances of the revelation and the secrets of the Sharīʿa. One Companion's qiyās is not left for the saying of another, since either may have been heard. Al-Karkhī (and Qāḍī Abū Zayd): obligatory only where qiyās cannot reach, because then hearing is the only source; where qiyās reaches, it may be his own mistaken opinion. Al-Shāfiʿī: no Companion is followed whatever the case, since they differ among themselves and none is infallible.tap to reveal
In what do the Ḥanafī imams agree on following a Companion?
In what qiyās cannot reach: Abū Ḥanīfa and the two followed ʿĀʾisha's statement that the minimum ḥayḍ is three days and nights, and the maximum ten, since the intellect cannot establish measures; and the ruling that buying back for less before the first price is paid is invalid (ʿĀʾisha told Zayd b. Bāb al-Ijmāʿ: definition, rukn and silent ijmāʿ

Ijmāʿ lexically means agreement (and also resolve); in the Sharīʿa it is the agreement of righteous mujtahids of the Umma of Muḥammad ﷺ in one era on a matter of word or act. Its rukn is of two kinds: ʿazīma, where they all speak (or all begin to act: as when the mujtahids all take up muḍāraba, muzāraʿa or partnership, which is an ijmāʿ on their lawfulness), and rukhṣa, where some speak or act and the rest are silent after the period of reflection (three days or a session of learning). This silent ijmāʿ is accepted by us against al-Shāfiʿī, who says silence may be from awe. The ḥāshiya notes that a single case, Ibn ʿAbbās's alleged silence in the ʿawl dispute, is answered by ʿUmar's own readiness to hear the truth.
What is ijmāʿ according to the Manār, and what does each qualification exclude?
وهو في اللغة الاتفاق
The agreement of righteous mujtahids of the Umma of Muḥammad ﷺ in one era on a matter of word or deed. 'Mujtahids' excludes the muqallids; 'righteous' excludes mujtahids who are people of whim or sinners; 'of the Umma of Muḥammad' excludes mujtahids of earlier laws; 'in one era' means the mujtahids existing at one time. The ḥāshiya notes the matter may be sharʿī, rational or customary (such as war affairs) as long as it is not established decisively by the Book and Sunna, and that the definition would be more complete as 'agreement in every era of all those qualified', covering matters where the laity and the mujtahids share.tap to reveal
What is the rukn of ijmāʿ?
ركن الإجماع نوعان
Two kinds. ʿAzīma: that all of them speak on the matter ('we agree on this'), or all begin the act, as when all the mujtahids start muḍāraba, muzāraʿa or a partnership (an ijmāʿ on their legality); the ḥāshiya adds the caliphate of Abū Bakr, to which the Companions pledged with hands and accepted with tongues. Rukhṣa: some speak or act while the rest are silent and do not respond after the time of reflection, three days or a session of knowledge, which is called silent ijmāʿ and is accepted among us.tap to reveal
Is silent ijmāʿ a proof?
For us, yes. Al-Shāfiʿī: no, because silence may be agreement or awe and does not show consent; as reported of Ibn ʿAbbās who differed from ʿUmar over ʿawl and was asked why he did not state his proof, replying 'he was a man to be feared, and his whip kept me back'. The people of ijmāʿ and what is not a condition

The people of ijmāʿ are mujtahids who are righteous: free of whim and sin, and except in matters where no opinion is needed. In matters needing no opinion (the five prayers, fasting, zakāt, the transmission of the Qurʾān, the number of rakʿas), a rejection by even one of the laity or elite stops it being ijmāʿ. In matters of ijtihād the laity are not counted: they must follow mujtahids (al-Bāqillānī, who accepted the laity's word, is answered). Not conditions: being a Companion, or of the Prophet's family (ʿitra), or the people of Madīna, or the dying out of the era (al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad), nor absence of earlier disagreement.
Who counts in ijmāʿ?
Righteous mujtahids: someone who is not a mujtahid is not counted in matters of ijtihād (he must follow mujtahids, and his disagreement does not count), and one who has whim (innovation) or sin has no place, because the Umma's honour in infallibility is for those whose opinion is sound. But in what needs no opinion, where laity and elite share (prayers, fasting, zakāt, the transmission of the Qurʾān, the number of rakʿas, the amounts of zakāt, borrowing bread and using the bathhouse), even one dissenter from the laity or elite prevents ijmāʿ. Al-Bāqillānī: ijtihād is not a condition even in ijtihādī matters and the laity's word suffices; the reply is that the laity must follow mujtahids. Al-Āmidī and al-Ghazālī do not require the mujtahid to be just, but the ḥāshiya says the truth is that it is required, for the sinner's word needs verifying.tap to reveal
Are being a Companion, the Prophet's family, the people of Madīna, or the dying out of the era conditions?
No. Some said there is no ijmāʿ except by the Companions (the best generations: 'khayr al-qurūn qarnī'), others (the Shīʿa) only by the Prophet's family (ʿitra), citing 'I leave among you what, if you hold to it, you will never go astray: the Book of Allah and my family'; the reply is that this shows their merit, not that their ijmāʿ alone is a proof. Mālik: the people of Madīna only, citing 'Madīna expels its impurities as the bellows expel the dross of iron', and error is impurity; the reply is that this is for their merit, and that a mistake in ijtihād is not impurity, since the mujtahid is rewarded. Al-Shāfiʿī (and Aḥmad in a report): the era must die out first, because retraction before then is possible; the reply is that the texts establishing authority do not distinguish between their dying and not dying, and adding it would be naskh by qiyās; so if one retracts after ijmāʿ is established it does not count.tap to reveal

The condition of ijmāʿ, its ruling and proofs of its authority

The condition is the agreement of all: the dissent of one prevents ijmāʿ as does the dissent of most (since 'Umma' covers all). The ruling of ijmāʿ established in the Sharʿ is that it gives certainty (yaqīn) which obliges both belief and action, and its denier is a kāfir (as the scholars of Bukhārā and Balkh held of the Rawāfiḍ for rejecting Abū Bakr's imamate). The proofs: the ḥadīth 'Allah's hand is over the group', the verses 'thus We made you a middle nation' (Q 2:143), 'you are the best nation brought out for mankind' (Q 3:110) and 'whoever opposes the Messenger… and follows other than the way of the believers' (Q 4:115), and the rational argument that every era has held the dissenter from ijmāʿ to be certainly wrong. Some Muʿtazila and Rawāfiḍ deny it, arguing that if each is fallible so is the sum; the reply is the strength of a rope of many hairs.
What is the condition of ijmāʿ, and how many mujtahids are needed?
The agreement of all: the dissent of one prevents it, as does the dissent of the majority, since the word 'Umma' covers all, and if one differs the 'all', on which infallibility depends, is not achieved. The ḥāshiya mentions views that the minimum is three (al-Sarakhsī), two, or even one if only one mujtahid remains (since 'Ibrāhīm was a nation', Q 16:120); and that the ijmāʿ of the majority is held by some to be a probable proof but not ijmāʿ.tap to reveal
What does ijmāʿ yield, and what is the status of its denier?

Ibn al-ʿArabī (cited by the ḥāshiya): a person does not become a kāfir as long as he holds to the Book and Sunna, even with faulty interpretation; what is indispensable in religion known to all is different. The basis (dāʿī) of ijmāʿ, and its transmission

Must ijmāʿ rest on a sanad? The Manār holds that it needs a dāʿī (a cause drawing the mujtahids to agree), because a fatwā without a legal proof is forbidden and unanimity without a cause is unimaginable (as with agreeing on one dish). The dāʿī may be a khabar al-wāḥid (the ijmāʿ on not selling food before possession, from 'lā tabīʿū al-ṭaʿām qabl al-qabḍ') or qiyās (the ijmāʿ on ribā in rice from analogy with the six items; the prohibition of granddaughters from Q 4:23). Opponents (the Ẓāhirīs, Ibn Jarīr) deny that qiyās may be the sanad. The use of ijmāʿ after a sanad is to end debate and make the ruling decisive. Ijmāʿ transmitted by tawātur gives certainty and action; by solitary reports (like ʿUbayda's 'the Companions agreed to preserve the four rakʿas') only action (against al-Ghazālī and some Ḥanafīs).
Must ijmāʿ rest on a sanad, and of what kind may it be?
Yes, according to the sounder, preferred view: a fatwā without a legal proof is forbidden, so the people of ijmāʿ need a basis from which they derive a ruling; and it is habitually impossible that all agree without a cause, as it is impossible that all agree on one dish. Some said no dāʿī is needed. Opponents of the requirement say the ijmāʿ would be pointless if the sanad suffices; the answer is that after the sanad exists, investigation stops and the ruling becomes decisive. Ranks of ijmāʿ and the composite ijmāʿ (end of Volume 1)

The Manār ranks ijmāʿ by strength (in itself, regardless of how it is transmitted). First: the Companions' explicit ijmāʿ (all say 'we agree'), like the Qurʾān in certainty, whose denier is a kāfir (e.g. the ijmāʿ on Abū Bakr's caliphate). Second: where some Companions speak and the rest are silent (silent ijmāʿ), whose denier is not a kāfir but is declared astray (because of al-Shāfiʿī's disagreement), as with fighting those who withheld zakāt. Third: the ijmāʿ of those after the Companions on a matter not previously disputed, like a mashhūr report: it gives ṭumaʾnīna, not certainty. Fourth: their ijmāʿ after a prior disagreement, which gives action but not knowledge and outranks qiyās like a solitary report. The Manār then states the rule: if the Umma differ on several opinions in any era, it is an ijmāʿ that any other is false (so the third opinion is void: the pregnant widow's ʿidda, either delivery or the longer term, not the four months and ten days). Some limit this to the Companions. This is the composite ijmāʿ (ijmāʿ murakkab) and 'ʿadam al-qāʾil bi-l-faṣl' is one species of it. The volume ends with the problem of squaring this with the four madhhabs.
What are the four ranks of ijmāʿ in the Manār?
(1) The Companions' ijmāʿ stated in words: like the verse in certainty, its denier a kāfir (e.g. ijmāʿ on the caliphate of Abū Bakr, which the Muhājirūn and Anṣār agreed on before the burial of the Prophet). (2) Some Companions speak and the rest are silent (silent ijmāʿ): the denier is not called a kāfir but is misguided, because of al-Shāfiʿī's disagreement (as the certainty of the ʿāmm: our ruling is certain and its denier is not a kāfir), as the ijmāʿ to fight those who refused zakāt. (3) The ijmāʿ of those after the Companions on what had no earlier dispute, which is like the mashhūr report: it gives ṭumaʾnīna, not certainty. (4) Their ijmāʿ after earlier disagreement: like a solitary report, it obliges action and outranks qiyās. The ḥāshiya says this ranking is Fakhr al-Islām's.tap to reveal
If the Umma disagree on opinions, may a new opinion be added?
No: if the Umma differ in any era on several opinions, that is an ijmāʿ that everything outside them is false, and it is not allowed to produce a new opinion. Example: the pregnant widow: some (Ibn Masʿūd, Abū Hurayra) say her ʿidda is delivery, and some (ʿAlī, Ibn ʿAbbās) say the longer of the two terms; both agree that the four months and ten days alone is not enough, so it is not valid for her to count the widow's term if it is not the longer one. Some limit this to the Companions' disagreement, because of their precedence in ijtihād and knowledge of the contexts of the texts; the ḥāshiya adds that some Ḥanafīs allow a third opinion after a later era's disagreement, though a real distinction is not apparent.tap to reveal
What is composite ijmāʿ, and what is 'ʿadam al-qāʾil bi-l-faṣl'?
The composite ijmāʿ arises from the disagreement of two opinions that establishes the invalidity of a third. One species is called 'no one separates': for example, in annulment of marriage for defects (leprosy, madness, impotence, ratq, qarn), one group said no annulment, another annulment in all; a distinction between them is a third opinion no one holds. The ḥāshiya (from al-Tawḍīḥ) divides cases by whether the disputed matter is one ruling in one object (impurity from a non-passage: wuḍūʾ for us, washing the place for al-Shāfiʿī, so 'nothing is required' is void) or in several (touching a woman and non-passage emission: saying both nullify, or neither, breaks both the Ḥanafī and the Shāfiʿī). Another example: the father or grandfather's compulsion of the adult girl for marriage: for us neither has it, for al-Shāfiʿī both; so granting the father but not the grandfather is void.tap to reveal

1 · Re-teach

Prefatory matter: the author, the translation, and what ʿilm al-ṣarf is

The Arabic edition opens with the publisher's note, the translator's preface (Wālī Khān al-Muẓaffar), a life of Muftī ʿInāyat Aḥmad Kākorawī, the annotator's foreword and a short chapter on what ʿilm al-ṣarf is (subject, aim, benefit, founder). The author composed the book in exile on the Andaman islands in 1271 AH for his friend Ḥāfiẓ Wazīr ʿAlī; its Persian original was taught for generations in the subcontinent and was rendered into Urdu (Muftī Rafīʿ ʿUthmānī) and then Arabic. Remember the layout: a muqaddima, four abwāb, a khātima, and an appended mulḥaq on the khawāṣṣ of the abwāb.
Who first compiled ṣarf as an independent science according to this edition?
The note lists Abū ʿUthmān al-Māzinī (d. c. 248/249 AH) as the commonly cited first compiler, but the author's school prefers Imām Abū Ḥanīfa, who wrote a treatise al-Maqṣūd on ṣarf; before them ṣarf questions were discussed inside naḥw.tap to reveal
What is the architecture of ʿIlm al-Ṣīgha?
Muqaddima (classification of kalima/fiʿl/ism); Bāb 1 on the ṣiyagh; Bāb 2 on the abwāb and what relates to them; Bāb 3 on the taṣārīf of non-ṣaḥīḥ verbs; Bāb 4 on useful ifādāt; Khātima on difficult Qurʾānic forms; plus the Mulḥaq on khawāṣṣ al-abwāb.tap to reveal
How did the Maktaba al-Bushrā edition reorganize the book for teaching?
It divided the text into 53 numbered lessons (durūs), each followed by questions and two exercises (one on words, one on Qurʾānic verses and Arabic sentences), added extra maṣādir and difficult ṣiyagh, and printed matn first for memorisation.tap to reveal

Muqaddima: kalima, the five taqsīmāt of the verb, and kinds of ism

The muqaddima classifies the word. A kalima is a single-meaning utterance and is fiʿl, ism or ḥarf. The verb is then cut five ways: by meaning/time (māḍī, muḍāriʿ, amr), by agent (maʿrūf/majhūl), by affirmation (muthbat/manfī), by number of root letters (thulāthī/rubāʿī, each mujarrad or mazīd) and by kind of root letters (ṣaḥīḥ, mahmūz, muʿtall, muḍāʿaf). The ten sub-kinds collapse into the seven that ṣarf cares about. The ism is mashtaq, maṣdar or jāmid. A sixth classification (lāzim/mutaʿaddī) is postponed to the thulāthī mujarrad.
How many kinds of verb arise from taqsīm 5, and how many does ṣarf actually treat?
Ten: one ṣaḥīḥ, three mahmūz (fāʾ/ʿayn/lām), four muʿtall (mithāl, ajwaf, nāqiṣ, lafīf, with lafīf split in two), one muḍāʿaf; but because of the volume of ṣarf rules the weight falls on seven: ṣaḥīḥ, mithāl, muḍāʿaf, lafīf, nāqiṣ, mahmūz, ajwaf.tap to reveal
What is ṣaḥīḥ?
A verb whose roots contain neither hamza nor weak letters nor two identical letters, like ḍaraba.tap to reveal
Define māḍī, muḍāriʿ, amr as the book does.
Māḍī indicates occurrence of an act in the past (faʿala); muḍāriʿ indicates occurrence now or in the future (yafʿalu); amr is a request for an act from the addressed agent in the future (ifʿal).tap to reveal

Bāb 1 (ṣiyagh): māḍī, muḍāriʿ, lan/an/kay/idhan, lam/lammā

Bāb 1 sets out the ṣiyagh before the abwāb. The māḍī and muḍāriʿ each have fourteen forms (maʿrūf and majhūl, muthbat and manfī): three each for the absent masculine and feminine, three for the addressed masculine and feminine and two for the speaker. The muḍāriʿ is recognised by the letters of 'anaytu' and by sin/sawfa/lan; a particle then changes it: lan (and an, kay, idhan) puts it in naṣb and drops the nūn of the five afʿāl; lam (and its sisters) jazms it and turns its time to the past. Differences between lam and lammā are given.
How are the fourteen ṣiyagh of the māḍī arranged?
Three for the absent masculine (faʿala, faʿalā, faʿalū), three for the absent feminine (faʿalat, faʿalatā, faʿalna), three for the addressed masculine, three for the addressed feminine, and two for the speaker (one speaker, and plural/dual together).tap to reveal
What is the only pattern of the māḍī majhūl from the thulāthī mujarrad?
Always fuʿila, with ḍamma on the fāʾ and kasra on the ʿayn; whichever of the six abwāb the verb belongs to.tap to reveal
What is special about tafʿalu and tafʿalāni in the muḍāriʿ?
tafʿalu serves two ṣiyagh (absent feminine singular and addressed masculine singular) and tafʿalāni serves three (absent feminine dual, addressed masculine dual, addressed feminine dual); so the fourteen forms are visually fewer, and the ṣiyagh are told apart by context/origin.tap to reveal

Why is lā not used alone in the negation of the māḍī?

Because the māḍī is a completed event and a bare lā is the ḥarf of nahy/future negation in Arabic; for a past negation it must be repeated (lā … wa lā …) to be acceptable, otherwise mā is used.explain it in your own words — then tap

Nahy and the emphasised future: lām al-taʾkīd with nūn thaqīla / khafīfa

The nahy is the muḍāriʿ jazmed by lā (fourteen ṣiyagh, maʿrūf and majhūl). A general rule: a weak final letter is dropped from a majzūm muḍāriʿ. The emphasised future is built with lām al-taʾkīd at the front and nūn thaqīla or khafīfa at the end. The nūn khafīfa is not used with the dual or the feminine plural. The vowel before the nūn is fatḥa in five ṣiyagh, the iʿrāb-nūn and the weak wāw/yāʾ of the plural are dropped, an alif fāṣila separates the nūn of the feminine plural from the heavy nūn, and the verb is restricted to the future.
What happens to a weak letter at the end of a muḍāriʿ in jazm?
It drops, whether the jazm is by lam or another jāzim.tap to reveal
How is the muḍāriʿ emphasised, and where do lām and nūn stand?
By a fatḥa-lām at its start and a nūn thaqīla or khafīfa at its end; the lām always precedes and the nūn follows; the nūn khafīfa is excluded from the dual and the feminine plural.tap to reveal
What vowel stands before the emphasis nūn?
Fatḥa in five ṣiyagh (yafʿalanna, tafʿalanna (fem. 3 / masc. 2), ʾafʿalanna, nafʿalanna).tap to reveal

Amr: derivation from the muḍāriʿ, its six ṣiyagh, and amr by lām

The amr is built from the muḍāriʿ by dropping its sign; if the letter that follows is vowelled nothing is added, and if sākin a hamzat al-waṣl is added (ḍamma if the ʿayn is ḍamma, kasra if the ʿayn is kasra or fatḥa). The last letter is jazm, the iʿrāb-nūn drops while the nūn of the feminine plural stays, and a weak last letter is dropped. There are six amr ṣiyagh for the addressed; the absent and speaker forms and the whole majhūl use lām al-ṭalab and are really muḍāriʿ. Nūn thaqīla/khafīfa can be added to all.

6 · Re-teach

The six derived nouns: fāʿil, mafʿūl, tafḍīl, ṣifa mushabbaha, āla, ẓarf

From the verb six nouns are derived. Ism al-fāʿil: fāʿil on the thulāthī mujarrad; elsewhere the muḍāriʿ maʿrūf with mīm maḍmūma in place of the sign and kasra before the end. Ism al-mafʿūl: mafʿūl, or the majhūl muḍāriʿ with mīm maḍmūma and fatḥa before the end. Tafḍīl: afʿal (fem. fuʿlā), not from colour or defect nor from beyond thulāthī mujarrad. Ṣifa mushabbaha: stable quality, from a lāzim verb. Ism al-āla: mifʿal, mifʿala, mifʿāl. Ism al-ẓarf (time/place): mafʿal or mafʿil. Dual nūn is kasra, plural nūn fatḥa.
What are the forms of ism al-fāʿil?
Thulāthī mujarrad: fāʿil. Beyond it: the muḍāriʿ maʿrūf of that bāb with the muḍāriʿ sign replaced by a ḍamma-mīm and kasra on the letter before the last (unless already kasra).tap to reveal
What are the forms of ism al-mafʿūl?
Thulāthī mujarrad: mafʿūl; beyond it: the muḍāriʿ majhūl with ḍamma-mīm in place of the sign and fatḥa before the last letter.tap to reveal
What are the conditions and forms of ism al-tafḍīl?
It is on afʿal (fem. fuʿlā), not from colours and defects (where afʿal is a ṣifa mushabbaha, fem. faʿlāʾ), and not from beyond thulāthī mujarrad; for those it uses ashadd (or akthar) + the maṣdar; jamʿ sālim: afʿalūn, fuʿlayāt; jamʿ mukassar: afāʿil, fuʿal.tap to reveal

Assorted fawāʾid on the derived nouns, maṣdars and ordinals

Lesson 10 collects loose points: place nouns that come on mafʿil by usage, the Kūfan view that the maṣdar itself derives from the verb, the faʿla/fiʿla/fuʿla nouns of single act, manner and quantity, the intensive forms (fa ʿʿāl, mifʿāl, faʿūl, faʿīl, faʿil), the difference between mubālagha and tafḍīl, and the ordinal numbers on fāʿil with their compound forms.
How are the noun of once (marra), manner (nawʿ) and quantity formed?
From the thulāthī: faʿla (marra: ḍarba), fiʿla (manner: jilsa, ṣibgha), fuʿla (a measure: luqma); beyond the thulāthī: marra by adding tāʾ to the maṣdar; manner by adding a qarīna.tap to reveal
What distinguishes mubālagha from tafḍīl?
Mubālagha expresses intensity in the doer himself with no comparison (ḍarrāb = one who strikes much); tafḍīl expresses more than something else (aḍrab min Zayd, more strikes than Zayd).tap to reveal

Bāb 2, faṣl 1: the six abwāb of the thulāthī mujarrad; lāzim/mutaʿaddī

Bāb 2 is about the abwāb. The thulāthī mujarrad has six, according to the vowel of the ʿayn in the māḍī and the muḍāriʿ: naṣara yanṣuru, ḍaraba yaḍribu, samiʿa yasmaʿu, fataḥa yaftaḥu, karuma yakrumu, ḥasiba yaḥsibu. The book lists for each the full taṣrīf (māḍī, muḍāriʿ, maṣdar, ism fāʿil/mafʿūl, amr, nahy, ẓarf, āla, tafḍīl). The fatḥ–fatḥ bāb needs a ḥalqī letter as ʿayn or lām; karuma is always lāzim; ḥasiba yaḥsibu is the only ṣaḥīḥ example of its type. Qawāʿid of iftiʿāl: iddaʿā, iṭṭalaba, ithʾara, khaṣṣama

The tāʾ of iftiʿāl changes according to the fāʾ (and for the fourth rule the ʿayn) before it. After dāl, dhāl or zāy it becomes dāl; after ṭāʾ, ẓāʾ, ṣād, ḍād it becomes ṭāʾ; after thāʾ it may become thāʾ; and if the ʿayn of the verb is one of the sibilant/dental letters it may be assimilated and the vowel moved with the hamzat waṣl dropped. The book gives four numbered qawāʿid and explains the reason: the tāʾ is mahmūs while those letters are majhūra, so it is changed to a majhūr letter of the same makhraj.
What becomes of the tāʾ of iftiʿāl after dāl, dhāl and zāy?
It becomes dāl; if the fāʾ is dāl the two dāls are assimilated (iddaʿā); if dhāl three forms occur: iddakara (dhāl→dāl, assimilated), idhdakara (dāl kept), idhdhakara (dāl→dhāl, assimilated); if zāy the dāl stays: izdajara.tap to reveal
What becomes of the tāʾ after ṭāʾ, ẓāʾ, ṣād, ḍād?
It becomes ṭāʾ: iṭṭalaba (necessary idghām), iṭṭalama / iẓṭalama, iṣṭabara / iṣṣabara, iḍṭaraba / iḍḍaraba.tap to reveal
When does the iftiʿāl become khaṣṣama?

Bāb 3: the mahmūz — rules of takhfīf al-hamza and taṣārīf

Bāb 3 turns from the abwāb to the rules of takhfīf (changing the hamza), iʿlāl (changing a weak letter) and idghām. The tamhīd defines them: changing the hamza is takhfīf, changing a weak letter taʿlīl, putting a letter into the next doubled is idghām. The mahmūz rules are nine: the sākina hamza matches the previous vowel (rās, būs, dhīb); a hamza sākina after a vowelled hamza becomes the vowel's letter (āmana); the maftūḥa after ḍamma/kasra may become wāw/yāʾ (jūn, mīr); two vowelled hamzas (awādim, ayimma); khaṭāyā; the transfer of the vowel (yasal, qad aflaḥ); bayna bayna; and the double hamza of the interrogative (a-ʾantum). Faṣl 3: the muḍāʿaf — qawāʿid al-idghām (1–5)
